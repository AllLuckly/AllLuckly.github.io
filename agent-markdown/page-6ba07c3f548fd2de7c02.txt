Draw an original cylindrical teapot with lid, side spout and handle attached to one body. A cylindrical teapot in the Smithsonian American Art Museum collection includes a spout, lid and metal handle. Use that description to understand part roles without copying its particular shape, decoration or dimensions; a static drawing is not a manufacturing plan for a usable vessel.

## Place the body and surrounding space

Create an approximately 1800 × 1600 canvas, with the body in the lower middle, handle space above, spout space at left and shadow room at right. Use a light vertical axis for the body center, then draw a top ellipse, side walls and visible bottom curve. Separate sketch, body, lid, spout, handle and shadow layers and save an editable draft. Size and viewpoint are exercise choices. If the spout touches the frame when reduced, reposition the composition rather than removing its opening.

## Organize the cylinder around shared ellipses

Keep the top opening and bottom curve around the same vertical axis and join their sides smoothly to the walls. If the far bottom line is hidden by the body, do not draw it through the exterior. The near cylindrical face can be lighter, gradually turning darker at the side; avoid a dark line that cuts the body into two pieces. Visible ellipses depend on viewpoint and need not both have complete heavy outlines. If the body looks flat, check curve-to-wall connections before adding value rather than hiding the outline with decoration.

On the sketch, mark the top opening's left and right widest points and front midpoint to inspect their relationship to the vertical axis. Mark the lowest point of the visible bottom curve and check it does not cross the side wall diagonally. If changing the viewpoint, redraw both opening and bottom rather than flattening the lid ellipse alone. Hide the checking marks afterward; they are not grooves on the vessel.

## Seat the lid on the opening

Place a slightly smaller lid face inside the top ellipse, leaving readable thickness at the front. The knob grows near the lid center rather than floating in air. Lid and rim are separate contacting objects: with highlights hidden, the two edges should still be identifiable. If the lid covers the opening without visible support, reduce it or reveal some rim. Do not draw a complete far line through the knob; establish the knob on the lid, then remove the hidden segment.

## Connect the spout to the side and keep its outlet

From the upper middle left side draw a short tapered spout, returning both edges toward the wall, with a small opening at its outer end. Whether it sits before or behind the body depends on the chosen view; neither edge should pass unexplained through the complete wall. Match the opening direction to the tube's bend rather than placing a black dot on the body. If the spout floats like a separate cone, repair the join before adding contact shadow. The illustration does not establish real pouring performance.

## Give the handle two clear attachment points

Choose a narrow metal handle arching over the pot. Mark left and right attachments, connect them with one arc and use a neighboring line for width. Visible segments may pass behind the body or appear at attachments, but depth order must stay consistent. Neither end should stop in the sky or pass through the lid knob. Keep metal handle and ceramic body on separate layers. The exercise borrows only the roles of body, spout, lid and handle; it does not claim to reproduce the museum object's spiral handle.

Keep a body copy without the handle and compare lid legibility before and after adding it. If the arc and knob merge, adjust handle height or local overlap instead of erasing the knob. Unequal end heights can be a perspective choice, but both attachments must remain clear; there is no need to force identical page-space heights.

## Light ceramic and metal differently

Assume upper-left light. Use broad middle values and gradual changes to show the ceramic cylinder turning, while reserving one or two narrower highlights for metal and retaining a darker side. Add limited contact shadows at lid thickness, spout underside and handle attachments without outlining every part in equal solid black. If glaze and background are close, separate them with a local background value or contour edge. Highlights need not all be identical white straight lines.

## Hide texture and inspect all connections

Turn off pattern, highlights and shadow and inspect the body, lid, spout and handle shapes. Trace spout edges back to the body, both handle ends to attachments and the lid rim near the top ellipse. For white gaps check layer order and contour edges; if the spout seems to exit the far side of the pot, repair overlap rather than darken the shadow. Keep the previous draft and fix one junction at a time, comparing reduced views.

## Save and inspect an output separately

Save the editable draft and export through a control actually available in the installed version, then open the image elsewhere. Check balance at small size and inspect spout opening, knob and both handle ends at larger size. Reopen the draft and hide only the metal handle layer; ceramic should remain. If output differs, check the chosen file and hidden layers first. Describe an original ceramic teapot composition; leave unperformed export, reopening or device steps pending. The depicted opening and spout do not establish capacity, heat resistance or safe use.
