“So punctual” can be praise or form a contrast in a lateness scene. Those words alone often cannot decide. Select a contextual passage in Book Isle and separately record literal meaning, scene evidence and interpretation. Irony is not a code that reverses every statement, and the app is not assumed to identify tone, authorial intention or a character’s true mental state automatically.

## Record the speaker with surrounding context

Choose a short permitted passage from fiction, an essay or drama. Record book, edition, chapter, sentence locator and speaker, distinguishing dialogue, narrator commentary and quotation. Words in quotation marks are not automatically an authorial confession. Retain locators for a little context on either side rather than copying the entire text into notes. Save necessary short quotations through supported selection or manual locators/descriptions without an assumed recognition tool. Check one possible instance of literal praise rather than preclassifying the whole work as satire and forcing every sentence to fit.

## Write the literal proposition before reversing anything

First explain the sentence in its ordinary literal sense, identifying target and degree: “I am so punctual” literally praises the speaker’s arrival timing. Do not begin with “it certainly means I am late”, which would reduce evidence to confirmation of a predetermined answer. The distinction between verbal irony and literal meaning in [McGraw-Hill’s poetry glossary](<https://glencoe.mheducation.com/sites/dl/free/0073124265/322267/poetryglossary.html>) supplies a concept; judge the selected passage from its own context. The term does not infer intention for you, and the glossary’s literary examples are not copied.

## Find contrast in an invented lateness scenario

For an original miniature, assume he agreed to enter at seven, the performance ended at eight, and he reached the door only at eight ten, saying “I am so punctual”. Record the three facts—agreement at seven, end at eight, arrival at eight ten—before comparing literal punctuality with the agreement. Self-mockery or irony becomes a supported reading, but this alone does not establish an author’s criticism of a social institution. These times are invented, not Book Isle records or literary quotations. The late speaker refers to himself; another person’s comment would need separate attribution and tonal checks.

## Change the scene to test against a fixed decoding rule

Make a control changing only arrival to six fifty, while the agreement remains seven. Without other contrary evidence, the same “I am so punctual” may be read literally; the previous irony reading does not reverse every identical phrase. Compare both, retaining the sentence and the changed condition, and attribute the difference to context rather than typography or colour. This tests your interpretive rule, not a claim that every early arriver lacks ironic intent. Actual works may contain additional tone, action or relationship clues requiring separate textual checks.

## Separate textual contrast from psychological speculation

Return to locatable behaviour, outcomes and scene descriptions, keeping interpretation and alternatives in a separate field. A smile does not automatically mean mockery, and exaggeration does not automatically establish verbal irony; specify the relationship to the literal proposition. Do not invent intonation without audio. With audio, record performer identity rather than equating performance choices with an author’s mind. Preserve uncertainty among sincere praise, a slip or self-mockery with the evidence each would need. Book Isle notes retain clues without deciding their truth for you.

## Separate character, author and reader judgements

Use three labelled fields: what the character literally says, what current context supports and what you provisionally infer. Clearly distinguish quotations from interpretation. A character’s ironic utterance does not establish authorial endorsement, and a reader noticing contrast does not prove the character recognises it. Add later revisions with reasons and source locations rather than deleting the first note to imply uninterrupted correctness. This is a reading process, not improved app-analysis accuracy. Source content and changes in your judgement are different objects.

## Retrieve the statement together with its context

Leave the reader and retrieve the sentence through chapter, wording and locator, then recover its contextual evidence, checking the same speaker and scene. If only an excerpt card remains, restore provenance rather than treating attractive presentation as completed interpretation. Export important notes through available means and inspect literal proposition, evidence, interpretation and uncertainty as separate fields outside the app, preserving the book separately. Share only necessary lawful quotations and label the invented exercise. Do not quote a personal rewrite as something a real author said. Restore your prior reading position afterward.

## Complete evidence before choosing an irony label

Without contextual contrast, retain a literal reading instead of forcing irony to finish the exercise. For mismatched time or target, inspect context and attribution rather than resetting the book or editing source wording. Explain new evidence when interpretations change instead of overwriting notes into a process-free conclusion. Retain literal meaning, locatable context, comparison reading and limits, each tied to text or an explicitly invented example. The result is supported, revisable close reading, not a diagnosis of mental state or automatic software identification of every instance of humour and irony.
