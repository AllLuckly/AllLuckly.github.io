Study planar turns, same-colour values and overlapping wings through an origami crane.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-paper-crane-planes/>), translated on 2026-10-27. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

An origami crane depends on planes and overlaps rather than fur or foliage. Draw a tabletop crane by connecting chest, neck, tail and wings as folded planes. Establish a single paper colour before adding selected edges and creases, without using decoration to hide unclear construction.

## Establish the crane’s silhouette

Create an approximately 1600×1400 px canvas with head upper left and tail upper right, leaving space around the wings. Begin with a diamond-shaped chest, then extend neck and tail from opposite corners. Make one wing nearer than the other. Check head, tail and wing identity before internal creases.

Separate background, construction, paper colour and shadow. Test broad-plane and fine-edge brushes. Bend the head tip into a short beak, keeping the tail longer and straighter. Correct these silhouette differences before decorations and retain a silhouette-stage version for later comparison.

## Build the chest from three connected planes

Divide the chest into lit, side and small lower planes meeting at clear folds. Use three related cream-grey values rather than unrelated bright colours. Begin without the deepest dark. A coherent central junction establishes folded paper more effectively than numerous crease lines.

Light from the upper left, judging each plane’s orientation instead of dividing the object mechanically into white left and black right. Keep planes broadly even. Restore clear folds if the chest becomes spherical; if it resembles disconnected paper pieces, align their shared junctions.

## Give the neck and tail thin side planes

Build the tapering neck from a main face and narrow side, joined to the chest by a definite fold. Turn the head planes with the beak and use only a tiny underside dark. Establish the connected angular path before thin edges, avoiding a black outline that resembles a metal rod.

Keep the tail straighter but retain a narrow turning plane. Interrupt its root where the chest occludes it. Repair a detached-looking tail through the shared fold at its attachment, not a heavier outline. Neck and tail values may differ because their orientations differ.

## Separate the wings through overlap and width

Make the near wing broader and the far wing narrower with its root behind the chest. Vary tip heights but connect both to the same body. The near wing overlaps a chest edge while the chest hides the far root. Check these colour-mass overlaps without the edge layer first.

Add a narrow folded lip with a small value difference rather than covering each wing in wrinkles. Check wing axes, attachments and lighting if they seem detached. Keep the far wing simpler and allow selected edges to approach the background instead of enclosing it with the darkest line.

## Use paper edges and shadows for different purposes

Use slightly darker thin edges only at selected near tips, neck turns and chest underside. Keep two or three meaningful crease lines rather than a grid. Restrained planes and varied edges suggest paper; small hand-drawn deviations need not be corrected into technical drafting.

Add a small deep contact below the chest and broader pale wing shadows to the lower right. Approximate the silhouettes while preserving their relation to the object. Correct contact if it floats; reduce heavy outlines and shadow areas if it resembles a solid plastic toy.

## Review the undecorated structure and save

Check head and tail small, shared folds at normal size and accidental gaps close up. Save the plain version before optional patterns. A pattern should turn with planes and disappear behind occluded edges. The single-colour structure must work before decoration is allowed to conceal anything.

Keep the native layers and a PNG named with view and version. Next, raise only the near wing while keeping chest, light and background fixed, rebuilding its attachment and shadow. Record a specific changed plane rather than merely saying it looks better; test connections rather than memorised outlines.
