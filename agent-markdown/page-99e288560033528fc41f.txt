Observe a tapered takeaway cup, fitted lid rim and visible sipping opening to practice elliptical perspective.

## Choose a specific reference

Huhtamaki’s fiber-lid material shows lids with sipping openings fitted to paper cups and also lists versions without openings. Lid material and shape vary by product. Choose a permitted oblique photo showing cup and lid connection, recording source and model. Whether the opening is visible depends on the view; do not make every lid identical. This exercise depicts appearance and makes no claim about insulation, leak resistance or recycling.

## Save the canvas and arrange space

Create an approximately 1400 by 1800 portrait canvas in the current Aquarell interface and save an editable original; dimensions are suggestions. Place the cup slightly below center with space above its lid and below the base, and decide where the table lies. Separate sketch, cup, lid, background and shadow using available layers and actual device labels. Lightly mark a vertical center axis and table guide.

## Build the tapered cup body

Mark top and bottom widths from the reference, then connect them with sides that narrow slightly downward to suggest a frustum. Follow the actual taper rather than imposing one standard cone. Compare height with top width and reserve any visible narrow elliptical bottom edge. At thumbnail size check that the axis passes through the body; if side slopes conflict, revise the sketch rather than covering the problem with lettering or decoration.

## Draw the lid silhouette

At the cup top draw an elliptical lid rim aligned in perspective with the bottom edge. If the lid has a raised center, first suggest its top with a smaller ellipse or curve and connect it to the outer rim; follow a flat lid’s actual shape instead. The lid should meet or fit the cup rather than float above it as a separate disc. Hide color temporarily and check whether the rim extends too far beyond either side.

## Separate front and back rim

From a slightly elevated view the near rim commonly hides part of the cup opening. Draw the visible near edge first, then the far edge as the photo shows, stopping hidden lines at occlusion. Two close curves can suggest rim thickness, but avoid a horizontal band as thick as the body. A fiber lid may reflect differently from hard plastic; depict the photo without inferring physical specifications. Check that the near rim meets both cup sides consistently in perspective.

## Place the opening only if visible

If the reference shows a sipping opening, mark its relative position on the lid and suggest it with a small flattened shape or dark patch. Omit it if on the far side or hidden by the viewpoint; do not let it cross the rim or float outside. Huhtamaki also lists lids without openings, so the feature is not universal. Keep it restrained at small display sizes instead of making it as large as the whole lid.

## Separate cup and lid with values

Block the cup in a middle value from the reference, using a lighter vertical area and darker side to suggest curvature. Give the lid a different light gray or dark color and preserve local contrast with the cup; paper need not look like polished metal. Lit and shaded rim areas should follow the same light source as the body. If the cup looks like a flat trapezoid, broaden middle and side transitions and recheck the rim ellipse before adding decoration.

## Place the table shadow

Let the lowest point of the cup meet the table and add a simplified shadow toward the unlit side, clearer at contact and softer farther away. Hide it first to check the base; if the cup floats, fix the bottom-to-table relationship rather than covering it with a darker shadow. For a white-background product photo, table and light direction can be composition choices and should not be presented as observed source details.

## Compare lid perspective in thumbnails

Duplicate the sketch into one thumbnail with coordinated lid and base ellipse directions and another with the lid deliberately tilted differently. Keep cup body and table fixed and choose the version closer to the reference. If the entire image uses a tilted viewpoint, observe cup and table consistently rather than rotating the lid alone. Keep the guide axis only in the sketch layer.

## Check connections and crop

Zoom into both rim ends, opening-to-lid relationship and cup-to-table junction. If a white gap appears, decide whether it is visible background or a layer mismatch; if the opening crosses the rim, revise its position in the sketch. At phone reading size ensure lid and body remain distinct and corners do not clip rim or base. Omit tiny lettering that cannot be verified or read.

## Save and reopen output

Save the editable original, then use a share or export entry actually shown in the current version to save an image separately. Reopen and inspect rim, opening, body, shadow and crop, then return to identify sketch, object and background layers. If the lid is missing, check layer visibility and file location first; a flattened image without layers does not establish original damage. Keep source and permission notes and mark unperformed device and export steps pending.

## Record an observation checklist

Before finishing, note where rim meets cup, whether the opening is actually visible in the photograph, and whether the base meets the table. With a white-background reference the third item can be a design choice rather than an observation; mark a hidden opening unknown. This checks drawn connections and does not establish whether a real lid is sealed.
