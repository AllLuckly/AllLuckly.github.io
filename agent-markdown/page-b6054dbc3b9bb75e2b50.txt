## Declare exhaustive states and a row-vector convention

A and B are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. Given the present state, the next-state distribution does not depend on earlier history, and the same transition rule applies at every step. ## Solve stationarity and substitute both components

Let π=(a,1−a) satisfy πP=π. Then a=(3/4)a+(1/2)(1−a), giving a=2/3 and b=1/3. Substitution yields 1/2+1/6=2/3 for A and 1/6+1/6=1/3 for B, summing to 1. Split the 960px stationary bar 640/320. ## Prove approach rather than finite-step equality

A probabilities satisfy a\_next=1/2+a\_current/4, hence a\_next−2/3=(a\_current−2/3)/4. From a0=1, a\_n=2/3+1/(3·4^n). The extra term is positive for every finite nonnegative integer n, so the distribution approaches stationarity without exactly reaching it in finite steps. Distinguish equality from approximation; a displayed 0.667 does not prove exact equality. This recurrence proves convergence for this model, not unconditionally for chains with other rules, absorbing classes or periodicity. Starting at π would keep π at every step, consistent with the strict finite-step deviation from certain A. Deliver the initial condition with the closed form instead of applying that solution unchanged to every starting distribution.
