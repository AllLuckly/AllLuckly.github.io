Use two distinguishable public practice highlights to check source identity, widget display and original notes separately.

## Prepare two nonprivate highlights

In a short authorized book, select two self-written or public practice passages such as “Spring wind crosses the bridge” and “The steps are wet after rain.” Create two highlights or notes in Book Isle and record their book, chapter and original text. Avoid private excerpts: other people may see the Home Screen. Stop if the installed version does not let you confirm that the notes were saved.

## Make a two-note comparison card

Label the two practice passages A and B, recording book, chapter, full source text and any words you entered when creating them. If highlights and notes are distinct objects in the app, list them separately and do not treat a color or highlight state as note text. Distinctive wording helps distinguish partially displayed excerpts. Leave the page and return to confirm both objects remain before using the record as your widget baseline.

## Find the widget actually available

Use the system Home Screen editing interface to open the widget list, search for Book Isle and look for Note Review. Apple’s widget guide covers the general operation; availability depends on installed version and device. If absent, record the system version and actual state. Do not treat the app icon as a widget or reinstall the app or delete notes.

## Read the information the widget actually displays

Add an available size and record the displayed title, excerpt and any controls. Compare every visible excerpt with the two prepared notes; truncation is not missing data. If another book appears, record its content and whether the source can be identified rather than assuming replacement. Do not claim a book-specific scope when no selection option is visible.

## Check one visible change or opening action

If a switching control is visible, record the current excerpt, operate it once and observe the next excerpt and feedback. Without a control, record only the display rather than repeatedly tapping to simulate a switch. Open the corresponding original note in Book Isle and verify its text and book. Opening the app, opening a book and reaching the precise highlight are distinct outcomes.

## Troubleshoot apparent mismatches

First confirm the original note is still available in the app, then compare its chapter and keywords. If a widget shows partial or older text, record the observation time, revisit the app and inspect again without assuming a fixed refresh interval. Mark identity unknown if the excerpt is insufficient. Do not erase notes or change sync settings to force a refresh.

## Avoid treating display order as completeness

A widget may show one item, and visible contents may change with system refresh or a real control. Record observation time and visible text, then match them to the full A and B records. Seeing A alone does not prove B was deleted; seeing B first does not prove its original time or order changed. If identity cannot be determined, mark it unknown and check the originals in the app. Do not infer random selection, sorting rules or automatic rotation without evidence.

## Check location after viewing an excerpt

When part of A appears, compare the contiguous visible wording with your A card before opening the original note. One book can repeat the same sentence in different chapters; source identity requires title, chapter and surrounding text together. If a tap only opens the app home, record that result separately from manually finding the note. Preserve any mismatch if it opens elsewhere. Page layout can change page numbers, so use chapter and text anchors.

## Record the time boundary between widget and source notes

Record the visible in-app states of A and B separately from the excerpt and time observed on the widget. Seeing A first and B later proves only that two observations differed; it does not establish a fixed rotation interval or instant system refresh. After leaving and returning to Book Isle, check the original notes first, then inspect the widget. If notes remain but the widget has not updated, record the two outcomes separately. Do not edit or delete a sole note to manufacture a difference or invent an exact refresh time. If the widget reveals private text, use the nonprivate sample and remove the widget temporarily if the user wishes, preserving the original notes.

## Finish by checking book identity

If both excerpts come from the same book, check chapter and original context; repeated short wording alone does not identify the source. Confirm both practice highlights remain in the app before deciding whether to remove the test widget.

## Check display privacy and restore the setup

Before leaving, inspect whether the Home Screen reveals text you do not want exposed; adjust or remove the test widget through the actual system interface. Record system and app versions, widget size, visible excerpts, actions and comparisons with original notes; label unperformed behavior untested. Confirm both practice notes remain. Public release notes describe widgets; no physical-device or privacy-visibility test is claimed.
