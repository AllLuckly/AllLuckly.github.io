Draw an adult southern cassowary standing sideways with its head turned slightly towards the viewer. ## Connect head, neck and body in a tall composition

Use a portrait canvas of about 1700 × 2200 pixels. Put the body in the lower middle, raise the neck upper-left, leave casque clearance above and shadow space below. Block a broad body ellipse and attach the neck ahead of the shoulder rather than as a thin tube from mid-back. Support the body with ventral legs and keep the head modest, not a hat-wearing human bust. Separate feathers, bare head/neck, wattles and feet from the sketch on grey-green. ## Place the casque on the crown, not ahead of the bill base

Build a raised brown-grey casque on the crown, connecting its front and rear edges to the head and varying thickness with the three-quarter turn. Extend the bill from the face, with the eye on the side beneath the casque, not a third bill above. Establish volume in two values rather than a black triangular sticker. ## Hang two wattles from the bare neck

Draw two soft wattles hanging from the lower front neck, each with an attachment and tip. Make the near one broader and allow the neck or near wattle to hide part of the far one. They are not earrings beside the eyes or two open bills. Temporarily number them one and two, preserving occlusion. If only one reads, clarify the far attachment rather than inventing a third. Red simplifies the reference individual’s colour, while blue may vary over the neck; colour does not diagnose mood or physiology.

## Turn the bare-neck to black-feather boundary into the shoulder

Join blue bare neck to the black shoulder with a few irregular feather edges, not a tidy clothing collar or black marks covering the face. Model back and belly first with warm black, cool black and mid-grey, retaining lighter planes for volume. ## Ground both legs before separating three toes and the inner claw

Extend both legs below the body, clearer near and offset far, with feet sharing the ground. Each foot has three toes; show only visible parts in the main view and optionally add a frontal checking diagram locating the inner claw. Emphasise that claw without making every toe an identical huge curved blade or inventing a long backward toe for perching. For a fork-like foot, restore toe roots and foot volume. For a lost far foot, adjust contact values first. This checks form without simulating kicking, running or human contact.

## Let hair-like feather strokes hang with the body, not radiate

Select three small feather groups from shoulder through back to flank, directing strokes rear-downwards or hanging with the body. Keep near groups clearer and far groups weaker, with no steel-needle tips. Begin with five to seven strokes per group as drawing practice, not anatomical counts. Use a broad grey shoulder/back light under upper-left illumination rather than outlining every feather white. Shade bare neck more smoothly and casque more firmly. For tangled wool, remove crossing marks and restore broad colour masses instead of extending every stroke outside the silhouette.

## Check casque, bill, paired wattles and the foot diagram

Repair a second-bill appearance at the casque root and move ear-like wattles back to the neck. If occlusion obscures toe counts, keep the main perspective and explain with the diagram.
