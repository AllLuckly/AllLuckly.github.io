English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-banyan-aerial-prop-root-branch-ground/>), translated on 2026-11-12. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Banyan roots can become a row of ropes beneath foliage with no origin or ground status. Reference Ficus benghalensis aerial/prop roots and draw one horizontal branch, two grounded columns and one fine hanging root in Aquarell. Three roots define an original composition, not a whole-tree total. Prop roots are not separately planted trees, and a still image establishes no age, stability or health.

## Establish main trunk, horizontal branch and ground

Use an approximately1900×1400 px landscape canvas with pale grey-green background, main trunk left and a broad branch extending slightly upward right. Recede the ground gently and retain three readable spaces below the branch; distant crown remains quiet. Separate trunk, branch, columns, hanging root and ground shadow; use warm grey-brown. Soften roof-frame-like right angles and vary thickness naturally. Hide roots temporarily if three independent trees and check trunk-to-branch continuity first. Size/tilt are composition, not height, area or load data. Add no climbing action requiring safety assessment.

## Give each aerial root a clear branch origin

Mark three origins beneath the branch, not hanging from blank canopy or leaf tips. Trace guides to their ends, then add basal wrapping and occlusion while keeping the branch continuous. A root may emerge from behind the branch, but its hidden path must connect without showing through. Repair thickness/texture if rope-like rather than adding knots. Clarify downward direction and soil status if downward branches. Spacing is composition, not a fixed biological interval or random filling presented as a growth rule.

## Give two grounded columns distinct contact points

Make the middle-left and right roots thicker grounded columns, near clearer/broader and far quieter/narrower, both meeting the same ground plane. Allow modest base spread, not umbrella feet. Check depth-dependent contact heights so a higher screen position is not a floating far root. Vary equal-diameter stone columns and soften straight edges. Remove separate crowns if independent trees and retain the original overhead branch origin. Illustrated thickness proves neither age, load capacity nor independence from the parent; preserve connection.

## Keep one fine aerial root visibly above ground

Place the third finer root between columns, ending visibly above soil with background beneath its tip. Do not bridge it to the ground with dark shadow. Slight bends or sparse branching are possible without knotting around columns. Taper and reconnect its origin if a cut rope, not a sliced end face. Check the upper attachment if an isolated line. Ungrounded is this pose’s state, not a prediction of rooting, death or human cutting, nor grounds for real plant treatment.

## Distinguish similar woody surfaces by attachment

Trunk and thick prop roots can share grey-brown wood and longitudinal texture, but name their relationships separately: trunk rises into branch, prop roots descend from it to soil. Equal thickness does not make three trees. Use temporary colours to check origins/endpoints and restore material after confirming all three belong to this branch. Resolve crossing occlusion rather than attaching a blocked column to a distant tree. Real individual identification needs separate evidence; this illustration explains selected connections, not automatic counting of genetic individuals in a banyan stand.

## Separate grounded contact shadows from a hanging-root shadow

Keep grounded-base shadows firmer and softer outward. Any hanging-root shadow must not become a solid extension from its tip; a ground gap may remain. Light upper left, coordinating branch underside and column-side shade with sparse foliage shadows. Repair ground contacts if floating instead of adding grass. Restore a light gap below the fine root if it becomes a third column. Quiet background vegetation and retain origins rather than hiding them in dark foliage and invoking natural complexity to avoid checking. Spatial evidence matters more than leaf count.

## Trace every root from branch to endpoint

At roughly quarter size, identify main trunk and branch, then two grounded roots and one hanging, enlarging three origins afterward. Quiet distant columns and repeated texture if a forest-row, emphasising one branch rather than numbered explanations. Align an occluded fine-root segment if floating beside it. Use limited canopy masses without counting every leaf or inventing rootless trees for naturalism. Check in silhouette, then restore brown/green. If texture hides joints again, adjust contrast locally instead of adding more marks.

## Save shared-tree attachment and root-position evidence

Keep native layers and inspect a separate PNG for branch-root origins, two soil contacts, one gap above soil and continuous trunk/branch. Repair woody taper and natural joints if a rope tent rather than adding rope decoration. Restore endpoints if columns look like independent trees, not merely rename them. Record Indian banyan and three illustrative roots in a local study. Other figs need freshly checked aerial-root forms; not every tree has such columns. This establishes visible connections, not real stability, age, pruning, visitor safety or measured specimens.
