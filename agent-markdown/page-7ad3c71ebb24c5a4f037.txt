A wooden clothespin needs jaws, handles and spring region to form one connected arrangement, not two loose wood strips. Draw a simplified stationary pin on a thin fabric edge, focusing on shared axis, visible coil and cloth occlusion without squeezing or assembly instruction. Inspect layers manually in Aquarell. Grain follows wood and cannot hide disconnected parts; a readable exterior is not proof of real grip or load capacity.

## Establish jaws and both body directions

Use an approximately 1800×1300 px landscape canvas with pale blue-grey background, jaws upper right and handles lower left. An oblique side view separates two slightly waisted body envelopes, leaving a central spring region rather than parallel chopsticks. Separate sketch, far/near wood, spring, cloth and shadow; use pale honey brown. Label which jaw and handle belong to each body. Repair middle/jaw relations if stacked-ruler-like or reduce excessive crossing if scissor-like without metal blades. Pixels are compositional, not standard dimensions.

## Give both bodies one central pivot region

Set one central pivot region and relate each body’s jaw and handle directions to it, not separate left/right centres. Inspect thin axes and coherent middle occlusion without unexplained penetration. If a small jaw opening accompanies collapsed handles, repair their directional relation instead of darkening gaps. This slightly open pose holds thin fabric; it is not a product range-of-motion specification. Body depth order should remain readable with spring hidden; a spring cannot supply a mysterious connection for misplaced wood.

## Draw only visible spring portions

Place a small visible coil at the shared transverse axis, partly hidden between wooden bodies. Show a few readable arcs, not every turn through the wood. Suggest short spring legs reaching appropriate wood surfaces without floating ends. Reduce size and correct axis if band-like, or simplify dense dark arcs if tangled. Visible turn count is illustrative, not an elasticity measurement. Omit hidden winding, preload and assembly details; mark concealed portions only in the diagnostic sketch.

## Insert fabric between both jaws

Insert a thin pale grey-red fabric piece from the right between jaws, with modest edge thickness rather than a plank forcing them apart. Near wood covers a small edge portion and far wood lies behind; solve this order prior to texture. Repair enclosure if fabric is pasted outside, or soften a biting black line and retain continuity if apparently severed. Give fabric a readable extent within the frame rather than disappearance along a jaw-parallel line. Illustrated gripping does not prove slip resistance, and hanging weights would not validate the drawing.

## Run grain separately along each body

Light upper left, with pale warm near wood, grey-brown sides and quieter far wood sharing that light. Use two or three quiet longitudinal grain marks on each body, never across the spring joining them into one. Add restrained grain/end colour if plastic-like; reduce deep cracks and restore broad light if rotten-looking. Give spring sparse cool grey-white reflection, not white every turn. Keep visible end-face texture distinct from side grain without rings on every corner. Material marks must preserve body continuity.

## Locate shadows at jaws and support

Keep cloth/wood contact short and tight, with lower-right ground shadow clearer near low support and softer higher up. Avoid one black seam joining jaws, pivot and whole side. Hide shadow to test complete wood and fabric, then restore placement. Reconnect low contour if floating or reduce black base and restore depth if flattened. Simplify spring shadow rather than duplicating a dark coil tangle. Keep background quiet; a clothesline or clutter cannot substitute for an unclear central connection.

## Trace both sketch axes back to the jaws

Keep both wooden centrelines, shared axis and fabric edge. Trace near handle to near jaw and far handle to far jaw without switching bodies at occlusion. Repair wrong joins rather than relying on matching wood colour. Check spring legs reaching plausible wood surfaces, concealed paths in sketch only. Change one contour or spring arc with cloth and light fixed. Another clip type needs fresh reference; this wooden torsion-spring appearance does not apply universally to one-piece plastic or leaf-spring designs.

## Keep the spring from swallowing the pivot in export

Save the layered original and inspect fabric at jaws, coil and handle ends in a PNG at use size. Reduce arc count and darkness if the spring becomes a black dot rather than brightening all wood. Separate local temperature and edges where fabric merges. Record faults such as a far spring leg crossing near wood. Judge two bodies, one axis region and clear cloth occlusion, not maximum coil detail. No squeezing, assembly or load test occurred. A changed opening needs revised shared-axis and cloth-contact relations, not simply separated jaw tips.
