## 1. Identify playlist type and evidence scope

Retain an authorized playlist copy, address, acquisition time and provenance. Without it, use the excerpt and mark real fields unavailable instead of guessing tags from video. Separate master, regular media and I-frame-only identities. A shared m3u8 extension does not establish complete sound/action playback. Preserve full media/configuration and do not modify remote files or remove sources during inspection.

## 2. Define model times for three entries

The excerpt has three four-second entries. Stipulate presentation times zero/four/eight and an ending at twelve seconds. Names supply no files, encoding, initialization data or access. This text cannot certify playable media or three episodes. Keep frame times and EXTINF separate; optional empty titles and filename letters do not identify plot events.

## 3. Read intervals under the special rule

Under [RFC 8216’s I-frame-playlist rule](<https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216.html#section-4.3.3.6>), each entry describes one I-frame, with duration to the next frame’s presentation or the end for the last. The model has three four-second intervals totaling twelve. An interval is neither complete action nor a one-frame-per-second claim. Mark points before spacing, without turning a time interval into an image count.

## 4. Keep regular-playlist semantics distinct

Compare a separately identified regular playlist and check for I-FRAMES-ONLY. Removing a tag does not convert specialized resources into a normal film or restore intermediate pictures/sound. Separate declarations from resource contents. Equal four-second numbers do not establish equal completeness; unavailable media bytes remain unknown.

## 5. Check initialization and access separately

The specification also imposes initialization conditions; nonexistent teaching resources cannot be certified from plausible syntax. Real creators must supply inspectable encoding/initialization evidence rather than one success screenshot. Accessible URLs may still return HTML or errors instead of required media. Record the actual object, preserve normal checks and retain complete owned media as a separate fallback. Do not bypass restricted access or disable validation to make the example work.

## 6. Record only actual iCouldPlay controls and observations

Play an actual source through existing controls and verify identity, opening anchor and sound. Record preview/seeking behavior only if the installed app exposes it; a format’s possible trick-play use is not an implemented button. Visible stills do not establish missing action in the complete film. Identify complete and specialized sources separately so similarly named playlists do not merge observations.

## 7. Troubleshoot interval and frame-count confusion

Twelve frames confuses three I-frame entries with twelve seconds. Treating every entry as four seconds of full video calls for checking playlist type rather than speed changes. Preserve selected URL/time/controls for differences from normal playback without inventing internal resource requests. Change one identified condition and retain originals; unperformed resource checks remain unresolved, not all-HLS certification.

## 8. Return to the complete source and keep the timeline

Deliver type, three times/intervals, total range, resource-check status and observations. Explain frame versus interval and unresolved initialization/encoding/app features.
