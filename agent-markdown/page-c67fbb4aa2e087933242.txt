Paint an adult bald eagle perched in profile, using its white head and tail, yellow hooked bill and dark brown body to establish recognition. The U.S. National Park Service describes adult white head and tail, brown plumage and yellow bill and feet. This is a still pose; plumage in an illustration does not establish an individual's sex, health or exact age.

## Read the source and prepare a canvas

Read the source and study photographs for the relationships among head, hooked bill, body, folded wing, tail, feet and branch. Juvenile plumage may differ; this exercise explicitly chooses adult appearance. Do not approach or disturb wild birds. Make an approximately 1600 × 1800 portrait canvas, save an editable draft and separate sketch, branch, body, head and tail, feet and background. Dimensions organize the picture; use controls actually visible in your version.

## Place branch and body first

Sketch a gently slanted branch low in the image and a large dark body above it. Place the head toward the upper left and tail toward the lower right so gaze and balance are clear. Mark chest and back arcs lightly, leaving space for feet to connect with the branch. At thumbnail size, is the bird near the branch rather than floating? Move the sketch before drawing feathers if it sits too far away. Do not use shadows to disguise an unconnected foot.

## Connect the white head to the dark body

Make a smaller rounded head joined at its rear to shoulder and back, leaving the front for the hooked bill. Block the head in warm light gray-white, using brighter white on its lit side so it remains distinct from a pale sky. A slightly irregular feather edge can mark the transition to the dark body without making the head look pasted on. Hide facial details: are head, neck and body still one bird? If white vanishes into the background, darken the background slightly instead of uniformly outlining the head in black.

## Draw the yellow hooked bill and eye

Extend a yellow bill from the front of the head, broad at its base and curving downward at the tip. Start with one continuous color shape; a short fine line can distinguish upper and lower bill. Do not make the tip a disconnected triangle. Keep the eye within the white head and add only a little gray around it, avoiding a black mask across the entire face. Leave space among bill, eye and crown. If only a yellow blob remains at thumbnail size, correct outline and contrast before adding more fine lines.

## Layer body and folded wing

Block the body in dark brown, slightly lighter on the lit shoulder and darker below. Let a mid-brown folded wing cover the body's side, with a few short marks suggesting feather groups along its lower edge; the wing tip should not disappear abruptly halfway across the body. Use value and a few feather edges to distinguish back and wing instead of drawing every feather with equal heavy lines. If the wing looks like a pasted-on leaf, repair shoulder connection and overlap before adding texture.

## Check the white tail and yellow feet separately

Extend a short light tail from the rear underside, with its root hidden by the body and outer edge slightly spread. NPS describes the adult white tail as somewhat wedge-shaped; the picture need not claim exact angles for every feather. Extend two yellow feet from below the body to the branch, one possibly obscuring part of the other. Establish visible toes wrapping above and below the branch before adding dark talon tips. Do not force occluded toes into view. Both feet must grip the same branch rather than float beneath it.

## Separate branch, background and subject

Give the gray-brown branch a lighter top and darker underside, with small contact shadows at the feet rather than a black outline around the entire branch. Use a low-contrast blue-gray sky and a few blurred distant trees. Slightly darken the area behind the head to make its white edge legible and leave room around the white tail. Avoid distant branches that seem to extend directly from the eye or bill tip. Hide the background to inspect the silhouette, then restore it and look for new tangencies.

## Compare the white head against the sky

Copy a small head silhouette into the sketch area and place it once on pale gray and once on slightly darker blue-gray, keeping the head color fixed. Reduce both to the same size and compare the crown and rear neck before choosing a background value that keeps the feather edge legible. Only background changes in this control. In the final picture also check the white tail against distant trees of similar brightness. If a small sky area needs darkening, blend it into its surroundings rather than creating a conspicuous frame around the head. Hide both samples before export and retain the layered original.

## Check export and evidence limits

At thumbnail size inspect white head, yellow bill, dark body, white tail and both feet on the branch. Save the editable draft, export a separate image using a control actually visible in the installed version and open it elsewhere to inspect bill tip, tail and frame. Reopen the draft and independently hide head and tail and background layers. If export differs, check file identity and layer visibility first. Record operations actually completed and mark unperformed export or reopening pending. This is not field identification, and no physical-device drawing test is claimed.
