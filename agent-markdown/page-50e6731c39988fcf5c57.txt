## Separate position diagrams from level cards

A shows ideal Sun–Earth–Moon geometry; B lists high/low levels at one location and common vertical datum; C leaves real place/date blank. A explains geometry, B range arithmetic and C missing prediction data. Record legend and non-scale construction, without measuring astronomical distances from drawing gaps. Keep original numbers free of real-port labels so they are not mistaken for a travel tide table. No observations means no claim of a measured high on a particular date.

## Check approximate alignment at new and full Moon

Show new Moon roughly between Sun and Earth and full Moon roughly across Earth from Sun. Both approximate alignments relate to larger average spring ranges. Full Moon is not excluded, and alignment sketches do not imply an eclipse every time. Tidal effects reinforce range here; do not claim the gravitational vectors point identically everywhere. The diagram supplies a relation, not local high-water time.

## Read quarter geometry and smaller range

Quarter positions place Earth–Sun and Earth–Moon directions roughly perpendicular, associated with smaller neap ranges. Smaller does not mean still water or no highs/lows. Without orbital direction, retain two quarter-position candidates rather than force first/last labels. Changes are gradual, not instantaneous jumps between four constants. Phase provides context, while local lag and geographic response require data absent here.

## Subtract low from high at a common datum

Card one has high three metres, low one, range two. Card two has high four, low two, also range two. A higher high alone does not establish greater range. Card three has high two point five and low one point five, range one. Use common vertical datum and comparable pairing; differing places or datums require resolution before direct level comparisons. These are arithmetic cards, not observations tied to a real date in A.

## Keep the name separate from season and weekday

NOAA explains that the historical name spring tide is unrelated to spring; spring/neap conditions recur throughout the year. Sunday or November alone cannot determine a local spring tide. The longer cycle differs from the daily number of highs, which this position sketch does not prescribe identically for all coasts. Tide types and current speeds require their own definitions; maximum flow speed is not automatically maximum water level, and a name is not a complete tide table.
