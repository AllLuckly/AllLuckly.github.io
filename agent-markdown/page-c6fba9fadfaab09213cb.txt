Prepare a few answerable questions for one chapter, retain evidence and personal reasoning separately, and verify qualifications before writing sourced answers.

A highlighted chapter can still leave its reader unable to explain the lesson. Work from a few initial questions through source evidence to final answers within one chapter. Book Isle supplies navigation and notes; you determine the questions and assess understanding.

## Limit initial questions to the chapter

Use the contents and opening to write two or three questions about definitions, conditions or exceptions. Date them with edition/chapter details and keep guesses distinct from answers. Mark questions not addressed in this chapter instead of forcing unrelated keyword matches into evidence.

## Read the argument before extracting

Read the whole unit once and locate definitions, examples, objections and conclusions before extensive highlighting. Keep a few return anchors appropriate to the format. Check surrounding passages after navigation. Understanding structure prevents a counterexample from becoming an alleged recommendation.

## Choose the minimum evidence for each question

Select a short passage retaining the relevant conditions and label the question it addresses. Separate quotation from explanation and test the return reference. Use external page/phrase notes for nonselectable formats. Evidence length should follow the material, not an equal-size template.

## Look deliberately for limits

Check qualifiers and counterexamples after drafting each answer. Search can locate terms, but read their context. Revise overly broad questions or answers rather than removing contrary evidence. If no exception is found, state that limited observation instead of declaring the rule exception-free.

## Test understanding with your own example

Write a clearly labelled personal scenario and proposed action, then change one condition. This checks understanding without inventing an author case or proving effectiveness. Revisit definitions when the explanation fails and retain uncertainty. Ordinary notes are sufficient; no automatic reasoning feature is assumed.

## Write the answer in your own words first

Draft without copying the open passage, then verify missing conditions and attach a source anchor. Reread when unable to explain rather than adding highlights. Preserve original book, extracts and personal answers separately. A brief answer identifying subject, conditions and conclusion is useful for review.

## Revisit one question later

Later, explain one saved answer before returning to its evidence. Date corrections and retain unresolved questions for further reading. Export important records through actual controls and reopen them to check attribution. A focused revisit is sufficient without rereading every highlight.

## Acceptance and evidence limits

Finish with supported answers or explicit unanswered items, working references and distinct personal reasoning. Not every question needs a highlight if the chapter does not address it. Dated revisions are part of close reading. Explain applicability and remaining uncertainty rather than substituting time or colour counts for understanding.
