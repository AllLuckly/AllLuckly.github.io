Paint a potted columnar cactus, using ribs, spaced spine clusters and the pot rim to distinguish volume from surface detail.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-cactus-spines/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Cactus spines are conspicuous, but they should not be the first step. Paint a simplified columnar cactus by building its ribbed body before placing selected spine clusters. Use hand-drawn marks rather than automatic repetition, mirroring or a specialised plant brush.

## Set the plant and pot proportions

Create a roughly 1200×1600 px portrait canvas with a warm grey background. Sketch a tapered pot near the lower third and make the cactus about twice its height. Mark the plant tip, widest point and soil insertion together so the pot remains large enough to support it.

Separate the background, pot, plant base colour and spines into layers. Give the cactus a slight curve, rounded top and subtly uneven sides. Check its plain green silhouette at small size. Adjust a stiff outline before relying on dense spines to make it look natural.

## Wrap the vertical ribs around the body

Sketch three to five lengthwise divisions, wider at the centre and narrower at the sides. Let them follow the bend of the plant and turn smoothly over the rounded top. Establish one coherent ribbed cylinder before deciding which dividing lines should remain visible.

Use yellow-green, mid-green and grey-green for lit, frontal and shaded planes, with light from the upper left. Keep some transitions soft. The right side is darker overall, though a small left-facing plane can still catch light. Add groove darks after the broad lighting is established.

## Place areoles before individual spines

Place small pale grey-yellow areoles along the visible ribs. Use roughly six to eight on a central rib, shrinking them at the sides and tightening the spacing near the tip. Stagger neighbouring rows rather than creating a grid. Check the dots at small size before drawing radiating spines.

Give focal areoles three to five short spines with slight variations. Spines facing the viewer are shorter; side spines may cross the silhouette. Keep most within the body, using a few outer groups to interrupt the contour. Test a thin brush before making spines heavier than the grooves.

## Use the pot rim to show overlap

Make the pot opening a flattened ellipse. Its back edge sits behind the plant, while the front edge overlaps soil and the base. Use brown-grey soil with a small root shadow. Keep the front rim clearer and interrupt the back rim where the cactus occludes it.

Use a muted terracotta pot to contrast gently with the green. Light its left side and extend a grounded shadow to the right. Restore rim curvature if the opening looks flat. If the plant floats, correct the front overlap and root shadow before decorating the pot.

## Control contrast between detail and background

If pale spines disappear, darken a small shaded plant area behind them rather than turning the entire background black. Only one or two groups need complete clarity. Others can dissolve in light or at small size; varied visibility enriches the surface instead of indicating unfinished work.

Leave quiet space around the cactus and avoid background edges tangent to its contour. Hide and restore the spine layer. The spines should add texture and rhythm without carrying the entire form. If the body becomes a flat green block when hidden, improve the widths and values of the ribs.

## Troubleshoot stripes and visual clutter

A striped-cloth appearance often comes from equally wide grooves and equally bright ribs. Strengthen one central rib, soften the sides and join values naturally at the top. Avoid matching highlights on both sides of every groove. Try one narrow lit edge before adding more.

When the spines become chaotic, remove about half the interior marks and retain three focal groups before adding essentials back. Each line should emerge from its own areole. Repaint confused groups over the base colour; keeping spines separate protects the finished ribs underneath.

## Check at a small size and retain versions

At roughly quarter view, check the silhouette, turning ribs, pot overlap and selected areoles. Fine adjustments made only while zoomed in may become noise at normal size. Save a preview before adding spines and compare it with the finish to judge whether detail adds information.

Save the native layered project and PNG previews with separate names for the structural and finished versions. Next, try a rounder cactus with the same order: body, areoles, spines. Its ribs compress more strongly at the sides, revealing whether you understand volume rather than a vertical stripe pattern.
