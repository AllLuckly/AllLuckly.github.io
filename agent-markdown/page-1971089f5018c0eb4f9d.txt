English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-obtuse-triangle-orthocenter-altitude-side-extensions/>), translated on 2026-11-16. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Triangle centres need not lie inside the outline. Draw a fixed obtuse triangle and extend its three altitude lines to one external point, checking perpendicularity rather than connecting vertices to side midpoints.

## Establish that the chosen triangle is obtuse

Use A=(0,0), B=(6,0), C=(2,1), joined in that order. At C, vectors (-2,-1) and (4,-1) have dot product -7, establishing an obtuse angle rather than relying on a flat appearance. The points are noncollinear, with base 6 and height 1. Reserve substantial space above for the orthocentre. On a roughly 1600 by 1400 canvas, screen origin (400,1100) and 90 pixels per unit keep equal scales. Grid-based placement may be approximate while the paper vertices remain fixed.

## Associate each altitude with its opposite side

An altitude starts at a vertex and is perpendicular to the full line containing the opposite side. Its foot can lie beyond that side segment. The ledger lists three line equations: A’s altitude is not its median, and C’s vertical is not a decorative upward line. Distinguish solid triangle sides from lighter dashed extensions and name the intersection separately. Use available layers/groups to separate outline, construction and labels so selecting an altitude does not accidentally move a defining vertex.

```text
A=(0,0); B=(6,0); C=(2,1)
CA=(-2,-1); CB=(4,-1); CA dot CB=-7
altitude(C): x=2
altitude(A): y=4*x
altitude(B): y=12-2*x
H=(2,8)
AH dot BC=(2,8) dot (4,-1)=0
BH dot AC=(-4,8) dot (2,1)=0
centroid=(8/3,1/3)
```

## Draw C’s vertical altitude and its baseline foot

AB is horizontal, so C’s altitude has x=2. Its foot on AB is (2,0), distinct from C=(2,1), and the line also extends upward. Put a right-angle marker at the foot, not at every crossing. This line alone has infinitely many candidate points; the other altitudes must locate the orthocentre. Convert increasing mathematical height to decreasing screen y when placing the later point of height 8, instead of drawing it below the baseline.

## Draw A’s altitude perpendicular to BC

BC has direction (4,-1) and slope -1/4. The perpendicular through A has slope 4, giving y=4x. Together with x=2, it meets at H=(2,8), well above C’s height 1. That external position is expected. Draw A’s altitude and extend BC beyond C toward its foot rather than bend the original side toward H. Crossing the triangle outline is not an error. If H is clipped, enlarge space or uniformly rescale the diagram instead of lowering only H to fit a frame.

## Use B’s altitude as an independent third check

AC has direction (2,1), slope 1/2, so B’s altitude has slope -2 and equation y=12-2x. At x=2 it also gives y=8. Use this as an independent check instead of stopping after two lines intersect. Confirm its origin at B and perpendicularity to the line AC. Direction BH=(-4,8) dotted with AC=(2,1) is zero; AH=(2,8) dotted with BC=(4,-1) is also zero. These relations establish perpendicularity; a drawn right-angle icon by itself does not.

## Separate orthocentre, medians and altitude feet

Here H=(2,8), while the median centroid is (8/3,1/3). Medians join opposite midpoints; altitudes require perpendicularity. Changing colour does not exchange these conditions. An altitude foot is also distinct from the orthocentre: C’s foot is (2,0), while H is the common intersection of altitude lines. A marginal control note is enough without crowding every centre onto the main image. Joining A to BC’s midpoint (4,1/2) reaches that side but is not perpendicular. Keep such a wrong-direction control separate, not as an alternative valid altitude.

## Diagnose vertices, perpendicularity and extension scope

For nonconcurrence, first check unchanged vertices, then altitude/opposite-side pairing, then slope signs and axis scales. A foot beyond a side is valid and need not be dragged inside. Move crowded labels without changing H. Correct one altitude using the other two as controls. Canvas-centre alignment is not an altitude construction, and no unpublished triangle-centre tool is assumed. If export simplifies dashes, retain a clear distinction between original sides and extensions rather than let dense auxiliaries imply another polygon.

## Reopen the external intersection and three zero-dot checks

Save the editable original and reopen an export, identifying obtuse C, sides, three altitude lines, C’s foot and external H. Include the line equations and perpendicular-direction checks; C’s vertical direction (0,1) also has zero dot product with base direction (6,0). Check the shared point and each originating vertex rather than mere accidental concurrence. Separate C/foot labels at small size while retaining height 1. This is an original obtuse-triangle construction with paper verification, not measured geometry or physical-device certification.
