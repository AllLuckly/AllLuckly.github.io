Use public flashlight information and a permitted photograph to practice cylindrical perspective, component connections and metal values.

## Choose one specific reference

Switch placement varies among flashlights. Public product information for the Fenix PD25R describes a tail-cap switch and a mode-selection switch on the head. For this exercise choose a permitted photograph of a single flashlight with similar visible components, and observe head, barrel, side switch and tail. Record model, viewpoint and usage rights. If a button is obscured, draw only what is visible rather than treating all flashlight models as identical.

## Compose a diagonal object

Create an approximately 1800 by 1400 pixel landscape canvas using the current Aquarell interface and save an editable original. Draw a faint long axis with the flashlight lying diagonally, leaving room around head and tail; dimensions are suggestions. Separate sketch, background, object and shadow with available layers, using identifiable features if labels differ. Decide whether the object rests on a table or floats; this example uses a tabletop.

## Block the barrel

Draw the two main barrel boundaries along the axis. Compare length and width before adjusting perspective from the photograph; do not treat it as a flat rectangle without end-face direction. Both sides should share the same long axis. Even if the reference has texture or curved transitions, preserve a simple overall form first. Shrink the view and compare barrel, head and tail proportions before adding switches or details.

## Connect head and front

Draw the head at one end of the barrel. If the photograph shows a slightly wider head, connect it with a gradual contour instead of a detached disk. Where the front is visible, use ellipses consistent with the barrel axis for outer rim and lens; near a strict side view the ellipse becomes narrow. Inner and outer rings should share center and tilt so the front and barrel do not point in different directions. Do not force a full front view when the lens faces away.

## Place side switch and tail

From the chosen reference, mark a side button on the head and the tail cap at the other end of the barrel. The side switch should sit on the visible surface; a small ellipse and slight shadow can suggest it without making a floating ball. Tail-switch visibility depends on camera angle; do not add a circle where the cap hides it. Draw the visible exterior only; there is no need to open the battery compartment, remove the head or infer internal wiring.

## Use value to suggest a cylinder

Block the barrel in middle gray or a dark value. Follow the reference light direction with a lighter lengthwise highlight and a darker far side; the highlight should follow the barrel rather than crossing into the background. Use a local bright mark on the head rim for reflected metal, but do not outline every ring with identical white. If the material looks flat, hide texture and compare light, dark and front ellipse before proceeding.

## Handle texture and visible seams

If the barrel has grip texture, observe its location and density, then suggest it with a few short marks or sparse shapes. At small size it should not become a black mass. Head-barrel and barrel-tail seams should follow the same cylinder perspective and the photograph’s positions. Do not add a charging port, clip or lettering where none is visible; models differ and this illustration is not a repair or operating guide.

## Set the object on the tabletop

Draw a contact shadow under the barrel consistent with the reference light direction. Keep it clearer near contact and optionally softer farther away. It need not reproduce each switch, but should indicate that head and barrel rest in the same environment. If the flashlight floats, inspect contact between its lowest edge and shadow before adjusting blur or transparency. Use a low-contrast background so the head contour remains readable.

## Check both end-face directions

Make two thumbnail copies: in one the front ellipse agrees with the barrel axis, and in the other deliberately tilt it differently. Compare which reads as the same cylinder in the reference. If the tail face is visible, its projection should also have a coherent relationship to the barrel rather than being an arbitrary circle. Keep the better sketch and check that the side switch sits on the head, texture does not hide seams and the highlight does not run through the button. Record one useful revision.

## Compare contour and negative space

Draw a rectangular envelope around the flashlight in the sketch and compare the portions of total length occupied by head, barrel and tail. Inspect the gaps between object and envelope. If one gap vanishes suddenly, a barrel edge may be overly tilted, or the reference head may actually be wider. Compare the photograph before changing it; these proportions are not measurements of any model. Hide the envelope afterward and confirm that the object contour still reads.

## Save and reopen

Save the editable original and use a share or export entry actually visible in the current version to save a separate image. Reopen and inspect head, tail, side switch, table shadow and corners. Return to identify object, background and sketch in the original. If details are missing, check visibility, crop and file location before overwriting the only original. Keep public product information and photographic source; mark device, export or reopening steps pending if unperformed.

## Keep a model and visible-parts checklist

Beside the reference list the head-side switch, tail cap and tail switch, marking each visible, obscured, or described in documentation but not confirmed in the photograph. Draw only what the photograph shows; a visible tail cap does not by itself establish a visible tail switch. Use a new column for a different model to avoid carrying over button locations. This checklist does not replace actual product documentation.
