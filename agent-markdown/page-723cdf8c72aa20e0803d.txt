Ebook text, audio and an external webpage can come from different sources. Confirm the sample, then observe online and offline results separately.

## Distinguish three target types

An EPUB can contain text or resources inside its container and, under specified conditions, refer to outside publication resources such as audio. An ordinary external webpage link is another kind of access. W3C EPUB 3.3 limits which publication-resource types may be remote; do not treat every remote image as a conforming example. Whether Book Isle loads or plays a resource requires observation and cannot be inferred from the specification.

## Prepare authorized test material

Choose an EPUB you created or are authorized to inspect, containing offline-readable text, a short authorized audio clip and an ordinary link to a publisher information page. Establish whether the audio is in the container or is a permitted remote audio resource; if uncertain, the owner can inspect an isolated copy read-only. Preserve the original and avoid private tokens, temporary signed URLs or unknown-source network content.

## Make a local and remote ledger

Write three rows for text position, audio location and ordinary webpage link, with chapter, label, source and whether a network is required. Container audio is not automatically a device-supported format. Remote resources may fail because of server, network, authorization or reader policy. A webpage opening in a browser does not prove audio plays in Book Isle.

## Check an online baseline

On a trusted and permitted network, open the same authorized file in Book Isle, inspect the text and then any actual audio control or link. Before visiting the external webpage, confirm it matches the publisher and that you wish to open it. Do not enter passwords or bypass certificate errors. Record visibility or opening result, time, OS and app version; mark absent controls unavailable.

## Verify audio identity

If audio plays, listen for the self-made clip’s opening marker and record duration or prompt. Do not assume the audio corresponds to the current page. Use a safe volume and avoid changing other settings during playback. If it fails, distinguish no control, no response and a specific error. Format, resource authorization or network may all matter; do not immediately edit the book source.

## Run a reversible offline comparison

First ensure other work and calls will not be affected and record the device’s original network state. With the user’s permission, temporarily disconnect and reopen the same test book. Check text, audio entry and ordinary webpage link separately without repeatedly trying unknown addresses. Record offline visibility or playback and restore the original network state afterward. If disconnection cannot be done safely, mark this comparison not performed.

## Separate cache from resource location

Offline audio playback could reflect a container resource, a previous cache or another cause; one result cannot establish its physical location. Offline failure does not by itself prove a remote resource. Only an owner’s file and link inspection can confirm location. Without evidence mark unknown, and do not clear all app data merely to force a test.

## Compare the external webpage link

An ordinary webpage link may open inside a reader interface or an external browser, depending on actual Book Isle and system behavior. Online success and offline failure describe those visits only; a webpage does not thereby become audio inside the EPUB. Do not submit credentials or private information on an unfamiliar page, and do not make opening an unsafe link an acceptance condition.

## Investigate source and authorization

If the webpage works online but audio does not, check that audio is authorized, the target remains accessible, format and the controls actually offered by Book Isle. If both fail, inspect network and book source. Version and server policy can affect behavior; record the original error and stage. Ask the publisher for a legal offline edition when needed rather than downloading or redistributing protected media without authorization.

## Align online and offline observations

Give text, audio and webpage one row each, with online state, offline state and times. Use the same chapter and self-made text marker, the same opening audio marker, and only the verified publisher webpage. Do not fill audio success from readable text or describe a failed link as an unreadable entire EPUB. Record precise states such as no control, load failure or unconfirmed.

Note any audio playback before going offline, since prior loading or caching may affect the later observation. Do not delete caches, reinstall the app or clear a shelf to remove that variable. To investigate further, the owner can inspect the authorized copy or use a separately prepared unplayed sample, explicitly recording remaining uncertainty. Even container audio requires reader support for its format and playback method.

External targets can change. If a publisher link redirects, check the final destination and the user’s willingness before proceeding. Stop at certificate or login warnings rather than bypassing them or entering credentials. This controlled sample is not a compatibility guarantee for all EPUBs, servers or audio formats.

## Restore and record

Restore the device network to its original state and confirm the original book, bookmarks and notes remain unchanged. Separate text, audio and webpage results online and offline, and whether resource location was actually inspected. Keep unperformed and unknown states explicit. Based on W3C specifications, this guide does not claim Book Isle supports a particular remote audio resource or that any device or private server was tested.
