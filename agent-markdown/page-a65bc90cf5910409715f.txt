Separate flattened thallus, cup-contained gemmae and underside rhizoids without turning cups into flowers, seeds or through-holes.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-marchantia-thallus-branching-gemma-cups-rhizoid-scope/>), translated on 2026-11-18. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Draw a small Marchantia thallus bearing circular gemma cups, viewed slightly from above with a separate small underside inset. Trace flattened branching, cup rims and gemma identity. This is not a complete life cycle or a model for every liverwort.

## Separate main view from the underside inset

On an approximately 1800×1400 px canvas give most left/central space to the main view and reserve a small lower-right underside inset, separated by whitespace. Block low green ribbons on substrate, not an upright leafy trunk. Separate sketch, thallus, gemma cups and annotations, then save native work. The inset explains a turned-over patch; it is not another physically connected branch. Label main dorsal surface and schematic ventral inset so concealed structures are not all mistaken for one top view.

## Branch one flattened body rather than string separate leaves

Begin with one short broad ribbon, fork its tip into two and fork just one branch again, producing an original three-main-tip layout. Three tips are a choice, not a fixed Marchantia count. Keep surfaces continuous at forks, with substrate visible in notches, rather than paste heart-shaped leaves onto a stem. Vary edges and projected width gently without collapsing them into wires. Connect the sheet in one base green before adding a faint middle guide; that guide explains form, not a vascular leaf vein.

## Seat three gemma cups on the dorsal surface

Choose three sufficiently broad surface positions A,B,C for circular gemma cups, projected as compressed ellipses. Draw outer wall, firmer near rim and short interior shadow, retaining plant colour at the bottom rather than a through-hole revealing soil. Three cups are a checking choice; not every branch must bear one. Seat their bases on the dorsal surface rather than lift them on stalks like flowerpots. Move an unsupported rim back onto a broad patch instead of expanding the whole thallus into an implausible disc.

## Identify gemmae as propagules rather than seeds or pollen

Place a few small green gemmae in the near A cup, some face-on as flattened discs and others partly hidden by its rim. Separate cup wall from propagules before adding detail rather than fill it with noise. Gemmae serve asexual reproduction; they are not flowers, seeds or spore capsules. Although the reference explains raindrop dispersal, draw no measured-distance arrows or completed splash observation. Selected disc counts and sizes serve readability, not specimen statistics or microscopic scale.

## Confine rhizoids to the underside inset

Draw a turned-over thallus patch in the lower-right inset, darkening its ventral surface and attaching fine rhizoids toward substrate. Their anchoring role does not make them thick tree roots with a taproot/lateral hierarchy, nor do they grow from dorsal cup interiors. A dashed leader can locate the patch on the main view but must not resemble a rhizoid. Keep the main view dorsal without transparent roots through its green surface. Use an explicit underside reference when the main photograph lacks one, omitting unsupported cellular detail.

## Use surface tone and restrained air-pore marks

Use two or three related greens with narrow near-edge shadows for thickness, brighter lit dorsal planes and broken substrate shadows. If indicating air pores, use a few supported tiny marks. These pores lack guard cells and are not opening-and-closing leaf stomata. Do not make soil-coloured holes or a texture covering every area. Light cup walls separately from the thallus; a short near-rim highlight must not bridge the cavity. With gemma detail hidden, branching and cup placement should remain readable. Restore main planes and background notches before adding texture to an indistinct green mass.

## Troubleshoot branch, cup-bottom and ventral identities

Check three main tips connected into one flattened body, A–C cups with bottoms and walls, and rhizoids confined to the ventral inset. Restore plant-coloured cup bottoms if they resemble holes; reconnect broad surfaces if branching becomes separate leaves; correct a seed label and its legend. Preserve a faulty copy and repair one item before comparing fit-to-window and enlarged views. Do not add umbrella-like receptacles, petals or unverified spore structures to manufacture a complete life cycle. Scope ends at the drawn thallus and gemma cups.

## Reopen and check both main view and inset

Save native work, hide sketch lines but retain dorsal/ventral labels, then reopen a PNG. At reduced size read three tips, three cups and a separate inset; enlarge rim occlusion of gemmae and rhizoids not penetrating the dorsal surface. Reopen separately editable walls, gemmae and annotations. Document Marchantia schematic, original counts and nonphysical scale.
