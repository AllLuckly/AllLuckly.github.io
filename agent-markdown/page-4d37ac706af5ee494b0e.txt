Paint a croissant with a crescent silhouette, rolled sections and baked colour changes, separating its main volume from flaky texture.

Croissant layers are a study in direction: the marks must wrap around the rolled pastry. Paint one lying diagonally, first building a full centre and tapered crescent tips, then using section shadows and selected flakes to suggest crispness.

## Set up a separate study

Create an approximately 1400×1600 px canvas with the croissant diagonal and table space around both tips. Separate the main pastry, rolled edges and flakes. Test a medium brush for curved planes and a fine one for short marks, prioritising the roll direction over a ready-made grain texture.

Check a plain silhouette for a full centre and tapered unequal tips. Save it before dividing rolls. If the later image resembles linked balls, compare it with the silhouette and shared light plane rather than outlining every section more strongly. All sections must belong to one pastry.

## Give the centre more weight than the tips

Sketch a bent thick wedge, fullest centrally and narrowing inward at the tips. Avoid a crescent of uniform thickness; vary the tip lengths. Mark the lower contact and upper arch. Check a plain ochre silhouette for a full bread-like mass before adding rolled sections.

Light the upper left with warm yellow-orange and shade the lower right with brown-orange. Wrap the broad values around the whole centre before shading sections. If it resembles linked sausages, connect the lit areas and restrict separation shadows to narrow rolled edges.

## Curve section boundaries around the roll

Add three to five curved section boundaries, broad in the centre and compressed near the tips. Show one rolled lip overlapping the next and let the other side fade. Break dark divisions in lit areas rather than running identical lines across the full width.

Resolve one foreground lip with a narrow brown shadow below and a lighter thin edge above. Repeat the principle with varied width and contrast. If the layers resemble plastic rings, soften the middle divisions and clarify only selected foreground and shaded overlaps.

## Keep baked colour variation within the lighting

Add caramel or red-brown baked patches on selected raised tops while keeping some lower pastry pale. These are local colour changes, not new light sources. Shade the darker patches with the same volume. Begin subtly and confirm the main lighting before adding the deepest accents.

Use selected yellowish lit edges and muted browns in shade rather than black. If every area is saturated orange, compare against a quiet beige-grey background and mute some darks. Restrained areas allow the warm top to feel crisp without making the entire picture heavy.

## Group flaky texture along the rolled layers

Place broken fine marks along the rolled edges, following their curvature. Clarify some on raised surfaces and interrupt them in shade. Leave gaps between groups so the main sections read before the flakes. Concentrate texture in two or three areas and simplify the tips and base.

For one or two lifted flakes, paint a pale small plane with a narrow shadow underneath. Keep their projection subtle. If the texture becomes muddy, hide the layer and remove marks that explain no direction. A few clear lifted edges describe crispness better than uniform fine stripes.

## Keep crumbs and shadows secondary

Add a soft shadow extending right, darker at contact. Place three to five unequal crumbs, with clearer ones near the pastry. Avoid a neat decorative row or heavy shadows beneath every crumb. Their role is to reinforce flakiness without making the table the subject.

If the small image resembles a brown rock, enlarge the shared light area and soften dividing darks. If it looks like a crescent biscuit, strengthen central thickness and grounding. For another study, reverse the light and reconsider every lip shadow rather than tracing the same pattern.

## Review and plan the next study

Review the mass as a thumbnail, overlaps at normal size and a few lifted flakes close up. If only brown texture remains at small size, merge a light area before adding white accents. Cover the crumbs and confirm that the pastry itself still communicates layered crispness.

Export block-in and finished previews, retaining editable rolled edges. Next, change only the central bend while keeping palette and light fixed, then rebuild each section around the swelling form. Record a concrete observation, such as a narrower shadow making an overlap clearer, rather than calling it simply more detailed.
