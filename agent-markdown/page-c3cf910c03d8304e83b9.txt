Disguise, childhood scenes and miniature caricature can change one character’s appearance, while uniforms can make different people look alike. Compare two completed appearances in ManYue to make a revisable identity judgement. This is not a relationship chart and assumes no face recognition, automatic labels or unpublished character information; the reader checks the pages.

## Choose two appearances that need comparison

Choose two uncertain appearances within completed material and record edition, chapter, scene and surrounding events, initially labelling the figures A and B. Giving them the same name immediately can bias evidence collection. Exclude mismatched translations or revised images and avoid starting from isolated online portraits. Begin with one comparison before twins or crowds in uniform. If the work later identifies the person, cite that location rather than rewriting an earlier guess as longstanding certainty.

## List visible features without personality judgements

List facial marks, objects, proportions, posture and clothing, distinguishing visible from obscured details. Anger, timidity and resemblance to a protagonist are interpretations, not stable visual identifiers. Treat colour as a clue under matching edition/display conditions, not a direct black-and-white versus colour comparison. Hair may hide a scar, so absence from view is not absence. Seek two checkable features and retain differences instead of dismissing every mismatch as casual drawing.

## Check the source of names and introductions

Check who names whom, whether the person responds, and whether identity comes from narration, self-introduction or another character’s guess. A disguised speaker may use a false name, so dialogue is not automatic certification. For differing translations, check edition and context rather than word length. Record the exact panel and addressee; a name in a crowd need not identify the nearest face. Use names only where their visual connection is established and leave uncertain matching unresolved.

## Seek intermediate evidence in continuous action

Look between appearances for changing clothes, removing a mask, transferring objects or uninterrupted movement. Continuity can be stronger than similar cropped faces, but check for substitution or time jumps. In an invented example, a hat-wearer entering a door and another emerging does not establish identity; continuous removal revealing a known mark is stronger. Leave omitted portions unknown rather than supplying an undrawn change or substituting plot prediction for intermediate evidence.

## Separate stylistic distortion from in-story change

Inspect stated conditions for caricature, dream, childhood or animalisation, including whether nearby figures change style too. Expressive miniaturisation need not mean physical shrinking. Conversely, do not dismiss a stated transformation as mere style. Separate stylistic layer, story state and uncertainty, explaining which features remain useful. Pixel-ratio comparison between a caricature and ordinary portrait does not replace narrative evidence or constitute face-similarity measurement.

## Log support and objections to merging identities

Record two supporting clues and an objection or limitation, then state a scoped judgement such as likely same person without confirmation. Later twins, doubles or borrowed objects should revise a specific inference rather than erase the earlier log. Objects can be lent and uniforms repeat, so combine them with other evidence. Keep identity merging within your personal notes, without altering the text, publishing conjecture as settled character metadata or spoiling an unreached revelation.

## Preserve appearance and revelation references

Save supported references or external locators for both appearances and intermediate evidence, including panel, sleeve or nearby object rather than only a name. Leave the reader, then use the record to find appearance A, move to appearance B and return to A, excluding similarly dressed neighbouring figures. Attach chapters to collected portraits lacking context. Recheck after revisions, added pages or translation changes. A bookmark helps return to a page; it is not assumed to identify people automatically or notify you at every appearance.

## Finish the identity judgement with uncertainty intact

Finish with visual, naming or action support for each merged identity and at least one alternative source of confusion. Common hairstyles can belong to different people; a hidden scar may reflect occlusion or simplification. Keep two provisional labels where evidence is inadequate. State what is known and what is still needed, without treating identity confirmation as proof of motive or truthfulness. One person can lie, so establishing identity does not settle every plot interpretation.
