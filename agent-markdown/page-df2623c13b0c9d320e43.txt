Study a permitted Blue Jay photograph and paint its crest, necklace, wing markings and perch in layers.

## Choose and check a reference

Choose a permitted side-view photograph of a Blue Jay and record its source, viewpoint and date. Cornell describes a prominent crest, a white to light-gray underside and blue, black and white upperparts. The visible necklace and wing or tail bars vary with pose. Identify the individual and lighting in the chosen image; do not copy unseen details from another view as if they were visible here.

## Set up the canvas and sketch

Create a landscape canvas in Aquarell, for example 1800 by 1400 pixels; this is an exercise size. Save an editable original and use the visible layer controls to separate background, branch, bird base color and sketch. Lightly place the branch direction and block the body with a large oval, leaving space above the crest and beyond the tail. If controls have different labels, use those visible in the installed version.

## Compare the silhouette

Mark bill tip, crown, front of breast, tail base and tail tip. Compare head to body, tail length and branch angle. Join the crest to the head contour instead of floating a triangle above it. Draw the tail as a single shape before separating feathers. Check the bird on the branch at thumbnail size; if the tail is cropped, move the composition instead of shortening it arbitrarily.

## Place eye, bill and crest

Compare the eye’s distance to the bill base and front of the crest and mark it as a small shape. Extend the bill from the face and indicate the upper and lower bill junction, without inventing structures hidden in the photograph. Follow the crest as a continuous edge and distinguish its tip from the nape. If the crest is lowered, preserve that pose. Inspect the bill connection up close and then confirm the head direction at thumbnail size.

## Paint the pale underside and dark necklace

Block in the breast and belly with light gray or warm white, then place the dark necklace along the visible boundary. It need not be an identical closed circle: pose and occlusion change what is visible. Mark uncertain regions instead of turning a wing shadow into the necklace. Separate pale belly, white throat and background by value, and hide the sketch to check whether the shapes remain legible.

## Build the blue wing and bars

Use a middle blue to block the folded wing separately from the side of the body. Observe the white wingbar, dark barring and feather edges in the reference before adding a few bands to suggest their direction; do not repeat identical stripes across the whole body. Blue changes with light, so a sampled screen color is not a species standard. If bars fragment the silhouette, reduce their number and preserve the wing outline.

## Arrange tail, toes and branch

Align the long tail with the reference before adding visible light and dark bars; follow the photo for any white at the tail tip. Front toe segments cover the branch while the branch hides rear segments, so stop lines at overlaps. Do not invent leg joints obscured by feathers. Hide the bird’s base color to inspect contact points, then restore it and check whether the feet read as resting on the branch.

## Separate subject and background

Place a muted background behind the branch and bird, with a different value near the crest and bill tip so the contour does not disappear. Simplified plant shapes are enough; there is no need to reproduce every leaf in the photograph. First check that head, tail and branch direction are clear, then decide whether a few background details help. Avoid a bright background spot overpowering the eye and bill.

## Troubleshoot and compare versions

If the bird looks like a blue oval, inspect the neck, folded wing boundary and tail base before adding feather lines. If the necklace resembles a solid black hood, bring it back to the visible reference boundary. If the feet float, inspect the front and back overlaps of leg, toes and branch. Duplicate a working version and compare broad color shapes with an otherwise identical barred version at thumbnail size.

## Check values and the reference

Reduce the artwork temporarily and identify the pale belly, dark necklace and blue wing. If an area merges into the background, adjust the adjacent value on a copy, then return to normal size and check the crest. Verify the white wingbar against the photograph instead of moving it for decoration. If the display seems inaccurate, compare a gray scale on the same screen rather than treating an uncalibrated second device as the only correct blue.

## Record revisions and permissions

Record the reference creator, license conditions and access date; before sharing, confirm that the license covers the intended use. Note the layer structure, export location and purpose of a revision, such as darkening the background to separate the crest. Do not claim that every detail has been checked in the field. Keep a copy from before the change; if markings become less clear, undo that change and adjust proportions, color and lines one at a time.

## Save and check the export

Save the editable original, then use the available share or export control to save a separate PNG or JPEG without replacing it. Reopen the output and inspect crest, necklace, white wingbar, toes and tail tip for clipping or missing layers. Return to the original and confirm layers remain editable. Record the reference link, canvas size and one overlap to improve. Mark steps not performed on a real device as pending.
