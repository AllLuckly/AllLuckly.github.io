Printed letter order does not establish that the next letter answers the previous. Select a few traceable letters, identify sender/recipient and map wording to incoming requests. This creates no automatic correspondence network, and an unseen reply proves no refusal; missing letters, selection and edition scope remain relevant.

## Fix the collection edition and a three-letter scope

Choose a permitted collection and record title, editor, translator and edition. Inspect abridgement, reordering or one-sided selection notes. Pick three entries with headings/salutations/reply clues, not necessarily consecutive pages. Record locations/stated dates and leave undated items undated. Dates locate correspondence, not replace it, and publication is not sending date. Preserve sources/necessary excerpts; manually locate unselectable scans without claiming handwriting recognition.

## Identify sender, recipient and signature for each letter

Read heading, salutation and signature for sender, recipient, possible scribe and editorial identifications. A “to X” heading may be editorial rather than quoted text; label provenance. Preserve aliases/initials/familiar names until reliable notes/context connect identities. Matching signatures do not imply matching recipients. Use one external row per letter with IDs and original markers. Do not change shelf authors to model correspondence; book metadata is not each letter’s participant list.

## Break incoming matters into checkable items

Select two or three explicit requests, questions or notifications in the first letter and number each item. Distinguish asking for material from reporting receipt, retaining negation/conditions such as not needed for now. Separate paraphrase, excerpt and editor explanation. An invented A-asks-B-for-place-and-time example must be labelled invented, not attributed to the book. Fix matters before searching replies so a shared word cannot masquerade as an answer to every question.

## Find explicit or content evidence for a reply

Record evidence such as acknowledging a dated letter, restating a specific request or answering its subject before matching the second letter. Neighbouring placement/near dates are insufficient with potentially missing letters. Distinguish established, possible and unconfirmed relations rather than forcing every connection. One answered matter leaves others unresolved. Preserve editorial missing-correspondence notes instead of inventing a complete exchange. Contents/search locate entries but do not decide whether polite greetings substantively answer a request.

## Use the third letter to check follow-up and resolved matters

Check whether letter three acknowledges receipt, follows up or revises a request, mapping specific matters. In the invented place/time example, answering place does not establish time; a later time question leaves it unresolved without proving avoidance. An unmentioned matter may be omitted rather than solved. Limit conclusions to these three visible texts and separate motive inference. Version the matter list while preserving original requests/revision reasons instead of rewriting the first letter’s conditions for apparent consistency.

## Separate quotations and postscripts from the current voice

A reply can quote an incoming request before answering. Mark quotation boundaries/speaker instead of counting the quote as a new outgoing request. Attribute postscripts, forwarding notes and footnotes separately. Retain only short relevant fragments, not entire unattributed letters. For unclear quotation, read context/layout guidance and preserve uncertainty rather than assigning voices by tone. Stable fields—entry, sender and recipient, matter, evidence, response scope, unresolved point—support retrieval after leaving.

## Retrieve all three letters through the matter log

Leave and retrieve the three letters through entry, salutation and short wording, rereading context for identity/reply evidence. Use titles/content after layout changes, not percentage alone. Export important notes through available means and inspect participant/matter alignment externally, keeping the book separately. Notes are not full-letter backups. Share necessary lawful fragments/selection scope, not private originals. Separate confirmed/possible replies; three completed arrows do not reconstruct an entire correspondence history.

## Check selection scope before claiming no answer

For missing replies, inspect one-sided selection, gaps and missing-letter notes before inferring refusal. Similar wording with different recipients needs salutation/signature checks. A repeated “new” question may quote an old letter. Retain uncertain date markers rather than inventing sortable certainty. Recheck participants, matters, reply evidence and unresolved scope so every relation returns to wording instead of an imagined story between adjacent texts.
