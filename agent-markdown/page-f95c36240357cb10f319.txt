Distinguish a snail’s shell spiral, opening and soft body through overlap and varied edges.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-snail-spiral-shell/>), translated on 2026-10-27. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A snail shell can easily become a flat lollipop spiral. Paint a simplified crawling snail with a spiral tightening over a rounded shell and an opening that overlaps the body. Contrast hard shell, soft foot and delicate tentacles without claiming anatomical precision or automatic material tools.

## Establish shell and movement

On an approximately 1700×1300 px landscape canvas, place the shell left of centre and extend the head right with room for tentacles. Connect a rounded shell to a broad shallow body, visible behind and raised toward the head. Save a spiral-free silhouette and correct any wheel-on-skateboard appearance through contact width and body height.

## Expand the spiral from an offset origin

Place the spiral origin slightly above the face centre, tightening inner turns and widening the final outer whorl. Two or three continuous turns suffice. Keep the origin on the visible shell face. Avoid disconnected concentric target rings and excessive perfect symmetry.

## Model the shell before emphasising the spiral

Light the ochre shell from upper left, turning darker toward the lower right. Vary groove strength around the form. Hide the spiral to test the rounded shell, restoring a shared light mass if it is flat. Avoid shading each turn as an independent tube; all belong to one volume.

## Make the opening overlap the body

Place the opening low on the head-facing side, joining the outer whorl. Use a partial curved rim with thickness and an inner dark overlapping the emerging body. Avoid a complete black oval pasted onto the shell. Correct the entrance and occlusion before highlights if the head seems externally attached.

## Describe the foot through broad soft changes

Use muted warm-grey or tan with a lighter back and darker ground contact. Keep gently uneven soft edges, not hair-like spikes. Connect the head to a few simplified tentacles with distinct origins. Establish soft masses before a restrained moisture accent rather than outlining the entire body in white.

## Follow growth direction with shell texture

Add selected short growth marks crossing the whorl direction, finer toward the centre. Restrict them to a few areas and turn any bands around the shell. Quiet texture when it competes with the spiral, testing groove, lit plane and fine marks in an enlarged sample first.

## Separate grounding from moisture

Use a thin contact shadow under the foot. Optional trail marks belong behind the snail along its path and should stay close to the ground colour. Check grounding without them. Review foot and shell/head proportions normally and small, repairing accidental tentacle gaps close up.

## Model one local spiral turn

Study a short outer-whorl section with a broad lit surface, narrow groove and turning shade. This is volume practice, not a literal anatomical cutaway. If a deeper groove makes a separate tube, widen the shared surface and soften the groove before applying it selectively to the main shell.

## Do not let tentacles replace the head

Hide tentacles and check the raised head and its connection to the foot. Repair a flat body first. Vary tentacle lengths slightly while preserving plausible roots, keeping endpoints small rather than bead-like. The head must have form independently; fine lines are supporting cues, not the entire anatomy.

## Compare spiral and volume separately

Export texture-free and finished PNGs with an editable spiral. Next, turn the shell face more toward the viewer and rebuild the origin, whorl width and visible opening while holding body direction fixed. Record one useful overlap and one texture area to simplify instead of assuming more detail is better.
