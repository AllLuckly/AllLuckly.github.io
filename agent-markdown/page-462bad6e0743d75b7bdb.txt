Build a seed head through its core, stems and parachutes, controlling fine-line density, depth and background contrast.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-dandelion-seedhead/>), translated on 2026-10-27. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A dandelion’s lightness comes from gaps rather than a fully filled white ball. Paint one head and a drifting seed, establishing volume and selected near and far groups before fine parachute marks. The test is readable form and air at normal size, not the largest number of lines.

## Prepare a background for pale fine marks

Use an approximately 1400×1700 px portrait canvas with a muted grey-green background and head above centre. Separate core, stems and parachutes. Test a pale fine line at normal size, adjusting width or local contrast rather than turning the entire background black.

## Establish the sphere and core

Mark a near-spherical envelope and a small slightly low curved brown-grey core. Connect a gently bending stem. Check volume and balance before radiating lines from a coherent origin, retaining a structural version instead of beginning with unrelated fine marks.

## Separate near and far seed stems

Paint fainter rear stems first and clearer near groups afterward, all radiating from the core. Vary projected lengths rather than ending every line on the outer circle. Break evenly spaced firework rays into denser groups and gaps to suggest a sphere instead of a flat burst.

## Suggest parachutes with selected short rays

Use three to five light rays for selected parachutes, varying their projected orientation. Clarify only a few near examples and simplify or omit others. Resolve a small area first and stop when it feels airy instead of filling every remaining gap.

## Keep the silhouette broken and airy

Do not outline the sphere in white. Let selected parachutes and stems imply an uneven airy boundary with background gaps. Remove continuous paint from a cotton-ball appearance, or add a few pale terminal parachutes to an overly bare branch-like head rather than thickening every stem.

## Give the drifting seed a complete structure

Place one drifting seed upper right, connecting a small brown seed body, fine stalk and pale parachute. Keep it a single directional accent rather than scattered decoration. Reduce an umbrella-toy appearance through a finer stalk and lighter rays, or clarify the seed body if it becomes an anonymous white tuft.

## Remove marks at three viewing distances

Check composition small, near/far clarity normally and accidental broken connections close up. Hide near detail to test the rear volume, then restore it as emphasis. Reduce distant clarity when both layers compete instead of repeatedly tracing all foreground marks.

## Study one quarter of the seed head

Study a quarter-sphere with three farther and two nearer parachutes plus a deliberate large gap. Compare five complete examples with only two clearly resolved at the same brush width. Use the result to judge when to stop, not as a stamp repeated unchanged around the sphere.

## Relate stem thickness to head size

Taper the stem toward the core with restrained light and shade. Check its thickness against the head envelope with parachutes hidden: too thin seems unsupported, too thick resembles a cotton ball on a stick. Resolve proportion before restoring detail instead of adding surrounding grass as an explanation.

## Keep density versions and vary the breeze

Keep sparse and finished versions with separate parachute marks. Next, remove a few seeds on one side and place the drifting seed there, preserving light and colour. Suggest breeze through related gaps and tilt rather than global blur, recording which omission best preserved air.
