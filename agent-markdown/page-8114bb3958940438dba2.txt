“This is no small matter” may emphasise through restraint, but cannot mechanically become “This is the greatest matter.” Read an original dialogue in Book Isle, retaining the negated expression, context and candidate interpretation before comparing ordinary denial. This is neither counting negatives nor calling every indirect statement irony. Readers explain the judgement; the app does not determine tone or true attitude automatically.

## Preserve speaker, addressee and negation scope

Record work, author, translator, edition, chapter/page and phrase location, then read two neighbouring dialogue turns. Identify speaker, addressee, negated expression and relevant event rather than excerpting only “not.” Preserve quotation boundaries when a narrator reports a character; a character’s evaluation is not automatically the author’s. Transcribe necessary text from unselectable scans and verify it, particularly distinctions between “not” and “only,” or “not necessarily” and “necessarily not.” Without audio, make textual observations only. Do not invent intonation or infer sarcasm from the reader’s font style.

## Separate the concept source from this example

Consult litotes as restrained expression, especially by denying an opposite, and record a context-dependent rhetorical device rather than a logic calculator. Textbooks may translate the term differently; compare definitions instead of declaring a label wrong. In our original dialogue B says “It is only an index repair,” A replies “This is no small matter,” then lists three tasks requiring coordination: organising entries, checking page numbers and correcting cross-references. The concept source does not supply these invented tasks or characters. Read this evidence before deciding whether the label fits; a term must not automatically certify an individual passage.

## Check literal exclusion before an emphatic reading

A’s literal wording rejects the “small matter” evaluation. The following task list can support a warning to take it seriously, with restrained emphasis on importance. Keep literal scope separate from pragmatic interpretation. It does not directly mean extreme difficulty, inevitable failure or superiority to every other task. With no duration given, “no small matter” cannot yield a three-day estimate. The three coordinating tasks support this reading here, not a rule assigning one strength to every negated “small.” If later text instead shows a category distinction, revise the candidate rather than forcing new evidence under the initial label.

## Use ordinary denial to avoid labelling every negative

For a control write “The key is not in the drawer,” in a scene merely locating an object, with no further emphasis cues. It denies a location; “not” alone does not make it litotes. Likewise “This shape is not round” can be ordinary classification. Retain events and context instead of stripping them away and comparing word counts with “no small matter.” A special context could give such a sentence another rhetorical role, but that needs evidence. No sentence pattern is banned from rhetoric here; the requirement is an explained current judgement rather than bulk classification from a searched keyword.

## Do not erase intermediate possibilities in adjective scales

“Not displeased” may tactfully suggest pleasure in context without guaranteeing extreme happiness. “Not small” is not automatically “largest,” because intermediate degrees may exist. Mark paraphrases as candidates and identify added strength. Preserve ambiguity when the source supplies only a vague attitude rather than turning brevity in notes into a definite character commitment. Read double-negation scope through the language and syntax; understated everyday expressions are not all strict propositional calculations. Paraphrases aid interpretation, while the quotation field retains the source instead of inserting your stronger wording inside quotation marks.

## Give irony and litotes candidates separate evidence

A character calling an obviously poor result “Really excellent” may involve contrast between praise and context, a different form from denying an opposite. Several devices may be discussed together, each with its own evidence; a tidy terminology table must not force one exclusive label. “No small matter” alone proves neither sarcasm nor a necessarily modest or truthful speaker. Motive requires more context. State what the current text supports and what it does not. Examples in a reference source do not establish identical psychology in the work you are reading.

## Troubleshoot omitted degree words and referents

For changed interpretations, check words such as “indeed,” “not,” “very,” “most” and “only,” then whether “this” refers to the current task or another matter. Open exported notes and verify negation and referent explanations, not just the title. For failed search, inspect scope and extractable text; retrieve scans by chapter/page before reimporting. Date personal paraphrases and cite their evidence locations, retaining earlier candidates when new context arrives rather than silently changing quotations. For uncertainty, record alternatives and their conditions instead of presenting your doubt as author-confirmed meaning.

## Exit and return to reread the sentence and three tasks

Exit the reader and return to the original dialogue or actual target. Read B’s “only,” A’s negative wording and the task list separately, explaining why restrained emphasis is a candidate here. Revisit the plain location-denial control to ensure classification did not rely only on “not.” Save six fields: edition/location, speaker/addressee, negation scope, context evidence, paraphrase strength and unknowns. Completion means others can discuss the same interpretation and counterexamples at the source, not a unique app-generated label or a judgement of every emotion or real commitment from one understated evaluation.
