A short quotation before a book or chapter may be an epigraph, not words already spoken by a character. Select one in Book Isle, identify it and trace specific textual echoes and changes. This goes beyond verifying a quotation’s source to distinguish attribution, opening placement and later function, without automatic theme or author-intention analysis.

## Identify the passage’s role first

Choose a permitted edition with a clearly placed epigraph and record book,edition,chapter and surrounding page identifiers. Distinguish epigraph,dedication,preliminary note and first narrative sentence; centring alone is insufficient. The [Academy of American Poets account](<https://poets.org/glossary/epigraph>) explains opening quotations in relation to a work, while the actual edition establishes identity. Preserve the file and necessary short excerpts, using manual locators when selection is unavailable without assuming scan copying. Start with one rather than dozens of untracked quotations.

## Separate attribution from narrative speakers

Separate wording,visible attribution,source work and translator, marking unlabelled fields rather than supplying a familiar famous name. The quoted writer is not automatically this book’s author, and the epigraph is not automatically the narrator’s stated position. Preserve quotation/omission marks; verify sources through the existing quotation lesson rather than rewriting text to fit the later theme. Echo analysis can continue with unresolved attribution clearly marked. Similar later words cannot establish provenance.

## Record one limited opening expectation

After reading, note “what I provisionally expect the text to explore”, explicitly a reader expectation rather than authorial guarantee. Use an invented miniature, “There is always a sliver of light beyond the door”, labelled original here without a fictitious poetry-book citation. Waiting,return or illumination may arise, with literal wording separated from association. Later evidence is not yet established. Use the actual book’s epigraph in practice rather than substituting this sentence or inserting your expectation as editorial text.

## Locate later echoes and their changes

Read forward, saving locators for related words/scenes and noting similarities and differences. An invented sequence has the protagonist see light outside initially, the light go out midway and the protagonist light it for a visitor at the end. Record three scenes rather than equating repeated “light” with identical function. Receiving-to-providing light is a possible interpretation supported by those scenes, not automatically a whole-book conclusion. Mark absent echoes not found instead of forcing one per chapter. Search locates wording, not its contextual meaning.

## Distinguish related wording from explicit quotation

Label an explicit complete requotation separately, checking speaker and possible disagreement. Mere door/light wording is a lexical association, a different evidential strength. Similar subject matter is not confirmed intertextuality, and translated wording differences need not be deliberate authorial changes. Record editions/translators/locators separately rather than splicing translations into one passage. These are personal classifications, not an app relevance score. Each inference needs actual wording, not only a scholarly label.

## Compare initial expectations with later evidence

Return to initial expectations after the selected range, marking support,revision or uncertainty with locators. Preserve contrast when the epigraph conflicts with later text rather than rewriting it as an invariably affirmative summary. Add changed interpretations separately, retaining expectations to show how opening guidance evolved. State chapter/excerpt scope if the whole book remains unread; near-end progress does not establish every echo. This is your reading process, not an app-generated thematic review.

## Keep three identities traceable through export

Leave and retrieve the epigraph by chapter/wording, then revisit the three scene records, excluding dedication or another chapter. Export through available means and inspect attribution,expectations,evidence and interpretation as separate identities. Preserve the book separately; notes are not a full backup. Share necessary lawful excerpts and label the original miniature without a fabricated famous attribution. Restore reading position afterward; retrieval-induced progress changes do not establish interpretive mastery.

## Restore context before concluding an echo

For a missing epigraph check edition/preliminaries. For repeated wording with differing function restore scenes rather than overwriting paraphrase as quotation. Unknown attribution remains unknown; echoes do not prove source. An attractive quote card still needs opening location and later evidence. Retain epigraph identity,initial expectation,specific echoes and revision reasons, each text-linked. This supports discussion without establishing a single authorial intention or automatic discovery of an entire thematic network.
