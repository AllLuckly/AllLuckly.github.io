English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/manyue-magnetic-force-charge-sign-cross-product-no-work-panels/>), translated on 2026-11-19. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A field arrow does not mean every particle feels a force along it. Make positive/negative-charge and parallel-velocity cards and inspect three-dimensional directions and force magnitudes in ManYue. Calculations are external ideal-field models, without real magnets, particle beams or phone sensors.

## Declare axes and out-of-page symbols

Set right=+x, up=+y, out toward the reader=+z, with a dot for outward and cross for inward. Use uniform static B=(0,0,0.5)T, v=(3,0,0)m/s and initially q=+2μC, with no electric field and other forces neglected in a classical model. Put axes/positive charge on P1, negative/parallel controls on P2 and work checks on P3; export PDF and retain source work. Check the dot/cross legend before bookmarking P1; a field cross is not a negative-charge label.

## Compute the cross product before applying charge sign

v×B=(0,−1.5,0); multiplying by +2×10⁻⁶C gives F=(0,−3×10⁻⁶,0)N, downward 3μN. +x×+z=−y, not a force along +z. Give velocity, field and force distinct legends and units m/s, T, N instead of colour alone. Attach the force arrow to the charge, not an empty location beside a field symbol.

Check units: T=N/(A·m), A=C/s, so qvB yields N, not joules. A separate 0.5mT field gives 0.003μN=3nN, one thousandth of the original. Only B magnitude changes; dot/cross direction remains. Enlarge a faint m prefix and consult the clear source rather than treat an unreadable unit as absent.

## Keep charge-sign and field-reversal controls separate

Changing only q to −2μC with v, B unchanged makes force upward +3μN, still magnitude 3. A separate positive-charge case with B=(0,0,−0.5)T also points upward; reversing both charge and field restores downward force. Label changed inputs. The magnitude formula uses |q| while the vector formula retains signed q; do not discard sign after applying the right-hand rule or conceal two simultaneous changes.

## Give parallel and stationary zero-force cards distinct reasons

For v=(0,0,3)m/s parallel to B, θ=0 gives |q|vB sin0=0. Antiparallel θ=180° also gives zero. A stationary charge has zero magnetic force too, while charge and field still exist. These zeros do not establish absent B. The original perpendicular case has sin90°=1 and 3μN. Measure θ between velocity and field, not an arbitrary paper edge.

```text
axes: right +x; up +y; out of page +z
q=+2e-6 C; v=(3,0,0) m/s; B=(0,0,0.5) T
v cross B=(0,-1.5,0); F=(0,-3e-6,0) N
q=-2e-6 C, same v/B: F=(0,+3e-6,0) N
v parallel or antiparallel B: magnetic_F=0
magnetic power=F dot v=0
```

## Check instantaneous dot product rather than erase the force

P3 uses instantaneous magnetic power F·v: downward force and rightward velocity are perpendicular, giving zero. In the ideal magnetic-only model this remains true, so speed/kinetic energy stay unchanged while velocity direction may change. Zero work does not imply zero acceleration. This concerns the magnetic term, not all real-scene power; electric forces/collisions need separate consideration. Without further dynamical data such as mass, invent neither orbit radius nor period.

As velocity turns, magnetic-force direction turns too; initial F dotted with an entire endpoint displacement is not a constant-force work shortcut. Zero work follows from perpendicularity to instantaneous velocity throughout. Keep magnetic-only/constant-mass conditions when inferring unchanged kinetic energy from endpoint panels. Initial tangent is not the whole trajectory, and panel count supplies no measured turning frequency.

## Use only the velocity component perpendicular to the field

For a separate speed 3m/s case at 30° to B, magnitude 2×10⁻⁶×3×0.5×1/2=1.5μN, half the perpendicular case. The along-field component contributes no magnetic force. Declare the three-dimensional angle; a projected angle is not automatically the spatial 30°. An oblique drawing without viewing/angle information leaves θ unknown rather than supplying an exact force from appearance.

An explicit 30° vector is v=(1.5,0,3√3/2)m/s: 2.25+6.75=9 preserves speed 3. Its cross product with B has y=−0.75, so positive charge gives downward 1.5μN; the z velocity contributes nothing. This independently checks the sine formula. Equal angle with another azimuth can preserve magnitude while changing force direction, so downward is not universal.

## Diagnose charge, field direction and dot/cross symbols

An upward original positive-charge force suggests x×z sign error or reading an outward dot as inward cross. Downward force for negative charge suggests missing q’s sign; 3μN for parallel motion suggests missing sinθ. Enlarge legends and μ, correct one card and retain errors before revisiting the axes. A force drawn along v to imply continuous speeding contradicts the dot-product check; longer arrows or claimed automatic App handedness cannot repair it.

## Reopen and verify directions and zero magnetic work

At reopened P1 recover downward force, upward for negative charge and downward after both reversals, then zero for parallel motion. Explain zero magnetic work alongside changing velocity direction, and check 1.5μN at 30°. Deliver axes, signed cross product, controls and unknown dynamics. Leave unperformed reader actions pending. This is no App field measurement, real-particle tracking, 3D simulation or completed phone-sensor test.
