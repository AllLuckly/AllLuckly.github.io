An acorn’s cap is a cupule. A disc floating above its nut loses that enclosing relation. Draw one invented acorn, establishing nut and cupule volumes separately, then letting the cupule rim cover the nut’s upper portion. This manual Aquarell study does not make scale count or cap depth universal oak traits, nor infer species, edibility or germination from an illustration.

## Establish nut axis and cupule orientation

Use an approximately 1500×1700 px portrait canvas with pale grey-green background. Tilt the axis slightly right, nut tip below and cupule above. Establish elongated nut and broader cap envelopes on one axis, not conflicting frontal/side views. Separate nut, cupule, stalk and shadow; use warm-brown nut and grey-brown cap. Taper gently, not needle-sharp, with modest rim asymmetry. Repair proportion and tip if head-like rather than adding eye-like spots. Pixels and tilt are compositional, not species measurements.

## Give the cupule a wall rather than a flat lid

Establish a gently curved cupule rim and shallow rounded body above, with visible near wall thickness. The nut enters it and is locally covered rather than separated by a complete background gap. Add enclosure if disc-like or reduce thickness if jar-lid-like. Hide scales to check contact, then restore detail. Cap depth is illustrative, not a fixed fraction for every oak. Different cupules vary; a shallow cup and black rim line cannot substitute for actual spatial enclosure.

## Keep the occluded nut continuous

Sketch the complete nut upper curve and cover it with the cupule rather than pinching a neck beneath the cap. Keep sides smoothly tapering despite a slightly irregular rim. Align nut axis and opening centre if glued-together-looking; repair width and occlusion if it spills sideways. Hidden lines remain in the sketch only. Raising the cap to expose the entire nut creates a separate detached-parts task. A cap can be slightly broader without becoming an umbrella over a stalk; both volumes must remain plausible and compatible.

## Confine scales to the cupule exterior

Suggest roughness with sparse overlapping blocks, varying width around the curved cap and quieting the far side. Do not continue scales onto smooth nut. Test one near patch small, then expand while leaving quiet areas. Reduce spikes and deep individual grooves if pinecone-like, or equal grids if basket-like. Omit many scales without claiming a diagnostic count or complete diamonds everywhere. Check rim enclosure first; texture must help turning rather than bury incorrect contact.

## Attach the stalk to the cupule

Optionally add a short grey-brown stalk from near the cupule top, modestly angled, not a shaft through the nut. Use local root shading and keep its end inside the frame. Correct tip attachment if horn-like or shorten and connect a toothpick-like stalk. A stalk-free version may omit this feature with revised wording; do not invent leaves to establish identity. This is not a complete branch/flowering diagram, and the stalk does not establish season, shedding cause or germination.

## Differentiate smooth nut and rough cap edges

Light upper left with broad warm-brown nut light and grey-brown lower-right shade; keep cap more matte. Avoid identical white outlines or mirror-metal nut reflections. Reduce sharp glare and restore slight colour variation if sweet-like, or deep grooves if carved-wood-like. Cast shadow lower right, tight near contact and softer farther out; rim occlusion is narrower. Hide texture/reflection to test enclosure, then restore without bright lines through the cap rim. Rendering establishes no real ripeness, hardness or food quality.

## Audit proportion and connection without texture

Keep broad nut, cap and stalk shapes, checking opening alignment and near-rim occlusion. There is one nut; reflections or shadows cannot invent another tip. Repair cap depth, length and connection if untextured recognition fails rather than adding scales. Change one contour with lighting/tilt fixed. Reassess wall and surface for another oak reference rather than copying this invented shallow cap. Remove diagnostic near-rim/hidden-curve labels; real occlusion should explain enclosure.

## Retain rim-to-nut separation in export

Save the layered original and inspect rim, stalk root and nut tip in a PNG at use size. Reduce scale density/contrast if cap becomes black rather than whitening the nut. Lighten a crack-like rim and repair its contour. Record a specific background gap at the right wall for connection repair. Judge enclosure, material distinction and shared axis rather than scale count. Sources establish basic identity, not diagnostic proportions. No growing, food or material test occurred; a new view needs new rim/hidden geometry, not a shifted cap layer.
