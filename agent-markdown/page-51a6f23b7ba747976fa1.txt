Design an imaginary double-wall insulated-flask section, identifying contents cavity, inner wall, gap, outer wall and lid with leaders. A NEED Project teacher guide hosted by the U.S. Department of Energy explains how a partial vacuum between walls reduces conduction and convection. This picture illustrates one simplified structure; not all bottles are identical, and it cannot establish vacuum level, insulation duration or safety.

## Plan a vertical section and labeling area

On an approximately 1600 × 2000 portrait canvas place the flask centrally, reserving the right side for five labels and leaders and the top for a title. Sketch a vertical centerline and mark exterior width, cavity width and bottom thickness. These are illustration choices, not manufacturing parameters. Group outer wall, inner wall, gap, cavity, lid, leaders and guides and save. Begin in neutral grays without water, graduation marks or highlights; if labels crowd the flask, adjust available canvas space first.

## Draw continuous outer and inner contours

Draw an elongated outer shell section, then a narrower inner-cavity contour, leaving visible wall thickness on each side. Join side walls to curved bottoms; do not draw the inner wall beyond the shell. The cavity holds contents, the exterior outline meets the outside world, and different material or space lies between. Hide colors temporarily and trace down one side, across the bottom and up the other. If inner and outer lines cross, correct nodes and curves rather than covering them with a white rectangle.

## Represent the inter-wall gap with its own plane

Keep a continuous light-colored gap between inner and outer walls, connected along left, right and bottom in this diagram. It is neither beverage in the cavity nor outside background. Label it “schematic gap”; blue coloring does not make it real liquid. The NEED Project guide hosted by the U.S. Department of Energy describes partial vacuum reducing conduction and convection over most wall areas, not zero heat transfer everywhere. Neck, connections and lid require separate consideration; this diagram does not calculate their heat flow.

Keep a copy with only inner wall, gap and outer wall and hide the lid and background. Trace the three regions down the left and across the bottom, then restore the neck. If the gap accidentally opens into the cavity, inspect closed boundaries before adding arrows. A light color is a visual label and does not establish pressure or filler material.

## Distinguish neck, connection and lid

Draw a narrower neck at the top, with its edges connected to the body. This schematic can have an inner–outer wall connection near the neck; do not erase all supports just to show an uninterrupted gap. Draw a separate lid above the opening with a readable contact or gap at the rim. Do not color it as beverage, or infer that every product uses the same seal, threads or construction. If the lid floats, decide whether the picture shows closed or open and retain one state consistently.

## Place simple contents inside the cavity

Draw a horizontal liquid plane below the mouth, ending at the inner wall rather than entering the gap. Retain air space above so the lid and entire bottle do not appear full of liquid. Color and height are illustration choices, not safe capacity, drinking temperature or material suitability. If inner wall and liquid merge, adjust adjacent values or edges instead of deleting the wall.

## Connect five labels with leaders

Label lid, cavity, inner wall, schematic gap and outer wall on the right. Point each leader to its visible target, minimizing crossing and avoiding adjacent layers. Keep a monochrome copy with line styles or text so color is not the only distinction. A reflective surface in the reference is one possible means of reducing radiative transfer; this schematic lacks the information to calculate actual performance. Do not add insulation hours or an absolute no-heat-transfer slogan. A legend can state that this section is schematic. If lines crowd at smaller size, move labels and extend leaders while retaining their original targets.

## Reduce the view and check relationships

Hide all labels and see whether cavity, both walls and gap can still be traced; restore them and follow each leader to its target. If “cavity” points at the gap, move the leader endpoint rather than rename the region. Keep another copy without liquid and verify the inner wall still encloses a space; an empty bottle is not a bottle without a cavity. Preserve the earlier version and fix one junction at a time so changing all planes together does not conceal the cause.

## Save and inspect output separately

Save the editable draft and export SVG, PDF or PNG through an actually available entry, then open it elsewhere to inspect neck, bottom, five labels and crop. PNG is raster, and a bitmap inside SVG or PDF does not automatically become vector. Reopen the original and hide leaders, ensuring walls remain editable. Leave unperformed export, reopening or device work pending. The illustration is not container design, heat-resistance testing, food-contact certification or a performance promise for a product.
