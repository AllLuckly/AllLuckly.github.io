Draw a dorsal-view cownose ray with a notched two-lobed front, broad pectoral disc and tail extending behind.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-cownose-ray-two-lobed-snout-disc/>), translated on 2026-11-20. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Paint a cownose ray from above its back, head toward the top, broad pectoral disc extended to both sides and tail trailing behind. A NOAA identification resource describes cownose rays by a two-lobed notched front, an unspotted dorsal surface and a broad pectoral disc. This original static composition does not establish a real animal's size, behavior or field conditions.

## Fix the dorsal viewpoint and margins

Create an approximately 1800 × 1600 canvas with head up, tail down, room at the sides for the broad pectoral disc and space below for the slender tail. Lightly mark a vertical axis and a wide body sketch before adding eyes or water marks. Separate sketch, body, details, tail and water into layers and save a native draft. Overall widths may be similar from above, but the composition can be slightly asymmetric; real poses are not all perfectly symmetric flat planes.

## Keep the broad pectoral fins continuous with the body

Extend a broad pectoral outline left and right from the central body, joining the front to the head and curving each outer end back toward the posterior body. The pectoral fins belong to the disc, not two detached wings beside a fish. Start with one flat color; with texture hidden you should trace from left fin through body to right fin. If it resembles an ordinary fish with triangular stickers, repair the fin–body joins before adding heavy dividing lines.

## Distinguish two front lobes with a notch

At the front draw two neighboring rounded protrusions with a readable shallow central notch. Both reconnect to one head; do not turn them into long antennae or horns. NOAA's key illustrates the cownose ray's two-lobed front, while viewpoint and occlusion affect actual appearance. Check the notch at reduced size before adding detail. If it resembles a long split into the background, shorten it while keeping both sides continuous.

On the sketch, mark the front outline and a center axis, locate the notch ends on either side and hide the axis to check legibility. If the front becomes one smooth arc, adjust around the notch rather than lengthening the whole head. If the lobes resemble two separate balls, restore their shared base with the head first. These guides and points are drawing checks, not measured angles or markings to add to an animal.

## Connect the slender tail to the posterior center

Draw a slender tail downward from the middle of the posterior edge, attaching it to the same body rather than to a water mark. It may curve slightly and should taper along its length, not become a second broad fin. Ending the tip within the frame helps legibility but does not establish a fixed real length. For a white gap at the root, inspect layer order and connection before painting a large black patch. The exercise does not ask anyone to approach, catch or touch a real ray.

## Restrict details to the dorsal view

Block the dorsal surface in gray-brown or brown and use gentle value changes on both fins. The cited key describes an unspotted back; do not make dense white spots a required identifying feature. Consult reliable imagery for limited details visible from above around the head, but do not invent underside structures when they cannot be seen. Label the sketch “dorsal view” so the underside mouth or gill slits are not presented as mandatory marks on the back. Color supports the picture, not a health assessment.

## Use water color and contour for depth

Use low-contrast blue-green water with enough value separation near fin edges. A few curved water marks can suggest a surface direction, but not every mark should connect to the tail. Keep near fin edges readable and simplify distant background lines. If the dark subject disappears into dark water, adjust local water value before outlining the entire animal uniformly in white. Water color and shadow are visual choices, not evidence of depth, current speed or location.

Keep a no-water-mark copy beside the complete draft and check that the two-lobed notch, broad disc and tail read in both. If a water line continues exactly from the tip and appears to lengthen the tail, move or shorten the water line while retaining the subject's tail. This comparison checks visual connections in the design, not actual movement or tail measurement.

## Hide detail and trace the complete silhouette

Turn off water marks and dorsal highlights. Trace from one fin tip along the front to the two-lobed notch, then across to the other fin and back along the rear to the tail base. Check that neither lobe is detached, both fins join the center and the tail root is not floating. If a contour was erased with background, inspect the sketch and current layer and repair that segment before restoring texture. Two lobes alone do not establish species identification; proportions, range and observation conditions need separate evidence.

## Save and inspect separately

Save an editable draft, export an image using a control actually available in the installed version and open it elsewhere. Reduce it to inspect disc and notch, then enlarge the tail root and water marks for accidental joins. Reopen the original and hide water marks, ensuring the subject remains. Mark unperformed export, reopening or device operations pending and identify the result as a dorsal-view exercise with its source. It is not a field-identification, wildlife-handling or safety guide.
