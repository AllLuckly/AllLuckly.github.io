Use original three-dimensional vectors to check six-minus-three-one, two orthogonality tests and square-root forty-six, retaining order and zero-vector conditions.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/nasbook-cross-product-orientation-area-zero-vector/>), translated on 2026-11-16. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A three-dimensional cross product is a vector; its magnitude gives the parallelogram area, not any single component. Read an original coordinate card in Book Isle, compute components, dot against both inputs and check area/direction. A spatial sketch aids placement without automatic handedness detection, angle calculation or perpendicularity grading by the app.

## Fix a right-handed basis and vector order

Card V1 uses a right-handed orthonormal basis, a=(1, 2, 0), b=(0, 1, 3), first seeking a×b. Copy x/y/z consistently; an upward screen axis is not automatically z. Make an explicitly non-scale sketch and import coordinates/definition/checks as PDF or EPUB. Verify V1, names and symbol. A cross is neither scalar multiplication nor the dot-product mark. Enlarge or repair ambiguous typography before calculating.

## Expand components with the middle sign intact

Compute components a\_y b\_z−a\_z b\_y=2×3−0×1=6, a\_z b\_x−a\_x b\_z=0×0−1×3=−3, and a\_x b\_y−a\_y b\_x=1×1−2×0=1. Restore coordinate order to(6,−3, 1), not(6, 3, 1) after losing the middle minus. A determinant mnemonic still requires explaining the alternating sign, not treating diagonal marks as completed arithmetic.

## Test orthogonality against both input vectors

Let c be the result. c·a=6×1+(−3)×2+1×0=0 and c·b=6×0+(−3)×1+1×3=0. Check both: perpendicularity to one input alone proves neither correct direction nor magnitude. Here c is nonzero with a definite perpendicular direction; a projection need not show 90 degrees on screen. Wrong candidate(6, 3, 1) has dot product 12 with a, exposing the sign. This remains external arithmetic, not app feedback.

−c also dots to zero with both inputs and has magnitude√46. Perpendicularity plus length leaves opposite directions; order and handedness still decide. This explains why reversed order matters and cannot be removed from a summary that retains only area.

## Reverse order and check the opposite direction

Independently compute b×a=(−6, 3,−1)=−c, not the same directed result. The right-hand rule curls from the first vector toward the second; reversing them reverses direction. Equal area does not make the operation commutative. i×j=k and j×i=−k require the same right-handed basis. Mirroring a drawing changes apparent handedness, so retain declared coordinates rather than silently preserving mirrored direction marks. No device spatial-orientation measurement is involved.

## Cross-check area by magnitude and dot products

magnitude(c)=√(36+9+1)=√46≈6.7823 gives the parallelogram area; triangle area is half,≈3.3912. Independently magnitude(a)²=5, magnitude(b)²=10, a·b=2, so 5×10−2²=46 matches area squared. Dot product two is not area, and component−3 is not negative area. Length-coordinate units produce square units. These are invented units, not measurements from screen dimensions.

For a unit normal divide c by√46 to get(6/√46,−3/√46, 1/√46), magnitude one. Normalization preserves direction but changes length, not the original area into one. Distinguish cross product/unit normal. A zero cross product cannot be normalized by dividing by its zero magnitude.

## Keep zero for parallel vectors without assigning its direction

Let d=2a=(2, 4, 0). Then a×d=(0, 0, 0) and area is zero. Nonzero a/d are parallel, but their zero cross product has no assigned upward/downward direction; do not replace it by a unit normal. A zero input also yields zero product, and its angle is undefined under the usual nonzero-vector definition. Retain degenerate conditions rather than inferring a unique perpendicular direction merely from a zero dot product.

## Separate vector, direction and area notes

Where supported, save coordinates, reversed order and area as separate highlights or external notes, linked to V1/heading. Record vector(6,−3, 1), right-handed order/nonzero conditions and area√46 in distinct columns rather than one six. Return to the zero-vector control and check omitted qualifiers. Search V1/text if the cross symbol is not searchable, without assuming formula search. Separate source definition, drawing and your arithmetic from an automatic vector-analysis report.
