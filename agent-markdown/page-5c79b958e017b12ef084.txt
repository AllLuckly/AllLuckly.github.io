Build three Lagrange basis polynomials, verify node values and add a cubic nodal factor to test the degree condition.

Construct a polynomial of degree at most two through three distinct x nodes. Each basis polynomial is one at its own node and zero at the others, then weighted by the supplied y values. Passing through three points alone does not specify every possible curve or imply built-in fitting/formula plotting.

## Fix three nodes and degree at most two

Use (0,2),(1,1),(3,5), with distinct x nodes zero, one and three. Seek a real polynomial of degree at most two, not any smooth line; constants/linears are included without requiring a nonzero quadratic term in advance. Save nodes/conditions and separate basis, result, points and controls. Values are fictional, not experimental. Two different y values at one x would fail even a single-valued function and cannot be repaired with a vertical graphic segment.

## Normalize each basis at its own node

Set L0=(x−1)(x−3)/3, L1=−x(x−3)/2 and L3=x(x−1)/6. Denominators are products of node differences; retain L1’s minus sign. Substitute zero,one,three into each to obtain its one/zero/zero pattern. Subscript three names x=3, not a fourth basis. These are basis functions, not the final answer. Weight them by matching y values two,one,five, preserving node assignment throughout.

```text
nodes: (0,2),(1,1),(3,5)
L0=(x-1)*(x-3)/3
L1=-x*(x-3)/2
L3=x*(x-1)/6
p=2*L0+L1+5*L3=x^2-2*x+2
at x=2: (L0,L1,L3)=(-1/3,1,1/3); p=2
q=p+x*(x-1)*(x-3); q(2)=0; degree(q)=3
```

## Combine, expand and check all three nodes

Expand p=2L0+L1+5L3: x² coefficient 2/3−1/2+5/6=1, x coefficient −8/3+3/2−5/6=−2, constant two. Thus p=x²−2x+2, returning 2,1,5 at 0,1,3. At the extra check x=2, basis values −1/3,1,1/3 sum to one and yield −2/3+1+5/3=2. This catches summing y values without bases, but x=2 is calculated, not a fourth supplied observation. The bases sum identically to one: quadratic coefficients 1/3−1/2+1/6 and linear coefficients −4/3+3/2−1/6 cancel. This check is independent of y values. Equal values c would produce constant c, not compulsory quadratic curvature.

Use a suggested 1800×1800 canvas, origin (400,1500), 200 pixels per unit equally: X=400+200x, Y=1500−200y. Nodes map to (400,1100),(600,1300),(1000,500); calculated (2,2) to (800,1100). Display p over zero to three with continuation notes, not a function defined only at three sites. Approximate via pen nodes from external values, without claiming exact polynomial export. Correct bulging handles against check points rather than move supplied nodes.

## Keep basis plots separate from the final curve

If plotting bases, use separate panels sharing x ticks and clearly label L0,L1,L3 and p rather than overlay four tangled curves. Negative basis values do not invalidate inputs: L0 is negative at x=2 while the combination works. Transparency distinguishes membership, not algebraic addition. Different panel y ranges need labels and cannot support height-based coefficient comparisons. A basis-value table suffices without claiming the app converts expressions automatically into curves.

## Add a nodal-zero factor to test the degree premise

Define q=p+x(x−1)(x−3). The extra factor vanishes at all three nodes, so q also passes through them but is cubic, outside the prescribed space. At x=2 it gives 2−2=0 versus p(2)=2. Use a dashed control or table without replacing p labels. Uniqueness follows because two degree-at-most-two candidates differ by a polynomial with three distinct roots and therefore zero identically, not because three points determine every curve. Repeated equal nodes do not create independent constraints; distinctness and degree bound both matter. At x=−1, p=5 is the chosen polynomial’s extension, not a new measurement. For data from an unknown real function, uniqueness holds only inside the stipulated polynomial space, without automatic between-node error or extrapolation guarantees.

## Troubleshoot node differences, signs and degree

A basis not one at its node calls for its difference-product denominator. Wrong result values call for weights two,one,five; wrong x=2 calls for L1’s sign and fraction arithmetic. Never drag nodes to fit an approximate path or remove q’s leading term while retaining the same control claim. Fix one copy factor and recheck nodes plus extra point. A polygonal export concerns manual path design, not failure of interpolation. No automatic interpolation or polynomial-check button is assumed.
