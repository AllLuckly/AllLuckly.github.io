A perched bird can be modelled through feather groups; a flying barn swallow first needs wings distinguished from tail. Reference adult Hirundo rustica with pointed wings and a deeply forked tail, drawing an invented mildly oblique overhead pose. Aquarell does not verify a flight-feather formula or derive wingbeat rate, migration direction or speed from a still image. Simplify feather tips without confusing appendage ownership.

## Let the body axis establish flight direction

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px landscape canvas with pale grey-blue background. Aim head upper right and tail lower left, with a slender spindle body, not a round ball. Locate small head, chest and tail root, beak projecting along the action. Separate body, far wing, near wing, tail and sparse feather marks; use deep blue-grey above and pale warm beige below. Clarify head-neck and wing roots if fish-like rather than adding tail spikes. Size and tilt are original composition, not a scale. Adult reference must not dictate juvenile tails or identical deep forks for all swallows.

## Attach two wing roots to opposite body sides

Place two wing roots on opposite chest sides, near wing spreading upper left and far wing lower right. Join broad-to-narrow envelopes to the body. Hidden checking lines can establish bilateral attachment without showing through. Perspective may shorten and narrow the far wing, which must not grow from the tail root. Trace each tip back to the chest and confirm two wings. Revise body orientation and gentle bends if windmill-cross-like rather than blackening the centre. Repair root overlap if the far wing floats instead of hanging it from a line.

## Form pointed wings with leading and trailing edges

Build each wing from a smoother leading edge and gently bent trailing edge, tapering toward a long point rather than a uniformly triangular paper sheet. Show more upper surface near and less far, retaining connection to the original roots. Reduce deep scallops and radiating fingers if bat-like; thin outer tips and retain swept rhythm if thick-leaf-like. Group feathers sparingly without treating illustrative groups as anatomical feather counts. Do not bend hidden wing surfaces around the body merely to display completeness.

## Make the fork two ends of one tail

Begin a narrow tail root behind the body, broaden the tail surface and let elongated outer ends enclose a deep V-shaped negative space. They are neither chest appendages nor independent thin legs. Check with background colour that the V opens outside, not a hole within the tail, while retaining central feather surface near the root rather than only two lines. Soften rigid tips and restore feather surface if scissor-like. Narrow and realign a third-wing-like tail. Adult deep-fork reference does not impose one V angle or length ratio on every bird.

## Keep wings and tail from false junctions

State which wing-root portions the body occludes, while tail continues only from the rear. Leave a small background gap if near trailing edge approaches a tail tip, avoiding an invented membrane. Temporarily distinguish body, wings and tail in three greys, then restore colours after checking connections. Remove a tail-edge line wrongly extended to chest if four-winged; retain the actual fork. Reduce a deep mid-wing split if one wing reads as two. Legs may be inconspicuous in the selected flight pose, but a wingtip is not a foot, and no unrelated thick perch is needed to display legs.

## Wrap the dark back and pale underside around the body

Use deep blue-grey rather than pure black above. Wrap pale warm underparts onto the side with the volume instead of a straight line cutting the bird in half. Add restrained cool-blue reflections on the near upper wing and quiet the far wing under the same light. Keep eye and beak small rather than competing cartoon expressions. Soften selected edges and revise belly width if pasted-white-paper-like. Lift shoulder/back and belly locally if black silhouette rather than outlining every feather white. Adult upper/lower contrast does not establish sex or regional subspecies here.

## Check the flight pose as a silhouette

Temporarily make a single-colour checking version and reduce it: identify one head, one body, two wings and one forked tail immediately. Preserve fork and wing/body gaps without motion lines. Revise body axis and wingtip visual balance if falling-looking without claiming one angle necessarily proves gliding or ascent. Avoid speed streaks and blur implying unrecorded motion data. Slightly uneven feather tips must not fragment the long pointed silhouette. Restore original colour after checking without damaging the retained working layers.

## Save clear wing and tail connections

Keep the native work and open an exported PNG. Count two wings, trace both fork ends to one tail root, then inspect near/far root occlusion. Open the fork gap slightly or adjust background if merged, rather than thickening ends into legs. Restore chest/belly volume if arrow-shaft-like. Record adult barn swallow and invented pose, not frame-by-frame analysis of actual flight. Success means clear morphological attachment. A true side or overhead study needs new foreshortening and occlusion, not a simple mirror with unchanged conclusions.
