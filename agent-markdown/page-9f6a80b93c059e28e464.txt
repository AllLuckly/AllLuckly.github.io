Create an educational phase sequence distinguishing the Moon’s sunlit half from the portion visible from Earth.

## Set viewpoint and sequence

This exercise shows eight phases as seen from Earth’s Northern Hemisphere; the row of discs is not a scale orbital diagram. NASA gives the order new Moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full Moon, waning gibbous, third quarter and waning crescent. Label it “Northern Hemisphere illustration, not to scale.” Phases describe changes in the illuminated portion we see, not Earth’s shadow covering the Moon each month.

## Allocate canvas and positions

Create an approximately 2000 by 1000 landscape canvas in VectorPaint. Use controls visible in the installed version to separate background, Moon discs, illuminated areas, labels and arrows. Arrange eight phases in two rows of four, read across the top row first and then the bottom. Leave space for arrows and text, and save an editable original before adjustments.

## Create eight base discs

Draw one dark-gray circle as a base for the portion not seen illuminated, then duplicate it or draw eight circles of equal size. Arrange them in a grid with gaps. Colors and size are design choices, not actual lunar color or scale. Zoom in to inspect circle edges and out to check the eight positions; correct missing or duplicated discs before adding illuminated areas. Temporarily number the eight positions 1 to 8 and confirm the reading direction before adding names; if retained, keep the numbers distinct from labels so they are not mistaken for dates.

## Draw new and full Moon

Use a dark disc in position one for new Moon, when the near side seen from Earth is mostly unlit by the Sun; make the fifth disc nearly fully bright for full Moon. Preserve the circular outline in both so the new Moon does not look like a hole in the canvas. Use a light tone rather than necessarily pure white, and state that the images do not represent tonight’s actual phase.

## Draw first and third quarters

For this Northern Hemisphere view, make the right half of disc three bright for first quarter and the left half of disc seven bright for third quarter, with an approximately vertical division. Both show about half the visible disc illuminated, but they are different phases. Check order and bright sides against NASA rather than guessing from names; do not generalize this page’s orientation to every latitude. Compare the two enlarged, looking at the bright side before reading the label. If they face the same way, correct the seventh disc rather than simply swapping the names.

## Add waxing and waning crescents

Draw a thin bright crescent on the right in position two and on the left in position eight. You may overlay a dark shape over a light disc or construct the crescent with path or shape controls visible in the installed version, keeping the lit region inside the original circle. Check that the crescents do not become bright rings and that their sides match their labels.

## Add waxing and waning gibbous phases

For position four, draw a mostly bright waxing gibbous disc with a narrow dark region on the left; for position six, draw a mostly bright waning gibbous disc with dark on the right. Both show more than half the disc illuminated and occur before and after full Moon respectively. Compare neighbors: visible illumination increases from positions two to five and decreases from five to eight; the cards need not represent precise calendar dates.

## Label and connect the sequence

Place each phase name beneath its disc. Draw arrows left to right across each row, with a return arrow connecting the end of the upper row to the start of the lower row. An optional dashed arrow can show the cycle returning to new Moon, but must not suggest a scaled orbit. Keep labels, arrows and discs separate so the language can be changed without redrawing the Moon.

## Distinguish illumination from visibility

Add a note: the Sun illuminates half the Moon, while an observer on Earth sees different portions of that sunlit hemisphere at different positions. Seeing half the disc lit at first quarter does not mean the Sun illuminates only a quarter of the Moon. Do not explain ordinary phases as Earth blocking sunlight; a lunar eclipse is a different phenomenon whose conditions this card does not derive.

## Check sequence at small size

Read names in order and trace arrows, checking illumination in positions four, five and six and opposite bright sides at three and seven. At phone width, if crescents vanish, modestly widen the lit regions or enlarge the card while keeping them crescents. If labels are illegible, adjust spacing rather than relying on color alone. Before grayscale printing, preview the light and dark regions in a system viewer. If they merge, increase their value difference and inspect the crescents again. Verify that “Northern Hemisphere illustration” remains visible.

## Export and keep sources

Save the editable original and export a separate PNG through a control visible in the installed version. Reopen it to check all eight discs, arrows, text and illumination boundaries for cropping or blur. If a shape moves on export, inspect stacking in the original and export again. Record NASA sources, viewpoint and schematic scale; for an actual phase on a particular date and location, consult dated local astronomical information. Finally, place English labels on a duplicate and ensure longer names do not cover arrows or change the eight-position order.
