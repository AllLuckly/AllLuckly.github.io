## Declare the unit rectangle and reversed identification

Take(x,t)∈\[0,1\]×\[0,1\], identifying left(0,t) with right(1,1−t). The opposite-end parameter is 1−t, not the same height. Interior points do not all merge, and glued vertical edges become a seam rather than remaining boundary. Create rectangle, pairing, long-edge trace and cylinder-control layers; save native work. These are abstract parameters without centimetres/material thickness. The ideal surface definition, not stroke width, determines topology or boundary; it establishes no mechanical performance.

## Write two equivalence classes for four corners

Name L0=(0,0),L1=(0,1),R0=(1,0),R1=(1,1). Reversed gluing gives classes L0~R1 and L1~R0. Write two pairing rows before matching letters on opposite ends; do not pair equal-height corners. On a suggested 2200×1700 canvas use L0=(350,800),L1=(350,300),R0=(1750,800),R1=(1750,300). Screen Y increases downward, so increasing t goes upward. Use parameter-corner names rather than infer numbering from screen direction.

## Trace from the bottom edge through the seam to the top

Start L0 along bottom t=0 to R0, identified with L1. Continue along top t=1 from L1 to R1, identified with L0, closing one walk containing both long edges. Thus the remaining boundary has one connected component. Draw segment arrows and equivalence signs at seam jumps; external pairing leaders are not extra boundary. Glued vertical edges are excluded. Traversing all four rectangle edges instead traces the unglued sheet perimeter, not the quotient surface’s boundary.

## Use same-height gluing as a two-boundary cylinder control

Save a control changing only identification to(0,t)~(1,t): L0~R0 and L1~R1. The bottom closes on itself, while the top forms another closed walk, giving a cylinder two boundary components. Keep dimensions, long edges and corner names, changing only pairing. Both panels can remain flat rectangles yet have different results: identification, not a 3D-looking silhouette, determines topology. State one/two components in words rather than treating one/two colours as proof.

```text
rectangle: 0<=x<=1, 0<=t<=1
Mobius gluing: (0,t)~(1,1-t)
L0~R1; L1~R0
boundary: L0 ->bottom-> R0~L1 ->top-> R1~L0
remaining boundary components=1
cylinder gluing: (0,t)~(1,t)
L0~R0; L1~R1
bottom closes separately; top closes separately
remaining boundary components=2
```

## Distinguish boundary count from one-sided nonorientability

The Möbius strip is one-sided and nonorientable: transporting a local normal around its centre reverses direction, preventing a globally consistent continuous normal. This and “one boundary component” describe the same surface but are not interchangeable definitions or proofs. Note half-twist normal reversal separately; the main verification here is endpoint boundary tracing. Two paint colours or seeing only a front face do not determine orientability. Occlusion, view angle and planar fill do not change the identification.

## Prevent a crossing sketch from adding false identifications

If a band silhouette aids reading, use an over/under gap or pale shading at a projected crossing without identifying the surface points there. The rectangle pairing table remains authoritative; decoration cannot overwrite it. Organize paths, Bézier nodes and text manually, then adjust occlusion layers, with no requirement for automatic 3D twisting. Joining every crossing edge at one node suggests another quotient space. Recheck pairings rather than conceal the error with realistic gradients. The boundary proof remains complete without a 3D inset.

## Diagnose pairings rather than guess from appearance

Two components in the Möbius panel suggest mistaken L0~R0 pairing; four open edges suggest an unglued seam; one class for all corners suggests accidentally merging the two classes. Start at a long-edge endpoint, stop on returning to its equivalence class, then check for unvisited long edges. Retain failed and corrected traces with every seam jump justified. Traversing two segments does not mean two boundary components: count closed connected loops, not drawn segments or screen panels.
