Study an open umbrella through its silhouette, radiating ribs, curved panels and shaft connection.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-umbrella-ribs/>), translated on 2026-10-27. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

An open umbrella combines a rigid frame with tensioned fabric. Paint a long-handled umbrella tilted toward the viewer, connecting its crown, rib tips and shaft. Use one blue-green family without relying on patterns, establishing curved structure before a few edge details or raindrops.

## Set the canopy tilt with its crown and rim

On an approximately 1600×1600 px canvas, sketch a tilted shallow ellipse for the opening. Place the crown above its centre, not on the highest rim. Mark the side extremes and front and rear limits, establishing which direction the canopy opens before drawing every rib.

Separate background, sketch, fabric and shaft. Check a solid canopy with a clearer near rim and partially hidden far rim. Lower an excessively bowl-like crown or increase a flat disc’s rise. Repair the silhouette instead of using heavier outlines.

## Radiate the frame from one crown

Place six to eight rim tips, spread wider nearby and compressed farther away. Curve each rib from the shared crown to its tip. Establish the two frontal panels first, simplifying hidden rear segments. Keep a single structural origin instead of unrelated intersections.

The rib curves determine both frame and panel direction. Compare an odd curve with its neighbours before correcting it. Keep the guides faint and save the frame version for checking side-panel compression after painting; not every rib needs to remain visible.

## Make each fabric panel curved

Let fabric dip slightly between ribs, with tips projecting outward. Keep the rim dips shallow enough to suggest tension rather than petals. Paint two neighbouring panels in mid blue-green, opening and curving from the crown instead of forming rigid triangular metal plates.

Light from the upper left and turn gradually darker to the right. Avoid mechanically alternating light and dark panels. Establish the broad lighting first, then add narrow changes near selected ribs while retaining soft transitions across the fabric.

## Connect the shaft beneath the frame

Extend the central axis into a shaft partly hidden by the near canopy. Give it two fine edges for width. A simple hooked handle should maintain coherent inner and outer curves and connect to the shaft rather than abruptly becoming a detached thick ring.

Show only a few inner supports converging toward one centre. Use overlap breaks to prevent the shaft appearing to pierce the fabric. Correct the shared axis if the canopy seems tilted off the rod. Position and connection come before darker metal shading or reflections.

## Keep edges and raindrops restrained

Add a few near rib-tip lights and selected thin rim highlights. Optional drops should follow the canopy and shared lighting. Avoid identical white dots on every panel. They are hand-painted local cues, not an automatic rain effect, and should not obscure the frame.

Use a pale ground shadow without building an entire rainy street. A simple background exposes structure. Reduce rim scallops if it resembles a flower, check crown height and shaft if it resembles a saucer, and restore broad panel shading if it resembles a rigid paper fan.

## Study one canopy panel

In a small study, compare a straight panel edge with a gently dipping one between two ribs. Keep colour fixed and judge tension versus a petal-like sag. Apply the principle to the canopy without copying identical curvature across near and far panels.

## Compare the canopy with and without rib lines

Hide the darkest rib lines and check whether painted planes still form an open canopy. Restore them to assess their support rather than fragmentation. Judge tilt small, panels normally and accidental gaps close up, leaving distant ribs weaker than near ones.

Keep frame, painted and final PNG stages with an editable shaft. Next, tilt the canopy farther toward the viewer and rebuild ellipse compression, crown and far-panel width without changing colours. Record the rib most prone to error as an early checkpoint for the next study.
