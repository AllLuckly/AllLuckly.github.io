An enlarged subject does not make zooming and camera advance the same action. Observe owned or permitted material in iCouldPlay, comparing foreground/background relative size rather than subject width alone. The two versions below are original production plans and ideal-projection deductions. Without suitable equipment/external production, retain paper comparisons rather than claiming recordings or automatic focal-length, viewpoint or device recognition in iCouldPlay.

## Prepare equal-width cards and two versions

Plan equal-width near red and far blue cards, faces parallel to the image plane and offset sideways to avoid total occlusion. Record camera/card positions and versions separately: A changes magnification at fixed viewpoint; B advances the camera at fixed focal length. Produce only with familiar external equipment/tools; otherwise draw an untested timeline. External editing is not an iCouldPlay feature. Name sources, edited copies and plans separately instead of renaming one file as two recordings. Card colours in unfamiliar footage do not establish real depth.

## State ideal projection and distances

Set the paper baseline at axial depths of 1 metre to the near card plane and 2 metres to the far plane, measured along the shared optical axis rather than as slant distances to off-axis card centres, equal physical widths, ignoring distortion, focus changes and other processing. Use an ideal frontal-plane projection with image size inversely proportional to distance. Assign initial image widths of 100 px near and 50 px far, with ratio 2. Metres describe scene distance; pixels describe this illustration, not interchangeable units. These are not iCouldPlay measurements, nor a physical card width inferred from 100 px. Record departures in real equipment rather than imposing this simplified diagram exactly on every lens.

## Version A changes magnification at fixed viewpoint

Plan A uniformly magnifies the image twofold over0–2s without moving viewpoint or cards. Ideal widths100/50 become200/100px, still ratio2, not3. Paper or post-production enlargement demonstrates that relation without establishing a filmed optical zoom; record the method. Near width200 alone misses far-card enlargement. If cropping cuts off blue, mark incomplete comparison instead of reconstructing invisible edges for exact widths or claiming the background vanished physically.

## Version B advances the viewpoint and recomputes distances

Keep focal length fixed and plan a 0.5 m forward advance with stationary cards, yielding axial depths of 0.5 m and 1.5 m. Ideal near width 100 becomes 200, while far width 50 becomes approximately 66.67 px, exactly 200/3. Relative size becomes 3. Both versions end with near width 200, yet different far widths: equal subject size is not equal perspective. Compute inverse-distance relationships, then inspect references. Rounded values are not exact device measurements. This depends on stated planes/viewpoints/focal length, not every dolly shot or a half-metre movement guessed from unknown footage.

## Retain two content anchors during normal playback

For produced samples, open each version and play from complete-card visibility through the ending, noting both cards and background boundaries. Use available pause/replay controls, not assumed pixel rulers, camera trajectories or lens panels. ## Keep multiple production possibilities for unknown footage

Enlargement in unfamiliar footage may involve optical zoom, dolly, digital crop or combinations; pictures alone do not identify a camera/lens model. Relative size, occlusion and background position offer clues but need provenance, and moving objects can change them too. Do not call focus-related sharpness change a dolly; sharpness and size are separate fields. A stable subject with changing background may involve combined moves, not a forced A/B choice. Separate visible properties, credible production information and unresolved causes rather than replacing evidence with terminology.

## Check display conditions without rewriting production

For unexpectedly different backgrounds, verify file, anchor, bars and cropping; player display changes alter visible extent. Mark blurred card edges unmeasurable instead of recovering detail from interpolated screenshots. Temporarily disable optional captions obscuring edges and restore them; record burned-in text separately. Changing playback speed does not establish camera speed or restore unrecorded space. Scope observations to version/display conditions. Leave stabilization, distortion or external reconstruction effects unresolved if unchecked, without promising automatic perspective correction in iCouldPlay.
