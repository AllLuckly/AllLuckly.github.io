Late dialogue captions do not necessarily mean late audio, and moving captions cannot repair sound that differs from picture. Use a self-made clip with visible claps to compare contact and heard onset. These are repeatable observations, not frame-accurate measurement, and assume no audio-delay editor in iCouldPlay. Establish a familiar local-output reference before deciding whether another playback or sound path needs comparison.

## Record a clear action-and-sound sample

Record visible hands in a quiet setting, clapping near the beginning, middle and end with enough spacing to distinguish them. Use moderate action and playback level. Preserve the original recording without re-export, dubbed sound or added music. Each useful anchor needs recognizable contact and a short audible onset. Replace an obscured or noisy anchor instead of assigning a precise time to an ambiguous event.

## Observe picture and sound without captions

Open the sample without captions at normal speed and note whether sound seems earlier, picture earlier or the difference uncertain at each clap. The progress bar helps revisit a passage, not measure precise onset. A paused contact frame and remembered sound cannot establish a millisecond delay. Start with clearly noticeable or uncertain judgments and repeat the same passage. Use a clearer action if captions are needed to interpret it, avoiding three timing systems at once.

## Fix the current sound output

Record whether sound comes from the device, wired headphones or an existing wireless output, ensuring another screen or device is not producing it. Keep position and comfortable level stable, without simultaneously changing engine, speed or source. Connections and processing paths may matter, but one impression cannot diagnose a cause. Begin with familiar reproducible conditions without buying devices or granting new permissions. Create a separate set when changing output rather than combining different paths as one test.

## Repeat each of the three action anchors

Play from several seconds before each clap, letting playback settle so seek loading is not treated as an audiovisual difference. Repeat twice per anchor, noting direction and consistency rather than forced milliseconds. For one suspicious anchor, check recording clarity first. A similar direction at all three supports a more stable observation on this path, not an entire long film. Keep differing results rather than selecting only the one that supports the first guess.

## Compare paths with the identical original file

Use another trusted playback method you already have with the identical original file and same audio output. Similar results direct attention to recording and shared output; a difference narrows the path without proving a particular decoder is damaged. A same-name re-export is not the same file. Note any automatic track, speed or output changes in the other tool rather than calling an uncontrolled comparison a single-variable experiment.

## Check caption timing in a separate round

After recording audiovisual observations, optionally add self-written clap cues and compare text-to-picture and text-to-sound separately. If picture and sound align but captions are late, change the caption copy rather than playback speed. Where sound and picture differ, captions following either one do not establish a complete repair. List all three relationships, using the timing-drift workflow if needed. Report exactly what improved instead of calling a successful caption adjustment a repaired audiovisual path.

## Handle inconsistency without broad changes

For inconsistent early/late impressions, check changed conditions, unsettled loading and ambiguous anchors. Record uncertainty and use a clearer self-made sample if needed, not louder sound or library resets. Test another existing audio output separately and restore previous settings. If the installed app offers a related adjustment, understand its scope, retain the original value and change one setting at a time. Do not assume hidden delay controls when no entry exists.

## Keep a conclusion with explicit scope

Keep sample identity, three anchors, output, playback entry, repeats and observed direction. Describe a clear late-sound observation on this path or an unresolved result, not universal millisecond accuracy. For support, use a nonprivate self-made clip and short steps without private films or credentials. Repeat matching conditions after updates or device changes to assess improvement. Separating sound, picture and captions matters more than producing an impressive but unreproducible number.
