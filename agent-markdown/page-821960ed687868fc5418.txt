English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-folder-closed-open-document-icon-pair/>), translated on 2026-11-20. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Draw an original folder icon pair: the closed state emphasizes a tidy front outline, while the open state reveals one document behind a tilted front panel. Both share bounds, colors and line weight without requiring every node to have an identical shape. These are design exercises, not claims that software generates interactive states or that images replace accessible labels in a product.

## Fix icon frames, margins and deliverables

On a landscape canvas place two 120 × 120 square frames side by side, initially leaving about 12 units clear on each edge. Put closed and open states in separate groups; separate grid, outlines, document and notes into layers, and save an editable original. The dimensions are exercise choices, not a platform requirement. First compare optical balance in both frames. If one appears lower, adjust its position slightly and record the adjustment rather than claiming both coordinates remained identical.

## Draw the closed folder back and tab

In the left frame make a wide back panel with a low tab protruding at upper left. Use editable shapes or a path and adjust with controls actually present in the installed version. Back and tab need a continuous contour without an accidental projecting line at their join. Choose dark stroke and light fill, leaving text for later. If the tab disappears into the top edge at small scale, inspect its height difference and outline contrast before adding fine texture. With the front hidden, the back silhouette should remain recognizable.

## Let the front cover the lower back

Put a slightly narrower front panel ahead of the back, with similar bottom baseline and an upper edge below the tab so the tab remains visible. Use actual layer order for overlap rather than drawing a dark line and assuming objects are stacked. Inspect both side boundaries for unintended gaps and the bottom for doubled, misaligned lines. If available corner styles differ, adjust them toward a consistent appearance without assuming a specific rounding control exists.

## Copy the closed draft into an open state

Copy the entire closed group into the right frame and rename it, retaining back, tab, colors and baseline. Edit the right front panel into a shape with its top edge tilted gently rightward or downward, while its bottom corners remain attached: it is not a floating lid. Mark the bottom corners with light guides and check that they did not move accidentally. The angle is illustrative, not a mechanical measurement. If the necessary edit is unavailable, redraw the front in the copy and record that choice without claiming an automatic opening feature.

## Insert a document and check stacking

Place a pale vertical document rectangle between the right back and front. Its upper part shows above the front edge while the front covers its lower part; it must not pass through the folder bottom. Add one or two short dark marks as content without private information or real branding. Check the layer order: document in front of back, behind front; the closed group has no extra document. If the page looks pasted onto the outside, inspect layers before changing its exposed height instead of hiding the error with shadow.

Keep another open copy without a document and check the opening shape still reads. Both open copies share the same panels without changing back color or tab position, showing that “open” and “contains a document” are separate conditions. If exploring a second document, preserve gaps between exposed outlines so they do not merge into one rectangle; the deliverable for this lesson uses one.

## Check line weight, color and readable layers

Start both states with matching stroke width and palette. Where front edge and document edge meet, zoom in for accidental double lines or crowded intersections; adjust overlap before thickening every line in only one state. Hide color or use a monochrome copy and check that both outer shapes and the document remain distinct. Monochrome does not mean every part must form one solid black block: clear space also matters. Restore color and compare visual weight.

## Inspect small sizes against different backgrounds

Preview at selected 32- and 64-pixel sizes, checking tab, front opening and exposed page. These sizes are exercise choices and do not guarantee every interface. Compare both states against light and dark backgrounds. If details disappear, simplify document marks or adjust contour contrast first. Clarity in the enlarged draft does not establish clarity at small size; do not communicate state only through color. Shape and text labels can also help readers distinguish the states.

## Save source and inspect exports independently

Keep two clearly named editable groups and the no-document control. Export SVG or PNG separately through controls actually available in the installed version. An SVG containing an imported bitmap does not automatically become all vector; PNG is a raster output. Open each file elsewhere to inspect crop, transparency and document overlap. Reopen the original and move one guide to check that more than a flattened preview remains. Mark unperformed export, reopening or real interface tests pending; the drawing is not an integrated app button.
