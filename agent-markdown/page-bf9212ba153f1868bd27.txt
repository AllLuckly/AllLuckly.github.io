## Fix a noncollinear triangle and three point classes

Let A=(0,0), B=(8,0), C=(2,6) in dimensionless plane coordinates. They are noncollinear; base 8 and height 6 give area 24. Separate side midpoints, altitude feet and midpoints from vertices to the orthocentre. These are not all side midpoints. Create layers for triangle, guides, nine points/circle and labels, then save native work. Give classes colours and M/F/E letters rather than colour alone. Definitions determine positions; empty areas of the drawing are not places to add points merely to reach nine.

## Find circumcentre and orthocentre before their midpoint

The AB perpendicular bisector is x=4. The AC bisector through (1,3) has slope−1/3, giving circumcentre O=(4,2). BC has slope−1, so the altitude from A is y=x; the altitude from B is y=(8−x)/3. They meet at H=(2,2). Midpoint N=(3,2) of OH is not the mean of the three vertices. OA²=20, so circumradius√20 gives nine-point radius√5. Record O,H,N separately even though this example places them on one horizontal line. Also NO=NH=1 confirms the midpoint, without making N the triangle’s circumcentre. NC²=17, so vertex C is not on the radius-squared 5 circle. Keep the nine-point circle and circumcircle distinct.

## Use equal axis scales so the mathematical circle stays circular

Use a suggested 2200×1700 canvas with origin(300,1300) and 140px per unit on both axes: X=300+140x, Y=1300−140y. B maps to(1420,1300), C to(580,460), N to(720,1020), and radius to approximately 313.05px. Draw a thin triangle before placing the planned circle. If numeric controls are unavailable, approximate with guides and retain the coordinate ledger. Group circle and labels without stretching one axis. View zoom differs from coordinate transformation, and dragged approximations are not exact proof.

## Mark three side midpoints by coordinate averages

The AB,BC,CA midpoints are(4,0),(5,3),(1,3). Average each pair of endpoints rather than all three vertices. Label M1–M3 beside their sides; move crowded text with short leaders, not the points. Check(4−3)²+(0−2)²=5 and the other two squared distances likewise. This verifies only the first triple, not the altitude-foot group or every conceivable midpoint in the diagram. The other checks are(5−3)²+(3−2)²=5 and(1−3)²+(3−2)²=5. Keep all three equalities rather than rely on the circle covering the marks. Verify exact coordinates, not the outer edges of dot symbols.

## Check each foot for incidence and perpendicularity

The foot from A onto BC is(4,4): it lies on x+y=8 and(4,4)·(−6,6)=0. The B-to-AC foot is(4/5,12/5), satisfying y=3x; the C-to-AB foot is(2,0). Add right-angle marks without substituting them for checks. For the fractional foot,(−11/5)²+(2/5)²=5 independently checks denominators. Altitudes are not all screen-vertical, and their feet are not automatically opposite-side midpoints. Also the B-to-foot vector(−36/5,12/5) has dot product 0 with AC direction(2,6), checking perpendicularity as well as incidence for the second foot.

## Check the three vertex-to-orthocentre midpoints

The AH,BH,CH midpoints are(1,1),(5,1),(2,4), each at squared distance 5 from N. One endpoint is H, not O; a mistaken CH midpoint(3,4) suggests using CO. List three triples, whose nine coordinates are distinct here, and identify them by letter on the diagram. Hide the circle to inspect construction conditions, then restore it for concyclicity. This prevents dragging a wrong point onto the circumference while forgetting it ceased to be a midpoint.

```text
A=(0,0); B=(8,0); C=(2,6)
O=(4,2); H=(2,2); N=(3,2); radius^2=5
side midpoints: (4,0), (5,3), (1,3)
altitude feet: (4,4), (4/5,12/5), (2,0)
vertex-H midpoints: (1,1), (5,1), (2,4)
all nine satisfy (x-3)^2+(y-2)^2=5
```

## Use coincidence controls to limit the distinct-point claim

The name does not promise nine distinct locations for every triangle. In a right-triangle control H equals the right-angle vertex, so some feet or vertex-to-H midpoints coincide. Do not move them apart merely to display nine. Collinear inputs violate the nondegenerate-triangle premise and do not retain a finite circumcircle. Diagnose inputs, point identity and equal axis scale before decimals. A visually circular collection is only a clue. Preserve the faulty copy and change one category rather than conceal errors with a thicker circle.
