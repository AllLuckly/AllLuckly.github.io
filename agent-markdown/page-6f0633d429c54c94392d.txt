Paint a tree and path in spring, summer, autumn and winter, preserving viewpoint and positions while comparing colour, foliage and ground cues.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-seasonal-scene-quartet/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A four-season series is more than four recolourings. Keep one tree, path and distant hill in fixed positions, then change foliage, branches, ground and light for spring, summer, autumn and winter. Use separate studies without assuming multiple artboards or batch recolouring.

## Set up a separate study

Prepare four approximately 1400×1400 px studies with matching proportions. Fix tree, path and hill positions before seasonal foliage and ground. Mark three anchors with the same light sketch. Separate files do not require artboards, but names must clearly identify season and version.

Save a season-neutral native structure after checking the visual path among road, tree and hill. Recheck the same anchors for each study, allowing small variation but not drifting subjects. Give each change a reason, such as reduced foliage or added snow, rather than adding arbitrary objects merely for difference.

## Create one structural reference for all four studies

Sketch a tree near the left third, a path curving toward the rear right and a hill near the horizon. Mark crown extent, path entry and hill peak. Save a native reference. Reuse it through supported copy or import controls, or redraw the proportions by hand if needed.

Keep canvas size, viewpoint and three anchors consistent without requiring pixel-perfect alignment. Place season labels consistently in one corner. Stable composition makes environmental changes easier to compare; avoid redesigning the layout for each season.

## Suggest spring with new colour and an open canopy

Paint a loose pale-green canopy with some visible branches. Build volume in muted green before adding a few yellow-green lit edges, avoiding fluorescent colour. Add small new-grass patches and perhaps one restrained warm blossom group without replacing the canopy structure with dots.

Soften selected path edges into growing grass and keep shadows gentle but directionally consistent. Check the tree remains anchored in the same position. Spring should emerge from foliage, colour and ground together, remaining recognisable when the label is hidden.

## Make summer dense while preserving gaps

Expand the summer canopy toward the reference limit, using deeper greens in three or four masses with a few sky holes. Change overlap and volume rather than merely thickening spring’s paint. Give the tree a fuller ground shadow connected to its roots.

Let grass slightly overlap the unchanged path. If the image becomes one green field, mute the distant hill, warm selected foreground areas and control the darkest canopy mass. Do not rely on saturated blue sky to rescue the greens; establish values and depth first.

## Change foliage and exposed structure for autumn and winter

Make autumn sparser than summer, using ochre, orange-brown and restrained dark red. Gather fallen leaves to one side and shrink them with distance. Keep the path legible and organise broad warm masses before individual leaves, avoiding a confetti-like composition.

Reveal major branches in winter, tapering from trunk to fine divisions. Choose either bare grey-brown ground or pale snow rather than every winter symbol. Snow still needs coloured shade and terrain. Without snow, bare foliage, ground colour and subdued light can also suggest cold.

## Compare what stays fixed and what changes

Compare equal-size previews, checking the tree, path and hill anchors before seasonal differences. Correct drifting positions against the reference. Colour can vary strongly while spatial identity stays stable, allowing the viewer to recognise one location through time.

Hide the labels and identify at least two visible cues per season. Add foliage or ground changes if only colour differs, or remove unrelated objects if the shared scene is lost. Record the most effective change and reuse the comparison method with a different scene next time.

## Review and plan the next study

Compare all four at equal size, identifying the shared location before reading labels and finding two seasonal cues each. Inspect roots, path contacts and labels close up. Simplify an overly elaborate version so differences arise from the seasons rather than unequal time or decorative density.

Export season-named PNGs and retain the neutral structure separately. For another set, try a plant by a window, again fixing space before foliage and light. Reuse the comparison criteria to test observation rather than memorised green-spring, yellow-autumn and white-winter colour formulas.
