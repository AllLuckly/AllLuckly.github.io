Build a three-part text note for one argument, checking evidence identity, qualifiers and inferential gaps while preserving provisional judgement.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/nasbook-claim-evidence-bridge/>), translated on 2026-11-03. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A passage can mix the author’s conclusion, another study’s results and your own associations. Identical highlighting makes their relationships difficult to recover. Use ordinary Book Isle notes to separate claim, evidence and the reasoning between them, without assuming automatic argument identification or truth judgements. Accurately reconstruct the structure before deciding what needs further reading.

## Start with one contextualised passage

Choose one to three paragraphs with their preceding question and following limitations. Record edition and text anchors and bookmark the start. Read fully before categorising sentences, since later context may clarify their role. Reduce a book-wide issue to one tractable claim rather than labelling everything important without a next step.

## Identify speakers and endorsement

Check whether quotations and reported views are endorsed or presented for rebuttal. Record speaker and stance, retaining minimal necessary wording separately from paraphrase. Resolve ambiguous pronouns in context, or mark attribution unclear. Evaluation requires knowing whose claim the evidence is supposed to support.

## Preserve the claim’s scope and qualifiers

Paraphrase the main conclusion briefly while retaining “may,” conditions and population limits. Do not strengthen association into certainty or a local group into everyone. Compare modality with the original, preserving key qualifiers. A question is not an answer you may supply on the author’s behalf; shorter wording must not become more absolute.

## Identify evidence type and location

In a separate evidence section, identify data, case, observation, quotation or derivation and its location. Type guides checking without automatically ranking strength. Repeating a conclusion is not new evidence, and a vivid story is not population-wide statistics. Mark evidence absent from this passage without claiming it is absent from the entire book.

## Practise scope with an invented example

Use this explicitly invented example: ten readers report preferring short chapters, therefore short chapters improve everyone’s memory. Preference, memory improvement and universal scope need distinct support. Identify the extra inferential step rather than attacking an author or pretending this is a real study. Actual sources still require their specific evidence and methods.

## Write the inferential bridge as a question

Ask what relationship must hold for this evidence to support that claim—for example whether preference measures memory or the sample covers the claimed population. Mark it as your tentative analysis, not an unstated assumption quoted as the author’s words. A single clear bridge question is more useful than elaborate colour-coding without reasons.

## Search for limitations and counterarguments

Search key terms for conditions, failures or the author’s reservations, opening full context. Update an overstrong paraphrase when limits emerge. Try alternative terms or contents when search fails, especially for scans. Neither no result nor a matching word alone establishes absence or support; verify the same object and question.

## Keep three note identities distinct

Separate short source excerpts, paraphrases and further judgements with plain paragraph labels, not an assumed specialised argument database. Explain the role of a highlight rather than merely agreement. Date later interpretations separately, preserving earlier scope and evidence so your subsequent inferences are not mistaken for the author’s wording.

## Make provisional judgement actionable

Use a concrete status and one action, such as reading a table note or retrieving one reference, instead of a vague “needs research.” Defer judgement where support is missing; argument mapping is not expert certification. Check provenance in backed-up notes and verify anchors after changing book copies, keeping files and reading data distinct.

## Retell the structure, then verify it

Without looking, state claim, support and unresolved bridge in three sentences, then compare at the bookmark. Correct causal or universal terms you introduced. Repeat with another small passage. Success means someone can verify your reconstruction through provenance, not a high label count. Purdue’s argument guidance supports the terminology; Book Isle and this invented exercise do not automatically fact-check.
