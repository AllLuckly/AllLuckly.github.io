Orange and blue stand out together, but maximum saturation in both can become harsh. Use fruit and cloth to practise area, value and temperature. The orange should lead; cloth supports it with its own folds, without continually increasing saturation to create focus.

## Choose area before colour

Create a 1400×1100 pale warm-gray canvas. Place a roughly 430-pixel orange left of centre. Spread blue cloth from lower-right towards mid-left over about one-third of the image, leaving quiet background above. Avoid central placement and neat edge-following cloth; one diagonal fold connects them.

Prepare main orange, lighter yellow-orange, reddish-brown shadow, medium gray-blue and dark blue-gray. Test small corner swatches rather than copying exact codes. Separate palette, sketch and painting and hide tests at export. The palette still follows [Limited palette](<https://allluckly.cn/en/guides/aquarell-limited-palette/>)principle, assigning each colour a clear role.

## Establish the still life with broad colours

Use a controllable 80–120 pixel brush for medium gray-blue cloth, then fruit above. A slightly flattened orange is fine; do not endlessly repair it into a perfect circle. Three or four cloth turns suffice, especially a fold from lower-right towards the fruit base. Keep early opacity high for readable values without base colour everywhere.

Fix light upper-left: orange light there, shadow lower-right, and cloth planes following the same direction. Opposite lighting creates a collage despite attractive colours. Omit peel dots, fabric texture and leaves initially; zoom out and separate the two main masses.

## Do not use black directly for orange shadows

Paint the lower-right third with reddish-brown orange. Curve the boundary around the sphere, deepen near the base and retain faint reflected light at the right edge. Use broad yellow-orange light and add the smallest bright spot last, keeping orange through shadow rather than pure black.

If shadow is too red, add a little muted warm brown or compare lower layer opacity, not many unrelated overlays. Value grouping establishes volume before temperature. If it stays a flat circle when reduced, clarify the light–shadow boundary before peel texture.

## Connect objects through warm–cool folds

Paint a dark blue-gray fold band about 50–90 pixels wide along the cloth diagonal, beside a lighter plane. Converge towards the fruit without making every fold an arrow to one point. Add only one or two weaker secondary planes after the main fold.

A small warm gray-blue patch near the fruit suggests reflected warmth, with a little cool gray lower-right on the orange. Keep reflections darker than direct light and limited in area, not a blue outline. If unsure, establish volume first and compare reflections afterwards.

## Control the most saturated and brightest areas

Reserve the strongest orange for a small light–shadow boundary area and keep most cloth muted. Add one 20–35 pixel warm-light highlight upper-left on the orange, with a varied edge. Cloth highlights should be broader and dimmer, not an equal competing focus.

Use dark blue-gray or brown-gray for a short flat contact shadow, grounding fruit on cloth and softening outward. Hide the sketch and check depth. For more edge practice, see [Hard and soft edges on three stones](<https://allluckly.cn/en/guides/aquarell-edge-study-stones/>).

## Compare by changing only the background

Keep fruit and cloth colours fixed and try a cooler pale-gray background, judging orange warmth, then restore warm gray and compare blueness. One changed condition reveals relativity; changing all three hides the cause. Choose the calmer suitable version, restore formal layers and record test colours in a hidden export swatch layer. More vivid is not automatically better for a quiet still life.

## Judging results and common deviations

At reduced size, see fruit first, diagonal cloth planes second and background last. Squint to check fruit and cloth lights do not shine equally. Export PNG and keep layers; next change only the cloth to muted gray-green and compare the same orange.

**Why does blue cloth attract more attention than the orange?** Blue area, saturation or highlight contrast may exceed the orange. Reduce the cloth’s light–dark range before recolouring. Making the fruit ever more vivid often makes both objects harsh.

**Does warm–cool always mean blue and orange?** No. These easily distinguished colours practise relative temperature. Purple versus cyan within the same blue cloth can also differ in warmth. Beginners judge a few clear relationships more easily than many subtle hues together.
