Keep the original batch separate from retry rounds, correct one known condition and recheck unresolved items.

## 1. Start from a real incomplete result

Use an actually incomplete import batch and preserve original selection, feedback and current library state. Without a failure, prepare a small three-file reconciliation and mark failure/retry unperformed rather than damage files or interrupt important transfers. The example labels A/B confirmed and C unresolved; these are record roles, not a claimed result on your device. Preserve source files, imported items and exact errors. Clearing the library would remove the controls needed for recovery.

## 2. Retain evidence for confirmed A and B

Re-find A/B and verify originals, library titles and opening sounds, preserving their usable states. “Retain” means an external record, not an invented file-lock button or permission change. Selection marks without playback evidence are not confirmed success. A total increase of two does not identify A/B without item-level checking. Recovery addresses unresolved paths while retaining completed rows, not rewriting them as failures or deleting them to make a later total look neater.

## 3. Classify where C became unresolved

Separate unselected C, unreadable provider data, an import error, an absent library item and a present silent item. Selection, submission and playable counts are different; establish the last confirmed stage. A general notice does not provide a C-specific error code. Recheck its original provider: a listed cloud name is not readable content, but deleting the cloud original is no solution. Classify the gap to choose one small correction rather than label every unresolved result as network or format failure.

## 4. Correct one evidenced condition

Correct selection for an omitted C, establish readable provider content for unavailable data, or choose a properly identified compliant replacement for an oversized file. For silence, inspect track/output. Published formats, 512 MB and system readability still apply; renaming an extension to mp3 is not a repair. A different known-readable control can help an unknown cause but does not repair original C. Avoid changing file, account, network and theme together; preserve the prior state to explain the correction.

## 5. Select only the unresolved item for the retry

After preserving the original batch, manually select C or its clearly identified replacement through the available import flow, verify count/identity and submit once. No retry-failed-only button, byte-resume mechanism or rollback is assumed. A/B need not be repeatedly resubmitted for one unresolved row. Correct accidental selections before confirmation. Record time, entry and the single change without overwriting the original error. A duplicate notice calls for checking whether C already existed but was overlooked, not forcing another addition.

## 6. Verify the retried item and sound independently

Wait for retry feedback, then locate C and verify metadata, opening sound and pause/resume. Item presence and audible playback are separate; no error does not establish a playback pass. A replacement sample needs its own identity, with original C still unresolved rather than falsely repaired. Briefly recheck A/B without editing them. If another problem occurs, record its time separately instead of rewriting all earlier successes as though they never happened.

## 7. Preserve a stopping condition for repeated failure

If the same condition fails again, compare errors and retain version, provider type, size and both rounds rather than submit indefinitely. Return to the failure stage and identify missing evidence; unresolved work can remain pending while confirmed music stays usable. Do not delete the only copy, clear storage or upload a private collection to obtain a pass. Redacted samples/errors suffice. A working control does not prove original corruption, and incomplete recovery is not permission to invent success or claim every import fault repaired.

## 8. Deliver the original-to-retry relationship

Save original rows, the one correction, retry feedback and current outcomes, confirming retained originals and successful music. Explain A/B’s prior success, whether C truly recovered, any replacement and remaining uncertainty instead of reporting only one final addition. New batch sizes/providers need their own manifests; today’s method does not guarantee resumability after every interruption. Preserve original failures and do not invent corruption, disconnection or physical-device tests that were never performed.
