Use a permitted reference to draw the pumpkin silhouette, stem, surface ribs and contact with the table.

## Choose a specimen or permitted reference

Choose a real pumpkin or permitted photograph and record variety, viewpoint, light and source; if the variety is unknown, say so. Purdue Extension describes pumpkin varieties with different colors; observe ribbing in the actual specimen or photograph, so this orange round example is not a fixed form for every pumpkin. Draw only the visible stem and underside. Confirm photograph permission before sharing.

## Create canvas and composition

Create a landscape Aquarell canvas, for example 1600 by 1400 pixels, and save an editable original. Use visible layer controls to separate background, table, fruit, stem, ribs and sketch. Place the pumpkin near the center with room above the stem and at both sides; do not let the stem touch the frame. Dimensions are suggestions and control labels depend on the installed version.

## Block the silhouette

Use light lines to establish width and height, the top depression, widest point and base contact with the table. Observe whether the specimen is flattened, round or tall rather than forcing a perfect circle; the two edges may differ with viewpoint or variety. Check the silhouette at thumbnail size. Fix the whole form before drawing ribs if it does not sit convincingly on the table.

## Place the stem and top

Mark where the stem emerges from the top depression, then draw its direction, width and visible end. It need not be perfectly vertical; follow the reference tilt and join its base to the fruit rather than floating it above. If the other side is hidden, do not draw a complete contour through the fruit. Compare overlaps around the stem base and stop lines at the correct boundary.

## Arrange center and side ribs

Observe where ribs converge near the top and draw a few light curved boundaries down the fruit. Front ribs may appear wider and those near the edges narrower because of viewpoint; follow the photo instead of laying equal flat strips across a sphere. Ribs should relate to visible top and base structures and must not cross the stem or float outside the silhouette.

## Block base color and lighting

Apply a base color from the specimen, then distinguish lit, middle and shaded areas using one light source. Orange is an example; retain the actual color if the reference is green or pale. Do not darken the same side of every rib equally. Observe the overall value first, then use gentler differences to show ridges. With detail hidden, the fruit should remain a single volume.

## Give ribs gentle volume

Build gradual light and dark changes beside the ribs, following the curve of the surface. A groove can be darker than a neighboring ridge without becoming a pure-black line through the whole fruit. Compress areas near the contour so each segment does not resemble a separate orange slice. Reduce the image to compare silhouette and ridges; if stripes dominate, lower local contrast and compare again.

## Suggest stem material

Choose brown, green or gray from the photograph and observe the stem’s cut end and texture. A few value marks along its length can suggest roughness without crossing its base into the fruit. Use a soft shadow near the base to show the join, but do not obscure it entirely. If the stem is broken, draw the visible end instead of inventing an intact vine.

## Draw table and contact shadow

Establish table direction and the fruit’s contact area, then add a soft cast shadow consistent with the same light source. It may be concentrated near contact and fade farther away. Keep the background quiet enough to separate the stem and side silhouette. If the pumpkin floats, check whether the base meets the shadow rather than outlining the entire fruit in black.

## Compare silhouette and overlaps

Hide ribs and background detail to inspect the fruit, stem and table connection. Restore ribs and trace one visible curve from top toward base, checking that it follows the surface and stops at occlusion. If the stem looks pasted on, correct its insertion at the top. If ribs resemble a vertical fence, adjust curvature and near/far widths. Change one issue at a time and retain a before copy for comparison.

## Save and inspect the export

Save the editable original, then use a visible share or export control to create a separate PNG or JPEG. Reopen it to inspect stem tip, both sides, ribs and shadow for clipping. Return to the original and confirm fruit, stem and background layers remain editable. Record whether the variety is known, reference permission and one area to improve. Mark device steps not actually performed as pending.

## Compare center and edge ribs

Compare a broad front rib with a narrower rib near the silhouette. Both belong to one fruit and approach the visible depression around the stem, but their apparent curvature and width may differ. Temporarily lighten the rib layer to check that base colors and overall lighting already communicate volume. After restoring ribs, grooves should not dominate the cast shadow. If the stem covers a line, stop it at the stem rather than drawing across it. Follow table occlusion at the base and leave hidden areas unknown. Use a neutral-background copy to check separation of the fruit, then restore the original background and record the purpose and result of a revision.
