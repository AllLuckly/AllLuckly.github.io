Draw a Y-peeler in a near-front view with a slight view from above, blade across the top and handle below. The blade cannot float inside the fork, and brightening the whole fork is insufficient. Trace its two end mounts and elongated slot first, then distinguish the soft handle from the thin reflective blade. Include no fingers or food approaching the edge.

## Sketch one handle dividing into two arms

Use a canvas of about 1700 × 1900 pixels, with a nearly vertical handle axis and about 800 pixels of width reserved for the upper head. Leave shadow space below. Divide the top of the handle into two arms enclosing a broad negative shape; these are not separate handles. Follow the OXO Good Grips Y model rather than a straight-handled side-blade peeler. Separate sketch, handle/frame, blade and reflections on pale warm grey. Save, then hide the future blade area and check the continuous Y before adding pointed details.

## Return each blade end to its own arm

Draw a thin elongated blade between the arms, mounting its left end to the left arm and right end to the right arm. Simplify each attachment as a small pivot and contact edge, without a floating end or a long axle protruding like a wheel shaft. The slight view from above reveals some blade surface while its side remains thin. Keep both ends in the same perspective. If the blade bridges a different height from its arms, move the mounts before colouring; a thicker black line cannot repair disconnected geometry.

## Make the long slot an opening rather than a black sticker

Preserve a long narrow slot along the blade, with solid material on both sides rather than a black rectangle for the entire blade. Let the background read through it, limiting darks to edge thickness and ends. OXO describes two blades; suggest adjacent cutting edges without adding two complete crossbars. Temporarily use pale blue behind the slot and check that the same colour appears through it. If not, inspect covering paint. Restore the formal background afterwards rather than leaving the test colour inside the tool.

## Join the inner arms smoothly to the handle shoulders

Trace each inner arm from its blade mount towards the handle shoulder. Narrow the lower negative shape gradually without abruptly filling it as a solid triangle. The near arm may be thicker than the far one; copied symmetry must not contradict the blade’s slope. Treat the rounded shoulders differently from thin straight metal edges and inspect the frame in one colour. For a tuning-fork appearance, check the cross-blade’s end connections. For scissors, remove an erroneous second handle and crossing hinge rather than relying on a name label.

## Separate the thick grip from the blade’s highlight

Use dark grey or another muted colour for the handle, with a broad soft central light and darker sides, ending in a rounded tip. If showing the selected model’s visible hanging hole, put it at the handle end and reveal background through it, rather than drilling a large circle in the blade. Transition thickness where frame and grip meet; avoid a paper strip abruptly inserted into rubber. Hide the blade to inspect the solid grip, then compare them together. Colour proves no grip rating, and a drawn hole proves no load capacity or hanging safety.

## Keep metal reflections subordinate to the slot and thin edges

Use pale cool grey on the lit blade, narrow dark grey on the near edge and a few broken metal highlights. The slot must still read as empty. Do not fill both slot edges with white until they merge into a glowing solid bar. Keep grip reflections soft and selected frame edges firmer under the same upper-left light. Simplify a visible side potato-eye remover as a short projection matching the reference, not a third long knife; omit it if hidden. Extend the table shadow lower-right with firmer handle contact. For white-plastic appearance, adjust plane values rather than adding serrations.

## Use three paths to diagnose floating mounts and a sealed slot

Without highlights, trace from the handle tip up the left arm to the left blade end, then up the right arm to its mount, and finally along the background opening through the slot. Repair a broken mount locally rather than shrinking the head into a black lump. Reopen a sealed slot while retaining both solid edges. Keep the earlier draft and inspect at about quarter size for both Y and cross-blade. A slight reference-based blade tilt does not simulate force or cutting paths; drawn pivots do not validate real movement limits.

## Reopen and check the two ends and central negative shape

Save layered native work, hide connection guides and reopen an exported PNG. At fit-to-window size identify one handle and two arms; enlarge to inspect the blade mounts, long slot and handle hole as distinct features. Reopen editable work and alter the grip value without darkening the slot’s background. If the slot disappears when small, weaken nearby reflections or enlarge the subject rather than inventing a large product opening. Label the selected stationary Y model and mark unperformed export/reopening as pending. This is no blade-operation, peeling-performance or human-contact test.
