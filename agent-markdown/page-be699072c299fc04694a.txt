Observe four paper flaps, overlapping planes and tabletop contact in a folded model, then paint an editable illustration.

## Prepare a paper model

Use the Natural History Museum’s origami frog activity to fold a model from square paper, or choose a finished model photo with clear permission. Its four flaps form legs, but another fold can look different; do not treat one structure as universal. Place the model on a flat table and record viewpoint and light. It need not jump for this exercise, and the picture does not establish a tested jumping result.

## Set up the canvas and viewpoint

Create an approximately 1600 by 1200 landscape canvas in Aquarell and save an editable original; dimensions are suggestions. Use the layer controls visible in the installed version to separate background, model, sketch and shadow. Choose a slightly elevated oblique view so front and rear flaps do not completely overlap. Place the frog near the center with space around it, marking the tabletop and light direction lightly.

## Control proportions with a bounding box

Draw a light box that includes the four leg tips and mark the foremost, rearmost, leftmost and rightmost points. Compare the width and height of the main body and observe whether its center fold appears tilted in the chosen view. Treat each tip as a separate observation; distant flaps may look shorter. Do not assume a perfect mirror. Check the overall balance at thumbnail size before adding interior planes.

## Layer the four flaps

Determine which two flaps appear nearer and draw visible edges first. Stop rear flap lines where the body hides them. Render front and rear legs as folded paper rather than cylindrical animal limbs. Check that each visible tip connects to a paper plane so no triangle floats independently. If the chosen viewpoint hides a leg, preserve the occlusion instead of inventing a complete outline.

## Build the body planes

Observe which visible paper areas are approximately triangular and which edges look unequal in perspective. Connect the center fold point to visible edges without forcing screen angles into symmetry. Use a pale green, warmer green and darker green to distinguish three plane directions. Keep enough separation to read the folds without turning every crease into a black groove. Inspect edges close up, then zoom out to check that the model still reads as folded paper.

## Handle edges and creases

Paper edges are thin. Use a fine darker or lighter mark to distinguish a top layer without making the edge thicker than a paper leg. Some internal lines are creases while others are actual paper boundaries. Compare with the model and draw a distinct edge only where a layer visibly ends. Hide the sketch to look for unsupported crossings; restore it and revise which line is hidden where needed.

## Add a contact shadow

Using the chosen light direction, paint a soft shadow on a separate layer beneath the model. It can be darker near paper-to-table contact and softer farther away. Relate it to the actual visible flap positions rather than outlining the entire frog with black. Hide the shadow briefly: if the paper form remains clear but feels less grounded, the shadow is communicating contact rather than replacing structure.

## Compare two fold-line treatments

Duplicate the sketch. Keep all construction lines in one version and only the outline and confirmed visible creases in the other. View both at the same small size and decide which explains the four flaps and body more clearly. If the simplified version loses a key relation, add back one supported edge at a time rather than tracing every crease again. Keep the paper model and sketch for reference; this judgment is not automatic origami recognition.

## Troubleshoot outline and color

If the legs look pasted on, check whether each flap extends from a body edge and whether the overlap order is consistent. If the model looks like thick plastic, thin the edges and reduce highlights; paper values depend on light and material and need no metallic shine. If shadow separates from the frog, inspect contact points. For muddy color, check layer opacity and blend mode, making changes in a copy for comparison.

## Save and review

Save the editable original, then use an export or share control actually visible in the installed version to save a separate PNG or JPEG without overwriting the only original. Reopen the image to inspect all visible flaps, cropping and shadow edges; return to the original to verify editable sketch and subject layers. Record the model’s folding source, canvas dimensions and one uncertain overlap. Mark device, print or export steps not performed as pending rather than claiming a test.

## Check reference and image direction

Return the paper model to the recorded view, identify the flap nearest the observer and locate the same flap in the picture. If they differ, first check whether the image was flipped or the model rotated before revising the sketch. When adding a title, leave space between the text and paper legs; inspect a phone-sized preview to ensure text does not cover a fold or shadow. Save titled and untitled copies for different uses. Keep model proportions identical in both; adjusting the text should not move the paper flaps or table contacts.
