Equal-length velocity arrows in four circular-motion panels do not mean zero acceleration. Fix counterclockwise motion and a centre, then follow two arrow types in ManYue while checking speed, radius and period externally. These are original static diagrams, not an in-app animation or real speed measurements from screen arrows.

## Fix centre, radius and the observation frame

Externally draw a horizontal-plane circle centred at O=(0,0), radius 2m. A 0.5kg particle moves counterclockwise at constant speed 4m/s in the ground inertial frame. Put separate A–D panels at right, top, left and bottom, with P2 calculations and P3 controls. Export three PDF pages and retain source work. On import verify positions were not confused by image rotation and bookmark P1. Drawing radius is arbitrary but must carry the mathematical 2m label, not canvas centimetres or pixels.

## Read four tangential velocity directions first

At rightmost A=(2,0), velocity is upward, (0,4)m/s. At top B=(0,2) it is leftward, (−4,0); at C downward, (0,−4); at D rightward, (4,0). Speeds are all 4 but velocity vectors differ. Read this solid-arrow set alone in counterclockwise order, never pointing all velocities at the centre. A heading or orientation arrow would need a different label; these arrows explicitly denote velocity, not the direction suggested by an object’s drawn face.

## Add inward acceleration rather than a forward arrow

Externally compute a=v²/R=16/2=8m/s². Acceleration is (−8,0) at A, (0,−8) at B, (8,0) at C and (0,8) at D. Distinguish it by style and units instead of sharing a velocity arrow. At A, upward velocity and leftward acceleration are perpendicular: direction changes without increasing speed. The word acceleration does not require a forward tangential arrow at every point.

```text
R=2 m; speed=4 m/s; mass=0.5 kg
a_c=speed^2/R=8 m/s^2
net inward force=mass*a_c=4 N
T=2*pi*R/speed=pi s
A=(2,0): velocity=(0,4); acceleration=(-8,0)
B=(0,2): velocity=(-4,0); acceleration=(0,-8)
```

## Compute period from full circumference and speed

Circumference 2πR is 4πm; dividing by 4m/s gives period πs, about 3.142s. A quarter turn takes π/4s, about 0.785s. If panels represent equal-time samples, label that interval; four arbitrary images are not inherently equally timed. From right to left the path is half a circumference, 2πm, while displacement is 4m leftward. Displacement divided by speed does not give half-turn time. Retain π until final display rather than require rounded pieces to sum exactly.

## Centripetal describes the required net-force direction

Mass 0.5kg times acceleration 8 requires a 4N inward resultant. Tension could provide it, or several actual forces could combine to it; without a rope assumption do not name the entire value tension. Only a separate ideal horizontal-rope model with no other horizontal force permits tension=4N. Do not add another 4N centripetal force to an already counted tension. In this inertial frame, do not add an outward inertial force to manufacture static balance; the object has inward acceleration.

In a separate instant-at-A copy, remove every horizontal inward resultant. Existing velocity remains upward, not immediately radially outward. With no further horizontal force the horizontal model proceeds along that tangent. This is not a claim of perpetual gravity-free three-dimensional flight. Label the changed condition instead of retaining the original circle as the post-removal path.

## Test doubled speed, doubled radius and a full cycle

Change speed alone to 8m/s at radius 2m: acceleration becomes 32m/s² and period π/2s. Change radius alone to 4m at speed 4: acceleration becomes 4m/s² and period 2πs. Keep these controls separate instead of changing both and claiming a single-variable ratio. After a full original turn, final velocity equals initial velocity, giving zero average acceleration over the cycle. Instantaneous inward acceleration still has magnitude 8. A zero cycle difference does not erase the four inward arrows.

Original angular rate ω=v/R is 2rad/s. In a different control holding angular rate fixed at radius 4m, linear speed becomes 8m/s, acceleration 16m/s² and period remains πs. This does not conflict with halved acceleration at fixed linear speed 4m/s: the held quantity differs. Circle fixed linear speed or fixed angular rate before interpreting radius scaling.

## Troubleshoot tangent, inward direction and units panel by panel

For inward velocity, inspect the next nearby counterclockwise position and its travel direction. Forward acceleration may import increasing speed, absent here. An answer 2m/s² may omit the speed square; a one-second period may use diameter instead of circumference. Repair one arrow class in a fault copy, repeating the check at all four positions rather than rotating the whole page to fix one. Zoom small labels. A displayed arc alone does not establish an actually timed full revolution.
