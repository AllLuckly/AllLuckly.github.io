Draw an already dehusked, opened and emptied mature-type coconut half, not a whole green-husked fruit. Brown hard shell, white flesh and central cavity need separate forms that combine into a containing volume. This Aquarell observation study demonstrates no cutting or food preparation. Three visible pictorial levels do not mean complete coconut anatomy has only three layers, and the image establishes no freshness.

## Establish an oblique half-shell envelope

Use an approximately 1700×1500 px canvas with muted blue-grey background, opening toward the upper-left viewer and more near shell exposed. Establish elliptical rim and curved shell below on one axis, not a frontal circle above an extremely side-on base. Separate shell, flesh, cavity and shadow in deep grey-brown. Omit fibres initially and test a supporting half-volume. Add depth if plate-like or modest irregularity if overly perfect while retaining the cut plane. Pixels do not measure tissue thickness or identify cultivar; keep the specimen’s prepared state explicit.

## Place hard shell at the outer cut edge

Retain a narrow continuous deep-brown shell edge with perspective-varying width, not a black tyre. Expose a little near cut edge and narrower far edge. Shell encloses flesh rather than floating beside it. Check edge-to-exterior volume if black-painted-bowl-like or reduce an overly thick wooden-basin rim. The thick fibrous husk is already removed; residual fibres cannot become a heavy blanket. Other tissues exist in a whole fruit. This simplified view locates visible hard shell without replacing fuller botanical anatomy.

## Give white flesh thickness along the inner wall

Lay warm cream-white flesh inside shell, with visible rim thickness turning to soft grey-white within the cavity rather than a uniform flat ring. Near interior turns are clearer and far ones compressed, still lining the shell. Extend the inner wall if icing-like or reopen the cavity if plugged by a white cake. The visible flesh is endosperm, not a complete seed/embryo section. Do not label dark shell as flesh. Separate three areas without texture first rather than cutting black grooves between every tissue.

## Describe cavity through inner-wall values

This emptied half contains no depicted water surface. Turn the inner wall toward cooler pale-grey shade, slightly deeper below but not black. Flesh defines the cavity without a separate floating ellipse. Restore wall continuity/value turn if mirror-like or lighten the deepest area and retain flesh if it looks through the table. Near rim may hide part of the bottom; do not switch to overhead simply to reveal everything. Cavity shading establishes neither actual water quantity nor food-condition testing.

## Keep residual fibres on the shell

Add sparse grey-brown fibres following shell curvature, not dense radial spikes. Keep near cues clearer and distant ones quieter. Reduce protrusions if hedgehog-like or regular long grain if wooden-bowl-like. Keep fibres out of flesh/cavity and away from obscuring tissue edges. Hide them to test volume, then retain only useful material cues. The outer husk is absent in this specimen; neither text nor image should claim dehusking while restoring a complete thick fibrous layer.

## Differentiate flesh light from rough shell

Light upper left with broad soft flesh light and gentle interior greys. Keep shell matte deep brown with sparse accents, not sharp mirrored rims. Reduce white glare and separate material edge scales if plastic-like; restore cream areas and warm shell variation if stone-cave-like. Cast lower-right shadow tight at the lowest contact without swallowing the shell. Rendering does not establish fat, moisture or edibility. Omit decorative faces; inner-wall turning must explain the cavity.

## Audit tissue order separately from perspective

Keep a simplified section and read shell→flesh→cavity, then check near/far rims and base in one view. Correct order does not excuse flesh suddenly vanishing on one side, and good ellipses do not excuse reversed tissues. Change one wall segment or width with light fixed. A water-containing variant needs its own surface and occlusion, not a blue cavity bottom. New specimen states need new checks; neither mix emptied half with whole fruit nor infer complete anatomy from three visible areas.

## Retain inner-wall depth in export

Save the layered original and inspect shell edge, flesh turns and cavity bottom in a PNG at use size. Repair interior depth and near/far differences if only black-white rings remain rather than outlining everything. Reduce fibre noise. Record faults such as near flesh swallowed by shell shadow for layer repair. Judge containing half-volume, tissue order and coherent section rather than fibre quantity. Sources support identity/layers, not food or material acceptance. No cutting/tasting trial occurred; a new view or specimen state needs fresh observation and shading.
