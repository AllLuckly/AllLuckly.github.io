When you want to resume yesterday's comic but remember only its cover and a plot passage, reading history is more direct than searching everything again. ManYue's History and Continue Reading retain access clues, but Remove from Continue Reading and Delete Reading History do different things. This article recovers your position first, then explains how to organize lists without accidentally clearing records.

## Create a reading record you can verify

Choose a local comic or a reachable online work and read to the middle of chapter two. Note the chapter name, page number, and one distinctive visual detail, then return normally to the library. Do not record only the cover, since same-title works and different sources may share it. This sample lets you check history saving and reopening behavior.

For the first verification, do not disconnect the network or clear caches. Keep the original reading source available. History stores reading information, not automatically downloaded pages. If the source has failed, body pages may not load even though the record remains. Separating position records from content availability makes diagnosis clearer.

## Find a work through History or Continue Reading

Open History on the home page, or the Resume/Continue Reading scope in the library, and find the work you just read. Check its title, source, chapter, and latest reading time. A recently organized list usually makes newly read items easier to find, but filters and hidden states may affect visibility.

Choose Continue Reading and check whether the chapter and image match the recorded position. Page-number display can differ by reading mode, especially when double-page mode shows two images at once, so prioritize actual content. If chapter one opens, check whether you chose Start from Beginning or another source version before concluding that history is faulty.

## Narrow a long history gradually

If you do not know the full title, search by cover, recent reading time, or the available search feature. If you know the source, enter that library scope and inspect the history clues. Do not begin by clearing every filter: the current filter may help exclude irrelevant results. Understand each condition before deciding how to adjust it.

After finding a likely work, open details and compare chapters instead of immediately resetting progress. If you remember a specific plot passage, use chapter search to locate nearby installments and inspect existing progress. This confirms the work while preserving history and avoids overwriting positions in several similar works during repeated sampling.

## Remove items from Continue Reading first to simplify the home page

For a work you are postponing but whose record you want to retain, use Remove from Continue Reading if its library menu offers it. Confirm that it disappears from that scope while remaining findable in History or Favorites. This postpones reading; it does not delete comic files.

If the page offers undo feedback, use it promptly after a mistake and recheck the result. Do not treat a temporary undo entry point as a permanent recycle bin. Long-term recovery still needs an existing backup. Before organizing, decide whether you want fewer home-page items or complete removal of reading records to avoid choosing the wrong action.

## Understand the consequences before deleting history

Read the confirmation when using Delete Reading History. It clears the corresponding local reading position and read-chapter records, while files, favorites, and some server progress may be stored separately. Deleting local history does not perform the same cleanup on every server and device, nor is it the main way to free storage occupied by comic files.

If you simply do not want a book on the home page, consider removing it from Continue Reading or hiding it rather than clearing history. If you genuinely want to reread from the start, use the work's Start from Beginning action, but first decide whether old progress is worth retaining. Check important chapter bookmarks and notes separately instead of guessing the treatment of all associated data from a button name.

## Recover content after a source changes

Comic-source updates, server moves, or reordered chapters may require rechecking an old position. Check whether the source associated with the record still works, then locate the content by chapter title and visual details. Do not simply apply an old chapter-twelve number to a new source; it may insert extras or combine two installments into one chapter.

If the same server has moved, use the actual migration options in connection editing. If changing to another server or account, treat it as a separate source and keep the original records. History tells you what you originally read; deleting it too early loses that clue. Confirm the new entry point works before deciding how to organize old records.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does a work in History fail to open offline?** History is not a complete comic file. Check whether the relevant chapter has been downloaded or open a local copy instead. A cached cover and last page number do not prove that the body pages were saved offline.

**Can I find an item again after removing it from Continue Reading?** That action generally adjusts the resume display rather than deleting all reading data. Look in History or the work's details and restore it through the operations available in the current interface. Do not confuse it with deleting history or files.

**Does deleting local history clear server records?** Do not assume so. Server progress-synchronization capabilities differ, and native server integrations are not the same implementation as script sources. Check the relevant service when organizing server-side records rather than assuming one local operation covered every location.
