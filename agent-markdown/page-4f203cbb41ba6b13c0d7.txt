Use a permitted reference to observe the rounded face, horizontal breast bars, vertical belly markings and perch.

## Select a reference and check features

Choose a permitted photograph of a Barred Owl and record creator, source, viewpoint and access date. Cornell describes a rounded head without ear tufts and dark brown, nearly black eyes; upper breast bars run horizontally while the belly is mostly vertically marked. Pose and occlusion change visible patterns. Paint one perched individual without borrowing unseen feet or wings from another photograph.

## Create canvas and layers

Create a portrait Aquarell canvas, for example 1500 by 1800 pixels, and save an editable original. Use visible layer controls to separate background, branch, bird base colors, face, feather markings and sketch. Keep the crown, tail tip and branch ends inside the canvas. Dimensions are suggestions; use controls actually visible if labels differ across installed versions.

## Block the body and round head

Block the body with a large oval and mark head center, facial area, tail base and tip. Join the rounded head naturally to the body rather than floating a separate circle. Do not add pointed ear tufts seen on other owls. Compare head width, body height and branch direction, then inspect balance at thumbnail size. Move the whole composition if necessary rather than shortening the tail.

## Place the face and eyes

Mark facial disks, both eyes and bill from the reference. Compare the eye spacing and their distances to the sides of the head. In a nonfrontal photo the farther eye may be less visible; do not force perfect symmetry. Use dark brown or near-black eyes and add small highlights only if visible or helpful. Place the bill near the facial center without turning its tip into a third circular eye.

## Block brown and pale areas

Lay a light gray-white or pale brown body base, then use brown to summarize darker areas of head, back and folded wings. Keep the body volume intact rather than enclosing every feather in black. Maintain enough value contrast between face and background. Temporarily inspect in grayscale or squint to check the pale face and dark eyes; color temperature follows the reference light, not a unique species palette.

## Draw horizontal upper-breast bars

Place a few horizontal brown bars across the upper breast, curving gently with the body. Compare their width and pale gaps to the reference rather than making rigid ruler lines or extending them across the entire belly. Stop bars appropriately at the folded wing or body edge. At thumbnail size the horizontal rhythm should remain readable while the base color stays visible.

## Distinguish vertical belly marks

Below the upper breast, use shorter vertical brown marks with the visible interruptions and width variations. There is no need to outline every feather. Let the transition from horizontal to vertical follow the body instead of imposing a hard dividing line. If a branch or feathers hide the belly, stop at the visible boundary rather than extending marks into hidden regions.

## Connect toes and branch

Draw the branch direction and thickness under the body. Follow visible legs and toes: front toes cover the branch, while rear portions may be hidden by it, so stop lines at overlaps. Leave unseen parts unknown. Hide the body base to inspect leg and branch connections, then restore color to check that the owl appears perched rather than floating.

## Arrange background and branch

Place a muted green-brown woodland background behind the owl and branch without reproducing every leaf. Separate the crown value from nearby background so the rounded head does not vanish into a dark trunk. Avoid making the branch brighter than the face and dark eyes. If foreground leaves cover the bird, follow the reference overlap order rather than allowing background lines to pass through it.

## Check face and feather markings

First hide markings and confirm the head, body, folded wing, tail and toes form a coherent pose. Restore marks and identify horizontal breast bars versus vertical belly markings; do not turn both into one grid. Compare eye positions with the face center. If expression is uneven, correct structure before adding symmetric highlights. Change one issue on a copy to judge its effect.

## Troubleshoot and inspect export

If the eyes look pasted on, check their relationship to the facial disk. If markings resemble fabric, reduce them and curve them with the body. If the feet float, revisit toe and branch overlap. Save the editable original and use an available export control to save a separate PNG or JPEG. Reopen it to check the head, tail, branch and markings for clipping; return to confirm editable layers, record permissions and mark unperformed device steps.

## Compare markings with body curves

Follow one upper-breast bar across the bird and check that it curves gently with the body and stops where the folded wing obscures it. Follow one vertical belly mark downward; neither pattern should cross the face or branch. With the sketch hidden, pale gaps between feathers should remain visible. If dense marks pull attention from the eyes, reduce contrast on farther markings and retain a few visible nearby features. Compare the permitted photo beside the canvas without tracing it, and do not invent feet or tail feathers cropped from the reference. Record one concrete revision, such as darkening the background to preserve the round silhouette, and retain a before copy.
