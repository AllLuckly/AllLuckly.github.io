A projected crossing need not be a vertex, and a dot on an edge need not add a geometric corner. Draw a triangular prism in VectorPaint, check 6 vertices,9 edges,5 faces, then explicitly subdivide one graph edge to obtain 7,10,5 while retaining two. Keep geometric and graph conventions distinct, without automatic topology detection.

## Use two triangles as ends of one prism

On an approximately 1800×1500 canvas draw ABC, then translate it into A′B′C′ and join corresponding vertices. Example front screen points are (400,1000),(900,1000),(500,500), translated (200,-150). This oblique projection gives no three-dimensional volume from screen lengths. Preserve endpoint order and six identities, separating end faces, connecting/hidden edges and labels. Large dots must not turn crossings into nodes.

## List six vertices as two groups of three

List A,B,C and A′,B′,C′, exactly six. A prime is part of a name, not an edge, and projected overlap does not merge spatial vertices. Add no solid node at an unconnected crossing. Adjust an ambiguous projection instead. Here vertices mean prism corners; later P requires an explicit graph-subdivision convention, not a silent change within one ledger.

## Count nine edges as three plus three plus three

Count front AB,BC,CA; rear A′B′,B′C′,C′A′; and connectors AA′,BB′,CC′: 3+3+3=9. Hidden/dashed edges remain, and shared face edges count once. Six degree-three vertices give 18 incidences, divided by two to recover 9. Eighteen confuses incidences with edges; six may omit the rear triangle. Visibility, colour and width change no model count.

## List two triangular and three lateral faces

Faces are ABC, A′B′C′ and ABB′A′,BCC′B′,CAA′C′: five. Projected lateral parallelograms are not split by decorative diagonals. Face-edge incidences total 2×3+3×4=18; each closed-prism edge belongs to two faces, giving 9. Screen background is no sixth polyhedral face. In a plane embedding one of the five serves as the exterior face, rather than adding exterior to all five.

## Use six minus nine plus five as a consistency check

Use V=6,E=9,F=5, yielding 2. This checks consistency for the closed convex prism, not complete correctness from one number. Adding one spurious vertex and edge may still give two, so retain all ledgers. Visible colour regions/segments are not face/edge definitions. Removing an end changes to a surface with boundary; do not retain the closed-model acceptance condition. Keep both ends intact here.

## Declare graph vertices when subdividing one edge

In a separate copy place interior P on AB, explicitly replacing AB by AP and PB. P is a graph vertex, not a new geometric corner. Thus V=7,E=10,F=5 still gives two. An annotation-only P leaves geometric counts 6/9/5; never count AB,AP,PB all together. P has degree two, no third branch. This declared subdivision illustrates invariant counts without automatic node creation from every dot.

## Troubleshoot projected crossings and double-counted shared edges

For seven vertices without P inspect projected crossing dots. For twelve edges inspect repeated shared outlines. Keep each named edge once, regardless of overlapping strokes. Hidden lines preserve identities. Subdivision replaces one by two, net +1, not +2. Recheck ledgers and V/E/F after each correction instead of editing the final equation. Keep original and P versions separate to avoid mixed edge names.
