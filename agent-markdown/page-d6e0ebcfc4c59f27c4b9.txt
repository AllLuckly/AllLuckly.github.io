Reading statistics are most useful for showing when you find it easy to keep reading and which books actually received your time. Book Isle provides statistics and reading-goal entries. This guide uses a week's records to establish a small, sustainable plan, without treating total time as the sole measure of reading quality.

## Accumulate a few real reading sessions first

Choose a book you are currently reading, read a short section normally each day, and return to the shelf when you finish. During the first week, do not aim for impressive numbers; simply record the time you can realistically set aside, such as ten minutes on your commute, fifteen minutes at lunch, or one chapter before bed. Leaving the reader screen on does not count as completing your reading. Time spent on a page and actually understanding its content are different things.

Empty statistics on a newly installed device are unsurprising. It cannot automatically know time previously spent on paper books or other apps, and imported book counts do not establish historical reading. Complete a session with a clear beginning and end, then inspect statistics to obtain a verifiable baseline.

## Check the date range when opening Statistics

Open Statistics from the shelf menu or Settings. First identify whether the page shows today, this week, or a particular month, then inspect time, pages, or book summaries. Do not subtract figures from different ranges and call the difference an error, particularly across days, weeks, or a device change. Establish the date range first.

Compare a day's actual reading with its records. If you read only one novel on Tuesday, first check that it appears, then whether the time roughly matches your use. Statistics reveal trends; every stopwatch measurement need not match the displayed number exactly. Pauses, backgrounding, and timer completion behavior affect comparisons.

## Look at investment in individual books, not only totals

If the current page offers statistics for individual books, look at a longer work you are reading. A high total reading time with slow progress may mean that the content needs repeated study, or that you often leave the page open without continuing to read. Consider your notes and the chapters before deciding that you are reading inefficiently.

You might consult only a few pages of a reference book at a time but use it repeatedly over a long period. Comparing its completion speed with that of a novel read from beginning to end is not appropriate. Write the week’s main takeaway in one sentence, such as ‘I finally understood the exposure triangle,’ then compare it with the time you recorded. This allows the statistics to help you adjust your plans for the next week.

## Set an achievable goal

Open “Reading Goals” from “Settings” and set daily reading and weekly completion goals within the choices currently offered. Start a little below your ideal plan, such as fifteen minutes daily, then observe consistency for a week. This is a personal planning example, not a universal target or a guarantee that every version accepts arbitrary numbers.

A long novel or professional book may take more than a week, so a high weekly book target may be inappropriate. Match the goal to difficulty instead of repeatedly switching to short material just to meet a count. After setting a target, return to the page and verify the saved value before reading. A tap in a selection menu does not necessarily mean the final state changed.

## Configure notifications only when reminders are useful

For a daily reminder at a fixed time, enable it in reading goals or the relevant settings and choose a practical time, such as after dinner if you are usually free. System notification permission must be granted separately. An enabled in-app switch does not guarantee that the system displays notifications.

After setting a goal, check Book Isle's system notification permission and the current Focus mode. Reminders help bring you back to reading; do not depend on them for important schedules. If reminders arrive repeatedly when you have no time to read, adjust the time or lower the goal rather than changing actual reading status just to preserve a streak.

## A weekly review is more useful than chasing numbers every day

At the weekend, review this week's statistics and identify two time slots you find easiest to maintain, then choose suitable books for them. Short works can fit a commute; difficult materials deserve uninterrupted time. Change just one thing next week, such as replacing half an hour before bed with fifteen minutes at lunch, then observe the result. Changing books, times, and goals together makes the cause hard to identify.

For long-term comparisons, export a copy of the statistics. Put the date range in its filename and compare equal-length weeks rather than three days this week with seven last week. Page counts also depend on content and reading method. One hundred comic pages and one hundred professional-book pages do not imply equal reading effort.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do statistics differ after changing devices?** Check whether reading-data synchronization is configured and completed and whether both devices show the same date range. Importing book files does not bring all historical statistics. Covers on a new device do not prove that records synchronized.

**Why did the total not change immediately after I read?** End the current reading session normally and return to the shelf before checking. If uncertain, record a short session's time, device, and book and compare the actual result. Do not test statistics by deleting books or reinstalling the app, which may remove existing evidence.

**Can completed-book count represent the quality of completion?** No. Read status, reading time, and understanding are different information. For reference books you revisit, specific notes often have more value than pursuing a completion mark.
