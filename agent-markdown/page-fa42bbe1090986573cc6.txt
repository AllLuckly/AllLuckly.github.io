A hydrant illustration can accumulate so many bolts, chains and reflections that its outlet connections disappear. Treat it as a fictional red still-life object, depicting exterior connections without instructions for operating, repairing or using real equipment. Establish the main body and two short side outlets before adding caps and a few chain links. Layers allow separate inspection; component counts and proportions are simplified choices, not claims of compliance with equipment standards.

## Establish the main body and ground support

Use an approximately 1500×1800 px canvas with a pale grey-green background. Draw a vertical axis and short cylindrical body, adding a rounded top and slightly wider foot resting on one ground plane. Test upper-left lighting in three greys first. Separate sketch, body, outlets, caps and chain, leaving bolts out. If the top floats like a hat, reconnect its lower rim with the cylinder. If the foot resembles a separate sideways disc, redraw its ellipse around the axis. Hide the guide in the finished image if desired, but keep top, middle and bottom within one upright form rather than forcing unrelated shapes together with black outlines.

## Extend outlets with visible width from the body

Place a short outlet on either side, with slightly different visible orientations. Establish each axis, then upper and lower boundaries so its root joins the main body across a real width rather than emerging as one line. Use terminal ellipses compatible with their axes instead of two viewer-facing circles. Inspect roots in one colour before detailing edges. If outlets resemble hanging ears, shorten soft curves and clarify cylindrical sides. The invented pair need not show equal lengths; the far outlet can be partly hidden by the body but should not disconnect inexplicably at its root.

## Distinguish cap face from outlet rim

Add a slightly projecting cap to the near outlet, drawing the rim first and covering part of its front with the cap while leaving a narrow side thickness. Align cap face and outlet axes instead of turning the cap toward the viewer to accommodate a bolt. One simplified central projection is enough without claiming real specifications. Hide it and check that cap and outlet still separate. If the cap looks like a flat circular sticker, add side thickness rather than blackening the centre. Keep seam shadow local instead of forming a large black ring. These exterior forms explain attachment, not turning direction or real disassembly.

## Turn projections with their supporting planes

For small projections on top, cap or foot, identify the supporting plane before choosing orientation. Describe near ones more clearly and omit some far ones instead of stamping front-facing hexagons everywhere. Small blocks or short polygons can simplify them; establish relative sizes without rendering each realistically. If detail becomes black-dot clutter, remove some so main body and outlets remain dominant. Keep projections attached inside their cap boundaries rather than floating in the background. Return to normal size after refining edges. If details outcompete outlets, lower their contrast rather than enlarging the whole object to accommodate uncontrolled detail.

## Model red paint with broad reflections

Use muted brick red, lighter upper left and dark red-brown lower right. Describe reflections with one or two pale bands of varying width rather than identical white edges everywhere. Caps have their own turning surfaces under the same light. If mirror-polished, reduce sharp white spots; if flat red cut paper, strengthen continuous cylinder midtones and side shadow. A little wear can sit near the foot without identical paint chips on every projection. Colour and material serve form; random white dots cannot substitute for metal. Hide details to check the complete red mass, then restore them and verify distinct components.

## Give the short chain a route before links

If adding a short decorative chain, first set a drooping route from the cap side to an attachment on the main body, connecting both ends before drawing a few similarly sized links. Turn links along the route rather than repeating identically oriented circles. A few clear overlaps suffice and distant sections can simplify. Do not pass the chain through the cap centre and end it in midair, or cover the entire outlet root. Show chain and large forms alone and identify both endpoints; if impossible, repair the sketch route. Length and attachment positions are invented exterior details, not real accessory-configuration advice.

## Check connections along three directions

Hide chain and projections, tracing the body axis and both outlet axes separately. Each needs a continuous solid form around it. Inspect the silhouette for distinct top, outlets and foot rather than a gear-like cluster. If the far outlet seems to grow from behind the near one, correct body overlap. If both terminal faces are circles, reconsider their orientations. This does not demand symmetry; viewpoint can produce visible differences. Compare a deliberately viewer-facing near cap to see how it breaks the outlet direction, then repair the original. Do not fill unexplained connection gaps with extra parts.

## Inspect small attachments after export

Keep the layered artwork and inspect outlet roots, cap side thickness and chain endpoints in the PNG. If links merge into a black stripe, reduce their count and contrast rather than thickening the entire chain. If the red shadow side merges into the background, adjust background value locally without a bright-white outline around everything. Save a chain-free structural reference. A later colour variation may retain the connections without being called a new structural exercise. Record one precise fault, such as a right cap face misaligned with its outlet axis, to target the correct layer. Label the finished image as a fictional still life, consistent with its drawing purpose.
