Compare the system mono switch and distinguish playback output from the audio file.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/diaodiao-system-mono-output-versus-file-mix/>), translated on 2026-11-24. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

When listening through one earbud, the left and right sides of a stereo recording may contain different material. iPhone’s Mono Audio setting can send the same content to both outputs; it is a system output setting and does not directly rewrite audio files in the 调调 library. Compare the same owned recording with the setting off and on to decide when it is useful to you.

## Prepare audio with distinct channels

Choose a short stereo recording you are authorized to use, ideally one already known to have identifiable material on each side, such as “left” on the left and “right” on the right. On a computer you can inspect a two-channel waveform or listen to the channels separately in an audio editor. If you cannot establish the source channels, you can still observe, but hearing no difference does not prove that the setting failed. Do not use a test file of unknown origin or increase volume to hunt for a difference.

Copy the recording into a file location on the iPhone and import it into 调调, keeping the original backup. Play a few seconds in 调调 and confirm its title, duration, and sound identify this recording. Connect headphones with two working sides and listen in a quiet environment at a comfortable level; first check orientation and the output destination. If your hearing, environment, or equipment makes comparison unsuitable, skip listening rather than forcing a subjective judgment.

## Compare the system switch

In iPhone Settings, open Accessibility and then Audio & Visual, and find Mono Audio. Record its original setting and the left-right balance position so you can restore them afterwards. Labels can differ by system version and language; search Settings if needed, and do not mistake the system switch for a 调调-specific feature.

For the first round, switch Mono Audio off, return to 调调, seek to a passage where the channels are recognizable, and record what you actually hear on each side. Pause, return to system settings, and turn Mono Audio on. For the second round, return to the same passage in the same track, keeping the same headphones and roughly the same volume, and record both sides again. Do not also move the balance slider or change the output device; that would add variables that are hard to distinguish.

Apple explains that Mono Audio makes the left and right outputs play the same content. Perception can nevertheless vary with the source, headphones, and surroundings. When finished, restore the switch and balance to their recorded initial states and confirm 调调 can still play. If you use mono output in daily life, keep the setting that suits you rather than changing it merely to reach a supposed default.

## Keep the conditions on a record card

Make two rows on paper, one for mono off and one for mono on. For each, note track name, starting position, headphones, output destination, and what you heard in each ear. An approximate time is enough; do not demand precision you cannot hear. If playback pauses or the device changes midway, mark that round for repetition rather than comparing different passages.

If mono was already on, record that state, temporarily turn it off for the first round, and turn it back on for the second; leave it on afterwards. This ordering simply makes the two rounds comparable and does not imply that off is better. A quieter source channel, background sound, or differences in hearing can affect the observation; written notes are easier to check later than a vague memory that something seemed different.

## Interpret the result

Your checklist should identify the audio, switch state in each round, listening notes for both sides, and final restored state. Changing system output does not convert the file to mono: the original file remains in place and the library item is not a newly exported file. If you need a real mono copy, mix and export one separately in an audio editor, preserve the stereo original, and inspect the copy as a new file; this output switch is not a file conversion.

After listening, revisit Settings to verify that the switch is actually in the recorded state. System audio settings affect the device beyond 调调, so inform anyone sharing the device and restore the original value. Do not compare during a call, while driving, or when you need to hear your surroundings; choose a suitable break and keep the exercise short.

If both rounds sound the same, first establish that the recording has different channel content, that both headphone sides work, and that you returned to the same passage. The source may simply contain the same content on both sides. If only one side is silent, inspect the headphone connection, system output, and balance, and cross-check with another known-good recording. Do not conclude from one listening round that 调调 damaged the audio, and do not reimport or delete the original without a backup.

If several library items have the same name, identify the actual playing item by duration or recording content before attributing a difference to the switch. If volume changes noticeably, lower it to a fixed comfortable level and repeat both rounds; this is not a loudness measurement or proof of bit-identical channels. If the result is unclear, record uncertainty instead of forcing a conclusion. If you compare again, keep the same headphones, passage, and record method and see whether your observations are consistent.
