Five white petals are easy to count on a wood-strawberry flower, but its crowded centre can become a featureless yellow button. Draw one oblique five-petalled Fragaria vesca flower in Aquarell, separating petals, surrounding stamens and abbreviated numerous pistils on the central receptacle. Details are selectively simplified rather than counted from a specimen.

## Establish five petals without identical stickers

Use an approximately 1600×1600 px canvas with pale grey-green behind white petals. Mark a small centre and five axes, with two nearer petals wider and far petals compressed in the oblique view. Count five before adding width; no exact equal screen angles are needed. Inspect sepals or gaps if six appear. Gather bases if petals look like separate bowls. View and pixels are drawing choices, not measured flower diameter, and five petals need not be cloned outlines.

## Connect broad ends to narrow petal bases

Give each petal a rounded broad end and narrow base, with modest gaps allowed. Stop far contours at small overlaps. Separate boundaries if it resembles one five-pointed white sheet, and restore basal support if a background hole opens in the centre. Use very pale cool shade at roots and light outer edges. Avoid heavy plastic-like black borders and identical deep notches on every tip.

## Give the central receptacle gentle volume

Leave a small raised centre among petal bases, using pale yellow-green and warm grey for volume instead of a flat yellow disc. It supports many small organs and must not float like a bead. Overlap the roots subtly and compress the top into an oblique ellipse. Remove a uniform dark rim if it resembles a button. Reduce size and omit red fruit form if it resembles a ripe strawberry. This is flowering, without claims of completed pollination or future fruit set.

## Give surrounding stamens filaments and anthers

Suggest a surrounding stamen ring with varied density: a few near filaments ending in yellow anthers and shorter far strokes. Do not glue dots to petal tips or spread filaments as extra petals. Establish several readable filament-to-anther connections before group colour. Vary lengths and overlaps if they resemble rivets. Remove petal-like surrounds if dots become separate florets. Counts are schematic, not measured, and not every yellow mark denotes the same organ.

## Keep central pistil marks smaller than petals

Use smaller pale-green or yellow-green marks on the raised centre to suggest numerous pistils, distinct in position and form from outer anthers. Build a dense volume without complete sections or one giant lily-like pistil. Clarify near marks and compress distant ones, retaining ground colour. Reduce marks and restore light if it becomes a dark hole. Reduce seed-like bumps if it resembles fruit. This is pictorial abbreviation, not organ counting or diagnosis of reproductive function.

## Keep sepals and stalk behind the petals

Reveal selected green sepal tips behind petal gaps without changing the five-white-petal count. Curve the stalk down from the flower base, never a petal tip or through the centre. Abbreviate only visible green parts rather than exposing all hidden ones. Reduce contrast or exposed length if sepals dominate as petals. Clarify the short stalk-base junction if it floats. Background leaf washes are optional; omit a complex runner system that would replace this flower-organ task with an earlier propagation study.

## Inspect three groups before tiny marks

Hide fine marks and distinguish five petals, outer stamen ring and raised centre, then restore anthers and pistil suggestions. Remove invented mini-petals around dots. Repair filament occlusion instead of covering everything in dark yellow. Verify basal stalk and rear sepals. Note precise errors such as an unconnected third near filament/anther pair or a right-side sepal wrongly covering a white petal. Detail alone is not accuracy; establish categories and connections first.

## Reopen and verify petals and central layers

Save native work, hide axes and counts, export PNG and reopen. Count five white petals when reduced, then inspect selected filament/anther pairs, central marks and rear sepals. Separate group tones if compression merges yellow regions rather than outlining every dot black. Revise and export again, then reopen editable layers.
