A Venus flytrap’s trap is a leaf structure, not a mouth that appears after flowering. Reference Dionaea muscipula and enlarge one open trap, practising two lobes sharing a midline and marginal teeth turning with each rim. This is an original static Aquarell study, not closure timing or feeding experimentation. Simplify visible tooth count without turning two lobes into three petals or giving them separate stalks.

## Set the opening direction of one main trap

Use an approximately 1600×1500 px canvas with pale grey-green background. Tilt the main leaf slightly right, stalk entering from lower left and inner surfaces facing the viewer. Block one midline and two shallow-dish lobes opening around it, initially without teeth. Separate stalk, outer/inner surfaces, teeth and sparse background leaves; use green outside and dark red inside. Reduce symmetric decoration and trailing points if butterfly-like, clarifying the stalk. Remove an extra third petal if flower-like. Pixels, opening angle and colours are composition choices, not diagnoses of light, health or cultivation.

## Join two lobes along one leaf midline

Run the midline from stalk end toward the opposite end, joining both inner lobe edges without a full background split. Locate one continuous guide, then let inner surfaces meet over it rather than drawing a thick black hinge rod. Restore shared base and line if two separate shells; change surface orientations if a flat round leaf. This midline is morphological, not a metal pivot with invented springs. Perspective can offset distal heights, but both lobes must occupy compatible space.

## Attach the stalk at the shared base, not a tooth

Transition stalk and broader basal region from lower left into the midline’s start, allowing slight near-base occlusion while retaining traceability. Do not attach at an arbitrary outer rim or a tooth. Hide red interiors and inspect the green envelope as one leaf. Repair base width and soft turning if two clips holding a stick; revise occlusion if the stalk pierces mid-interior. Suggest one or two quiet background leaves, not a flowering whole plant that confuses trap and flower stalk. Do not invent unseen roots to fill the page.

## Turn between inner and outer surfaces at the rim

Use dark red inside, slightly deeper near the midline, with grey-green outer edge and back. Show a little outer thickness on the near lobe and more interior far, not equal back strips around every edge. Check orientation and folding without teeth, reshaping a lobe envelope if needed. Thin fleshy lip-like rims and reduce oily glints; add soft concavity if cardboard-flat. Red is not a universal diagnostic mark: coloured interiors clarify layers here, not injury, water shortage or digestion.

## Grow each marginal tooth from its own rim

Select a portion of each outer rim for teeth, roots attached to the edge and tips leaning toward the opposite side of the opening. Vary spacing and visible length around the turn. Add no row on the midline or detached white tooth blocks. Begin sparsely and add a few only after checking silhouette; visible simplification is not a fixed species count. Reduce identical rigid spacing if saw-disc-like. Revise tips or widen the gap if teeth interlock despite widely open surfaces. This open pose does not establish captured prey merely from a crossing.

## Distinguish inner hairs from marginal teeth

At sufficient scale, suggest a few short fine inner hairs rooted on the inner surface, finer than marginal teeth. Do not extend every tooth to the midline as a vein. Place visible hairs from reference; omit and record them if too small without claiming exhaustive anatomical checking. Keep texture faint rather than a red carpet obscuring form. Remove tongue-like bulges and tooth-block highlights if mouth-like, restoring leaf and stalk. Retain only informative lines if cluttered. No hair stimulation, induced closure or reaction-speed procedure is provided; a drawing establishes no physiological process.

## Use negative space to check the shared opening

At roughly quarter size, check that two lobes enclose one opening, their common midline stays continuous and teeth belong to their own rims. Background can show between teeth, not through the entire midline. Repair shared base and turning if two adjacent flowers. Show more interior and gap if a closed clip rather than adding a third lobe. Quiet rear companions so overlap does not create a third main lobe. Do not add prey to explain unclear structure; the paired relationship must work alone.

## Save the two-lobe, one-midline, one-stalk check

Keep the layered original and inspect an exported PNG for two lobes, midline, shared base and stalk in order, attached teeth and no checking guides. Reduce visible tooth count or enlarge the main image if gaps merge, rather than heavily outlining each tooth. Soften reflections and edge thickness locally if plastic-like while preserving connections. Record a static open-leaf illustration and visible omissions. A closed trap needs newly checked lobe occlusion and teeth, not a horizontally squashed image. Success concerns morphology, not experiments, identification or cultivation diagnosis.
