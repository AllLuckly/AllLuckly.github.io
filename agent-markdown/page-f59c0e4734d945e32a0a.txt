Draw a pestle leaning inside a mortar, distinguishing the cavity, outer wall and thick rim through overlap.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-marble-mortar-pestle-cavity-rim-contact/>), translated on 2026-11-19. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Draw an upward-opening stone mortar with its pestle leaning towards the upper right. Establish the opposing turns of the inner bowl and outer wall, then place the pestle contact and rim overlap. Show the visible upper cavity without cutting open the underside. Add veining only after the structure works; texture cannot conceal a missing hollow.

## Place the opening and pestle lean in the sketch

Use a canvas of about 1800 × 1600 pixels. Place the mortar towards the lower left and reserve upper-right space for the handle. Sketch an outer opening ellipse about 820 pixels wide and 300 high, then attach tapering outer walls; this is not a top-down circle. Separate sketch, body, interior, pestle and texture into layers against warm grey. These proportions set the composition, not product scale. Save native work, then check that the handle clears the frame and that a shadow can fit beneath the bowl.

## Build the thick rim from two related edges

Draw a smaller inner opening within the outer ellipse. Show a broader near rim and a narrower far strip, joined smoothly at the sides. Do not shift the opening into a small off-centre hole or substitute an equal-width white outline for a rim in perspective. Block in medium grey, with a lighter lit surface and a separate interior value. If it resembles a thin metal basin, check for a rim reduced to one line. If it resembles a solid stone cake, restore the opening-to-wall distinction before darkening the centre.

## Turn the cavity continuously down from the inner edge

Turn the inner wall down from the opening into the bowl bottom. The far inner wall may be darker without making the near wall equally black. Use broad, soft planes to describe concavity, retaining a midtone at the bottom rather than a hole through the table. Hide the outer-wall layer and trace from the lip into the cavity. No horizontal line should divide it like a liquid surface. Remove a water-like bright band and restore the curved surface. This drawing contains no liquid, ingredients or floating objects to explain depth.

## Place the pestle end inside before connecting its handle

First draw a thick, rounded pestle end towards the right of the bowl bottom, then extend a narrower handle diagonally upwards to the right. Do not begin with a long stick pasted across the rim. Keep both outlines continuous through a gradual thickening, without a detachable ball. Remove hidden lines according to depth: the near rim may hide part of the lower pestle, while the pestle hides the far interior. Both overlaps must read together. Do not make the pestle translucent to reveal the full bowl, or infer that this static pose balances unaided.

## Localise contact darks around the end and inner wall

Add a short contact dark where the pestle end approaches the bowl bottom, leaving adjacent interior midtone. Do not surround the whole pestle with an even black border. Light its upper-left side and darken the lower right, matching the mortar’s upper-left illumination. Keep the outer wall rounded as it tapers down, with its base contacting the table; a dark interior does not require a black outer base. For a floating pestle, check placement before adding contact. For penetration through the wall, correct outline endpoints rather than concealing them with stronger shadow.

## Turn veins over three different surfaces

Build stone with warm and cool greys, selecting two or three representative veins across the rim, outer wall and pestle. Vary direction and width around turns; a rim vein must not run straight across the empty cavity. The pestle is another piece of stone, so its veins need not join those on the bowl like a map. Try low-opacity strokes before adjusting contrast. If they read as cracks, weaken uninterrupted dark lines and break some sections. The reference product is reversible; this drawing shows only the upward-facing side and does not invent a solid underside section.

## Hide texture and check the cavity and opposing overlaps

Hide veins and highlights. Locate the thick rim, inner wall and outer wall in three values, then trace the pestle from handle to end. Identify the far interior hidden by the pestle and the pestle section hidden by the near rim; these need not occur on the same side. For a shallow plate, restore readable inner-wall turning. For a stick through a stone ring, restore the bottom and end contact. Retain a pre-edit sketch and correct connections locally rather than reversing all layers. The check concerns depicted space, not measured capacity.

## Reopen and check the rim and pestle end

Hide the axis and temporary colours, save native work, and export a PNG to open separately. At fit-to-window size check the overall support; enlarge to inspect accidental breaks in the near rim or veins crossing empty space. Reopen the editable file and lighten only the pestle texture to verify that the mortar shadow remains independent. If inner and outer walls merge when small, adjust their junction before adding white specks. Label the upper cavity and leaning pestle as an illustration with no underside section. Mark unperformed export or reopening as pending; this is not a grinding or durability test.
