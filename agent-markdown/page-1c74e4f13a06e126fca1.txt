Draw one hand’s castanet unit slightly open, showing one hollow more clearly and the other narrowed by its turn. The “two shells” form one hand unit; a usual two-hand set can contain two such units, or four shells. Include no player. Focus on inward-facing hollows and the upper cord, without inferring pitch from colour.

## Establish the shared attachment end of the two shells

Use a horizontal canvas of about 1900 × 1500 pixels. Place the cord attachment near the upper centre, with two rounded shells opening slightly below. Sketch whole outlines first, without making two spoons both face fully forwards: their hollow sides face each other. Separate near shell, far shell, cord and grain from the sketch on pale beige. This angle supports legibility, not a playing-range measurement. Save, then hide the cord and check that the shells approach at one end and separate at the other before placing the hollows.

## Enclose the near hollow with a thick rim

Draw the hollow inside the shell facing the viewer more directly, retaining a thick wooden rim and continuity into the upper ear. Let its contour turn with the exterior rather than pasting a perfect circular hole onto a pear shape. Use a narrow lit edge and an inner wall darkening towards a midtone bottom, not pure black. For a perforated wooden sheet, restore the cavity floor; for a leaf, check shell thickness rather than adding vein-like grain. Show only the visible hollow, without an X-ray view of its full interior.

## Narrow the far shell while keeping its hollow inward

The far shell exposes a narrower inner face and some outer thickness, but its hollow still faces the near shell rather than turning away. Establish the background-coloured wedge between them, wider below and narrower near the cord, then remove far edges hidden by the near shell. If the shells coincide completely, adjust the viewpoint slightly instead of attaching the far one to an unrelated cord. If both hollows face the viewer fully, turn the far shell correctly. Neither lower opacity nor extra shadow can repair opposing orientation.

## Attach the cord to the ears rather than the shell bottoms

Draw short cord sections at the adjacent upper ears, entering where visible in the reference, not sprouting from cavity floors like stems. A readable loop may overlap itself; stop the hidden line at the crossing without inventing a complex concealed knot. When holes are not fully visible, show only the visible entries rather than prescribing a universal hole count. Temporarily colour the shells and cord differently and trace the loop for an accidental third loose strand before restoring related natural colours.

## Give convex exteriors and concave interiors different turns

Under upper-left light, retain warm-brown midtones on convex exteriors with darker turns towards the edges. Shade each hollow according to its inner-wall orientation rather than copying the exterior highlight. Keep the ears integral with their shells; a narrow root dark must not cut them off. Add proximity shadow near the attachment, while most of the slightly open gap still reveals background. Do not blacken the whole gap into a glued seam. For a thick-wallet appearance, restore two separate rims and inward hollows rather than relying on one dividing line.

## Assign grain separately to each shell and turn it into hollows

Choose only two or three faint grain lines per shell. Turn exterior grain over the convex form and interior grain into the hollow; never continue across the empty gap into the other shell. Suggest cord fibres with short rhythms rather than the same long curves. Hide texture to verify the structure before restoring a few details. Shell colours may differ without being labelled high or low pitch. Pitch depends on construction, material and other factors; the drawing includes no listening or physical measurement. A wooden reference also does not imply that all castanets use this material.

## Check two shells, one loop and inward-facing hollows

Hide highlights and grain, count the two shells of this one-hand unit, identify their inward hollows and upper cord attachments, then check for background in the lower gap. If there seem to be four pieces, look for rims mistaken as extra shells; if only one, restore the far outline. A checking note may state two times two equals four for a usual two-hand set, without adding those extra shells to the main image. Retain the faulty version and repair orientation, overlap and cord path in order. Correct counting validates neither technique nor sound.
