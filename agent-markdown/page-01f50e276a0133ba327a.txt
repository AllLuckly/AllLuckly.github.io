Use a half-second overlap between two original voices to inspect intervals, caption coverage and unresolved words.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/icouldplay-overlapping-speech-turn-time-record/>), translated on 2026-11-10. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Two voices may overlap during a response, acknowledgement or competition. Timing alone does not establish rudeness. Use two original demonstration voices in iCouldPlay and record identity evidence, intervals and captions separately, without speaker separation, voice recognition, emotion analysis or identity inferred solely from screen position.

## Prepare a small sample with production notes

Use a self-recorded or participant-authorized small clip with filename, edition, production notes and speaker aliases, keeping private identities private. The original paper lines are A saying “This side is ready” and B saying “Let me look,” without real disputes or sensitive speech. Existing trusted editing tools can create a control; otherwise draw two time bands and leave listening untested. One person changing voice is not real two-person voice evidence. Aliases identify declared practice roles, not arbitrary unknown recordings.

## Mark each voice’s start and end separately

The original specification places A at file seconds one–three and B at 2.5–four. Draw separate bands; their intersection is 2.5–three, lasting 0.5 seconds. A lasts two seconds and B 1.5, totaling 3.5 voice-seconds, but their union spans three seconds because half a second overlaps. Do not add durations as elapsed clip length. These are production assumptions, not precise measurements from manual pauses. Label real listening estimates approximate or unresolved rather than reporting the planned numbers as measured.

## Listen to the complete passage before assigning continuation

Play normally from before either voice until after both finish, at a comfortable volume. Record intelligible words and unresolved mixtures before interpreting continuation. An isolated half-second does not show whether the previous sentence was complete; retain surrounding intonation and utterance scope. The [Cambridge study of overlapping talk](<https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-in-society/article/overlapping-talk-and-the-organization-of-turntaking-for-conversation/D12AD4181BEEFB073869BE7C55B07660>) provides contextual background, not a license to diagnose character, relationships or motives. Unknown words remain unclear rather than supplied from expected captions.

## Separate audible identity from people on screen

Map practice identities from known production notes. Without reliable evidence in a real clip, use observational labels Voice1/Voice2 instead of assigning A to the person on the left. Off-screen speech, dubbing and editing can break apparent face/voice correspondence; approximate lip motion is insufficient alone. Record visible person, attributable voice and evidence source separately. Unmatched identities are acceptable unknowns. Existing file audio tracks do not imply a separate track per person, and the app need not isolate voices.

## Check whether captions cover both voices

For original captions, separately inspect whether both lines appear, whether speakers are labeled and their cue intervals. A merged cue’s duration is not either person’s speech duration. Captions may paraphrase or omit acknowledgements; reading both sentences does not mean every spoken word was audible. Without captions, keep an auditory record without assuming transcription controls. Preserve wording differences and positions rather than overwriting regular captions. Top-line placement does not establish earlier onset or social importance.

## Change only B’s start for a non-overlap control

In a paper control, move B to seconds three–4.5, keeping A at one–three and B’s 1.5-second duration. Boundaries touch without a positive-duration overlap. The union now spans 3.5 seconds, rather than the original three, because simultaneity was removed rather than words added. Save any actually edited audio under a new name with notes; it does not correct historical dialogue. Old caption timings introduce a separate variable after editing, so record caption adjustments instead of blaming all resulting misalignment on playback.

## Replay with the same track and surrounding context

Replay from earlier while fixing track, output and caption selection, changing one available condition at a time. If slower playback helps, record and restore the original value; expanded wall-clock time is not an original file boundary. Retain unintelligible scope rather than forcing an answer through excessive volume. Audible overlap can be an observation, while acknowledgement, response or interruption requires context. Do not turn limited evidence into an accusation or a judgement of the whole person.

## Reopen and verify scope and unknowns

Reopen the same passage, check file, track and speed, listen through both lines, and compare original/control. Preserve evidence for voices, onset/end scope, overlap arithmetic, intelligible words, caption differences and candidate interpretations, marking untested items. Keep recording, production notes, captions and notes separately without overwriting the sole original. Completion makes the half-second overlap and differing union durations recalculable while retaining real listening unknowns, without reconstructing masked words automatically or deciding conversational motives.
