A zero discriminant does not mean x is zero, and a negative discriminant is not absence of roots in every number system. Read three original quadratics in Book Isle and separate normalization, coefficient extraction, distinct-real-root counts and candidates. Calculate on paper or in available notes, without assuming an automatic algebra engine.

## 1. Fix real solutions and the source location

Save an original card in an already readable format, explicitly counting distinct real solutions. Check the square exponent, minus and equals signs, retaining identity/anchor; x squared is not two times x. Preserve originals/dates/notes and keep answers separate. Use paper if a note feature is unavailable, without inventing a solve button. Obtain a clear question instead of inferring a convenient constant from an answer.

## 2. Extract coefficients from three zero-form equations

All three right sides are zero. Record a=1 and b=−4 for each, with c three/four/five. Keep b’s sign within the coefficient. Extract before solving to distinguish reading from formula errors. State a≠0 alongside the real domain: with a zero, the equation is not quadratic. Nearby constants do not justify copying one result across all three identities.

```
A: x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0
B: x^2 - 4x + 4 = 0
C: x^2 - 4x + 5 = 0
D = b^2 - 4*a*c; a != 0
Count distinct real solutions.
```

## 3. Square the whole signed coefficient

Use the discriminant in [OpenStax quadratic equations](<https://openstax.org/books/college-algebra-2e/pages/2-5-quadratic-equations>). Squaring the whole coefficient minus four yields sixteen, not minus sixteen. Obtain 16−12=4, 16−16=0 and 16−20=−4 separately. Preserve products/signs instead of recording only positive/zero/negative labels. D is an auxiliary coefficient calculation, not an x solution or file identifier; label its role.

## 4. Count distinct real roots from positive, zero and negative values

For real coefficients with a≠0, A has two distinct real roots, B one, C none. B’s repeated root can be counted twice by multiplicity, but this card asks distinct values; do not switch counting conventions mid-answer. C’s conclusion is restricted to real numbers. A text extending to complex numbers changes the domain, so preserve “no real roots” rather than generalizing to every number system.

## 5. Find candidates and substitute into the originals

A factors as (x−1)(x−3)=0, giving one and three; both make the original zero. B is (x−2)^2=0, giving two. Keep factor grouping and multiplication clear. B directly disproves D=0 meaning x=0: substituting zero yields four. Candidate checks and counts corroborate each other, but verifying one found root alone cannot establish completeness, so retain the discriminant reasoning. Expand the substitutions as 1−4+3=0 and 9−12+3=0, checking powers and multiplication first. Do not pair one root’s square with the other root’s linear term; candidate identity must persist through each row.

## 6. Use completing the square and a nonzero-right-side control

C becomes (x−2)^2+1=0, whose real left side is at least one, agreeing with D. For a separate control, change A’s right side to one; normalization changes c to two and D to eight. Do not reuse c=3 or call this a correction to the original. If a becomes zero, analyze a linear equation first rather than treating division by zero in the quadratic formula as a calculator fault or universal no-solution condition.

## 7. Troubleshoot sign loss and duplicate counting

If all three D values are negative, check whether (−4)^2 became −16. Two entries of two are not two distinct values. Invented ±2 for C suggests skipping the negative discriminant/domain. Correct one identified step and preserve input/reason/substitution, not just endpoint labels. Describe manual checking rather than unseen automatic feedback. Obtain missing question conditions instead of substituting an unrelated similar online exercise.

## 8. Leave, return and recalculate the three rows

Leave and return by the original anchor, confirming constants three/four/five and zero right sides. Cover answers, independently extract coefficients, obtain D four/zero/minus four and count two/one/zero distinct real roots. Keep questions, coefficients, discriminants, candidates/substitutions, domain and corrections. Preserve original dates/books and do not pass unperformed export/sync. Verification is reproducibility, not an app-wide automatic-proof claim.
