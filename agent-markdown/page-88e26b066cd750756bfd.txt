Draw a paper lantern’s outline, ribs and tassel with a small value range, checking curved form and reduced-size linework.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-paper-lantern/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A lantern’s rounded outline and curved ribs suggest its volume. Draw a fictional hanging paper lantern using open curves and closed colour shapes. Light is represented through chosen values, not automatic lighting or real electrical construction.

## Establish the body and hanging axis

Use a 700 by 900 pixel canvas with a central axis, body in the upper half and space below for a tassel. Test fullness with an ellipse and locate the top and bottom openings. Establish the hanging point and balance before decoration. Correct an overly flat body before adding ribs.

## Draw a continuous tapered outline

Build a closed outline through top opening, side bulges and bottom opening, using handles for continuous curves. Leave small opening widths for end caps rather than sharp tips. Inspect a plain fill for flat spots or corners. Keep a saved base silhouette before experimenting with the position of maximum width.

## Align the ribs with the rounded surface

Draw ribs from near the top opening toward the bottom, with a nearly straight central rib and increasingly curved side ribs. Keep them lighter than the body shape. Start with three and add only useful lines. Parallel straight stripes flatten the form; vary spacing and curvature deliberately rather than distributing copies mechanically.

## Suggest a luminous centre with values

Add a lighter central shape with darker sides and establish the value pattern before gradients. Keep ribs sufficiently visible. Check translucent highlights against different backgrounds. This is an illustrative light effect, not a simulation of a lamp lighting the room. Hide highlights temporarily to confirm the underlying shape still works.

## Keep caps and tassel independent

Add end caps matched to the openings, connect the hanging cord to the top and a knot and restrained tassel to the bottom. Keep tassel length subordinate to the body and clear of the canvas edge. Group components and recheck their joins after movement. Use a few readable strands rather than a dense tangle.

## Check the edges against light and dark backgrounds

Save the original and inspect outline, cord and tassel against light and dark backgrounds. Simplify fine structure for a small export. Keep poster wording clear of the hanging point. Reopen transparent output to ensure test backgrounds are absent. Compare a cooler colour copy using unchanged shape and value placement.

## Inspect the ribs in an outline version

In a copy, use a pale body and stronger outline/ribs to inspect where each curve starts and converges. Hide any line that does not explain the surface. Remove the tassel to assess body balance, then restore colour and check highlight boundaries near caps. Keep useful curves without accidentally carrying heavy diagnostic outlines into the lighter final version.

## Diagnose a flat lantern

For a striped-paper appearance, increase useful rib curvature and taper it into the openings. For a plastic appearance, soften the value contrast. Check whether an off-centre cord is intentional and visually balanced. Strengthen only lines that break at delivery size, and compare one type of correction at a time.
