Your scanned handouts, albums and old notes can be packaged as CBZ and read in Book Isle. Their pages are usually images rather than reflowable text. Use a short practice document to check preparation, import, sequence, clarity and references. Work with copies and retain the original scans separately.

## Check image orientation and boundaries first

Before archiving, inspect every scan for upside-down pages, cropped numbers and missing backs of double-sided sheets. Compare neighbouring pages for continuity, particularly illustration backs and blanks. Blank pages may preserve left-right relationships; establish your reading needs before removing them merely to reduce the count.

Correct skew or uneven lighting in copies with your own image tools, then check small text again. Do not assume Book Isle import automatically fixes perspective, restores cropping or recognises every word. Missing source edges usually require a fresh scan; changing reading type size cannot recover them.

## Establish a stable sequence before archiving

Use consistently padded filenames to express order, including cover, text and appendices. Avoid mixing single- and multiple-digit numbering or relying on modification time. Sort by name in a file tool before archiving and read page numbers and adjoining passages to confirm the sequence.

Keep a simple map from image numbers to printed pages. Covers and contents can offset file numbers from printed numbers. Do not overwrite your only scans to add numbering; filenames and an external list are sufficient and can also support future archive revisions.

## Create a genuine CBZ and import it

CBZ commonly holds image pages in a ZIP container. Package the prepared images with a reliable archive tool and inspect the contents for actual pages rather than unknown nested archives. Establish the container before naming it CBZ; renaming RAR or another compression format does not convert its internals.

Select the sample through local file import, check name and format, then read it. Start with few pages so a damaged image can be distinguished from an archive problem. If import fails, confirm that a system tool can extract the archive and open every source image.

## Check three representative locations

Inspect cover and first text page, a middle diagram and the last page. Compare order, orientation, edges and captions. A visible page can still lack scanned information: binding-side text and rightmost table columns may have been cropped during preparation and need human checking after import.

Zoom into small text. If it becomes blurred pixels, inspect scan resolution; if the original is clear but the reader is not, record page and orientation. Changing body type size does not affect text baked into images. A better source copy is the appropriate remedy when detail is missing.

## Distinguish image browsing from text search

Visible words in scans are not necessarily searchable text. Image pages do not automatically become a complete text ebook on the shelf, so no full-text match alone does not prove a broken index. For search or copying, check for a PDF with a text layer or an appropriately processed text version.

If you recognise text with an external tool, retain original images and check numbers, units and names. Recognition can assist finding material, but important quotations should still be compared with scans. Do not replace formal source material with unverified recognition or describe external processing as a required built-in Book Isle step.

## Record the relationship between page numbers

Use the navigation available in your installed reader. For durable references, record file sequence, printed page and a brief description in your notes. An unqualified page ten can be ambiguous after covers and introductions, especially when a rebuilt archive changes the sequence.

Read to the middle, leave and reopen to check a reasonable resume position. Adding a cover or removing blanks changes old locations. Retain the old file and records before importing and checking a revised archive. Identical names do not establish matching page counts, sequences or reading references.

## Finish with offline and preservation checks

Leave the reader, disconnect and jump to a later section you did not just load. Retain original scans, page map and final CBZ separately, noting your reading copy. Shelf or reading-data sync does not replace original image backups, particularly for scans you cannot easily recreate.

CBZ is convenient for image browsing; keep an appropriate PDF too when printed pagination, searchable text or exact layout matters. Choose by task rather than how much an extension resembles an ebook. Finally sample beginning, middle and end again in the actual saved version.
