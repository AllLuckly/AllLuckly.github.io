An unquoted complaint in fiction can remain close to a character’s wording rather than an author’s intervention. Free indirect discourse requires contextual judgement. Practise with an invented passage and retain voice candidates, using Book Isle for reading and location rather than automatic narrator detection, psychological diagnosis or intention scoring.

## Bound the passage and current viewpoint

Select a short passage with surrounding context and record book, edition, translator, chapter and position. Establish who perceives, acts and knows the immediate information. Work on two or three sentences without classifying the whole novel first. Save necessary wording through supported selection or manual page/opening-word locators for scans. Create five fields: source location, person/tense, evaluative wording, viewpoint cues and voice candidates. Viewpoint is evidence, not a guarantee that every word belongs exclusively to that character.

## Preserve the unquoted form in an invented example

Use this invented passage: Lin reached the locked door. Locked again today, of all days! She had to find the key immediately. The second sentence has neither quotation marks nor she said, so it does not establish an actual spoken action. Its complaint may reflect Lin within third-person narration, making free indirect presentation a candidate. These are not published-novel quotations. Preserve them before analysis rather than adding she shouted to eliminate ambiguity, which changes the evidence instead of finding it.

## Examine reference form and evaluation separately

Record third-person Lin/she separately from evaluative or immediate words such as of all days, today, again and immediately, considering whose situation they connect to. Third person is not automatically detached omniscience, and an exclamation mark does not prove audible speech. Record a translation’s actual tense and punctuation without claiming an original-language check. Combine cues: exclamation alone without viewpoint evidence cannot settle classification. Preserve both support and insufficiency instead of making one keyword a universal detector.

## Compare direct and ordinary indirect forms

In external copies, write an ordinary indirect version, Lin wondered why the door was locked again today, and direct thought, Lin thought, “Why was the door locked again today?” Label both as your rewrites. They explicitly attribute thought, unlike the original’s absent quotation and reporting frame. The comparison reveals what was not explicit; it does not make every unquoted sentence thought. Conversation context may support speech presentation, but without evidence of speech do not assume another person heard it. Name all forms separately rather than replacing the source with a rewrite.

## Do not attribute character-coloured wording to the real author

An impatient second sentence can support a reading close to Lin’s complaint without proving the real author dislikes locks or endorses her judgements. Narration and character perception can interweave; every word need not have one exclusive owner. For a narrator-evaluation alternative, specify needed contextual support rather than inventing a narrator’s history. Record biography or commentary separately with provenance instead of letting it override the passage unchecked. Attribute your interpretation and retain its provisional status.

## Append later evidence without rewriting earlier certainty

Read a little farther. If later wording denies that Lin thought this or establishes someone heard a complaint, append evidence and location before updating candidates. Later evidence may change judgement without making an earlier unknown something always known. Check one relevant recurrence rather than claiming automatic whole-book voice statistics; search hits still need context. Preserve competing readings and scopes when both remain possible. The note records reading, not an app accuracy score improving as pages turn.

## Check boundaries and translation when attributions clash

For an unquoted sentence labelled both narration and direct dialogue, inspect reporting verbs, paragraph boundaries and viewpoint rather than type size alone. Leave blurred punctuation unread; if conversion may have lost quotation marks, consult a trusted original before interpreting a display issue as technique. Identify editions when a translation makes implicit attribution explicit. Retain uncertainty rather than seeking an invented free-indirect-discourse button. Revise personal candidates, not source wording.

## Recheck the five evidence fields after returning

Leave the reader and return by chapter, phrase and location. Identify explicit third-person forms and character-coloured wording before explaining remaining candidates. Check that possible thought has not become confirmed speech or a character complaint author opinion. Reopen exported notes externally to verify five fields and source/rewrite identities, keeping the book separately. Completion traces attribution evidence and acknowledges limits rather than assigning every unquoted sentence one label or claiming access to a character’s actual mind.
