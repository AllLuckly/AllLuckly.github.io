Check a simplified rope knot’s weaving order, rounded form and contact shadows before fibres obscure its path.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-rope-knot-path/>), translated on 2026-11-03. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

The difficult part of a knot is often knowing where a rope segment comes from, what it covers and where it emerges. Draw a simplified knot by establishing its continuous path before a few twisted-fibre marks. This is visual-structure practice, not instructions for tying a load-bearing knot; a convincing drawn connection does not establish real-world safety.

## Trace the complete route with one line

Create an approximately 1600×1400 px canvas on pale beige-grey. Draw one curving centreline with two crossings and two visible ends. Trace it from end to end before thickness or fibres, marking over/under relationships lightly and saving the path. Do not assemble disconnected attractive loops: added width will magnify a route that cannot be followed.

## Build thickness around the centreline

Offset both sides of the centreline, tightening inner bends and opening outer bends, with slight compression at sharp turns. Separate path, rope body and texture. Check a solid mid-brown rope for continuity. Unequal end lengths are fine, but it remains one material. Repair sausage-like disconnected segments through joined contours rather than separate black outlines.

## Keep the upper contour at each crossing

Follow the saved over/under marks. Keep the upper rope continuous and interrupt the lower contours until they emerge. Hide transparent construction lines. Resolve one crossing before the next. If a segment seems to reverse impossibly, retrace its route rather than darkening the junction; continuity, not a central black spot, explains the crossing.

## Turn broad values around each segment

Under upper-left light, use pale ochre on lit surfaces and grey-brown as they turn downward or right. Different segment orientations share one light, not an identical pasted highlight. Compare straight and curved samples first. Restore broad middle-value turns to a flat ribbon-like rope instead of adding black lines to both edges.

## Put contact shadows on the covered segment

Place a narrow directional shadow on the covered rope, fading away from contact. Do not blacken both segments around the whole junction. Reserve deepest darks for a few contacts. Hide this layer to test the route itself, then restore it for compression. If the knot fails without shadows, repair contour interruptions and origins first.

## Turn twisted marks with the local rope axis

Add a few diagonal twist marks around a near segment’s local axis, changing and compressing them through bends. Reduce detail in shadow and occlusion. Compare three to five marks before adding more. Soften zebra-like contrast or unify grass-like random directions. Far segments may remain untextured when contour and selected marks already identify the material.

## Detail only one cut end

Use a small end face and two or three short fibres on one end, simplifying the other. Fibres emerge from the cut rather than float nearby. Shade the end consistently, avoiding a glowing dot. Shorten brush-like fringe or soften a metal-cap edge. Establish the rope’s endpoint before texture and keep fraying subordinate.

## Judge crowding through the gaps

Keep at least one clear opening and a narrower gap between segments. Remove fibres that clog the gaps; do not loosen the whole knot and alter its route merely to open them. Gaps need not be regular ellipses. Review negative spaces normally, silhouette small and stray marks close up; they often reveal accidental connections better than retracing outlines.

## Repair one crossing at a time

In a saved copy, temporarily colour the suspect segment conspicuously and trace it between its neighbouring bends before correcting over/under order. Retain other references. Restore brown afterward and update shadows and texture breaks too. Remove diagnostic colour from the finish. Separating route, surface and texture prevents dense marks from concealing impossible structure.

## Keep the path and change the view

Keep centreline, untextured-rope and finished PNG versions with editable crossings. Next, rebuild a side view of the same knot, reconsidering visible gaps and covered lengths while holding palette and light fixed. Rotating the flat picture is not a new view. Record the segment most easily misread and reiterate that this is an illustration, not load-bearing knot validation.
