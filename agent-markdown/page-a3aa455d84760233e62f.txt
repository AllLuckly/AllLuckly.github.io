## Separate hertz differences, ratios and intervals

Adjacent frequencies all increase by220 Hz, but adjacent ratios are2,3/2 and4/3. An octave uses a2:1 frequency ratio, so only the first adjacent pair is exactly an octave. Retain integer fractions and their source pair before decimals. Hertz counts cycles per second, not beats per minute, and three-second duration is not frequency. Increasing frequency labels do not imply increasing level; production gain and frequency are distinct conditions.

## Calculate cumulative octaves from one reference

With n=log2(f/220), the cumulative octave counts are 0, 1, about 1.584963, and 2. The ratio 660/220=3 does not mean three octaves: three octaves require an eightfold ratio. Moving 440→880 spans one octave, while 220→880 spans two, showing why the reference matters. Adjacent gaps are approximately 1, 0.584963, and 0.415037 octaves, summing to 2. Six displayed decimals are approximate; retain exact ratios and logarithmic definitions rather than manufacture discrepancies from rounding.

```text
common reference f0=220 Hz
f (Hz):              220        440        660          880
f/f0:                  1          2          3            4
log2(f/f0):            0          1          1.584963     2
adjacent ratios:             2         3/2          4/3
adjacent octave gaps:        1         0.584963     0.415037
doubling control: 220 -> 440 -> 880; one octave per step
440 -> 880 is ONE octave; 220 -> 880 is TWO octaves
```
