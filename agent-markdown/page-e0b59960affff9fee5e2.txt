Vertical-strip comics build rhythm through downward movement, unlike page-turning volumes. ManYue's continuous vertical mode suits this structure. Use a chapter composed of several tall images to check joins, width, text size, and the restored position after leaving.

## Confirm the original is a vertical strip first

Open the work and see whether images are long and narrow and designed to connect downward. Ordinary comics can also be split into many images, so file count alone does not identify vertical strips. Note dialogue or background spanning two images as a later reference for joins and order.

Start with a chapter that already loads correctly instead of troubleshooting network and layout together. For local files, check image numbering; for an online source, inspect the first few images in default mode. Reading settings improve presentation but cannot repair missing or misordered source images automatically.

## Choose continuous top-to-bottom reading

Open reader settings and choose Continuous (Top to Bottom), or the equivalent label, under Reading Mode. It differs from top-to-bottom paged mode: continuous mode extends pages along one scrolling direction, while paged mode primarily advances by page or screen. Return to the same chapter and inspect the effect instead of judging by the option name.

If automatic mode selection is enabled, confirm that a manual change actually changes the display. Different works may have individual preferences. If single-book and global scopes are offered, choose before editing, or a vertical-strip adjustment may also affect ordinary page-based works.

## Check continuity at image joins

Scroll slowly to the recorded join between two images, checking whether background lines, dialogue, and whitespace connect sensibly. For a large blank area, inspect the original's whitespace before reader spacing or display settings. If the sequence makes no sense, check source files or the chapter's page list first.

Do not arbitrarily crop image edges merely to hide joins; the creator may use whitespace for pauses. Distances between images are part of a strip's rhythm, and continuous does not mean no whitespace. Judge correct order, complete text, and natural scrolling rather than forcing a whole chapter into one gapless image.

## Keep text readable while adjusting width

On a landscape iPad, strips may be too wide, making one dialogue bubble occupy a huge area. If side margins or image-width limits are available, add a little margin and reread the same passage. Around ten percent is a possible starting point within the interface's range, not a universal value for every device.

Excessive margins can make text too small and require constant zooming. Read a passage at your normal holding distance and check whether you can continue downward without enlarging every bubble. Phone portrait often fits strip proportions better, while tablet orientation can follow your posture. Filling more screen space does not always mean more comfortable reading.

## Handle occasional horizontal illustrations separately

Some vertical chapters include a wide illustration. View it whole first, then decide whether to zoom. If advanced horizontal-image splitting is available, check entitlements and results before using it, including left-right order. Do not change the whole chapter's reading direction for one unusual image and make later strips harder to read.

Splitting a horizontal image adds no detail and does not suit every composition. A complete spread remains valuable when the center contains important content. Adjust the view temporarily for that page, then return to continuous vertical reading to preserve the other sections' rhythm.

## Check reading position and chapter boundaries

Scroll to distinctive dialogue, exit normally, and reopen to compare nearby content. Page numbering and screen position differ in continuous mode: one long image may span several screens, so page eight alone does not prove the same vertical position was restored. Record chapter and image details for important locations.

At chapter end, check how your version enters the next chapter and use the actual button or navigation. Continuous vertical mode primarily handles scrolling within the current chapter; moving to another depends on available controls. Change chapters manually once and confirm continuity. For offline reading, confirm the next chapter downloaded separately; reaching this chapter's end does not prove later content is local.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are there still gaps in continuous mode?** Gaps may come from whitespace in the original, different image dimensions, or display settings. Compare the source page first, then available options individually. Do not immediately assume loading failed or damage the original to remove whitespace.

**Should I zoom or reduce margins when vertical-strip text is too small?** First reduce excessive side margins to give the image a reasonable width, then zoom if needed. If the source has low resolution, enlargement only reveals blur more clearly; obtain a clearer authorized source.

**Can I read every ordinary page-based comic as a vertical strip?** You can try according to preference, but the original's left-right panels and spreads may suit paged mode better. Judge using two or three pages of actual dialogue rather than treating one mode as best for every work.
