Check phrase boundaries, line breaks and dwell time on a self-made explanation clip.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/icouldplay-caption-phrase-chunking/>), translated on 2026-11-04. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Visible, approximately synchronized captions can still be hard to read: half a sentence remains in one cue while its qualification appears in another, or the cue disappears before reading finishes. On a caption copy you may edit, use an ordinary explanation to organize each cue’s meaning. View it in iCouldPlay and edit with an existing caption or text tool, without assuming an in-player segmentation editor or a universal character count or reading speed.

## Choose a short passage with conditions and actions

Choose roughly thirty seconds of self-made material with simple background sound, containing a condition, action and result. Understand it before transcribing accurately, without copying someone else’s captions. Preserve originals and name a separate segmentation test. Record language, duration and the matching video so an older caption is not accidentally loaded. Include a phrase whose parts belong together, such as saving a sample before closing a page, to expose the cost of separating prerequisite and action.

## Divide by meaning before screen width

Mark complete ideas in the text tool, keeping negatives, quantities and conditions attached to the actions they qualify. Equal-looking line lengths do not justify separating “do not” from “close the window.” A natural pause may help, but a breath is not necessarily an idea boundary. Review text order before fitting screen width. If the next cue needs half a word or phrase remembered from the previous cue, reconsider segmentation rather than merely reducing type size.

## Treat a line break within a cue separately

Two lines within a cue still share one time interval. Break at understandable phrase boundaries rather than splitting names, numbers from units or fixed expressions. Smooth reading matters more than symmetrical line lengths. Check at the intended viewing size after controls hide. If text reaches the edge, reconsider the cue’s content before deciding to split it into sequential cues. Avoid changing font, position, timing and wording together, which makes comparison difficult.

## Check dwell time during normal playback

Load the test in iCouldPlay and watch normally from the preceding sentence, not only paused word by word. Note unreadable dwell times, premature next-step disclosure and captions that arrive after an action. Adjust timing in the external copy and reload it. Sentence length alone cannot establish a universal number of seconds; terminology, language and picture complexity matter. Read the key visual action too: a cue that consumes all attention while the demonstration is missed still needs reconsideration.

## Check cue order and unintended overlap

Check cue starts and ends in order, watching for copied times that create simultaneous cues or omitted words. Overlap can be intentional, but use a straightforward sequence for this simple explanation rather than treating accidental overlap as advanced layout. [W3C WebVTT](<https://www.w3.org/TR/webvtt1/>) illustrates time intervals; verify the actual syntax of your chosen format and tool. Renaming an extension does not convert incompatible caption syntax into a readable format.

## Compare versions with one small change

Choose the hardest cue and change only its line or cue boundary, retaining other captions and media. Compare old and new versions for both reading and understanding the action rather than appearance alone. Label copies clearly instead of overwriting the original. Revert an unhelpful change before trying another boundary, avoiding a global replacement. The comparison informs this content, not an automatic rule for fast dialogue, poetry or bilingual captions.

## Separate reading load from rendering failure

Missing characters or boxes suggest a character or font issue rather than segmentation. A consistent timing error belongs in timing diagnosis; fragmented meaning belongs here. Confirm the selected subtitle, since two active versions can also produce overlap. Separating these problems avoids rewriting every timestamp for one missing glyph. Keep the original wording, position and a screenshot description for uncertain cases so they can be revisited under matching conditions.

## Keep versions and reading observations

Keep media identity, caption copy, changed cue numbers, rationale and unchecked passages. Describe improved reading of this explanation at the tested size rather than automatic optimization by the app. Preserve a few concrete examples and understand the next text before reusing them. Share only permitted material, with matching edition checks before someone reuses timings. Editing should serve content and viewers rather than cutting every cue to an equal length while leaving fragmented meaning.
