A peeled banana is not white flesh surrounded by yellow petals. Peel flaps extend from the still-enclosing part and expose different surfaces as they fold downward. Use an invented still life with three visible flaps, not a claim that every banana peels into three. Establish attachment, surface reversal and droop manually in Aquarell, then markings. This is drawing, not peeling instruction or an assessment of ripeness, quality or edibility.

## Establish the fruit curve and enclosed lower portion

Use an approximately 1500×1900 px portrait canvas with pale grey-violet background. Curve the fruit gently upper left and retain a short yellow enclosing lower portion. Establish axis and contours with warm cream-white flesh, not a straight cylinder or sharp cone. Separate sketch, far peel, fruit, near peel and ground shadow. Let the fruit occupy somewhat over half the image height, leaving room for drooping flaps; this is compositional. Lengthen coherent fruit and correct its curve if flower-like instead of adding flaps. Vary width modestly if hose-like, omitting brown spots and fibres initially.

## Return three flaps to the enclosing peel

Label flaps left, right and rear in the sketch. Each folds outward and down from the enclosing lower peel, not from midway up exposed flesh. Use three broad-strip centrelines and contours, checking stray edges for an accidental fourth flap. The rear one may hide, with its root retained only in the diagnostic sketch. Reconnect detached petals by tracing each to the enclosing portion. Broaden finger-like forms and establish thin-sheet turning. Root continuity matters more than decorative tips; brown spots or a black ring cannot conceal incorrect attachment.

## Separate inner peel from yellow exterior at turns

Use muted golden yellow outside and grey cream-white inside. Solve one clear left-flap reversal: inner face toward the viewer near its root, with exterior appearing as it bends down. Orient other faces individually rather than painting random white halves on yellow strips. Explain two sides of one peel through turning boundary and slight thickness, not a split. Reconnect colour boundaries to turns if striped-cloth-like; reduce edge thickness if flesh-like. Light can alter colours, but differing positions need not expose identical faces or copied highlights.

## Give drooping curves width and endpoints

Place the left flap lower, right slightly higher and rear partly exposed, creating distinct gaps. Let each narrow and widen with turning rather than stay equal-width to its end. Taper tips gently, not into spikes, and keep them inside the frame. Check silhouettes for droop rather than upright leaves or shadow-cut ends. Reduce excessive loops if curl-like; add restrained edge/value turning if paper-flat. These are static pose choices, not measurements of elasticity, and equal flap length is unnecessary.

## Model flesh without dense longitudinal stripes

Light upper left with broad warm-white flesh and grey-cream lower-right turning, using only two or three quiet longitudinal ridge cues. Avoid black fibre grooves and plastic reflections. Hide texture to test curved volume, then restore sparse soft lines. Correct tip and width variation if candle-like rather than adding a food label; restore broad light if crumpled-paper-like. Narrow contact dark near peel is enough, not a black base ring. This palette does not establish real ripeness, spoilage or edibility.

## Place markings on surfaces away from key joins

Add two or three quiet irregular brown exterior marks, compressed with surface turns rather than equal dots. Keep inner fibre cues lighter and do not apply identical patterns to both sides. Leave key roots free of heavy spots initially. Reduce markings if leopard-cloth-like and manually repair marks crossing silhouettes. Hide markings and recheck three attachments; decoration cannot change flap count. Keep a clean unmarked version to distinguish recognition through form from reliance on a familiar yellow symbol.

## Check lower support separately from flap tips

Place the enclosed lower portion near the table, with one flap tip lightly touching and two slightly raised if desired. Tighten contacts and soften raised shadows, generally lower right, without identical black pads under all tips. Hide shadow to count left/right/rear flaps, then restore and reject false shadow flaps. Reconnect the lowest root to the table if floating or restore rising fruit volume if flower-flat. Adjust one endpoint or contact shadow at a time, holding light and overall tilt fixed to diagnose the apparent float.

## Check three pale surfaces at output size

Save the layered original and distinguish pale flesh, inner peel and background in a PNG at use size. Separate local temperature or value if they merge rather than outlining every pale surface black. Repair a broken-looking root at the connection layer, not with spots. Keep numbered flaps and record faults such as a rear root mistakenly joining the right flap midway. A two- or four-visible-flap variant needs revised sketch counts and wording; three is not fixed plant anatomy. Judge attachment, reversal and droop, not real food or material condition.
