## Choose the reference object

Handles and supports differ across strainers, so follow the selected object rather than treating one product’s construction as universal. ## Arrange canvas and long axis

Create an approximately 1800 by 1400 pixel landscape canvas in the current Aquarell interface and save an editable original. Draw a light axis from the basket center toward the handle, leaving space for the handle end and the support loop on the other side. Separate sketch, table, strainer and shadow using available layers; choose identifiable equivalents if labels differ. Decide whether the strainer rests on a table or cup; this example uses a tabletop still life.

## Draw the basket opening and rim

Start with an ellipse for the oblique opening and compare its major axis, minor axis and tilt. An opening generally looks rounder from above and flatter near a side view. Draw a nearby outer ellipse for the wide rim, keeping both around the same opening rather than giving them unrelated centers. At small size check the tilt against the handle connection. Viewing angles differ, so the illustrative ellipse is not a dimensional measurement.

## Build basket depth from the rim

Curve down from both sides of the rim ellipse and join the sides with a soft lower curve for a shallow basket beneath it. Use the photograph for depth and curvature. The far side may be hidden by the wide rim; draw visible portions first instead of making the basket a transparent complete sphere. Before adding mesh, check that bottom and opening create volume and the rim remains continuous. If it reads as flat, adjust contour and value rather than adding more mesh dots.

## Connect the double-rod handle

Find the two handle attachment points on one side of the rim. Draw two thin rods outward along the chosen axis, then add the grip according to the reference. Rods should connect at the visible rim attachments, not emerge through the basket bottom; perspective may overlap the far rod. If the photograph clearly shows only one rod, keep to that observation rather than forcing another for the text. Check their direction and endpoints so they do not read as two floating lines.

## Draw the opposite support loop

The cited product has a support loop at the end opposite its handle. Where visible in the photograph, draw a small curved loop and connect it back to the rim. Follow the selected reference for shape, direction and size; if hidden or absent on another model, mark it not visible or not applicable rather than inventing it. A small darker attachment or overlap can separate the loop from the tabletop and prevent it reading as a detached decoration.

## Suggest mesh with perspective

Block the visible basket in light gray, then draw a few fine lines curving with its surface. A second direction crosses them to suggest a sparse mesh. Near the bottom, direction follows the curved surface; individual real holes need not all be drawn. Stop hidden far-side lines at the rim. If the mesh becomes a dark mass at reduced size, lighten or reduce lines so rim and basket remain readable. Invented line density is not a product specification.

## Arrange metal reflections and table

Use neutral gray and a few light highlights to distinguish metal rim, rods and grip. Highlights should follow rings and rods rather than cut across the whole object. Use a muted tabletop with local contact shadows. If the support loop raises one side, retain the corresponding gap in the reference instead of forcing every part flat against the table. If the strainer floats, inspect basket-bottom and handle contact with the shadow first.

## Compare opening angle and attachments

Make two thumbnails, one with a rounder opening ellipse and one flatter, keeping handle direction and approximate length. Choose the one closer to the photograph’s viewpoint, then check that rods, basket and support loop connect to the same rim. This comparison explores perspective, not a change in physical size. Hide mesh and shadow to confirm the strainer silhouette reads before restoring restrained detail.

Compare before-and-after thumbnails at the same display size, noting one change to the opening ellipse, basket bottom and attachments. Enlarging mesh alone does not prove basket perspective improved. If changing photographs, record the new viewpoint too.

If mesh or connections are missing, check visibility, crop and location before overwriting the only original. Keep product and photographic sources, marking device, export and reopening steps pending if unperformed.

## Compare mesh lines at different display sizes

In a separate study draw two identical basket details: use thinner lighter lines in one and thicker darker lines in the other, keeping spacing the same. Inspect first near the intended display size, then enlarged, noting whether lines merge or fine lines disappear. Choose the treatment that suits the composition after recording both sizes. The display difference concerns this visual exercise and does not establish the product’s actual mesh specification.
