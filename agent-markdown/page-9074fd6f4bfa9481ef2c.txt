For one verified track with available timed lyrics, use one clock for three audible onsets and lyric changes, distinguishing direction, trends and observation error.

## 1. Start with an already working timed-lyric source

Choose an authorised playable edition whose source already supplies available timed lyrics. Verify title, edition, duration and source, including basic text presence and playback-linked changes. This task studies three timing correspondences. Plain text or missing lyrics does not qualify; prepare a suitable source rather than pretend a paper table generates timed lyrics in 调调. Live/studio editions need separate identities despite shared words. Matching covers do not establish identical edits, introductions or timelines. Preserve audio/data before judging timing.

## 2. Choose three clear onsets and corresponding lines

Choose early, middle and late passages A/B/C with clear audible onsets and recognizable matching lines. Avoid ambiguous sustained introductions or unclear line-change behavior. Define whether the anchor is phrase onset or a particular syllable and keep that rule consistent. Define the actual visible change/emphasis offered by this theme rather than assume one universal highlight color. Retain brief owned words or position descriptions for return, without copying an entire protected lyric into the report.

## 3. Put both events on one time reference

Record audio onset a and the defined visible line change v, with difference v−a. Use one identifiable timing reference, not player elapsed seconds minus an unsynchronized wall-clock hour. If the lyric page hides readable timing, approximate both events in one continuous run from a known start using an external timer, stating reaction/whole-second precision. Pausing, seeking or changing tracks breaks that reference and needs a new round. Do not combine separate hearings into an apparently millisecond-precise pair. Convert remaining time to elapsed before subtraction. On a ninety-second track, remaining 80 at audio and 78 at the visual event means elapsed 10/12, hence+2. Subtracting 78−80 directly reverses the interpretation. Record reference type in the heading.

## 4. Define the sign before recording observations

Use a labelled paper example: audio at 10/30/70 seconds and visual changes at 12/32/72 give+2 each, meaning the visual event is later. Visual 8/28/68 gives−2, meaning earlier. Equal differences at three points support an approximate shared-offset candidate for that sample, not every line of the track. Keep actual-result rows separate and fill your own times. These numbers were not measured on a device and are not instructions to edit source timestamps.

## 5. Collect three rows during continuous playback

Play from the verified beginning with source, output, theme and event definitions fixed, collecting A/B/C pairs. Repeated seeking to make a line align is not continuous synchronization evidence. Mark a missed event unavailable rather than fill+2 from memory. If a line appears before its emphasis changes, state which event defines v and do not mix both. End the round before pausing to organize notes, retain captured rows and label a new run instead of overwriting earlier observations.

## 6. Repeat and inspect variation across passages

Repeat all three under the same conditions and compare differences with observation precision. A paper sequence+2,+3,+5 is not one constant number, yet three points cannot diagnose device-clock drift: source timing, edition edits and observer error remain relevant. Whole-second readings can place a one-second discrepancy within rounding/reaction uncertainty. Retain a possible trend rather than average to 3.3 and claim that fixes every line. Published functionality does not promise a lyric-offset editor. If each event error is known to be at most one second, difference error can reach two, not automatically one. This bound applies only when its premises are known. Unmeasured reaction error remains unknown; extra displayed decimals do not establish more accurate observation.

## 7. Diagnose edition, source and event definition

Opposite signs across runs call for checking v−a versus a−v, appearance versus emphasis, and timing origin. Divergence later can require edition/edit inspection rather than a blanket network-delay explanation. A missing line is missing data, not an infinite timing difference. A changed server/file needs a new identity card. Do not infer lyric APIs, caching, protocols or an unpublished settings page. Check one condition at a time while retaining original tables and source material. For+2,+3,+5, the mean is 10/3, about 3.33. Subtracting one mean leaves different residuals, so averaging cannot prove variation was removed. Preserve all three differences. Evidence should first establish whether a common shift or passage-dependent differences are the relevant question.

## 8. Deliver three anchors with credible precision

Re-enter the same source/edition and repeat one observation, then retain anchor definitions, both runs’ raw times, sign rule and uncertainty. Explain+2 versus−2, why three equal samples do not calibrate the entire track and what remains unknown with missing data. This records current correspondence without altering audio/lyrics or certifying every device, theme or source. A later file/app revision needs an additional record rather than edited historical data that conceals change.
