Prepare a partial world-grid sketch with latitude vertically labelled, longitude horizontally labelled and north at top, without claiming an equidistant projection. ## Locate latitude and longitude separately

Latitude gives north/south angular position relative to the equator; longitude gives east/west angular position relative to the prime meridian. Follow their own lines: meridians connect the poles, while latitude parallels circle the globe east–west in this conventional depiction. Straight sketch lines are a representation. Write thirty north and thirty east with coordinate type, direction and degrees, not just thirty. Use the legend rather than assume that every number on the upper border is latitude.

## State this exercise’s latitude-first order

Here ordered pairs are latitude first: A is thirty degrees north, sixty east; B is sixty north, thirty east. The same numbers in reversed roles give different points. Other data formats may use longitude first, so inspect their declared order rather than universalise this example. Label A and B with full coordinate wording, retaining the convention instead of an unexplained parenthesised pair.

## Retain signs and hemisphere letters

For C, thirty south and sixty west, locate hemispheres first and then apply this example’s north/east-positive sign convention. Do not treat a west label plus a negative value as an instruction to reverse direction twice. Zero latitude identifies the equator; zero longitude the prime meridian. Identical numbers do not make identical lines. This is not a date-line route calculation, and a zero-to-three-hundred-sixty convention needs reconciliation before comparison.

## Do not turn equal angles into equal distances

NOAA explains that meridians are farther apart at the equator and converge toward the poles, so equal longitude differences do not give equal ground distances everywhere. Equal-looking map cells also depend on projection. Thirty degrees is not thirty kilometres, and a degree of latitude is not universally identical to a degree of longitude. Without an appropriate distance definition, model and parameters, retain the known angular difference rather than invent an exact kilometre conversion.
