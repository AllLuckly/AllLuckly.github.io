Use a permitted reference to study red plumage, the crest and thick bill in a perched side view.

## Identify the subject

Choose a permitted photograph of an adult Northern Cardinal and record source, viewpoint and season. Cornell describes males as mostly bright red with a dark face around the bill, while females are mostly pale brown with red accents. This exercise shows a male in profile; do not combine the two plumages. Crest height changes with posture, so follow the visible silhouette rather than inferring sex or health from a drawing.

## Set up an editable canvas

Create an approximately 1800 by 1400 landscape canvas in Aquarell and save an editable original; the dimensions are an exercise choice. Use available layer controls to separate background, branch, bird color and sketch, keeping the sketch visible initially. Draw a slightly inclined branch axis and place the bird near the middle with space around the tail. If labels differ, locate the layer and save controls in the installed version.

## Compare body and tail proportions

Block in the oval body lightly and mark the head, bill tip, tail base and tail tip. Compare total length from bill to tail with body height, then compare the tail angle to the branch; do not impose a universal ratio. Connect the head to the neck instead of placing a circle on top. Inspect the silhouette at thumbnail size. If the tail is cropped, move the composition before shortening the tail.

## Draw crest and stout bill

Follow the reference silhouette for the pointed crest, separating its rearward tip from the back of the neck; it need not become a row of identical spikes. Block the short thick bill as one shape, then add the division and connection to the face. Compare the eye with the bill base and crest front so it lies within the face rather than on the outline. Omit tiny grooves that cannot be seen clearly.

## Place toes and branch overlaps

Observe whether both feet are visible and draw only supported portions. Connect legs to the body, then add visible toe segments around the branch. Front toes cover the branch and the branch hides rear segments; stop lines at each overlap. Do not put all toes in one plane or invent joints hidden by feathers. Hide the color layer to inspect the grip silhouette, then restore it and inspect contact points.

## Separate red plumage and mask

Lay a mid-value red body on the bird color layer as one readable shape. Use the photograph to place the black mask around the bill and near the eye, following the feather boundary rather than filling the whole head black. Give the bill a red-orange color with enough value contrast against the plumage. Mark the dark eye and add a tiny highlight only if useful. At thumbnail size the bill, mask and head should remain distinguishable; adjust value first if they merge.

## Suggest feathers with limited values

Choose one light direction and divide wing, shaded belly and tail into a few broad value areas, keeping the red relationship of head and body. A few directional lines can suggest overlapping wing feathers; avoid drawing a grid across the wing. Use two red values within the crest while preserving its silhouette. If detail obscures the large form, hide the detail layer temporarily and compare the two states.

## Support the silhouette with background

Use muted green or gray-brown behind the subject, avoiding large background areas with the same value as the red bird. Keep the branch edge slightly clearer than the background. Place the background behind bird and branch and check that crest and bill tips do not merge with bright background marks. Instead of making the whole background black, adjust local values. At phone size the bird’s direction and branch should remain readable.

## Troubleshoot shape and color

If the bird reads as a red ball, revisit head-neck transition, wing shape and tail base before adding feather marks. If the face resembles a black hood, compare the reference and confine the mask to the bill and eye region. If the foot appears to float, inspect leg, toe and branch overlaps and ensure at least one visible toe crosses the branch. For muddy colors, inspect opacity and blend mode on a copy before changing the only original.

## Save, export and review

Save the editable artwork first, then use the available share or export control to save a separate PNG or JPEG without replacing the original. Reopen the image and inspect crest, bill, toes, branch and tail tip for cropping; return to the original and confirm layers remain editable. Record the reference, canvas dimensions and one overlap to improve. If output misses an element, inspect visibility, canvas boundaries and save location. Mark steps not actually performed as pending.

## Check silhouette and mirrored view

Duplicate the working image and temporarily simplify bird, branch and background into three different values. Check whether crest, stout bill, tail and grip remain recognizable. Restore color, then flip the view horizontally or inspect a mirrored temporary export in an available image viewer if the installed app has no flip-view control. This reveals proportion problems without changing the reference direction. If the bill or feet are misplaced, revise their connections on the sketch, then recheck overlaps and color so the old contour does not remain behind.
