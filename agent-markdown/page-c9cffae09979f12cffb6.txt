Paint a misty forest with three tree groups and decreasing value contrast instead of covering everything with uniform white.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-fog-pine-forest/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Depth in mist comes from differences between distances, not fading every tree equally. Paint far trees close to the background, middle trees with clearer silhouettes and near trees with the strongest darks. Hand-painted overlap and contrast create the mist without automatic weather or depth effects.

## Set up a separate study

Use an approximately 1400×1600 px portrait canvas with room for tall pines. Separate sky, far, middle and near trees and mist. Test broad mist strokes and reserve fine marks for near branches. Compare three grey-blue values, including a far-tree colour subtly distinct from the sky.

Sketch group heights and the bright passage without every trunk. Check that the passage connects and the near dark area is restrained. Save broad masses before branches, keeping mist and tree groups editable so a distant correction does not become another white veil over the whole scene.

## Plan the areas of distance, middle and foreground

Use pale grey-blue with sky covering roughly half the frame. Keep distant trees low and continuous, middle trees in two or three unequal groups and only a few tall near trees on one side. Leave mist-coloured gaps so the layers do not become stacked horizontal stage curtains.

Test three grey values in a thumbnail, keeping the darkest area restrained. Vary where foreground trees enter the frame. Leave a brighter, irregular passage through the groups to guide the eye; it need not become a straight road, just connected gaps with changing widths.

## Simplify distant trees with background-like colours

Use grey-blue only slightly darker than the sky for uneven pointed distant crowns. Vary height without individual branch tiers, and omit trunks if unnecessary. If the distance dominates, reduce value contrast and merge sharp peaks rather than preserving every crisp edge under a faded layer.

Let the bases merge into mist by brushing a near-background colour on a separate layer with uneven edges. Avoid a straight white strip cutting every trunk at the same height. Concentrate thicker mist in two areas while retaining faint tree shapes elsewhere, keeping the original tree layer intact.

## Give the middle distance clear but restrained contours

Use a darker grey-green. Build each middle tree around a slim axis with short downward branch masses, narrow above and fuller below. Overlap uneven crowns with small trunk gaps. Design the group silhouette first, then clarify branch direction on only a couple of trees.

Keep middle shadows lighter than foreground darks. Allow mist to cross selected bases. If the trees resemble paper cutouts, interrupt some edges with background colour while retaining clearer edges elsewhere. Edge variation and reduced contrast work together without highlights on every tree.

## Concentrate foreground detail on one side

Use deeper grey-green or blue-grey for near trees, with coherent trunks and unequal branches. Add a few bark marks on the nearest tree, leaving others mostly silhouetted. Preserve small sky gaps so the dark mass can breathe. Choose detail according to viewing size, not maximum zoom.

Reserve the deepest dark for selected near areas instead of pure black throughout. Connect trunks to the ground with broader darks that soften outward. Some near branch tips may still disappear in mist, varying the distance rule so the forest does not become a mechanical three-value diagram.

## Check the mist with three layer comparisons

Hide the near trees to check the far and middle distances, view the foreground alone for stable forms, then restore everything to check the bright passage. Adjust a problematic group against its neighbours instead of adding another white veil over the whole image. Change one distance at a time.

If the forest resembles transparent stickers, vary aligned bases and straight mist boundaries. Bring part of the middle group forward, obscure part of the foreground and connect the ground with broad colour. For a cooler version, preserve value relationships first so colour changes can be assessed separately from depth.

## Review and plan the next study

Hide each distance group in turn to check its rhythm and the combined reduction in contrast. Keep detailed branch groups mainly near. Do not add equal distant detail simply because you can zoom in. Distinguish a deliberate bright mist passage from accidental unpainted holes.

Export a far-scene preview without near trees and the complete PNG, retaining separate native distance groups. Next, move only the thickest mist while keeping trees and palette fixed. Compare the changed passage and visible range, focusing on occlusion and space rather than treating recolouring alone as atmosphere.
