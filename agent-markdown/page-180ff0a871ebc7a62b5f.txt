A complete mailbox front can still look pasted onto a box if its side fails to connect with the arched roof. Draw a blue decorative arched mailbox with a closed door, visible slot and short supporting post. Rather than reproducing postal branding or equipment specifications, check front/rear form, openings and support. Hand-drawn layers separate seams from large colour shapes without assuming automatic architectural components.

## Start with the front section and receding depth

Use an approximately 1700×1600 px canvas with a pale warm-grey background. Draw the full front section first: an arched upper half above straight lower sides. Extend the depth backward in one consistent receding direction, exposing more of the right side. Keep the rear arch consistent instead of turning it into a pointed roof. Separate sketch, body, door, slot and post. Inspect continuous roof and side surfaces in two or three greys. If front and rear resemble separate little houses, correct shared turns and connecting edges rather than covering the gap with a heavy black line. Show enough depth without squeezing the front door into a sliver.

## Join the curved roof continuously to side walls

Turn the blue roof into a darker side wall under upper-left lighting, retaining a broad transition. Establish curved volume with large shapes rather than filling the roof with parallel fine lines. Join the side-wall bottom continuously to the box base without an unexplained upward kick on one side. Hide door and slot marks to inspect a complete container. If roof-like and pointed, weaken a hard central ridge. If bread-soft, restore straight lower sides and bottom-plane turns. Slight rounding is fine, but the front frame must remain traceable; blur cannot replace a missing front-to-rear join.

## Give the closed door a seam and thickness

Inset a narrow door seam following the front arch and straight sides. Keep the door within the body silhouette for this simple fitted design rather than implying an unspecified projecting rim. Mark the seam in low-contrast dark blue, perhaps exposing slight thickness along a lower near edge instead of an equally wide black frame. Hide handle or label and check that the door remains distinct. If it resembles a blue sticker, add narrow thickness and edge contact. If it seems open yet filled into the box, inspect whether one boundary accidentally became a separate side plane. Maintain a closed state rather than introducing half-open angles without adjusting overlap.

## Make the slot a narrow opening, not a black label

Place a long narrow slot in the upper door, aligning its ends with the door plane. Let the upper lip cover part of the dark interior and retain a narrow lower edge. Keep darkness inside rather than making a large black sign. Check without lettering so thickness and inside/outside relationships explain the opening. If it resembles tape, add a little inner grey and correct lip overlap. If a wide mouth, reduce height while preserving its elongated proportion. Keep it inside the door and below the whole arched top. This invented exterior specifies neither real mail dimensions nor how an actual facility is used.

## Align the handle and label with the door

Add either a small handle or blank label below the slot, choosing one first rather than crowding the door with badges, numbers and decoration. Align its horizontal edges with the local door plane instead of attaching a large canvas-facing rectangle to a turned box. If adding letters, use an invented short word and leave room for seams and opening. Hide small additions to confirm the main structure, then restore them and check dominance. Attach handles to the door rather than extending an unsupported line from inside the slot. If an addition resembles a third opening at small size, weaken its dark mass or clarify thickness instead of making every dark bar equally ambiguous.

## Connect the post visibly to the box bottom

Place a short post beneath a sensible point near the box’s centre, in darker blue-grey or wood brown. Establish its width and a simple supporting surface. Let the box bottom hide part of the post top rather than having the post pierce the middle side wall. Add a short ground contact shadow and a lower-right box shadow consistent with the light. If the box floats beside its post, move the attachment instead of using a long black shadow as a substitute bracket. Hide background decorations and confirm the connection. These support proportions are illustration choices, not load guarantees; show where the box rests and what overlaps the post top.

## Inspect three structural levels without detail

In a copy, inspect body alone, then door, then slot and post, ensuring each addition preserves the earlier large form. Use roof/side for body orientation, seam for door state, lips for opening and post-top overlap for support. If black lines distinguish everything, separate planes with broad values and reduce duplicated contours. A deliberately canvas-facing rectangular slot in another sketch can reveal its mismatch with a turned door; restore the original slot to the door plane. Do not add opening action, flying letters and dramatic movement merely to enrich the story and lose this connection exercise’s scope.

## Inspect slot, seam and side wall after export

Save the layered original and inspect the slot for a black blob and the seam for a doubled frame against the side-wall boundary in the PNG at use size. If dark blues merge, modestly separate door and body values rather than whitening every seam. If the post top disappears and the box floats, improve the local contact edge instead of enlarging the whole shadow. Keep a label-free preview and recheck door direction even in a later colour-only version. Record a precise failed join, such as the rear arch missing the right wall’s top edge, rather than covering it with more stamps or lettering.
