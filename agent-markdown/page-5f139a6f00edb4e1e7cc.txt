Light spreads through a paper lantern while its frame still affects shape and values. Draw a fictional hanging lantern, establishing openings, rounded body and suspension before ribs and warmth. This is not lamp-construction or fire-use guidance; hand-painted values create light without requiring an automatic glow filter.

## Align openings and suspension

Use an approximately 1400×1800 px portrait canvas. Align small top opening, broad belly and narrower lower opening on one axis with matching ellipses. Attach suspension to the top structure. Separate body, ribs and light. Correct a crooked-gourd silhouette before warmth or rings obscure faulty proportions.

## Build an expanding and narrowing paper body

Expand from the top into the belly and narrow toward the bottom, allowing slight asymmetry. Avoid both a uniform cylinder and an obligatory perfect sphere. Soften selected lit edges. Add opening structure and a few paper turns to a balloon-like result, or reduce wall weight in a heavy ceramic-pot appearance.

## Compress the ribs around the rounded body

Place five to seven curved ribs, wider centrally and compressed near openings. Keep selected warm-brown segments faint and narrower toward sides. Restore continuous paper when it resembles stacked plates, or clarify only two near segments when too weak. Ribs explain supporting rhythm, not disconnected circular slices.

## Separate inner brightness from darker edges

Use pale warm yellow centrally and slightly deeper warm edges and rib areas against muted cool grey. Test centre, edge and background values instead of uniform yellow. Reduce excessive central brightness or halo in a sun-like result, or strengthen local value contrast rather than saturating the entire scene when unlit-looking.

## Keep rib hierarchy within transmitted light

Vary rib strength with the paper’s light, clarifying near segments and omitting far ones rather than drawing transparent full rings. Reduce wire-like hard reflections and deep darks. Check the intact lit body with ribs hidden, restoring them for support rather than fragmentation; transmission does not require every internal structure to be visible.

## Turn any pattern with the paper surface

Add at most a small invented motif or short lettering, compressed with the curved paper and affected by its lighting. Dark print does not emit light. Hand-letter if useful without assuming automatic surface mapping, or omit decoration. The blank illuminated paper must establish volume independently of the pattern.

## Retain necessary opening thickness

Use narrow darker opening rings with perspective and hidden rear edges. An optional short tassel needs a clear attachment. Keep some neighbouring warmth inside openings. Reduce a lid-like oversized top or repair a suspension line piercing paper through connection and overlap, keeping hardware subordinate under the same ambient light.

## Check halo separately from object edges

An optional separate broad warm surrounding fade should have no hard orange-cloud boundary. Test the lantern without it, then compare whether it adds space. Repair silhouette errors directly and reduce an entirely orange background. Omit the halo when local values already suffice, retaining both previews rather than equating more effects with completion.

## Compare ribs and bright area at small size

Review silhouette and bright area small, rib curvature normally and edge or pattern errors close up. Quiet stripe-dominated ribs or restore side turns and openings in a flat sign-like result. Test without decoration and record one useful broad transmitted-light plane and one dispensable rib segment.

## Keep the frame and vary the body proportions

Keep unlit structure, plain lit paper and finished versions with editable decoration. Next, rebuild a longer body’s belly, rib spacing and openings under the same palette. Stretching the flat finish is not that study. Identify changed planes and retain the limit: this fictional illustration gives no construction, electrical or flame-safety validation.
