## Specify equal distances rather than equal durations

Each leg covers nine kilometres, at constant positive speeds 18 km/h and 30 km/h respectively. Distance means path length, not directed displacement; calculate average speed only. On about 1800 × 1500 pixels group graph, units, ledger and controls, then save. Retain distances nine/nine together with speeds eighteen/thirty instead of discarding the weighting condition. Use kilometres and hours consistently; switching to minutes requires conversion, not treating thirty minutes as thirty hours.

## Divide each distance by speed for half and three-tenths hour

Times are 9/18=1/2 hour, thirty minutes, and 9/30=3/10 hour, eighteen minutes. Total distance eighteen over time 4/5 hour, forty-eight minutes, gives 18÷(4/5)=45/2=22.5 km/h. The slower leg lasts longer, so equal time weights are wrong. Retain both rows and totals with traceable units. Dividing 18 by 48 gives kilometres per minute; multiply by sixty to obtain 22.5 km/h rather than merely relabelling the unit.

## Place the equal-area average line at twenty-two and a half

Draw average line 22.5 across t=0 to 0.8. Its rectangle is 450 by 960 pixels, area 432000, equal to the two original areas combined. Mathematically 22.5×0.8=18. Its height follows total quantity over duration, not the geometric midpoint of the two tops. Both (18×0.5+30×0.3)/0.8 and, for equal distances, 2/(1/18+1/30) give 22.5. Preserve their derivation conditions; the unweighted harmonic mean cannot be applied indiscriminately to unequal distances. The time weights are 5/8 for the slower leg and 3/8 for the faster, summing to one; 18×5/8+30×3/8 remains 22.5. They are duration divided by total duration, not equal one-half weights inferred from equal distances. Check the shared total-time denominator before averaging.
