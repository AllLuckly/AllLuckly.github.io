Use a front-view reference of a guarded desk fan to practice concentric circles, occlusion and stand connections.

## Identify the object and reference

Vornado’s small air-circulator description identifies a grille and blade; models differ in shape and blade count, and some fan designs have no exposed blade grille. Choose a permitted front-view photograph of a bladed desk fan showing grille, hub and stand, recording model, source and angle. Draw a stationary exterior only; do not operate, disassemble or touch a fan or infer its performance from the picture.

## Create the canvas and sketch

Create an approximately 1500 by 1800 portrait canvas in the current VectorPaint interface and save an editable original; dimensions are suggestions. Place the circular fan head above, leaving room for stand, base and table shadow. Separate sketch, housing, blades, grille, stand and background with available layers; labels may differ. Lightly mark the center, vertical axis and tabletop.

## Draw outer ring and hub

Draw the fan-head outer ring from the reference and place a smaller hub at the same approximate center. A frontal view can use circles; an oblique photo may produce ellipses whose directions should agree. Compare hub and outer-ring diameters without making both excessively large. At thumbnail size check for unwanted hub displacement; if perspective truly shifts the photo’s apparent center, follow the reference rather than forcing perfect concentricity.

## Arrange stationary blades

Observe visible blade count, curvature and position in the reference. Block one simplified blade from near the hub outward without passing through the outer ring, then place the others following the photo and their turning direction. If using copy and rotation, confirm the tool actually visible in the current interface and the rotation center rather than assuming a particular menu. Stop blade contours hidden by the grille; unseen complete edges need not be invented.

## Build the foreground grille

Place the grille in front of blades according to the reference: start with an outer ring and necessary inner rings, then add a restrained set of radial lines or simplify the actual grid pattern. Grille lines are in front and should occlude blade boundaries where they cross. If the photo is not radial, do not force equally spaced spokes. Avoid so many lines that blades disappear; at small size the grille, blades and hub should remain distinguishable.

## Connect head and stand

Draw the neck or support beneath the head from the reference, making its connections to housing and base readable. Do not draw the hidden support through the fan head; use stacking or occlusion contours. Establish base width and table contact before refining support sides. If the reference is a clip-on fan without a desktop base, choose another reference or redraw its actual structure rather than imposing this base.

## Use line weight to distinguish depth

Use a clear housing outline, somewhat finer grille lines and restrained internal blade edges. Grille and blades can differ in value, but color alone should not carry depth: crossings also need correct occlusion. Follow the reference for whether the visible hub is in front of or behind portions of the grille. If the image becomes a dense black mesh, reduce line count or contrast instead of darkening all parts.

## Compare grille and blade stacking

Duplicate the sketch: put the grille in front of blades in one and deliberately put blades in front of grille lines in the other, retaining positions and colors. Compare with the photo and mark at least one front-to-back relationship at a crossing. This exercise analyzes visible imagery, not grille safety performance, and requires no physical contact or disassembly. Label the unused version as a stacking control.

## Check repetition and center

If the grille uses radial repetition, inspect two neighboring lines and see whether they converge on the same center rather than unrelated points. Compare equal spacing only when the reference actually approximates it; perspective, other grille designs or local occlusion can make a photo uneven. Zoom in for unintended gaps where lines meet rings, then shrink and ensure repeated lines do not merge into a dark mass at target size.

## Check crop and tabletop

Inspect the head top, both outer-ring sides, base and table line to avoid unintentionally clipping grille or support. Add a simplified contact shadow beneath the base, hiding it first to confirm the base touches the table. For a plain-background reference, the table is a design choice. Omit controls, cord or branding that cannot be resolved rather than borrowing them from another model.

## Save and reopen

Save the editable original, then use a share or export entry actually shown in the current version to save an image or SVG separately. Reopen to inspect outer ring, blades, grille, stand and crop, then return to identify object and background layers. If the grille is missing, check visibility, line width and export bounds first; a matching bitmap does not prove editable paths. Keep source and permission notes and mark unperformed device or export steps pending.

## Record observed and unknown details

Beside the sketch list visible blades, blade edges hidden by the grille and stand connections obscured by the view. Draw the first from the reference and mark the others unknown; do not add unseen parts solely for apparent symmetry. Compare grille lines at visible crossings and retain a version for later review.
