## Fix the reference scope by species

## Use one sheath as the shared starting point

Place a narrow grey-brown sheath at one short twig attachment, oriented toward the needle fan, like a small sleeve around bases rather than a large bud. Connect all three needle guides there, then cover short basal segments with its rim. Gather separately stalked needles back into the shared sheath. ## Trace each of the three needles separately

## Model slender volume without outlining both edges everywhere

Give near needle portions extremely narrow colour bands, tapering toward single-line tips. Quiet distant parts without abrupt cuts. Widths and lengths may vary while each path stays continuous, not flat-ended. Use cooler green on dark sides and sparse near highlights rather than two white borders everywhere. ## Occlude only short segments at crossings

Cross two needles lightly at mid-length, deciding which is nearer. Hide only a tiny rear segment and continue its original curve on both sides. Do not add a black knot or merge two needles into one tip. Check full curves in the hidden sketch, then inspect visible continuity. Change curves or gaps if counting becomes difficult rather than adding tips. Reduce crossings if rope-like. Require clear counting in the main bundle; note occlusion in companions without forcing every hidden needle through.

## Give companion fascicles their own bases

After establishing the main bundle, add two quieter fascicles at other twig positions, each with its own small sheath rather than one large hole for all needles. Companions can be occluded or cropped, but record visible omissions without implying arbitrary within-fascicle counts. Add sparse axial twig marks, not dense cone scales. ## Keep fine lines continuous against a pale background

Maintain enough value contrast with the pale background: tips may be quiet without all disappearing. Reduce companion density and crossings if a green fuzz-ball; soften curves and regular spacing if three swords. Keep background variation very faint rather than adding a dense forest.
