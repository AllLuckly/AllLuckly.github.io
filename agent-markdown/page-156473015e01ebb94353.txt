Draw three flute joints manually, keeping the embouchure hole in its lip plate and key cups attached along the tube.

Draw a modern transverse flute tilted slightly upward to the right, concentrating on connections along its narrow tube: the head-joint embouchure hole, body keys and rods, and foot-joint seam. Establish a continuous axis before reflections so metal detail cannot hide structure. This exterior drawing provides neither fingering nor tuning results.

## Arrange three joints along one axis

Create an approximately 2200×1000 px landscape canvas and sketch a faint axis rising slightly rightward with margins at both ends. Arrange connected head, body and foot joints along it, making the body longest and using the selected official reference rather than equal thirds. Separate sketch, tube colour, keys/rods and highlights into layers, then save native work. Pixels are compositional, not physical length. Show mainly a side view with a little upper surface; all seams share one axis and perspective.

## Keep the side embouchure hole inside its lip plate

Place a lip plate against the upper side of the head joint and a small dark elliptical hole within it. The hole belongs on the tube’s side, not its left end as an end-blown opening; the plate must not float like a leaf. Draw its near rim and a short thickness shadow while retaining plate surface around the hole. Indicate the head screw with one or two narrow rings, not a large bell. Inspect hole–plate–tube containment enlarged and the head joint at fit-to-window size; reflections must stop at the dark hole rather than fill it with silver.

## Connect body and foot with narrow seams

Make the tube a narrow cylinder before adding seam rings at head-to-body and body-to-foot joints. These are fitted connections, not wide background gaps or flexible bends. If the far opening is visible, its ellipse must fit the axis; a predominantly side view need not show a complete circular opening. Hide detailed keys and trace the silhouette left to right. Joints may change slightly at connections, but the body must not become a flat ruler or the foot turn in another direction.

## Group representative key cups without inventing a total

Place six faint guides for representative body key cups, grouping them along the axis rather than spacing beads mechanically. Six is the set clearly represented here, not the instrument’s complete key count. Give cups thickness and short arms, larger near and potentially concealed far. Not every highlight needs a black hole, and tone holes beneath cups are not holes through to the background. Add selected smaller foot-joint keys with separate guide labels, fade labels later and document omitted linkages. Illustrative cups cannot establish fingering.

## Run rods continuously beside the tube

Draw slender dark-grey rods beside the tube, supported by posts and joined to cups with short arms. Keep rods parallel but slightly separate from the body instead of merging everything into a heavy outline. Stop a rod behind an occluding cup and resume it beyond; an arm must not cross another cup and end in empty space. Check that every representative cup has visible support or an occlusion explanation. Omit fine springs rather than add unexplained silver wires. This illustrates exterior linkage without disassembly or claiming the simplified mechanism would function.

## Separate tube reflections from small hardware

Use cool grey, warmer reflections and a few near-white accents. Vary long tube highlights and break them at seams and cup shadows. Turn cup highlights around their own small surfaces instead of drawing one white line through every centre. Keep a soft underside band, reserving deepest values for the hole and contact seams so black outlines do not flatten the flute. If six cups merge into a sawtooth silhouette, subdue far detail and restore gaps rather than thicken the tube. Invented lighting and silver colour establish neither material nor price.

## Trace from embouchure to foot for floating parts

Hide highlights and trace hole to plate, plate to head joint, two seams to the common axis, and representative cups through arms and rods to the body. A floating cup may have a deleted arm; a broken-looking seam needs a narrower gap. Correct a bent main axis in the base drawing before moving hardware. Preserve a prior copy and repair locally instead of globally reordering layers and breaking correct occlusion. Check the hole and six body guides together, avoiding a replacement key added arbitrarily at the foot to restore a count.

## Reopen and keep fine rods subordinate to the embouchure

Save native artwork, hide axes and labels, and reopen a PNG export. Read tube direction and lip plate at fit-to-window size, then inspect rod interruptions and six representative cups enlarged. If rods merge into a black stripe, lower contrast or omit far segments rather than enlarge the hole to fill its plate. Reopen native layers independently. Document the three joints, six principal cups and omitted complex mechanisms. Mark unperformed reopening/export pending; static review is not a sound test or physical-device app test.
