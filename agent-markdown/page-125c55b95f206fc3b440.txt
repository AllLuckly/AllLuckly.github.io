## 2. Identify the unknown to eliminate

The y coefficients are one and minus one, so addition eliminates y. Add both left sides and both right sides; eliminating the left while leaving seven unchanged on the right is invalid. The consequence 3x=9 is necessary for a common solution. To preserve the full problem, replace equation one with this consequence while retaining equation two. Keeping only 3x=9 would incorrectly allow arbitrary y. Show the retained system in a two-line box.

```
(1) 2*x + y = 7
(2) x - y = 2
(1)+(2): 3*x = 9
Retained system: 3*x = 9 and x - y = 2
```

## 5. Scale a whole equation for another elimination route

For another route, multiply the entire second equation by two to obtain 2x−2y=4. Subtract that from the first to get 3y=3, then retain an original equation to recover x=3. ## 6. Distinguish identity from contradiction

Use two separate controls. x+y=2 with 2x+2y=4 eliminates to 0=0, leaving a relation rather than a unique solution. x+y=2 with 2x+2y=6 gives 0=2, a contradiction over reals. A zero does not always mean no solution, and 0=0 does not force x=y=0. Preserve each original system and keep its result out of the initial problem. Judge all constraints, not the number of zero symbols.
