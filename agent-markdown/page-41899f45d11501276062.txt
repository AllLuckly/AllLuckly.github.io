Draw an adult lowland tapir walking slowly leftwards, with a full body, clear near feet and partly hidden far limbs. Its nose and upper lip form a short flexible snout, not an elephant trunk reaching the ground. Distinguish four toes on a front foot from three on a hind foot, while showing only those visible in the main view and using a small diagram for hidden counts.

## Establish the lowland tapir’s narrower front and broader rear

Use a horizontal canvas of about 2100 × 1500 pixels, head left, body central and fuller at the rump, narrowing towards head and neck. Sketch a low barrel-like body and continuous back, with short sturdy legs rather than slender deer legs. Separate body, head/snout, feet and coat from the sketch on pale green-grey. Use an adult lowland tapir’s darker back and lighter underside, without a Malayan tapir’s large black-white pattern or a calf’s stripes. Pixels set composition only; save and inspect the silhouette first.

## Connect the snout to the face rather than hanging on a tube

Draw a short tapering snout from the face, turning slightly down and then gently up at the tip. Let the upper lip participate in its form, with the mouth line beneath rather than a second mouth pasted onto the nose. Keep the extension proportionate to the head instead of curling it into a rope. For a pig-like result, check the continuous snout and head outline before enlarging nostrils. For a small elephant, shorten the nose and reassess ears and body. This pose describes appearance, not smelling direction, grasping force or internal nasal anatomy.

## Place small ears and eyes on the same turning head

Place a small near eye behind and above the snout, using a dark mark and minimal light; the far eye may be hidden by the turn. Attach ears at the upper rear of the head, showing a broader near ear and narrow far contour rather than floating discs. Connect cheek and neck without cutting them apart with an ear-root dark. Hide eye detail and check the volume linking snout root, forehead and ear roots before refining features. Chosen colour differences describe light, not age, sex or identification of every tapir species.

## Connect all four limbs from shoulder or hip to the ground

Establish near forelimb and hindlimb roots beneath shoulder and rump, then add only exposed far-limb portions. Keep feet on a credible shared ground rather than attaching a far foot arbitrarily to the belly centreline. Show the near front foot clearly and the near hind foot obliquely, with a body shadow beneath. For a floating foot, correct landing and limb length before darkening shadow. For a fish-like body, restore shoulder and hip support rather than adding a tail fin. Keep the tail short at the rump, not a pangolin-like long supporting tail.

## Separate total toe counts from visible counts

On a checking layer, draw small frontal diagrams with four numbered front toes and three numbered hind toes, explicitly separate from the main perspective feet. Do not splay a hidden small front toe into view, or turn a hind foot fully frontwards while its leg remains sideways. Use a few dark separations for visible toe ends and retain foot thickness. Four gaps do not mean four toes: four adjacent toes normally need three internal divisions. These counts explain anatomy, not missing toes, injury or classification inferred from visible strokes.

## Turn the darker back and lighter belly around one body

Lay dark warm grey or grey-brown on the back with a lighter underside, joining shoulder and rump through broad soft planes rather than a straight white dividing strip. Suggest short hair only at selected edges and turns. Keep wet nose and eye highlights small instead of polishing the body. Follow upper-left light with firm foot contact and weaker far limbs. For a blanket-like pattern, soften the division and restore volume; for hairless plastic, add faint short strokes following the body, not another species’ stripes. Colour measures neither coat properties nor health.

## Check the short snout, four limbs and foot diagrams

Hide hair texture and trace forehead to snout, shoulders/hips to four limbs, then compare the four-front/three-hind diagrams. Repair a hose-like snout at its broad root and upper lip. Move limbs growing from the back to the ventral support area. If overlapping toes are unclear, retain occlusion and improve the side diagram rather than making the main body transparent. Keep the faulty version and recheck small after one local change. Anatomical totals come from references, visibility from the chosen pose; stroke counts are not observation records.

## Reopen and check species scope and visible feet

Save native work, hide the main axes and reopen a PNG. At fit-to-window size check the tapered front, fuller rear, short snout and short limbs; enlarge for near toe ends and nose-to-lip continuity. Label any retained foot diagram separately. Reopen editable work and adjust only the belly value, keeping foot numbers and coat independently editable. State adult lowland tapir, an illustrative step and hidden toes; mark unperformed export/reopening as pending. No real animal was approached or tested, and this is no complete identification, gait analysis or health assessment.
