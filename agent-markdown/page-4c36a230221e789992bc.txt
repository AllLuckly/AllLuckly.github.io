Dialogue alone can omit meaningful sound: an offscreen doorbell, stopped music or a speaker outside the picture. Add necessary cues to a self-made clip and test understanding while muted. This assumes neither automatic transcription nor sound recognition and does not certify universal accessibility. You identify and edit the wording; iCouldPlay displays it for reading. Start with a short sample before extending the work.

## Choose sounds that affect understanding

Use permitted material with distinguishable speakers, an environmental sound and a music change. Watch and listen once, identifying sounds whose absence would obscure action or setting. Captioning every minor noise can overwhelm the content. Choose a concrete goal, such as explaining why someone turns their head, rather than describing every acoustic detail. Keep original media and captions, adding cues only to a test copy so they are not later mistaken for source text.

## Separate audible facts from guesses

Keep audible facts separate from inference: three short knocks differ from guessing that a neighbour is at the door. Use a specific source only when the picture or story establishes it; otherwise describe offscreen knocking without revealing an unintroduced character. Music cues should support understanding without inventing authorial intent or psychological explanations. Relisten to verify that the sound exists and mark uncertain details for checking rather than making the captions more dramatic through guesswork.

## Identify speakers when needed

Visible alternating speakers may already be identifiable. Add a concise label where an offscreen voice, similar voices or editing creates ambiguity. Use identity established by the video without spoiling an unknown character’s name. Keep neutral labels consistent when identity is uncertain, rather than alternating “narrator” and “speaker A” for the same person. Place the label with the relevant speech instead of far in advance. Establish identity before styling; appearance cannot repair a wrong speaker attribution.

## Time cues to the relevant event

Time short cues for knocks, bells and music changes to the event, checking premature disclosure and overlap with the next line. Sustained sound need not be relabelled every second, and its ending needs a cue only when meaningful. Retain changes required for this clip. In iCouldPlay, start several seconds before the cue rather than inspecting only a frame with text already visible. Paused readability does not establish enough reading time alongside the action.

## Watch once without sound

Under controlled conditions, watch the test without listening and note unclear actions, speakers and setting changes. This is not a substitute for deaf or hard-of-hearing users’ feedback, but can expose obvious omissions. Restore your previous level afterward so later playback is not mistaken for a silent file. Repair the actual gap—a bell or speaker identity—rather than lengthening all dialogue to compensate for every problem.

## Review with sound and remove redundancy

Rewatch with sound, checking that added cues do not crowd dialogue, redundantly describe the picture or overinterpret ambiguity. Shorten verbose descriptions while retaining meaningful sounds. A music change may be more useful than many adjectives. Edit a small cue set and preserve an earlier copy rather than globally forcing one pattern. Evaluate information that helps muted viewing without excessive interference when sound is available, not the total number of cues.

## Check loading and plain-text readability

Load only one test caption file and make its ordinary text understandable without special icons, colour or animation. Preserve wording when a bracket or character fails and investigate rendering separately. Different formats express styling differently, so an editor’s appearance is not guaranteed in iCouldPlay. Establish stable loading before adjusting size and position. Recheck identity and meaning in other languages rather than mechanically replacing labels and declaring the translation complete.

## Keep cue evidence and sharing scope

Keep the sound position, identity evidence and rationale for important cues, marking what needs another review. Confirm rights to share dialogue, people and background sound; adding captions creates no new rights to the media. Reuse the checking process, not the previous clip’s bell, music or speaker labels. [W3C WebVTT](<https://www.w3.org/TR/webvtt1/>) explains timed-text structure, but correct structure does not establish accurate sound descriptions or suitability for every viewer.
