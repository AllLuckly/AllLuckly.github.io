The flower-like dorsal pattern is a surface structure, not five projecting arms. Refer to Dendraster excentricus to draw a test without soft tissues or spines and a small accompanying underside view. ## Label the two views before detail

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px canvas with a large upper-surface view left and a small underside lower-right, clearly separated. Temporarily label both. Use related flattened outlines so the smaller view is not a decorative coin. A slightly elevated main view exposes petaloids and a narrow thickness edge; the underside may be more direct. These are declared views, not a software rotation of a specimen. Fix identities first and assign pores and mouth accordingly instead of putting every feature on one face.

## Give the flattened test a shallow dome

Draw one continuous flattened envelope with a slight central rise through broad pale tones rather than a thickness-free sheet. Add a narrow dark near edge, weaker far edge and restrained ground shadow. Modest irregularity is fine, without five projecting arms. Repair a sea-star-like silhouette before pattern, or lower an excessively bowl-like dome and wall. Sand-dollar shapes and openings vary; this selected reference is not a universal circular form for the group.

## Arrange five petaloids without forced equal angles

Place a gathering region on the upper face and five elongated rounded petaloids within the test. In an eccentric sand dollar, pattern position need not equal the outline’s geometric centre or use exact 72-degree divisions. Count five and preserve room around front and rear, keeping tips inside the edge. Replace rigid ruler-like rays with reference-based lengths and curves. The guide centre aids layout; it is not the mouth and should not become an opening on the upper surface.

## Place pore rows rather than five large holes

Use paired fine dots or short arcs along each petaloid’s sides, resolving a few groups and simplifying the rest. Follow curvature instead of scattering freckles over the entire test. Five petals are not five large background-filled holes; do not import slots from another species. Lighten heavy joined dots rather than hollow out each petal. If counting is unclear, hide pores and check main regions. Omission is a drawing-scale choice, not a claim that only the depicted pores exist.

## Draw the underside mouth separately

Place a small mouth cue near the underside centre with restrained inward-gathering grooves, distinct from the upper petaloids. Do not show internal jaws or copy the same flower underneath as though it were identical structure. Pair views through notes rather than a physical tube connecting two drawings. Keep the mouth small instead of hollowing the whole test into a ring. Omit unverified underside detail and state that this view identifies face and mouth location, not complete anatomy.

## Separate a pale test from a living exterior

Use warm off-white with grey-violet shade, not uniform glowing white. A small colour note can explain dense spine cover on a living animal without inferring living colour from a pale test. It need not become a third animal and confuse view identities. A separate living study would require spine cover, not merely a purple recolour. Reflections are not spine tips, and clear skeletal patterns establish no health or age. This delivery remains a test study.

## Audit petaloids and mouth by face

Hide materials and identify upper five-petaloid view left and underside mouth right. Check pore rows remain on their surfaces. Repair arm-like outer contours or petaloids painted as deep holes. Simplify an unsupported copied underside flower to mouth and restrained grooves. Keep temporary labels hidden for export while preserving view order in notes. Added spine dots cannot conceal wrong skeletal form; resolve skeleton, faces and openings before subtle grain.

## Reopen and check paired views

Save native work and reopen PNG. Count five petaloids fitted, then inspect paired pore cues and underside mouth enlarged. Some pores may merge small, but petals should become neither black holes nor arms. Reduce dot contrast without changing counts. Preserve the full smaller view and traceable note order. Reopen independent view layers and record species reference, skeletal scope and omitted detail.
