Draw a contour of f(x,y)=x²+2y² and the gradient at P=(1,1). Fix a unit direction before calculating its derivative, then compare a finite move to distinguish local rate from accumulated change. Differentiate externally and use layers only for derived curves, arrows and notes.

## Fix a differentiable function and the level-three point

The polynomial is differentiable everywhere and f(1,1)=3, so draw x²+2y²=3. This ellipse is centred at the origin with semiaxes √3 and √(3/2), not a circle centred at P. Save function/point/layer definitions and separate contour, gradient, direction and notes. Coordinates and function values are abstract, not real terrain slope or fluid speed. Common contour colour denotes one value, without assigning an undefined extra quantity to line thickness or brightness.

## Differentiate before anchoring the gradient at P

Partial derivatives are f\_x=2x and f\_y=4y, hence ∇f(P)=(2,4). Anchored at P, the arrow ends at P+(2,4)=(3,5), not coordinate (2,4). Its direction indicates steepest local increase, but magnitude is defined too; any shortened display needs an explicit scale factor. Retain full length here. Separate differentiation, point evaluation and displacement instead of eyeballing a plausible normal. Function value three is not gradient magnitude.

```text
f(x,y)=x^2+2*y^2; P=(1,1); f(P)=3
grad f(P)=(2,4); anchored endpoint=P+(2,4)=(3,5)
u=(3/5,4/5); |u|=1; D_u f(P)=22/5
tangent: x+2*y=3; tangent direction=(2,-1)
f(P+t*u)=3+(22/5)*t+(41/25)*t^2
f(P+u)-f(P)=151/25=6.04
```

## Draw ellipse, point and arrows at one scale

Use a suggested 1800×2000 canvas, screen origin (500,1400), 200 pixels per unit equally: X=500+200x, Y=1400−200y. P is (700,1200), gradient endpoint (1100,400); ellipse semiaxes are approximately 346.410 and 244.949 pixels. Approximate with shapes/guides, checking P algebraically rather than distorting the curve to a wrong point. Without numeric controls, place manually and label approximation; nodes carry no function constraints. Group arrows separately so caption moves do not drag the gradient.

## Separate a unit direction from a nonunit parameter

Take unit u=(3/5,4/5). D\_u f(P)=∇f(P)·u=6/5+16/5=22/5=4.4. For v=(3,4), dot product 22 is the derivative per t along P+tv, not the same per-unit-distance value; it is not intrinsically erroneous. Normalize before comparing distance-based rates. The maximum unit-direction derivative is |∇f|=2√5≈4.472, above 4.4, along (1/√5,2/√5), not any vaguely northeast arrow.

## Use the gradient normal to check the tangent

At P, tangent equation 2(x−1)+4(y−1)=0 becomes x+2y=3. Direction (2,−1) dots with the gradient to 4−4=0; both are nonzero, giving a meaningful right angle. Draw a thin dashed tangent through P at the gradient tail, not a gradient from the origin toward it. Motion along the contour keeps f=3 and the tangent direction has zero first-order change, but a finite straight tangent step need not preserve f. Label tangency at P, not a second global contour. The nonzero gradient gives this contour a definite normal. At the origin the gradient is zero and level zero is a single point, not a curve with the same tangent. Reassess local conditions before reusing this representation elsewhere.

## Keep the quadratic term for a finite-step control

Substitution along unit u gives f(P+tu)=3+(22/5)t+(41/25)t². At t=1, the increment is 151/25=6.04 versus instantaneous derivative 4.4; the extra 1.64 is quadratic. Derivative times a full step is not exact change, and finite-step error does not refute the derivative’s limit. If plotting P+u=(1.6,1.8), keep it off the level-three ellipse. Distinguish it visually rather than drag it onto the contour while retaining the same move label. Along tangent v=(2,−1), f(P+tv)=3+6t²: first derivative zero but increment six at t=1. The straight tangent and curved contour share a direction at P, not a globally constant-value path.

## Troubleshoot point evaluation, units and arrow anchoring

For a wrong gradient endpoint, check anchoring at P. A fivefold derivative mismatch may mean omitted normalization. Substitute P into the tangent if it misses. Inspect axis scales before rotating correct vectors to repair a screen angle. A full-step change unlike 4.4 may have lost its quadratic term, not require changing f. Repair one copy condition and retain calculations. Moving P requires new gradient, tangent and level, not simply dragging the arrow group. No automatic differentiation or constraint update is assumed.
