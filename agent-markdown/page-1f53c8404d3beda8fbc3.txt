Study broad colour masses, overlaps and information hierarchy in a fictional fruit stall.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-fruit-market-stall/>), translated on 2026-10-27. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A fruit stall can become a heap of coloured objects. Limit this fictional scene to a canopy, two shelves and three produce groups, establishing structure before colour rhythm. Names and prices are invented layout examples; extra fruit or signs cannot repair unstable space.

## Establish three principal areas

Use an approximately 1800×1500 px canvas with canopy above, produce centrally and grounded legs below. Build a slightly angled stall box with a broad front and narrower side, aligning canopy and counter. Separate structure, goods and lettering, establishing a readable silhouette before stripes.

## Give the canopy a top and hanging edge

Build a projecting fabric plane with a narrow hanging lip and supports. Turn stripes with the top and front surfaces, widening them toward the viewer. Add the plane change to a flag-like canopy or lighten excessive thickness on a roof-like one, keeping its attachments clear.

## Paint containers before produce

Give two shelves unequal heights with visible tops and front lips. Establish three shallow boxes and their overlaps before fruit masses. Let rims cover lower fruit portions. Keep some counter visible, reducing goods when they hide the containers and flatten the stall.

## Organise fruit as groups

Use three restrained colour groups, clarifying only a few near fruit in each. Keep a common light and simplify distant forms. Merge independent highlights and shadow fragments for a bead-like result, or restore selected rim and fruit overlaps if everything merges into one block.

## Reserve a readable sign area

Use a short stall name and three sample labels, testing the longest line and smallest quantity at normal size. Place each price unambiguously by its goods. Check available fonts if using text tools, or hand-letter without imports. Remove excess information before shrinking essential labels.

## Unify the stall with canopy shade

Keep the canopy interior in one quieter shadow mass with selected front fruit lights. Use local support contacts and a broad stall shadow rather than individual fruit shadows. Simplify scattered darks when muddy, or restore box sidewalls and counter depth when the scene resembles cutout scenery.

## Limit incidental objects

Choose one incidental basket or folded cloth, scaled to the stall and placed away from signs. Check goods and labels normally, canopy weight small and erroneous intersections close up. Preserve empty ground when the hierarchy works rather than filling every available area.

## Proof the produce labels independently

Keep a separate list of three labels and check names, sample numbers and units, then read them backward on the image to catch copied values. Move ambiguous labels into one box’s clear area. Verify real product information before use, catching length problems before fine painting.

## Judge three colour groups from a distance

At a palm-sized preview, judge canopy, produce and ground masses without reading. Quiet a competing canopy or create low-contrast space beside crowded boxes. Restore normal size to check the sign. Large-area balance cannot be repaired merely by adding more individual fruit.

## Check the scene without lettering

Keep block-in, sign-free and finished previews with editable lettering. Next, reduce produce from three groups to two while preserving structure and light. If the stall fails without words, repair canopy, shelves and containers before labels, recording which colour group best supports the scene.
