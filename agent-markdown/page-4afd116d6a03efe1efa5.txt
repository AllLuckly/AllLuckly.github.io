Use a permitted reference to observe paired claws, cephalothorax and abdomen in a top-view crayfish.

## Identify the reference and structures

Choose a permitted top-view photograph of a freshwater crayfish and record its source and orientation. Use the museum and park-service sources to identify paired large claws, cephalothorax, segmented abdomen and antennae. Color and claw shape vary across species, so color alone does not establish identity. A photograph is sufficient; the exercise does not require capturing or keeping an animal or inferring its range from an illustration.

## Set up canvas and axis

Create an approximately 1800 by 1400 landscape canvas in Aquarell and save an editable original. Use the layer controls visible in the installed version to separate background, sketch, body and appendages; dimensions are suggestions. Point the head and claws left and tail fan right, and lightly draw a body axis. Leave room around antennae and claw tips and check at a small scale that the subject does not touch the canvas edge.

## Separate cephalothorax and abdomen

Block the cephalothorax as one main form and connect the narrower segmented abdomen at its rear. The park-service source distinguishes these body regions; make their connection legible without drawing abdominal segments as isolated beads. Compare the widest body point, abdomen length and tail-fan position with the photo. Revise the large proportions before adding texture or every leg.

## Place the paired claws

The Western Australian Museum notes that the leg pair nearest the head forms the large claws. Draw connecting segments from both sides of the cephalothorax, then the palm and visible fingers of each claw. Follow the photo for size and direction; perspective can make the near claw larger, so exact mirroring is unnecessary. The fingers may be open or closed but should join the same palm rather than float like two leaves.

## Draw abdominal segments and tail fan

Mark abdominal segment boundaries along the axis from the rear of the cephalothorax. Observe which adjacent plates overlap in the chosen photograph. If the abdomen bends, boundary angles change; do not simply repeat parallel bars. At the end, draw the visible tail-fan outline and divisions, stopping lines at occlusions. Follow the reference width and do not require every division to be visible in all views.

## Handle legs and antenna overlaps

The museum describes decapods as having five pairs of legs on the main body, with the front pair forming the large claws. Mark visible leg roots before following the reference for the slender walking legs. Some may be hidden by the body or another leg in a top view; do not force ten complete lines into view. Extend the antennae from the head after checking direction, and keep them thin enough not to hide claws or eyes.

## Separate structures with color

Lay a mid-value reddish brown or the reference color across the cephalothorax and abdomen, then use a slightly darker value at segment boundaries and claw joints. Color is not proof of species. Separate coloring layers for claws, legs and antennae make overlaps easier to inspect. Avoid a heavy black border around every segment; zoom out and make sure head-to-tail remains a continuous body.

## Choose a bottom or observation background

Use muted blue-gray or brown for a simple background and retain a readable body outline. If suggesting a stream bottom, stones and shadow are compositional choices, not a claim that the image records a real habitat. Where thin legs blend into the background, adjust local value or remove texture before thickening each leg to the size of a claw. Hide the background briefly to check the structure.

## Troubleshoot appendages and connections

If claws look pasted beside the body, revisit their connecting segments at the cephalothorax. If the abdomen resembles disconnected beads, check for missing overlapping plate boundaries. Where legs cross, decide which is in front and stop the hidden segment instead of erasing every intersection. If an antenna tip is cropped, move the composition or acknowledge the crop rather than shortening the animal to hide it.

## Compare at thumbnail size and save

Shrink the picture to phone size and identify the cephalothorax, paired claws, abdominal segments and tail fan against the reference. Keep obscured fine legs simplified rather than inventing strokes to reach a count. Save the editable original, export a separate PNG or JPEG using a control visible in the current version, and reopen it to inspect color and cropping. Return to the original to check editable layers, record the source and one uncertain overlap, and mark unperformed device or export steps pending.

## Recheck claw-to-tail direction

Place the reference and output side by side. Without reading the title, trace from claw tip along the body to the tail fan and check that the image was not accidentally flipped on export. Check that both claw fingers join their palms, legs meet the body, and the tail fan follows the abdomen. After changing an overlap, inspect that layer against the background again. Save a revised copy alongside the first to compare clarity without interpreting the difference as observed animal motion. If the slender legs merge into a dark mass, reduce background texture and preserve the overlaps rather than moving leg roots away from their positions in the photograph merely to make counting easier.
