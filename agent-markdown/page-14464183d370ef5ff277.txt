Define a finite probability model: X is uniform on −2,−1,0,1,2 and Y=X². Plot five points, compute Pearson correlation and disprove independence through event probabilities. Five real observations with empirical zero correlation would not justify an unknown-population claim; this proof relies on the fully specified model.

## Fix the probability model without inventing observations

Write the five equally likely nodes and deterministic relation first, each with probability 1/5, not photographic evidence supposedly establishing equal probabilities. Ordered pairs are (−2,4),(−1,1),(0,0),(1,1),(2,4); retain negative and positive inputs separately. Values are abstract, with no real survey, trial or measurement. Separate nodes, mean guides, calculations and conclusions, then save native work. Real data later need new identity, sampling premises and scope, not a copied complete distribution or independence conclusion.

## Compute means before paired deviations

Sums are zero for x and ten for y, giving means zero and two. Keep original pairs while forming x deviations −2,−1,0,1,2 and y deviations 2,−1,−2,−1,2. Independently sorting columns would change the relationship. Draw faint guides x=0,y=2 for means, not a fitted conclusion. Equal left/right heights follow squaring. Mean y=2 need not occur at a node and implies no independence of deviations.

## Keep a zero cross-sum distinct from positive square sums

Paired products −4,1,0,−1,4 sum to Sxy=0, while Sxx=10 and Syy=14. Thus r=Sxy/√(Sxx Syy)=0 with a positive denominator, not an assigned value for 0/0. Uniform weights cancel in the complete five-node model’s ratio as well; with observations alone this would be empirical arithmetic first. Retain square sums as domain checks rather than only r. Cancellation does not mean every individual product is zero.

```text
X uniform on {-2,-1,0,1,2}; Y=X^2
x:       -2,-1,0,1,2
y:        4, 1,0,1,4
mean x=0; mean y=2
Sxy=-4+1+0-1+4=0; Sxx=10; Syy=14
r=Sxy/sqrt(Sxx*Syy)=0
P(X=0,Y=0)=1/5 != (1/5)*(1/5)=1/25
```

## Disprove independence with one event pair

Here P(X=0)=P(Y=0)=1/5, but P(X=0 and Y=0)=1/5, not their product 1/25, disproving independence. Equivalently P(Y=0 given X=0)=1 differs from 1/5, with positive conditioning probability. The proof uses the full model, not merely a bent-looking scatterplot or five observations of unknown origin. Zero Pearson correlation does not establish no relationship, no predictive information or independence. Keep this scope explicit beside the diagram. Y takes 0,1,4 with probabilities 1/5,2/5,2/5, not one third each. Aggregate inputs sharing an output to audit probability denominators rather than count distinct outputs. These probabilities come from the complete model, not unqualified observed frequencies.

## Use linear and constant controls to test the definition

With the same five x nodes and Y=2X+3, points lie on a positive line and correlation is one, without proving real causation. Constant Y=2 makes Syy zero and Pearson correlation undefined, not automatically zero. Keep separate controls without changing main Y=X² points. Retain x support/pairing and state that only Y’s rule changes. This explicit change is more auditable than three unexplained shapes. No confidence interval, significance test or real-population inference is performed. The linear control has y values −1,1,3,5,7, mean three, Sxy=20,Syy=40 and Sxx=10, giving r=20/√400=1. Compare this positive denominator with the constant case, avoiding a claim that every deterministic relationship has correlation one.
