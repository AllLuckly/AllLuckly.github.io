Calculate net charge through a section across three time intervals, separating electron motion, conventional current and zero net flow.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/manyue-current-net-charge-section-electron-opposite-direction-ledger/>), translated on 2026-11-19. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A leftward arrow in a panel need not mean leftward conventional current. Make a wire-section diagram, three time cards and a two-way-carrier control, then read charge-flow rates in ManYue. This is an abstract model, without connecting power or touching wires; the reader is not an ammeter.

## Name a section, positive direction and time intervals

Externally draw a horizontal ideal wire and select one vertical section S, with rightward positive-charge flow defined positive. Put directions on P1, cards for 0–3s,3–5s,5–6s on P2 and controls on P3; export a PDF and retain editable work. After importing, check that S is not cropped and bookmark the time cards. Q here is cumulative net charge crossing S, not charge stored throughout the wire. Arrow thickness supplies no values; label every input.

## Multiply each current by its own duration

Signed constant currents are +2A,−1A,0A over durations 3s,2s,1s. ΔQ=IΔt gives +6C,−2C,0C. The minus sign records net flow relative to the declared positive direction, not negative time. Three cards do not imply one second each. Mark endpoints, subtract times before multiplying, and leave accumulated charge unknown if duration is absent instead of inventing a convenient second.

```text
positive direction: rightward conventional current through S
0..3 s: I=+2 A; delta_Q=+6 C
3..5 s: I=-1 A; delta_Q=-2 C
5..6 s: I=0 A; delta_Q=0 C
net over 6 s=+4 C; average_I=+2/3 A
```

## Sum net charge before dividing by total time

Over six seconds net charge is 6−2+0=+4C and average current 4/6=+2/3A. An unweighted mean of the three currents gives 1/3A by treating unequal durations equally. Retain time weights and check \[2×3+(−1)×2\]/6. Positive-direction throughput in the first interval is 6C, whereas the full signed net is 4C. These are different questions and require labelled totals.

Integrating the magnitude of each interval’s net current gives 6+2+0=8C, distinct from signed net 4C. Calling 8C the entire carrier throughput requires further conditions excluding simultaneous cancelling streams. P3 shows net current alone cannot recover microscopic total crossing. Keep signed net, accumulated net-flow magnitude and all-carrier throughput as separate headings, leaving an undetermined one blank.

## Pair leftward electrons with rightward conventional current

In the separate metal model, explicitly assign the depicted drift to electrons only. Leftward electrons carry negative charge and correspond to rightward conventional current. Label arrows electron velocity or conventional current, not colour alone. A negative-current card carried only by electrons instead has rightward electron drift. No drift speed is calculated: area and carrier density are absent. Two amperes do not mean electrons moving at two metres per second.

Using specified approximate electron charge magnitude e=1.6×10⁻¹⁹C, the first 6C interval corresponds to 3.75×10¹⁹ electrons crossing S. Negative carrier charge affects conventional direction, not the sign of a particle count. This concerns three seconds at one section, not every electron in the wire or every branch after splitting. Retain the approximate constant and counted object.

## A zero net flow need not mean no particles cross

In a separate one-second control, +3C of positive charge crosses rightward and +3C crosses leftward. Contributions are +3C and−3C: zero net current but 6C total throughput magnitude. This permits two positive-carrier streams and is not silently the earlier electron-only metal model. Zero describes cancellation of a signed sum, not universal microscopic rest. Declare carrier type and direction before adding, distinguishing direction signs from charge signs.

## Cross-check units and cumulative-charge slopes

Read P2 alternatively as Q versus time: Q(0)=0, Q(3)=6C, Q(5)=4C, Q(6)=4C. Segment slopes 2,−1,0C/s reproduce the currents. A falling cumulative curve does not destroy electrons. Ampere means coulomb per second; area under current versus time is not watts or joules. Voltage and further conditions are needed for an energy calculation, rather than inventing consumption from current alone.

For a prefix control change the first interval to 2mA: over 3s it transfers 0.006C=6mC, not 6C. Separately, 2mA over three minutes uses 180s and gives 0.36C, not 0.006C. These test current/time units without changing the original ledger. A smaller current over longer time can accumulate more charge; compare windows before ranking cumulative amounts.

## Diagnose object, sign and weighting errors on the reading page

An 8C result suggests adding −2C as positive; an average 1/3A suggests missing duration weights. Matching electron/current directions calls for revisiting the negative-carrier condition. Enlarge signs and seconds, recording S and the positive direction externally without claiming automatic integration of the current in the app. Correct one error, then return to the bookmark. If a crop omits carrier identity, leave direction unconfirmed instead of assuming an electron model from a familiar circuit shape.

## Reopen and recover the section ledger before averaging

Reopen the PDF at its bookmark and, without answers, reconstruct +6,−2,0C, then net +4C and average+2/3A. Use P3 to explain two-way flow at zero net current and identify electron arrows. Deliver the signed section, time endpoints, interval charges and unequal-weight control. Mark unperformed import/bookmark/reopening pending. Acceptance is definition-based reading, not measured wire charge, circuit safety or App electrical recognition.
