Different personalities alone do not establish how a text uses contrast to highlight a character. Read an original lost-manuscript scene in Book Isle, pairing Ada’s and Bo’s actions, timing and consequences, then test scope against their later cooperation. Foil describes a relation/effect, not inferior worth or necessarily enmity. The app preserves references rather than assigning character types.

## Retain the complete shared-event passage

Card F1 reads:“While sorting the lost manuscript, Ada returned each page to its numbered slot; Bo turned to the ending and first wrote the conclusion he guessed. The bell rang twice. Ada was still correcting a label; Bo had submitted a summary that omitted one name.” This is original fiction, not a real job assessment. Verify F1/names after PDF/EPUB import and retain the shared task/two bells. Isolated still correcting/already submitted cannot rank them forever. Keep title, speakers and narration tied to this version.

## Choose one shared comparison dimension

Record the common manuscript task, then compare information-processing order: Ada first checks page order; Bo first forms an ending hypothesis. Separately compare timing/omission. Do not oppose one person’s height to the other’s speed. Derive dimensions from actions rather than preassigning cautious/brave labels and selecting agreeable phrases. Co-presence alone proves no foil: identify the highlighted trait and how contrast clarifies it beyond one isolated character. Unknown family background or motives remain unknown.

## Infer bounded traits from the contrast

Candidates are Ada’s attention to detail and Bo’s quick overall judgment, with costs retained: Ada has not finished label correction and Bo omitted a name. More checking does not guarantee correctness; early submission does not prove malice. Contrast can emphasize care/hesitation or decisiveness/haste, with wording scaled to evidence. Limit it to this F1 task rather than lifelong traits or an authorial verdict inferred from two bells.

## Test highlighting by removing one character

In an external analytical copy read only Ada’s sentences, then restore Bo. Label correction remains visible without him, but the alternative method/deadline comparison is lost. Reverse the test for Bo too. This deletion is an analysis control, not an overwrite of the book or an author-approved revision. It shows how contrast highlights details from different analytical focuses; first-mentioned names do not alone determine protagonist status. Overall narrative roles require wider context.

Deleting Bo’s omitted-name consequence makes speed look cost-free; deleting Ada’s unfinished label hides time cost. Identify analytical deletions and base the final claim on complete F1 rather than a sharpened but distorted excerpt.

## Later cooperation need not erase contrast

F2 reads:“The next day Bo made time for Ada’s checking, and Ada used Bo’s summary to locate a missing page.” Cooperation can coexist with contrasting methods. A goal-blocking antagonist is not automatically a foil; find separate conflict/highlighting evidence. F2 also qualifies absolutes: Bo can respect checking and Ada can use summaries. Add new evidence without erasing F1’s local contrast or forcing permanent opposites.

## Separate quoted actions from your judgment

Use visible highlights/notes to reference each action, without colors meaning good or bad people. Keep source sentence, common dimension, highlighted trait and counterevidence in four external columns with F1/F2/version. If names cannot be selected separately, excerpt whole sentences and identify subjects. Reread task context on return. Bo’s guessed ending is not established story fact, and omission alone does not prove deliberate deception.

## Check irrelevant differences and swapped names

“Ada wore blue; Bo wore gray” gives an appearance difference but no established highlighting function. An analytical name swap retaining actions preserves contrast while exchanging trait ownership; old color/name notes cannot simply be reused. Map identities across translations before quoting. Correct identity mapping or interpretation one at a time without rewriting whether the summary omitted a name or Ada had finished merely to preserve a moral ranking.

## Reopen and state the foil claim’s evidence and scope

Reopen F1 without old labels, reconstruct common task, two action pairs and consequences, then qualify claims through F2. State who highlights whom along which dimension and attach two returnable sentences, not merely opposite personalities. Reassess narrative function across chapters, keeping cooperation/conflict separate. Reading notes are not character recognition, real personality assessment, certain authorial motive or a rule that every character must form a foil pair.
