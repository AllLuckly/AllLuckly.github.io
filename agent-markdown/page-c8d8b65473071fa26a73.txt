Recalculate sine, cosine and tangent in a three-four-five right triangle, checking changed reference angles and angle units separately.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/nasbook-right-triangle-ratios-reference-angle-unit/>), translated on 2026-11-14. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

The opposite side is not always at the top of a diagram, and the adjacent side is not any edge touching the angle. Read an original right-triangle page in Book Isle, fixing the right angle, hypotenuse and specified acute angle before forming ratios. Separate rotating the page, changing the reference angle and changing angle units. Calculate on paper or externally; no automatic angle measurement or triangle solver is claimed.

## Name vertices and the right angle

Draw original triangle ABC with a right angle at C, lengths AC four, BC three and AB five in the same unit. Mark A as reference; labels, not apparent drawing lengths, define the data. Record source, example and location, and verify three squared plus four squared equals five squared. Without an explicit right-angle condition, a visually perpendicular drawing does not justify these right-triangle ratios. Confirm the problem instead. This is document reading, without camera measurement of real structures.

## Identify the hypotenuse from the right angle

AB, opposite the right angle at C, is the hypotenuse of length five. Choosing A or B as reference does not change it. For A, BC does not touch A and is opposite; AC is the touching nonhypotenuse side and is adjacent. Write names, lengths and reasons separately to avoid including the hypotenuse as a second candidate adjacent side. After rotation, find the right angle and reference again rather than label horizontal, vertical or slanted edges. Three-four-five is this example, not every right triangle.

## Form each of the three ratios

For A, sine is opposite over hypotenuse, three fifths; cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse, four fifths; tangent is opposite over adjacent, three quarters. Write side names before numbers rather than infer lengths from remembered decimals. Common length units cancel, so ratios have no centimetre unit. Check sine squared plus cosine squared: nine twenty-fifths plus sixteen twenty-fifths equals one. This checks the values but cannot detect every possible interchange of side names by itself.

## Relabel sides for the other acute angle

Switch the reference to B while retaining right angle C and hypotenuse AB. AC is now opposite and BC adjacent, giving sine four fifths, cosine three fifths and tangent four thirds. The acute angles are complementary: one sine equals the other cosine, and the nonzero tangents are reciprocal. Only the reference changed, not edge lengths. Do not copy A’s three-quarter tangent to B or reject a tangent above one; the below-one restriction here concerns acute-angle sine and cosine.

## Keep geometric relations after rotation

Rotate the entire original diagram ninety degrees into a control, carrying labels, right-angle mark and lengths with it without renaming vertices. For A, identify the same three sides and recover the same ratios. A device orientation change alters the view, not source lengths. If cropping hides a letter, restore a readable view rather than assume A remains in its former screen corner. This check ties labels to geometric objects rather than the screen’s directions.

## Attach degrees or radians to angles

Use thirty degrees as a separate unit check: it equals pi over six radians, with sine one half in either representation. Before entering thirty externally, confirm degree mode; in radian mode enter pi over six instead. Bare thirty does not specify the unit, and a unit error is not automatically ebook formatting damage. A in the three-four-five triangle is not thirty degrees. Retain its edge-derived ratios, recording inverse-function and rounding conditions only if an approximate angle is needed.

## Check inverted ratios and premature rounding

A sine of five thirds calls for checking the longest side and acute sine range, correcting the fraction rather than clipping it to one. Confusing three quarters with four thirds suggests a changed reference. An angle-based mismatch suggests degree/radian mode. Keep fractions until the end instead of rounding point six to point seven before a squared check. Map different book labels without editing the source. Missing right-angle or length data means insufficient evidence, not a reason to invent measurements.

## Reopen and verify both sets of ratios

Leave and reopen the same page, checking right angle C, all edges and A before writing both sets of ratios and explaining rotation invariance. Retain conditions, side-name reasons, fractions, complementary relations and unit control, all traceable to the diagram rather than calculator decimals alone. Restart naming if another diagram changes the right angle or units. A bookmark can locate the page but does not verify mathematics. Completion requires an explainable side-angle relation and a readable original.
