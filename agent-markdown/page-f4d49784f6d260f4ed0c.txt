Flowers, fracture patterns or blackness suddenly appearing behind a character need not mean a new location. Choose a completed background transition in ManYue and separate visible cues, story setting and interpretation. This is an expression-reading task, not diagnosis from inferred emotion, and assumes no background recognition, emotion labels or automatic interpretation.

## Choose a change with checkable surrounding setting

Choose a previously read passage where a background changes abruptly and later returns to a familiar setting. Record edition, chapter, character, panels before and after, and a recognisable landmark. Inspect the whole page rather than cropping only the most exaggerated panel. Start with few characters and stable surroundings, excluding simultaneous dream, time-travel and multiple-strand questions. Resolve incomplete image loading first: a loading blank is not automatically artistic empty space. Include both sides of the change so the expressive-insertion hypothesis can be tested rather than relying on remembered scenery.

## Describe the shapes actually visible

List flowers, pointed rays, black masses or repeated geometry, noting position, area and contact with the character before writing happy, afraid or injured. Record red only if identifiable in this edition, without importing another edition’s palette. Check ground, shadows, physical occlusion and whether the pattern surrounds only the head. Missing shadows do not prove metaphor because simplified settings may omit them too. Keep observations recoverable and feelings in another field so emotion words do not filter out inconvenient shapes from the outset.

## Use continuous action to test a location change

Compare footing, held objects, the following line and landmarks such as doors or windows. In an invented example, a figure holds a cup beside a table before and after a flower-filled panel. That supports an expressive reading, but the cup alone cannot exclude every location change. Keep an actual-setting explanation if wording explicitly mentions entering a garden. Attach each clue to its panel and do not skip spatial checks because an expression is intense. A pattern can be a physical object in one scene and expressive exaggeration in another.

## Separate character-visible from reader-visible information

Check whether others look at the pattern, step on objects in it or respond to it. A reader-visible pattern need not be shared character perception, but no response alone does not establish nonexistence. Label an image accompanying inner monologue without turning thoughts into spoken words. Record who has access to each item rather than explaining behaviour through the reader’s extra knowledge. You can describe its effect on your reading atmosphere as experience; a claim about the character’s psychology still needs dialogue and action support.

## Find a counterexample within the work

Find one previously read page where a similar background appears in another context and test whether the same feeling always accompanies it. Flowers associated with embarrassment or an actual flower shop cannot support a fixed flowers-equal-happiness rule. Keep the counterexample and narrow your interpretation rather than deleting it for neatness. One useful contrast is sufficient; do not collect every pattern in the book or merge different creators’ conventions into a universal chart. Scope conclusions to this edition and checked passages.

## Write alternatives and distinguishing evidence

For the least clear panel, write actual environmental change and expressive background as alternatives, then identify distinguishing evidence such as an object persisting into the next panel or explicit wording. Leave it unresolved if unavailable. Do not quote your interpretation as source wording or claim authorial confirmation. “The black background felt oppressive in this reading” is a valid experience, not evidence of a medical condition. Keep one limitation beside the interpretation so later evidence prompts a recheck instead of certainty concealing insufficient material.

## Save three locations and actually return

Save supported page bookmarks or an external log for before, during and after the change, adding panel positions and landmarks. Leave the reader, then return to each to exclude another appearance of the same figure. Tall images need object/action cues rather than percentage alone. Attach chapter and whole-page references to cropped favourites, which cannot independently establish surrounding setting. After changing source or edition, recheck patterns, wording and cropping rather than assuming matching titles preserve image relationships and coordinates.

## Finish the expression–setting distinction

Finish with shapes, action continuity, a counterexample, current interpretation and unknowns. A black background need not mean a power cut, but the actual setting still requires checking; flowers need not always mean happiness. If only an effect on reader experience is established, stop there rather than inventing an environmental event. Explain the atmosphere in a few sentences with source locations. Later rereading should test particular clues, not apply permanent emotion labels to characters or patterns.
