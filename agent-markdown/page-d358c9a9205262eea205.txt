A caliper can easily become a ruler with two arbitrary hooks. This is not a measurement demonstration: draw an ordinary caliper with slightly separated jaws, organising its fixed end, slider and downward outside jaws around one beam. The Aquarell task is legible mechanical connection at small size. Ticks indicate surface direction, not a particular model’s vernier ratio, reading or calibration result.

## Fix beam direction and viewing angle

Use an approximately 1800×1200 px landscape canvas with pale blue-grey background. Let the beam rise gently from lower left to upper right, leaving room at its right end. Draw a long narrow front rectangle and thin upper face, then locate the left fixed end and a slider centre somewhat left of centre. Separate beam, fixed end, slider, jaws and shadow; use a silver-grey main face with cooler dark edges. Do not make a widening wedge; narrow the far end only slightly for perspective. If blade-like, soften the tip and restore a flat end. Pixel dimensions set the layout, not the instrument’s size or measuring range.

## Connect the fixed jaw to the fixed end

Extend an outside jaw downward from the beam’s left fixed end, establishing its root and then its inward contact edge. Keep root and beam connected without a background gap suggesting a separate attachment. The inner edge is approximately perpendicular to the beam’s length and will face the other jaw. Test this jaw as a flat grey silhouette with some thickness, not a wire hook. Correct a skewed contact edge against the beam baseline rather than hiding the junction in shadow. Simplify the fixed end’s shape without also depicting it as a second freely travelling slider.

## Wrap the slider around the beam

Draw a short, broader slider at the chosen centre, with the beam continuing beyond both sides. Its near face covers a short beam segment; its bottom follows the same incline rather than suddenly becoming horizontal. Upper edge and side thickness show it straddling the beam; omit the locking knob initially. Shorten it and restore the through-beam relation if it resembles a parallel ruler. Define its separate edges if the beam merely looks swollen. Continue hidden geometry in the sketch, not through-painted ticks. Slider position determines this illustration’s jaw opening: do not shift it during colouring while leaving its jaw behind.

## Make the outside jaws face one gap

Attach the second outside jaw to the slider’s lower part. Its left-facing inner edge opposes the fixed jaw’s right-facing one. Set the illustrative opening to roughly one tenth of the main image width. Perspective may slightly change tip heights, but the contact edges must not face different depths. Run a pale check line across the gap to test shared space. Repair roots and inner edges if scissor-like rather than adding screws. If accidentally touching, move the whole slider and its jaw rather than bending only the tip. This gap is an illustration check, not a millimetre reading.

## Separate upper small jaws from lower jaws

Add shorter upward inside-jaw parts only if clearly visible in the reference, one belonging to the fixed end and one to the slider. They are neither full-size copies of the lower hooks nor growths from the beam’s middle. Trace the upper silhouette in a tick-free draft and check both roots. If four tips crowd together at small size, choose a clearer angle or reduce upper-detail contrast without turning them into uncountable serrations. Omit the depth rod in this study rather than inventing an unexplained line for an unobserved component.

## Keep ticks on the front face and respect occlusion

Add sparse long and short ticks to the beam’s front only at the end, arranged along its length and turning with that face. Do not continue covered ticks onto the slider. Treat illustrative slider ticks separately, without inventing 0.02 markings or a current reading. Keep lines pale grey and sparse enough that beam direction survives reduction. Remove ticks if they overwhelm jaw relations rather than thickening everything. An accurate instrument diagram needs a complete scale from a specified model. This study’s omissions cannot be used for readings or for inferring an object’s actual size.

## Keep metal reflections from breaking connections

Use medium-light silver grey with a narrow upper highlight and cool lower dark face under one light direction. Darken the slider’s near edge modestly without boxing every side in black; a white gap must not sever the fixed jaw root. Let shadows beneath beam and jaws vary with height above the table instead of flattening the whole tool. Narrow highlights and restore solid midtone if the slider looks glass-like. Adjust background value locally if jaws disappear rather than brightening all ticks. Hide reflections to recheck connections, then restore the material.

## Finish with three still-life checks

Hide ticks and check that the fixed end stays fixed, the beam passes through the slider and the outside jaws face one gap; then restore restrained surface lines. Keep the layered native file and export a PNG. Open it and compare reduced roots and occlusion. Strengthen slider enclosure and the gap’s negative shape if only a ruler remains legible. If it suggests gripping an absent object, retain an open pose and remove misleading contact shadows. Record illustrative still life and omitted parts, not measurement approval. Success means traceable connections, not precision-instrument operating guidance.
