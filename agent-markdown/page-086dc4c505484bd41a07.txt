Paint shoreline trees and their reflections, checking vertical alignment before using broken horizontal strokes to describe ripples.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-lake-reflection/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A reflection is neither an arbitrary underwater tree nor a copy of every leaf. Establish vertical correspondence between shore trees and reflected masses, then break the water shapes with horizontal marks. Draw it by hand to understand what must align and what can be omitted.

## Set up a separate study

Use an approximately 1800×1200 px landscape canvas with the shore above centre and room for reflections. Separate sky, shore trees, reflections and ripples. Test broad vertical masses and short horizontal interruptions. Establish alignment with guides before any surface effect.

Mark both tree widths and trunk contacts, then extend corresponding guides below the shore. Check vertical alignment and connected quiet water. Save this paired structure before breaking detail, keeping ripples separate so reflection identity and surface disturbance can be judged independently.

## Establish the shore and water regions

Place the shore above the centre, leaving more room for water. Arrange unequal tree groups off symmetrical positions. Drop faint vertical guides from crown tops, widest points and trunk contacts to establish reflected alignment. These are temporary construction marks, not final visible lines.

Use related colours in sky and water, with the water slightly darker or greyer. Vary the shoreline, clarifying tree contacts and softening distant sections. Establish land, water and trees as broad masses before working inside the reflection, keeping both halves from becoming equally busy.

## Keep reflected positions without copying twigs

Paint inverted tree masses below the shore, aligning trunks with reflection origins. Correspond the highest crown with the farthest reflected extent without copying every tip. Use slightly greyer colour and preserve relative widths. Excessive sideways displacement makes a separate shrub group rather than a reflection.

Simplify reflected lengths consistently rather than stretching one tree and compressing another arbitrarily. Once the masses correspond, omit most branching and retain a few crown cues. Keep a clear difference between the detailed shore trees and the simpler water image.

## Cross vertical reflections with horizontal ripples

Break selected vertical shapes with short horizontal strokes near the water colour. Vary their widths and lengths instead of spanning the entire frame. Use finer, closer ripples near the shore and broader ones in front. Begin with only a few interruptions and check whether the surface starts to move.

Add two or three pale reflected lines within and around the dark masses. Retain enough connected area to identify the correspondence. Restore a few longer dark passages if everything becomes horizontal bars; add selected irregular gaps if it remains too mirror-like rather than blurring the entire image.

## Clarify the transition at the waterline

Use a small darker contact between tree roots and shore. Begin the reflection nearby without welding both halves together with pure black. Grass can interrupt selected origins. Upright land marks meeting horizontal water marks help distinguish the two regions at the same boundary.

Keep the far bank a low-contrast strip rather than adding another equally sharp tree row. Establish depth through ripple scale before colour gradients. If the lake reads as a vertical wall, check an overly low shore and perfect top-bottom symmetry, then leave broader horizontal quiet areas.

## Troubleshoot misalignment and excessive regularity

Check each tree’s reflected boundaries against the guides. Correct a misplaced mass directly rather than hiding it beneath more ripples. Vary the lengths and offsets of interruptions without moving the whole reflection. Judge the object’s identity separately from the disturbance of the water surface.

Mute an overly vivid reflected patch so the water colour still participates. If land and reflection look identical, remove underwater twigs and vary selected edges rather than fading the whole layer. Check that the quiet water areas connect the scene, using a few shared ripples where needed.

## Review and plan the next study

Check correspondence small, shoreline contact at normal size and accidental interruptions at reflection origins close up. Preserve mass identity rather than every leaf. Hide the palest ripples to assess simplification, then restore them for surface direction instead of relying on more white lines.

Keep reflection-only, lightly rippled and finished stages, checking the final PNG for crushed darks. Next, widen only the horizontal interruptions to compare calm and breeze. Preserve object identity; if correspondence disappears, reduce gaps rather than adding matching horizontal marks to the shore.
