Establish an uncurled low body and short legs before grouping spine strokes across the back, leaving face and underside as fur.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-hedgehog-dorsal-spines-furred-face-short-legs/>), translated on 2026-11-18. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Draw a slowly walking side-view hedgehog with muzzle left, rounded back and short near feet exposed. The problem is not adding many spikes, but distinguishing their region, the body’s turn and softer fur areas. Pose and stroke counts are illustrative, not a complete species diagnosis or actual spine census.

## Block an uncurled body and head first

On an approximately 1800×1300 px canvas block a low oval torso and tapering head to the left, with a projecting nose rather than a long tube. Raise the back arc while keeping the belly near the ground and short clear limb attachments. Separate sketch, body colour, dorsal spines and facial detail on pale warm-grey, then save native work. This pose is uncurled, so head and feet cannot all disappear into a ball. Reduce the view and establish head/body direction before adding spines.

## Outline the dorsal spine region with a soft boundary

Plan the spine region behind the head, across back, upper flanks and rear, marking a faint transition into face, neck and underside fur. Do not wrap the whole animal in an equal-width spike ring or insert long spines around eyes, nose and belly. Allow slight overlap without creating a rigid shell lid. Separate regions with related neutral colours rather than a pure-black seam. These are hedgehog exterior regions, not plant needles, separate armour or an automatically recognized App texture template.

## Establish spine-group directions before individual tips

Choose three demonstration groups: behind the shoulder, lean strokes backward and upward; at mid-back, turn them along the body’s curve; at the rear, lean them slightly toward the tail. Give each six to eight pale longer strokes before a few shorter ones; six to eight is a practice count. Root them on the body and vary near/far tips instead of creating a radial sun. Leave colour-plane transitions and shorten/subdue the far side. Spines are modified hairs, but this draws no complete set and does not prescribe the same orientation for every animal or defensive state.

## Wrap facial fur around small eye and nose

Use softer short strokes on the face. Make the nose a small rounded dark form and place a small eye above and behind the muzzle, not over half the face. Attach ears near the rear head with readable near-ear thickness, leaving a concealed far ear incomplete. Turn fur toward muzzle and neck rather than surround the nose with long sea-urchin-like spikes. A few accents distinguish moist nose and eye without severing the nose with a white ring. Chosen mask or nose colours do not establish unspecified species or age.

## Support the body with short connected legs

Attach short legs below shoulder and hip regions, showing the two near feet clearly and only pose-consistent far fragments. Add narrow ground-contact shadows and restrained toe/claw divisions, never feet sprouting from spine tips. Toe counts vary among hedgehogs; visible drawn lines are not a complete digit ledger, and three decorative divisions do not establish three toes. Retain underside fur and shadow so feet support the torso. If the belly conceals every leg, adjust the pose before stretching toes through the body.

## Interweave tonal planes and banded spine strokes

Block broad grey-brown and cream turning planes before alternating dark/light marks in selected spines, suggesting bands without identical zebra stripes on every one. Reduce black density in light and increase it selectively in shadow while retaining some base colour. Avoid a volume-free wire ball. Soften face and underside shadows relative to spine tips and keep one ground-shadow direction. The body should remain rounded with the spine layer hidden; repair colour planes first if it does not. Drawn tips imply neither projectile spines nor a defensive experiment.

## Troubleshoot spine roots, fur regions and foot roots

Hide tiny accents and check spine groups rooted dorsally, fur retained on face/underside and legs attached below. A sawtooth outline needs less uniform edging and clearer near/far groups. If spikes swallow the head, restore the transition instead of enlarging eyes. A floating foot requires its contact and shadow checked. Preserve the earlier version, fix one category and inspect enlarged and fit-to-window views. Keep this uncurled pose distinct from an unexplained superimposed defensive ball.

## Reopen for both silhouette and texture density

Save native work, hide region guides and reopen a PNG. At reduced size identify muzzle, rounded back and short feet; enlarge directional spine roots and fur areas free of long spines. If groups merge into a heavy border, reduce far-side contrast before deleting body volume. Reopen separately editable dorsal spines and facial fur, documenting illustrative stroke counts, visible feet and unspecified taxonomy. Leave unperformed export/reopening pending; this exterior exercise is not device observation, pet care or a complete species-identification report.
