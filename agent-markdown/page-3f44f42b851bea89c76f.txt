Paint a strawberry through its heart-shaped volume, red value changes and seed dimples, balancing highlights against repeated detail.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-strawberry-seeds/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A strawberry is remembered for its seeds, but it is first a solid fruit. Paint one with a leafy cap, establishing red volume and lighting before placing selected seeds on its curved surface. The detail should describe form instead of becoming flat polka dots.

## Set up a separate study

Use an approximately 1400×1600 px canvas with table space around the fruit and room above the cap. Separate background, red body, seeds and leaves. Test a broad modelling brush and a fine dimple brush. Leave seeds empty until the plain red volume works.

Check the widest point, offset tip and leafy cap as one fruit. Avoid stretching the body after resolving the cap. Save a seed-free block-in and compare during detail passes to see whether seeds improve volume rather than merely filling the surface.

## Build an uneven heart-shaped mass

Sketch a broad-topped, tapered heart with a shallow notch and a slightly offset tip. Keep the widest point in the upper half and vary the two sides. Mark the leafy cap overlap and paint a solid mid-red. Check the untextured silhouette before adding seeds.

Light from the upper left. Turn a dark red shaded side toward the middle and deepen the lower contact area. Use a broad soft red-orange light rather than a white patch. Establish clear value differences in the red, not merely changes in saturation.

## Overlap the pointed cap leaves

Spread five to seven pointed leaves from the cap centre with front and back overlap. Foreground leaves cover the fruit; rear leaves are partly hidden. Avoid an equal-length green star. Establish two main leaves, vary the others and join them with a small central dark.

Give each leaf a lit and shaded plane, adding only a few pale fold marks. Green contrasts strongly with red, so keep the cap restrained. If it resembles an oversized bow, shorten the longest leaves and ensure the roots gather on the fruit rather than floating outside it.

## Arrange the seeds in curved rows

Imagine four to six curved rows, wide in front and compressed at the sides. Stagger small oval seeds along them. Establish a central group first, then narrow the ovals at the edges and reduce them toward the tip. Equal-sized dots would flatten the surface.

Paint a tiny dark-red dimple, then a pale yellow-cream seed on one side, retaining a dark edge to show embedding. Keep the light direction consistent. Omit some shaded-side detail and clarify selected central and lit groups rather than treating every seed equally.

## Avoid a grid of white highlights

Use a few broken pale-pink reflections on the upper-left curve, with varied edge softness. They should follow the main lighting, breaking around selected dimples rather than forming an unrelated plastic sheet. Do not place a white dot beside every seed.

A metallic appearance often comes from white lights, black shadows and hard transitions. Restore red middle values and keep only tiny brightest accents. If the fruit looks dry, strengthen a local reflection instead of fading the entire red layer. Test highlight size on a small curved swatch first.

## Review the contact shadow and omitted detail

Add a small red-brown contact dark at the fruit tip and a softer shadow extending lower right. Follow the fruit’s broad mass without copying every leaf tip. Lose selected lit edges into the background and keep the shaded side clearer instead of adding a continuous dark outline.

If seeds merge at small size, remove crowded ones while preserving the row rhythm. If they resemble loose sesame seeds, check for missing dimples and inconsistent perspective. Compare a preview with half the seeds; fewer correctly oriented details often describe volume more clearly.

## Review and plan the next study

Preview at a small card size. Check red volume against the cap before selected central seeds. Repair accidental gaps close up without making every seed equally distinct. If some disappear while curved rows and moisture remain convincing, do not thicken them simply to preserve their number.

Export seeded and seed-free PNGs and retain editable body modelling. Next, move only the light to the right, reconsidering dimple shadows before highlights. Do not copy the old seed group unchanged: embedded seed shading must respond to the new light.
