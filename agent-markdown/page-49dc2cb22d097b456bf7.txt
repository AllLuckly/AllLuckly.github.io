Draw a resting walrus on pale ground, its head slightly raised toward front-left, near foreflipper spread and hindflippers partly visible at the rear. Two tusks, a thick muzzle and grouped stiff whiskers are focal details, but connect them correctly before using wrinkles as surface texture.

## Establish a resting pose with heavy body and smaller head

Create an approximately 2000×1500 px canvas, blocking a large oval torso and a smaller upper-left head joined through a thick short neck/shoulder region, tapering rearward down-right. Do not equalize head and body size or connect them with a thin neck. Separate sketch, body colour, tusks and whisker/flipper detail, then save native work. A pale ground plane leaves space for foreflipper and tusk tips. Keep a resting body supported rather than floating to display anatomy. Drawing proportions establish neither mass, age nor individual measurements.

## Extend both tusks downward from the upper jaw

Block a thick muzzle, then extend tusks downward from both upper-jaw sides, their roots partly concealed by the lip. Trace each continuously to its tip; neither grows from eye or chin, and neither needs an extra tooth for symmetry. In this oblique view the near tusk is clearer and the far one may be partly hidden while both remain traceable. They are enlarged upper canines, not a trunk or horns. Keep tips consistent with the reference instead of using impossibly fine white needles to obscure length or direction errors.

## Root stiff whiskers in muzzle pads, not tusks

Build connected soft muzzle pads around nose and mouth, using restrained nostril and mouth shadows. Group stiff whiskers outward from these pads: two near groups of three to five strokes and fewer far strokes are illustrative, not an anatomical count. They do not sprout from tusk middles or the eye. Establish a few longer directions before shorter strokes, stopping lines behind the near tusk. Retain pad colour instead of filling it with black threads that resemble a detached brush hanging in front of the head.

## Attach foreflippers near shoulders and hindflippers at the rear

Connect the near foreflipper at the shoulder, thick at its root and spreading into a bounded flat surface. Conceal most of the far foreflipper behind the body. Overlap rear flippers according to the pose rather than treat them as one fish tail with two extra legs beside it. All four limbs are flippers, but visible fragments do not determine total organ count. Preserve separate roots for near foreflipper and hindflippers, suggesting digits lightly instead of making disconnected leaves.

## Distinguish ear openings from external ear flaps

Where a clear reference supports it, add a tiny ear-opening mark behind the eye, not a projecting sea-lion-like flap. Keep eyes proportionate to the small head and separate brow contour from neck folds rather than converge wrinkles into another face. Check nose, eye and ear-opening order on the head side, then reduce the view to confirm tusks still belong to the muzzle. This exterior study does not identify every pinniped, and concealed far-side organs need not be forced into symmetry for display.

## Turn skin folds around neck, shoulder and underside

Build volume with grey-brown, warmer light and cool shadow. Choose a few incomplete neck folds, softening as they turn around the form rather than becoming rigid rope rings. Keep reflected light in the grounded underside and firmer contact at the foreflipper. Pale warm-grey and narrow highlights give tusks thickness without turning them into luminous tubes or merging muzzle pads into one white flower. Actual skin colour varies; invented lighting determines neither water temperature, health nor sex.

## Check tusk, whisker and flipper roots separately

Hide texture and highlights. Identify upper-jaw roots of both tusks, muzzle roots of whiskers, shoulder root of the near foreflipper and rear roots of hindflippers. Move a misplaced tusk root before merely bending its tip; correct whisker occlusion and remove an extra fish tail by revisiting the four-limb ledger. Preserve the faulty copy and fix one issue before reviewing the whole. Record concealed structures as hidden or omitted instead of adding an unassigned flipper to fill a count. A resting illustration supplies no gait analysis.

## Reopen and retain distinct tusk and flipper silhouettes

Save native work, hide check lines and reopen a PNG. At fit-to-window size read the small-head/heavy-body relationship and separate tusks; enlarge whisker, tusk and flipper-root occlusion. If tusks merge when reduced, restore a readable dark gap instead of adding a third. Reopen separately editable whisker lines and tusk colours, recording view, illustrative stroke counts and omissions. Leave unperformed export/reopening pending.
