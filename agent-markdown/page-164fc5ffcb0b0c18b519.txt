## Choose a single-tang buckle and viewing angle

Use a slight top view with the frame’s long edges nearly horizontal and the tail extending right. On paper, mark the diagram’s entry through frame, tang-through-hole and returning extension; this is a visual relationship, not dressing instructions. Separate frame, rear bar/tang, front/rear strap portions, holes and checking lines. The [Met collection search](<https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search?q=buckle>) shows varied buckle appearances; this invented rectangular single-tang design adopts none of their dates, materials or dimensions.

## Keep frame and rear bar in one plane

Make a four-sided rectangular frame with background visible inside, rather than a solid metal plaque. A thicker near edge and narrower far edge may suggest the chosen view, but corners must join. Put the rear bar on one side as the tang-root reference, not floating in the opening. Before adding leather, trace the frame and inspect both bar ends. If it looks like unrelated rectangles, check whether thickness edges are displaced consistently. Repair corners before shading. Foreshortening belongs to this view, not a precision-measured projection.

## Run the tang from one hinge toward the hole

Place the tang root at the middle of the rear bar, extending toward the opposite frame edge and the planned hole. Suggest its wrap or hinge at the bar rather than placing a pointed stick on the strap’s centre. Give it one clear direction and restrained perspective taper, without splitting midway. A simplified version needs root, shaft and tip rather than an invented mechanism. The tip is a static shape, with no bending or hole-punching demonstration. Hide highlights to check that exactly one tang remains identifiable.

## Fit strap width to the opening

First draw a hole-free strap through the opening, narrower than its short dimension with readable side gaps. Separate drawing segments for occlusion still represent one continuous strap; it cannot vanish behind the frame. Decide which part crosses in front of the near frame edge before ordering objects. If the strap hides the buckle, narrow it or reveal the near edge; if string-like, widen it while keeping directional continuity. Real waist measurements do not determine these pixels, and unsupported durability or load figures are omitted.

## Make the selected hole contain the tang

Put the selected hole where the tang passes, separating its edge from the strap surface. Let leather locally occlude the tang at the opening, then reveal the short emerging portion, making an intersection readable rather than a black dot beside silver. Arrange unused holes along the strap without an unexplained route around the frame. If reduction loses the hole, enlarge it as an explicitly pictorial exaggeration, not a fabrication dimension. Black fill is not automatically transparency; a background change should distinguish painted darkness from a true cutout. Leader labels can identify selected and unused holes, pointing to hole rims rather than the frame. Remove the leaders temporarily: the tang/hole relationship must remain visible, rather than depending entirely on explanatory text.

## Do not turn the returning tail into another strap

In the checking diagram, trace a dashed route from rear entry across the fixed region and back out to the right; the final view shows only appropriate front surfaces. The returning tail may overlap the underlying strap, but edges follow the same longitudinal direction without becoming two unrelated belts at the hole. An optional keeper is a small loop around the tail, not a second buckle, and must not hide the selected hole. A full wearing scene is unnecessary. Shortening the illustrated tail is allowed after continuity works, not as a way to hide unexplained joins.

## Hide the strap to check metal identities

Hide the strap and inspect the frame loop, rear bar and single tang independently; restore it to check intended occlusion. A lost frame edge may be covered by the wrong strap object; a floating tang root requires hinge repair, not merely moving the tip into the hole. Highlights stay on metal rather than spanning openings. Save and open the export to check selected hole, return direction and unused holes. Ordinary checking objects can export; hide them deliberately rather than assuming a reference-named layer is excluded.

## Accept four connections

Identify bar-to-frame, tang-root-to-bar, tang-through-selected-hole and tail-to-same-strap connections. Repair the relevant structure when one is unclear, rather than thickening everything. A monochrome copy must preserve openings and front/back relations without colour alone explaining insertion. Keep labelled checking and unlabelled delivery versions; demonstrate errors only in copies.
