## Fix a nondegenerate triangle and three side points

Take A=(0,6), B=(0,0), C=(8,0) in mathematical coordinates; they are noncollinear. Put D inside BC, E inside CA and F inside AB, then connect AD, BE and CF without moving names to other sides. On about 1200 × 1100 pixels use u=180+100x, v=900−100y, with equal scales and reversed screen y. Group triangle, division points, cevians, ratios and control separately, then save. ## Place D at two to one rather than the midpoint

Set BD/DC=2 on the base of length eight, giving BD=16/3, DC=8/3 and D=(16/3,0). Take E=(4,3), the midpoint of CA, so CE/EA=1. Set F=(0,4): AF=2 and FB=4, hence AF/FB=1/2; F’s y-coordinate four is not AF. List numerators and denominators externally before plotting points and leaders. D’s screen x is about 713.333. If precise entry is unavailable, label placement approximate and retain fractions rather than calling a small drag error a theorem failure.

## Start each cevian at the opposite vertex

Draw unfilled AD, BE and CF, checking all six endpoints rather than drawing the small triangle DE–EF–FD. Use distinct line styles and one P marker, not three slightly displaced dots. Under these interior conditions Ceva states concurrency if and only if (BD/DC)(CE/EA)(AF/FB)=1; here 2×1×1/2=1. Cyclic reordering is harmless but inverting only one ratio is not. Name segment identities in text instead of relying on three colours.

## Solve two lines independently and test the third

AD has y=6−9x/8 and BE has y=3x/4, giving P=(16/5,12/5). CF has y=4−x/2, which also gives 12/5 at x=16/5. Use screen P=(500,660) as a plotting check instead of shifting P to accommodate faulty lines. These are not all medians, so do not label P the centroid: this triangle’s centroid is (8/3,2), a different point. Retain equations in a note box; the visible crossing approximates the calculation. As an independent area check, let X=\[ABP\], Y=\[BCP\], Z=\[CAP\]. Here X=48/5, Y=48/5, Z=24/5, summing to the triangle area 24. Shared heights or ratios along a common vertex line give side ratios X/Z, Y/X, Z/Y, whose product cancels to one. Lightly fill the three checking regions, touching only at boundaries, and keep equal-valued X/Y as distinct regions. This explains the product under the concurrent configuration; do not transfer the same area correspondence to control CF1 missing P. Areas have squared units; their ratios are dimensionless, and 48/5 is not an edge length. If pale regions overlap, check for fills crossing an edge rather than interpreting darker overlap as greater area. Hide these checking fills in the final diagram while retaining the fractional ledger.

## Move only F for a nonconcurrent control

Save a control moving only F to F1=(0,3), keeping A, B, C, D and E. Now AF1/F1B=1 and the product is two. AD and BE still meet at P, but CF1 is y=3−3x/8, giving 9/5 at x=16/5 rather than P’s 12/5. Thus the third line misses P. Update the old one-half label to one without changing the triangle or stroke widths. Two intersecting lines do not establish three-line concurrency; test the third.

## Separate ratio notes from division marks

## Diagnose side ownership and denominators first

For a product other than one, check that AF measures from A rather than B. For separated crossings, revisit the mathematical P and screen-y subtraction. If D leaves the base, check the fraction was not misread as sixteen divided by zero. If F1 remains at old F, move it by coordinates and reconnect CF1; ordinary paths do not maintain geometric dependencies automatically. Keep the faulty copy and change one item at a time. Restoring the baseline requires F=(0,4) and product one, not just its colour. Exterior points require a separately defined signed-ratio convention.
