## Identify existing hardware and the current session

Use only already-owned EarPods compatible with the current iPhone, requiring no purchase or adapter change. Identify the wired centre and volume buttons rather than substitute another brand or wireless AirPods. Establish owned audio in 调调 and its audible session at comfortable level. Without hardware, complete the app-button round and leave remote testing pending; documentation is not device evidence. Record original output/level and avoid active calls or competing audio that would change the command target.

## Keep two sources and a repeatable baseline

Prepare readable recordings opening 线甲/线乙 with real titles, sources and durations. Establish A current/B actually next through confirmed queue controls and identify the mode; library adjacency is insufficient. Rebuild the same route each round and inspect intervening old entries, since reselecting A may alter the queue. Retain sources without retagging/reimporting. Without a confirmed next item, stop rather than double-press an unknown queue and call any destination expected B.

## Make one app Next action the control round

While A plays, press the actual app Next control once, recording pre-action identity/approximate position and subsequent B title, opening sound and state. This validates only the app route. Preserve an unexpected destination and resolve queue/mode before remote trials. Rebuild A current/B next afterward, recording rebuilding separately. Avoid repeated jumps merely to force identical pre-action seconds; a truthful origin/action source matters more than pretend synchronization.

## Use Apple’s quick double press without holding

Apple documents two quick centre-button presses for forward skipping, one for pause/resume and a double press followed by holding for fast-forward. Use two short presses and release, without volume actions/holds or applying call-mode meanings. Record uncertainty in the gesture rather than infer correct pressing from the destination. The documented hardware action establishes an expectation, not identical responses in every app/source. Check title, sound and position afterward; a feedback tone is not B playback.

## Check the destination before a controlled repeat

After double pressing, make no further gesture before recording track, B opening, playhead and pause state. Preserve A pausing/fast-forwarding or other outcomes instead of pressing until B and calling the sequence one successful double press. For a repeat rebuild the same A/B arrangement as a new round with fixed foreground/mode/level. Agreement supports this session only; differences require gesture/session checks without deleting an unfavorable result. Use approximate positions when necessary, claiming no end-to-end latency measurement.

## Separate action source, session target and audio output

App button/remote are action sources, the current media session is the target, and EarPods sound is output. Audible headphones do not prove correct double-press handling; a B title alone does not prove correct sound. With other retained media cards, identify the actual playing source rather than infer target from an icon. A call/other audio changes conditions and needs a new round. Lock-screen testing is unnecessary for this foreground comparison and cannot be retrospectively claimed.

## Diagnose hardware, gesture and queue without unrelated changes

For no response inspect connection/current sound, centre button and two short presses before session/queue. A pause suggests checking one recognized press; fast-forward suggests a hold, but these are hypotheses, not internal diagnostics. Recreate one condition instead of changing apps, clearing libraries, permissions and reinstalling together. An incompatible accessory needs a stopping point, not substituted AirPods gestures or another brand’s remote. Verify audio separately through the app route; A remote-control failure does not establish an audio-decoding fault or failure of all background playback.
