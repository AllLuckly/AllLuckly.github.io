When 调调 plays your own music on an iPhone, Apple Watch’s system Now Playing may offer remote playback controls. This exercise separates the phone’s playback session, the device selected on the watch and the device actually producing sound. Apple describes system media controls; whether 调调 appears and responds with a particular phone, watch and OS combination must be observed. This does not establish a standalone watch app or a copy of the audio on the watch.

## Prepare devices and an identifiable track

You need an iPhone with 调调 installed and an Apple Watch already paired and functioning according to Apple’s guidance. Check that the watch and current OS support the relevant system media controls. If you have no watch or it is not paired, mark the watch observation pending; a phone-only or car observation is not a watch pass. Choose a small recording you own or may use that is already imported on the phone. Record its title, duration and a recognizable audio cue. Preserve the original and do not use someone else’s private playlist.

Choose a quiet, safe setting, not driving, exercising or a situation requiring attention to surrounding sounds. Use the phone speaker or known connected headphones at a comfortable volume, recording the original output and volume. There is no need to connect a new watch speaker or enable a setting that is unavailable on the device.

## Establish the iPhone playback baseline

Find the recording in 调调’s local library, play briefly and confirm the displayed item and progress match the audible cue. Pause and resume once using the phone controls, recording display and sound separately. If this baseline is not working, investigate the selected item, output or file before attributing silence to the watch.

Record the time, phone playback state and current audio output. If an iPad, HomePod or another audio app is playing, there is no need to delete or reset it, but the watch may show multiple targets. Do not interrupt another person’s device for the exercise. Stop if the controlled device cannot be identified.

## Select the actual playback device on the watch

Open the system Now Playing app on Apple Watch. Apple says it can control media on other Apple devices. If multiple devices are available, select the iPhone playing 调调 using the actual interface. Entry points can vary by system: if you cannot find the target, record what is visible instead of inferring selection from icon color. Compare the displayed title or other available information with the phone and audible cue.

If 调调 or the title is absent, delayed, or represented only by a phone icon, record those as distinct observations. A displayed title does not imply an audio copy on the watch. If no phone selection is offered or the current session is unclear, return to the phone to confirm playback and check pairing and connection; mark the exercise incomplete if still uncertain, rather than promising compatibility.

## Pause on the watch and check both results

When the phone is playing and the watch target is identified, use the pause control actually shown on the watch. Observe separately whether 调调 shows a paused state and whether sound stops; note the playback position. A changed watch button alone is not a successful pause, nor is muting another device. If the phone shows pause but sound continues from elsewhere, distinguish the selected output and other sessions.

If one action has no clear feedback, wait for the interface to update and recheck control and target instead of tapping repeatedly and losing the final state. Before a retry, return the phone to a known playing state; preserve both observations. If no pause control appears or it does not work, record that without declaring the app or watch damaged.

## Resume and distinguish volume

Use the visible play control in the same watch session to resume. Observe the phone state and prior audio output; record success only after hearing the target recording continue. If it resumes elsewhere, note the actual position rather than assuming millisecond precision. A working phone with no watch control differs from a visible watch control to which the phone does not respond.

Apple documents system volume adjustment on the watch, but this exercise does not require turning the Digital Crown. If you choose to test it, first identify the controlled device and output, make a small adjustment and restore the original volume. Pause, lowering volume and changing output are different actions: zero volume does not prove playback paused.

## Compare visible state on both devices

While the phone remains the target, record its track title, visible watch information, play or pause state and output. Fewer details on the watch do not prove the file’s tags disappeared. If the phone changes tracks, identify the new target rather than using the old recording’s sound to verify the new title. You may separately compare two authorized short recordings, changing one condition at a time and preserving the original queue; the basic exercise needs only one.

The watch interface, connection and scope of control depend on device and OS. Controlling one phone track does not prove it was transferred to the watch, works without the phone or applies equally to all third-party apps. Without actual pairing and operation do not mark a pass or claim a native watchOS library exists.

## Troubleshoot and restore

If the watch cannot find the session, confirm that 调调 is playing on the phone, that the watch is paired and connected to the right iPhone as the system indicates, and that a different device is not selected. If the target is visible but control fails, record the button, time, phone state and audible feedback. You may safely reopen system Now Playing and retry, retaining an incomplete result if it still fails. Do not begin by unpairing, reinstalling 调调 or clearing the library. Give support redacted versions and steps, not a private playlist.

Record the phone baseline, watch target, displayed and audible results for pause and resume, original output and remaining unknowns. Restore the original volume, output and playback arrangement. This guide uses public information and does not claim a real phone, watch or headphone test.
