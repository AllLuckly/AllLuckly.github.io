Use a permitted reference to draw a hermit crab in a borrowed shell, checking the opening and visible appendages.

## Choose a reference and understand the shell

Choose a permitted side-view photograph of a hermit crab and record creator, viewpoint and access date. The Natural History Museum explains that a hermit crab protects its soft abdomen in an empty snail shell; the borrowed shell is not its own carapace. Shell shape, claw size and leg colors vary across species and individuals. Follow visible shapes without inventing the abdomen hidden inside.

## Create canvas and layers

Create a landscape Aquarell canvas, for example 1800 by 1400 pixels, and save an editable original. Use visible controls to separate background, ground, shell, crab body and appendages, and sketch. Leave space above the shell and ahead of the claws so shell tip and legs do not touch the canvas edge. Dimensions are suggestions; use the controls present in the installed version.

## Block shell and crab proportions

Block the shell’s outer shape first, then place the visible body, eye stalks and claws near its opening. Compare shell height and width with the length of the exposed crab. The shell contributes to the whole object’s weight, not a floating decoration on its back. At thumbnail size check that opening and crab face the same direction. If they separate, revise construction before adding texture.

## Locate spiral and opening

Observe the shell tip, main whorls and opening in the photo and lightly indicate the visible spiral direction. The opening is where the crab emerges and should face the crab, not point away merely to display a spiral. Suggest shell thickness with a short inner and outer rim near the opening. Stop the rim where the crab obscures it instead of drawing through the body.

## Draw eye stalks and exposed body

Block the exposed front body near the shell opening and place eye stalks and eyes according to the photograph. Compare their directions and heights; in profile the farther eye may be occluded, so do not force identical sizes. Stalks should emerge from the visible body rather than being planted independently on the shell. Simplify or omit unclear antennae instead of inventing symmetry.

## Arrange both claws

Observe the position, direction and relative size of near and far claws. Draw an arm segment connected to the body before the palm and visible pincers; the nearer claw may hide the farther one or part of the head. Do not assume all hermit crabs have equal claws or that the same side is always larger. Hide color to inspect joins; if a claw floats like a glove, repair joints and overlaps before adding highlights.

## Draw visible walking legs and ground contact

Mark directions of visible walking legs from near the body and connect segments to the ground. Near legs may cover far legs, and the shell opening may hide rear portions. When the count is unclear, draw only what can be seen rather than forcing legs through the shell. Place contact points consistently on sand or rock. If the crab floats, check the leg tips against the ground first.

## Block shell and crab colors

Use the reference for broad shell and crab colors, first separating the inner opening, outer shell and foreground claws by value. Shell bands should curve with whorls rather than run straight across all surfaces; claw and leg shadows should share the shell’s light direction. Water, sand and lighting affect apparent color, so do not treat this palette as fixed for all hermit crabs.

## Add ground and a simple background

Use low-contrast sand or rock beneath the shell and legs while keeping the subject outline readable. Contact shadows may sit near the shell base and leg tips, but should not form a black ring around the animal. Follow the reference or a stated light source. Keep background detail limited and avoid aligning a background line exactly with an eye stalk, which could make the eye look attached to a rock.

## Check front-to-back overlaps

Trace the opening to the body and identify which visible claw, rim or leg lies in front; leave ambiguous positions unresolved. Hide texture and background and check that shell tip, opening and claws remain distinguishable from broad shapes alone. Restore detail. If leg lines form a knot, compare them individually with the reference instead of thickening every line.

## Troubleshoot, save and review

If the shell looks pasted onto the back, check that the crab emerges from its opening and that rim and body overlap correctly. If claws float, inspect arm-to-body joins. Save the editable original and use an available share or export control to create a separate PNG or JPEG. Reopen it to inspect shell tip, eye stalks and leg tips for clipping; return to confirm editable layers and retain permission details and unperformed device steps.

## Check shell curvature and contact

Trace visible whorls from shell tip to the main body and check that bands and contours follow the same curved surface. If each whorl resembles a pasted circle, correct the large silhouette before adding texture. Observe the shell’s distance from the ground: if legs lift the base, do not draw the whole shell length touching sand; if part contacts sand, place a soft concentrated shadow there. Hide sand texture and confirm claws and leg tips remain readable. Restore the ground, compare the opening with the body and record any hidden rear legs. Recheck permission before sharing; a museum’s written description does not automatically license someone else’s photograph.
