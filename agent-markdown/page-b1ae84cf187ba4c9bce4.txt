## Fix unique assignment and the five-box universe

## State the quantified guarantee

Every allowed assignment has at least one box containing at least 4. It does not give 4 in every box or identify the box in advance. 17/5=3.4 and integer occupancy gives ceiling 4, not rounding to 3. Mark the existence quantifier and lower bound separately. Retain the whole sentence; an isolated 4 cannot reveal whether it meant box count, possible maximum or guaranteed minimum.

## Prove it by the at-most-three contradiction

If every box held at most 3, the total would be at most 5×3=15, contradicting 17. Hence one exceeds 3 and contains at least 4. The negation concerns every box being at most 3, not merely one. Fifteen is a hypothetical cap, not two actually lost cards. Keep per-box and total caps separate. The extra two do not identify a particular box; the proof establishes existence rather than an assignment list.

```text
17 objects -> 5 boxes; each object assigned exactly once
if every box <=3: total <=5*3=15 <17, contradiction
therefore some box >=4
ceiling(17/5)=4
```

## Construct a tight example ruling out a guaranteed five

Loads 4, 4, 3, 3, 3 total 17 with maximum 4, so no guarantee of 5 is possible. Four is the best universal bound. Assign mathematical indices n=0..16 by remainder modulo 5 to get G0/G1 with 4 and the rest 3. This constructs one tight example, not a required allocation for every case. Explicitly map Q01 to n=0 so printed card numbering does not shift the ledger.

```text
Q01..Q17 correspond to n=0..16
G0: n=0,5,10,15       count4
G1: n=1,6,11,16       count4
G2: n=2,7,12          count3
G3: n=3,8,13          count3
G4: n=4,9,14          count3
total=17; maximum=4
```

## Use an extreme assignment to reject an averaging claim

Loads 17, 0, 0, 0, 0 also satisfy unique assignment and the guarantee while four boxes lack 4. The theorem neither recommends balancing nor forces the maximum to equal 4. A lower bound allows 17 and is not an upper bound. Average 3.4 does not specify actual integer loads. Equally spaced icons in the reader or diagram cannot establish equal quantities.

## Distinguish guarantee from possibility after changing totals

At 15, loads 3, 3, 3, 3, 3 disprove a guaranteed 4, but 5, 3, 3, 2, 2 show a load above 4 remains possible. At 16, the at-most-three cap 15 is exceeded, so 4 is already guaranteed. Not guaranteed does not mean impossible. For five boxes and a positive integer r, exceeding 5(r−1) guarantees some load at least r under the unique-assignment conditions. Changed box counts or membership rules need a new threshold.

With 17 objects but 4 boxes, the guarantee is 5, tight at 5, 4, 4, 4. With 6 boxes it is 3, tight at 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2. Verify sum 17 and each maximum; the six-box example disproves silently carrying over the five-box guarantee 4.
