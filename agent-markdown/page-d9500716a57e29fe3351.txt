Faster and higher are different statements, and equal durations can have different pitches. Distinguish external speed-plus-pitch processing from shortening with pitch preservation, then record iCouldPlay conditions separately. Use an ideal pure-tone model without inferring exact voice frequencies or promising one pitch-preservation algorithm across every playback path.

## 1. Fix the original material and performed scope

Choose an owned playable source and record file/track/duration/provenance. Prepare copies externally only with available audio/editing tools, retaining originals. Without tools, complete paper comparison and existing-material observation without claiming new files were created. iCouldPlay supplies playback and available speed controls, not assumed tone generation/editing/muxing. Fix output/volume and compare matching anchors rather than different sentences.

## 2. Identify all three model versions

Set a paper baseline of eight seconds and ideal 440 Hz tone. A halves the time axis while increasing frequency; B shortens duration while preserving pitch. Label processes rather than calling both simply “fast.” Hertz, seconds and dimensionless speed multiplier are different quantities. These are model targets, not device measurements or a claim that iCouldPlay exposes all three values.

```
Original model: duration=8 s; frequency=440 Hz
A speed-and-pitch model: speed factor=2; duration=4 s; frequency=880 Hz
B pitch-preserving model: tempo factor=2; duration=4 s; frequency=440 Hz
Playback of exported model copies: nominal speed=1x
```

## 3. Calculate the speed-and-pitch model

Following [Audacity Change Speed and Pitch](<https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/change_speed.html>), the two-times model gives 8/2=4 seconds and 440×2=880 Hz, one octave higher for the ideal tone. Higher frequency is not doubled volume or changed words. Keep both calculations. A sharper impression alone is not a measured 880 Hz; precise real export claims need identified files and measurements.

## 4. Shorten the interval while preserving pitch

[Audacity Change Tempo](<https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/change_tempo.html>) targets duration change without pitch change. B remains 440 Hz for four seconds on paper. Equal duration does not make A/B identical because their constraints differ. Real pitch-preserving processing may have artifacts, so do not promise lossless quality. Twice the tempo corresponds to a hundred-percent increase, not a two-percent increase or a confused final multiplier.

## 5. Prepare external copies and play them at nominal speed

With available tools, export A/B independently from the original through their actual processing controls, recording settings/results. If video is needed, use an existing editing workflow with the same owned identifiable visual and verify the export; renaming extensions is insufficient. Play exported copies in iCouldPlay at nominal 1x first, avoiding another 2x stage. Missing tools mean paper-only status, not an invented export. Keep files/settings identifiable.

## 6. Observe in-player speed in a separate round

Return to the original and use actual installed speed controls, recording options, selected multiplier, elapsed-time and pitch impressions between matching anchors. Record real available values rather than inventing a selected 2x option. Observe preservation per path; impressions cannot certify an exact retained 440 Hz. File-duration display and elapsed wall time are different fields, so do not rewrite source duration to match a four-second expectation.

## 7. Troubleshoot double processing and mismatched objects

A four-second copy ending near two seconds calls for checking an added 2x playback stage. Matching A/B pitches call for verifying file selection, processing mode and output changes before declaring export failure. Complex speech without a tone reference cannot establish exact frequency doubling. Preserve similar impressions honestly, change one factor and restore prior controls. Do not clear history to hide wrong selections or combine devices into one supposed complete test.

## 8. Reopen the original and retain separate conclusions

Leave playback and reopen the original, restoring nominal 1x/prior volume and checking the same passage. Keep model calculations, performed external processing, file identities and app observations separate, marking missing exports/measurements. Shorter duration does not necessarily mean higher pitch, and a preservation goal does not prove artifact-free algorithms. Preserve originals and create fresh path/version records so another reader can reproduce the comparison rather than infer all parameters from “faster.”
