A composite function value of eight is not its derivative. Prepare inner/outer cards, track the differentiated variable, evaluation point and multiplication in Book Isle, then check by expansion. The App supplies reading positions and manual notes; calculations are not performed by a built-in differentiator.

## Fix both rules and the differentiation point

Let g(x)=x²+1, f(u)=u³ and F(x)=f(g(x))=(x²+1)³, differentiable on their real domains, with target x=1. Put value/rate columns on P1, expansion on P2 and controls on P3; export a clear PDF and retain editable work. Check that the square applies only to x while the cube applies to the whole bracket, and bookmark P1. Highlight layers only if text controls permit; otherwise record the whole expression and card ID externally without claiming App function recognition.

## Compute values without confusing them with slopes

At x=1, g(1)=2 and f(2)=8, so F(1)=8. In a separate column write g′(x)=2x and f′(u)=3u², each differentiated with respect to its own input. Retain u/x identities. First state the value chain 1→2→8; its completion does not supply the derivative, which requires rates rather than repeating the same substitutions.

## Evaluate the outer derivative at two

The chain rule is F′(x)=f′(g(x))g′(x). Here f′(2)=12 and g′(1)=2 multiply to 24. Evaluating the outer derivative at the original x=1 instead gives the wrong 3×2=6; stopping at 12 omits the inner rate. Label each error. The operation multiplies rates rather than adding 12+2=14, because the inner output is the outer input.

```text
g(x)=x^2+1; f(u)=u^3; F(x)=f(g(x))
x=1: g=2; F=8; f_prime(g)=12; g_prime=2
F_prime(x)=3*(x^2+1)^2*(2*x)=6*x*(x^2+1)^2
F_prime(1)=24; F_prime(0)=0; F_prime(-1)=-24
wrong evaluation f_prime(1)*g_prime(1)=6
wrong missing inner derivative=12
```

## Compare a general formula with independent expansion

The rule gives 3(x²+1)²·2x=6x(x²+1)². Independently expand F=x⁶+3x⁴+3x²+1 and differentiate termwise to 6x⁵+12x³+6x, which factors identically. At one this is 6+12+6=24. The 3x⁴ term comes from binomial coefficients: writing (x²+1)³ as only x⁶+1 drops the cross terms. Do not check a silently changed function such as (x+1)². Both calculations must use the same original expression.

At a second point x=2, g(2)=5, the outer slope is 75 and the inner slope is 4, giving 300. The expanded derivative gives 192+96+12=300. The outer slope 75 is not F(2)=125. If a card still reports 24, check whether its target point remained one rather than reuse the first answer.

## Control zero inner slope and a negative input

At x=0, g=1, the outer slope is 3 and the inner slope zero, so F′(0)=0 while F(0)=1. This does not make F identically zero. At x=−1 the inner value and outer slope remain 2 and 12, but the inner slope is −2, giving −24. Equal function values eight at ±1 do not imply equal derivatives. Preserve the full chain and distinguish negative slope from negative value.

Since F is even, its values at ±1 agree while this derivative has opposite signs there. This polynomial symmetry does not imply opposite slopes at every pair of equal-valued points of any function. At zero the inner value is nonzero; the composite slope vanishes because the inner slope vanishes, not because the function value disappears. Keep these causal statements separate to diagnose conflated zeros.

## A failed rule premise need not make the composite nondifferentiable

A common sufficient condition is g differentiable at the input and f differentiable at its inner output. For a separate g(x)=|x|, f(u)=u² control at zero, g is not differentiable; “zero times an undefined derivative” does not apply the rule. Yet the composite equals x² and has derivative zero. Missing a sufficient hypothesis calls for another argument, not an unconditional nondifferentiability verdict. Keep this control separate from the polynomial task.

## Diagnose object, evaluation point and operation

Eight suggests using the value, six the wrong outer input, twelve a missing inner factor, and fourteen addition. Enlarge primes/brackets and preserve f′(g(1)) as a whole when selection is available. Correct one row, then check expansion. An unsearchable scan does not erase the rule; return by P1. Pixel slopes and reader zoom cannot replace exact symbolic calculation or supply missing hypotheses.

Finally check exponent scope: the square in three times the squared bracket covers all of x²+1, not merely the one. At x=1 the correct outer factor is 12; the erroneous 3(x²+1²) is 6 before multiplying the inner rate. Restore brackets before multiplication; a final answer alone cannot validate every intermediate expression.

## Reopen and recover twenty-four from two rates

At reopened P1 hide answers and restore g(1)=2, f′(2)=12 and g′(1)=2, then multiply to 24. Use expansion and the zero-point control to distinguish value eight from slope and a failed premise from nondifferentiability.
