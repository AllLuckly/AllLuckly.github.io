An RTF can expose every word yet lose styling that distinguishes terminology, negation or revisions. Inspect a short style-dependent passage in Book Isle to judge suitability for the task. Published RTF support does not establish faithful styling. Extracted wording, source formatting and reader-added highlights are three different information layers.

## Choose a passage where styling matters

Choose an authorised RTF with at least two meaningful styles, such as italic terms, bold emphasis or struck revisions. Verify the source in a trusted rich-text tool and record each span’s first and last words and function. Do not assume unexplained colour means authorial emphasis. Start without complex tables or attachments, isolating characters and style spans. Preserve the original and import a copy rather than clearing formatting before establishing the baseline.

## Verify wording and paragraph identity first

Check ordinary characters, negation, punctuation and paragraph order against the source before judging styling. A same-title plain-text derivative may have already lost it. Match a specific opening phrase where internal metadata differs from filenames. A searchable phrase establishes indexed characters, not preserved formatting. Evaluate wording completeness and distinguishable emphasis separately so success in one does not conceal failure in the other.

## Check the exact extent of each styled span

For each source span, check first and last words and neighbouring unstyled text. “Some bold is visible” is insufficient because an extra or missing word can change scope. In an invented “only local reading is permitted,” emphasising the locality differs from emphasising that permission is exclusive; preserve the actual scope. If everything appears as uniform body text, record styling as indistinguishable on this path rather than assuming theme or font-size changes restore it. Screenshots help observation but need source identity.

## Check meaning-bearing special styling

Superscripts may encode exponents, note numbers or citations, and strikeouts may mark rejected wording. Check both surviving characters and whether lost styling changes their identity. Struck text displayed without its strike could be misread as the final claim. Verify externally and note the source style. Do not guess additions or deletions, particularly in numbers or formulas; use a reliable alternative view when the current path is unsuitable.

## Separate source styling from your highlights

Label any reader-added highlight or note as your checking aid, not restored authorial bold or italics. A highlight may cover a larger phrase and cannot necessarily represent strikeout or superscript, so it is not a lossless repair. Record source spans rather than retaining only colour. Use an external checklist if this format lacks an annotation control. In shared quotations, identify whether emphasis belongs to the source or was added by you.

## Choose a reading copy suitable for the task

Uniform text may suffice for casual reading, while terminology, revisions and formulas need dependable styling. Make an authorised PDF or other suitable derivative with a familiar external tool and recheck every selected span and character. Conversion cannot create formatting absent from the source, and renaming an extension is not conversion. Label edition and process and retain the RTF. Remap old highlights and bookmarks through phrases after positions change rather than assuming inheritance.

## Verify the attribution of emphasis in quotations

Save one necessary short quotation and check wording, scope and emphasis origin against the source. If a card or export omits styling, state in accompanying prose which words were italicised or struck rather than assuming fidelity across outputs. Do not quote paraphrases as source text or embed an emphasis note as though authored in the passage. Revisit the paragraph and verify edition identity. Attach references to interpretive conclusions so an attractive card does not misrepresent the source.

## Acceptance and common style confusions

Finish with text identity, two source spans, special-character meaning, annotation origin and a chosen reading path. Searchability does not establish style relations, and personal highlighting does not restore source formatting. A naturally slanted font is not evidence of original italic spans. Report a minimal authorised sample and viewing conditions rather than complete private material. Where meaning depends on invisible formatting, stop guessing and use a dependable copy, stating the specific limit without declaring all RTF unusable.
