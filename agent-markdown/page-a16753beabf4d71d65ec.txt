An interview question can carry a premise while the answer addresses only part of it. Select a complete exchange in Book Isle and separately record the interviewer’s framing, the respondent’s actual statement and editorial connecting text. This is neither automatic viewpoint extraction nor position classification. Preserve quotation boundaries instead of extending one answer into acceptance of the entire question.

## Establish the edition and editing statement

Choose an interview collection you are entitled to read and record title, respondent, interviewer, translator and edition. Inspect the entry’s date and editing statement. Interview, transcription/editing and publication dates may differ; retain stated labels without inventing dates. Note abridgement, thematic reordering or translation rather than treating polished print as a live verbatim transcript. Start with one manageable exchange, not half a sentence representing a person’s entire position. Keep the file and entry title so later edition differences remain traceable beyond a screenshot heading.

## Separate the complete question’s premise from its request

Read the full question and preceding paragraph, identifying what the interviewer presupposes and what response is requested. A question may evaluate a work before asking about process; that evaluation remains the interviewer’s, not the respondent’s autobiography. Retain only necessary short original wording and separate paraphrase. Do not convert a tentative question into a fact. Number multiple subquestions to support answer-scope checks. If speaker markers are unclear, consult formatting/editorial explanation and retain uncertainty rather than inferring an interviewer’s identity from tone.

## Break the answer into what it actually addresses

Read the whole answer and segment it by function, such as accepting a condition, adding background, giving an example or redirecting. Keep short locators. An initial yes does not establish acceptance of every premise; qualifications or corrections may follow. An unanswered subquestion is unaddressed here, not evidence of a private attitude. Resolve pronouns in context: this or they need not refer to the nearest noun in the question. Establish scope before a one-sentence summary so brevity cannot erase conditions, time or subject.

## Preserve editorial bridges separately from spoken statements

For parentheses, background paragraphs or ellipses between exchanges, consult formatting and collection-wide notes to classify editorial text, respondent speech or unresolved attribution. An ellipsis may indicate hesitation or editing; do not always read it as reluctance. Translator additions are not automatically words spoken at the interview. Retain markers and do not claim verbatim verification without a complete recording. An external log may track source, position and text identity while app notes retain short clues; no specialised interview annotation or speaker recognition is needed.

## Check your summary against question-to-answer mappings

Map subquestions A/B to answering passages and remaining gaps, then review your summary. Claiming the respondent accepted an evaluation requires their actual acceptance, not the interviewer’s wording. Preserve corrections of the question’s premise rather than cutting them for smoother flow. Describe emphasis in this exchange instead of a person’s permanent belief. Other interviews remain separate sources. A later comparison needs distinct entries with dates and editions rather than splicing occasions into false agreement.

## Recheck negatives and exceptions after shortening quotations

Select a complete meaning unit when shortening, retaining not, only when, at the time and for example qualifiers. Mark your omissions clearly rather than making statements from different questions appear one continuous answer. Put entry title, speaker and locator beside the quotation and check before sharing whether removal of the question obscures its referent. Personal headings can overstate too, so audit them against the passage. No automatic summary score is assumed; the manual test is whether the shortened record still accurately returns to the exchange.

## Retrieve the surrounding exchanges from your record

Leave the page and retrieve the target question, complete answer and neighbouring exchanges using the record, checking continuation or later qualification. Screen pages change with layout; retain entry title and short text rather than percentage alone. For unselectable scans, record image pages manually; a failed search does not establish absence. Export important notes through actually available means and verify respondent/editorial distinctions externally while preserving the book separately. State that this exchange was checked, not that the collection’s accuracy or the person’s entire position is established.

## Check speaker and editing scope when interpretations conflict

If a summary reverses the answer, inspect omitted negatives/conditions and interviewer evaluation misattributed to the respondent. Different tones require checking date, subject and editing scope before claiming a changed position. Keep uncertain quotation attribution unresolved and consult issuer explanations rather than inventing a live scene. Narrow a long record to one subquestion instead of removing limitations. Explain separately who proposed which premise, what the answer covers, which bridges are editorial and what remains unknown. Preserve the dialogue rather than blending two speakers into one conclusion.
