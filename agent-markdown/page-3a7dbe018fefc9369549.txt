A rain boot must coordinate a hollow upper shaft, foot volume and continuous outer wall. Draw one invented unbranded boot from slightly above, its toe pointing right and opening visible. This Aquarell still life studies form and material, not waterproof performance or fit. The main check is a continuous transition from shaft to instep rather than two unrelated objects joined together.

## Begin with footprint and shaft axis

Use an approximately 1500×1800 px portrait canvas with pale warm-grey background. Place the boot centrally, toe toward the right foreground without pointing directly at the viewer. Block the sole footprint and an upright shaft axis near the left; put heel below that axis and make the toe broader and rounder. Separate sketch, shaft, opening, foot, sole and shadow, using grey-green. Lower an exaggerated heel and connect the bottom if high-heeled. Lengthen instep-to-toe distance if cup-handle-like. Pixels and proportions serve illustration, not shoe sizing or a single child/adult shape.

## Make an elliptical opening with inner walls

Draw a tilted top ellipse, near rim lower and far rim higher, joining both sides smoothly to the shaft. Use deep grey-green inside with a downward darkening far inner wall, not a flat black disc. Give the near rim narrow thickness that turns with its curve. Remove a centre-covering light face if lid-like; add wall and rim thickness if paper-thin. The two opening arcs must share a centre rather than producing contradictory thickness. Inspect the ellipse without texture, then decide whether to add a rim band.

## Taper the shaft toward the ankle continuously

Bring both shaft walls down from the rim, taper slightly at the ankle and continue into heel back and instep front. Use a soft ankle bend rather than a right-angle cylinder inserted into a shoe. A pale check line at ankle height tests whether both sides still enclose one shaft. Delete a hard horizontal join and repair contour if cylinder-on-shoe-like; widen a pinched neck. Decoration must not hide a false seam: continuity should work without it. Real designs vary; finish this selected plain shape consistently.

## Align instep rise and rounded toe

Define the toe with a broad shallow arc and transition from ankle into a modestly raised instep, leaving a near side face. Narrow the far edge slightly in perspective; toe and sole must not point in different directions. Check the highest instep area in one colour. Add toe-top volume if duck-billed, or round a pointed dress-shoe tip. Omit minor creases rather than using dense cross-lines to fake a sharp bend. Keep the shaft axis within the rear supporting region, not suspended beyond the toe’s side.

## Give the sole a continuous thick edge

Run a deep grey-green sole edge around the foot, thicker visibly near the viewer and thinner or occluded on the far side. Heel and forefoot share one ground plane rather than contradictory floating-toe and grounded-heel shadows. Fit its upper edge to the foot and return its lower contour to the heel without crossing at the toe. Narrow a black-hoop-like band and quiet the far side. Clarify ground contact if melted-looking. Suggest only visible side detail; do not expose an otherwise hidden underside merely to display tread.

## Turn rubber highlights with the surface

Use a broad soft lighter grey-green shaft highlight, turn the instep light with its rise and leave a small soft toe reflection. They share a light source without becoming one white stripe from rim to toe. Retain colour in shadows instead of pure-black contouring. Soften transitions and reduce sharp reflections if metallic; remove fibre specks if fabric-like. Keep the inner wall darker while preserving near-rim thickness. Adjust face-to-shadow value contrast, then saturation, rather than brightening the entire boot for one lost edge.

## Check contact shadow separately from the cavity

Make the shadow sharpest at sole contact and softer outward, consistent with shaft lighting. The dark opening comes from inner walls and occlusion, not a copy of the ground shadow pasted inside. Read the upper cavity and lower ground contact separately at small size without making both darkest. Recheck instep and toe if upside-down-container-like; restore inner walls and near-rim occlusion if solid-cast-looking. Trace a checking path from rim down the outer wall to sole to expose severing background gaps, then hide that guide.

## Finish by checking opening, continuity and sole

Keep the layered native file and open an exported PNG separately. Confirm an actual opening, then trace shaft front, instep, toe, sole and heel. Restore sole thickness and stable foot shape if reduced to a green sock. Repair ankle contour if two glued objects rather than separating them with stronger decoration. Record colour, light direction and omitted tread. A new view needs revised near/far thickness, not copied highlights. Success is a clear hollow-shaft-to-foot form, without inferring actual performance or comfort.
