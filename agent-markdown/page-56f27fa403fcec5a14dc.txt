## Preserve the original four-sentence passage

Preserve: “课堂是一座港口。问题的船接连驶入，证据的缆绳逐根系牢。引航人不替每条船走完旅程，只教船主检查船底的洞。铃响后，他们又把船推向开阔水面。” (“The classroom is a harbour. Boats of questions keep arriving; ropes of evidence are secured one by one. The pilot does not complete every voyage for the boats, but teaches their owners to check holes in the hull. After the bell, they push the boats toward open water again.”). Record source and all four sentences. This original exercise is neither an attributed work nor observed classroom data. Read a copy, using external transcription if needed, and save later controls separately.

## List the scene before decoding it

List harbour, boats, ropes, pilot, owners and water, with arriving, securing, checking and pushing. Establish agent/action relations before abstract labels. Classroom–harbour is explicit, and boats/ropes are qualified by questions/evidence. These anchor interpretation. The scene need not be a realistic harbour report, but figurative scope does not permit inventing storms, crew counts or shipwrecks.

## Mark three explicit correspondences

Classroom/harbour, questions/boats and evidence/ropes have explicit wording; mark them with source locations. Arrival may suggest questions entering discussion, and secured ropes evidential support or constraint, but those action interpretations go beyond noun mapping. Do not make ropes mean permanently unchangeable views or secured claims conclusively proved: evidence may prompt revision. Trace each conclusion to wording instead of a generic symbol dictionary.

## Keep role mappings provisional

Pilot may suggest teacher and owners students, supported by classroom/bell, but these equations are not explicitly stated. Mark them provisional; a general guiding role need not identify a real teacher. Not completing every voyage suggests non-substitutive guidance, not no help ever or universal eventual success. Separate roles, pedagogical interpretation and outcomes rather than manufacture an educational-results report.

## Check extended relations rather than count nouns

Harbour extends through boats, ropes and guidance across sentences, supporting sustained metaphor in the declared broad sense. One classroom–harbour sentence can be a metaphor without proving extended relations. Sustaining a mapping does not require decoding every word: the bell may anchor classroom context without becoming harbour equipment. Unlike tracing an animal fable’s action/moral, this audits cross-sentence mappings. Categories may overlap without having identical structures.

## Use a literal-harbour control

Save a labelled control replacing classroom with a seaside place, boats of questions with wooden boats and ropes of evidence with ordinary ropes, retaining actions where possible. Removing three explicit anchors changes support, even if a reader still proposes allegory. Boats do not inevitably mean questions. This does not exclude allegory in every harbour description. Do not insert the old interpretation into the control’s title and then call it body evidence; titles, prompts and text have distinct roles.

## Check universal symbol tables and invented outcomes

Invented boat colours or owner ages need removal: the passage supplies neither. “Not completing every voyage” differs from “never helping”. State terminology when traditions differ rather than deny supported mappings over labels. Check omitted sentences, especially the opening and noun qualifiers, because losing them changes evidence. Share the original passage and explicit/provisional map, not private classroom records or complete copyrighted works.
