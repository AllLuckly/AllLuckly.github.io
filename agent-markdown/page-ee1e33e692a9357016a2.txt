Draw a standing side pose with one supporting and one lifted leg, distinguishing bill bend, neck, ankle and folded wing.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-greater-flamingo-bent-bill-visible-ankle-folded-leg/>), translated on 2026-11-17. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Choose a pale greater flamingo and a left-facing standing pose with one leg folded. Long neck and slender legs need continuous attachments; the visible bend often mistaken for a backward knee is the ankle. Clarify the joints without humanizing its legs or deriving an energy-saving mechanism from one image.

## Reserve vertical space for neck and legs

Use an approximately 1500×2200 px portrait canvas. Put a slightly tilted horizontal body oval centrally, with long curved neck above and supporting leg/ground below. Locate body, head and main leg before feather strands. Do not let an oversized torso compress legs into sticks or reduce chest and belly to a line merely for slimness. Separate sketch, plumage, legs and bill; save native work. Pixels are not height measurements. Keep a standing shore pose without conflicting flight, running or reflection cues.

## Give the long neck two coherent edges

Extend the neck from the chest into the small head using related, nonmechanically spaced edges, tighter inside and gentler outside bends. Keep a forward bill base and small eye on the head rather than a perfect ball. Return the rear neck edge to the upper-front body, not the wing centre. Simplify a knotted curve to one main gesture before restoring thickness instead of hiding crossings with feathers. No upside-down feeding head is drawn; preserve a readable standing side view rather than burying the bill in the back.

## Distinguish the bent bill from the soft neck contour

Give the bill a marked downward bend, attached at the head, with a darker tip and mouth seam following its form rather than splitting through the head. Follow pale base/dark tip in a greater-flamingo reference, not a straight needle. Upper and lower mandibles form one thick bill; its bend is not an extra joint. Establish silhouette before nostril detail, subduing an overly dark nostril that dominates the eye. Palette does not make all flamingos equally crimson; identification requires real evidence beyond this drawing.

## Label the visible bend as ankle, not knee

Extend one supporting leg below the belly and fold the far leg at its visible ankle, bringing the foot beneath the body. Lightly mark ankle and long lower foot segment. The true knee is near the body under feathers, not at the exposed bend. Trace the lifted leg continuously before thickness rather than leave two disconnected pink sticks. Ground the supporting foot only; do not add another contact shadow under the lifted foot. This specifies a pose, not loads or joint-range measurements, and must not be corrected to a human knee pattern.

## Separate folded wing, tail and leg attachments

Place a folded wing on the body side, tapering feather groups rearward, and a separate small tail at the body’s end. Not every rear feather is tail. Plumage may conceal leg roots if attachment remains understandable; do not draw a complete skeleton through it. Stop hidden far-leg lines instead of relocating the leg to the back for counting. Folded wings need not reveal all black flight feathers; retain a restrained dark edge rather than paste a large black patch centrally. Establish body continuity before selected feather groups.

## Model pale pink without relying on saturation

Use near-white warm pink, pale grey-pink shadows and restrained darker wing edges, with firmer legs/bill. Turn neck shadows around volume rather than outline every bend equally black. Ground the webbed supporting foot with a narrow shadow, not a pink disc, and subdue the background. Increase leg/background value difference if fine legs disappear rather than thicken them into pillars. Dietary pigment information motivates no feeding or colour experiment here; painted intensity establishes neither health nor age.

## Trace head to foot and verify joint labels

Hide feather texture and trace bill, head, neck, chest and belly to both legs. Reconnect a tail-like lifted foot through its ankle and lower segment. If a visible bend is labelled knee, correct both terminology and pose check instead of merely deleting the label. Restore ground contact for the supporting foot if both float, rather than add a supporting branch. Preserve a failing copy and repair one occlusion at a time before rechecking near/far legs. Partial display is not a missing-limb diagnosis, and manual labels are not automatic skeleton recognition.

## Reopen and check grounded foot and folded-leg space

Save native work, hide joint guides and reopen a PNG. Read long neck, body and one grounded foot at fit-to-window size, then inspect bill bend, ankle and negative space around the lifted foot enlarged. Restore a small gap and soften shading if leg merges with belly instead of relocating the foot against its path. Reopen separate plumage, leg and bill layers. Document one supporting/one folded leg, concealed knee and no behavioural measurement. Leave unperformed actions pending; clarity is not an experiment or app organ count.
