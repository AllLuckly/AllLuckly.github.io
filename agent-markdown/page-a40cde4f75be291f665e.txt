A wireframe box has a different purpose from a three-filled-face illustration: it also locates rear edges. Draw eight vertices and twelve edges under one fixed view, auditing structure before visibility. You maintain the line conventions manually; the app is not reconstructing a 3D model or computing hidden edges.

## Define the front and shifted rear faces

On a 1000 by 800 pixel canvas, draw a square front reference and a matching rear outline shifted up and right. This convention shows front, top and right faces; it is a spatial illustration rather than a guarantee of engineering projection dimensions. Label front corners A, B, C, D clockwise from upper left and corresponding rear corners A′, B′, C′, D′ on a draft layer. Two rectangles alone are incomplete: corresponding corners still need connections. Verify neither face was distorted by unequal scaling and establish their common direction before line styles.

## Build all twelve edges from an inventory

The structure has four front edges, four rear edges and four front-to-rear connectors: twelve total. Draw them with one fine style first and check each against the list without double-counting overlapping strokes. Three edges meet at every vertex; inspect front vertices, then rear ones. Independent segments or maintainable open-path combinations are acceptable, but saving objects must not omit a rear edge. Establish connectivity before visibility. Moving a vertex requires checking all attached endpoints; ordinary lines do not know they represent one 3D corner, so moving one segment cannot repair the whole structure.

## Mark the three hidden edges for this view

Under this front/top/right-visible convention, hide the three edges meeting rear lower-left D′: D′–A′, D′–C′ and D′–D. Keep the others solid initially. Identify them individually using draft labels rather than deciding from which line looks more internal on screen. Hidden means occluded by a face, not physically absent or deleted. Another viewing direction requires reassessing this rule; mirroring the drawing does not authorise retaining the same named hidden edges. Preserve the view explanation so recipients understand the choices.

## Use legible broken-line conventions

Use an available dash-style control or manually drawn short segments in a copy, retaining full source edges as references. Give all three hidden edges the same rhythm and lighter weight rather than different styles. Near vertices, dashes must still imply connection rather than missing structure. Distinguish visible solid and hidden dashed lines even in monochrome; colour is supplementary. Do not add spatial diagonals, which are not among the twelve edges. Inspect without labels, then restore them and verify that sparse dashes do not imply three missing edges.

## Inspect crossings and depth ambiguity

A rear edge projected through the front region uses dashes to explain occlusion; raising the entire edge as a solid line falsely suggests it passes through the face. A crossing is not necessarily a vertex: only the eight defined corners join three structural edges. Do not add dots at every intersection and imply extra corners. Wireframes can admit competing readings; restrained face labels or a view arrow may help, but are explanations rather than automatic 3D detection. Clarify weights and dashes before adding faint faces, whose fills must not conceal edges needed for verification.

## Maintain related vertices during a height change

In a copy, make the box taller and check lower edges and side connections on both front and rear faces, keeping their changes consistent. Lengthening only the front verticals leaves an incorrect rear height. Update the eight vertices, then recheck twelve edges and three hidden ones; geometry changes do not automatically repair visibility styles. To keep width unchanged, do not substitute uniform overall scaling for height editing. Compare original and taller versions at the same display scale, confirming a defined height change instead of view enlargement. This tests wireframe maintenance without claiming an editable parametric 3D model.

## Recount vertices and edges before delivery

Save the complete wireframe source and dash representation. In the export, count eight structural vertices and verify twelve edges from the connectivity list. Inspect fine dashes and joins at actual size, ensuring background texture does not hide them. Retain a fixed-view, non-engineering purpose in the title so it is not used for dimensioned construction. Include view and revision in the filename and reopen the delivery image to exclude test corner markers or obsolete heights. For editable delivery, explain which objects make up hidden lines so recipients can maintain full sources instead of encountering unexplained short fragments.

## Recheck the view before changing hidden edges

For reversed depth, check whether the open file is a mirrored or alternate-view test and return to the three-visible-face convention. Inspect three endpoints at each corner for misaligned lines instead of concealing gaps with thicker strokes. Check colour, width and display size for missing dashes rather than deleting an edge. Remove meaningless dots and explain crossings that resemble extra vertices. Recheck eight corners, twelve edges and three hidden edges after repairs. A clear wireframe needs both correct counts and an explained viewpoint; a readable output does not establish verified real geometric dimensions.
