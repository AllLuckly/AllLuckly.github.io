Successive clauses beginning with one phrase invite anaphora checking rather than a hidden-message extraction from initials. Fix clause boundaries in Book Isle and retain both the repeated opening and each clause’s new content. This differs from acrostics, stanza refrains and word reversal. Readers mark, interpret and construct controls manually, without automatic rhetoric recognition.

## Fix the span using syntax and source punctuation

Record title, author, translator, edition, chapter/page and a phrase anchor. Read the paragraph, then specify three successive clauses being compared. Retain quotation boundaries, speaker changes and paragraph directions; a screen wrap does not start a clause. In verse, keep authorial lines separate from syntactic clauses instead of treating every displayed initial as one position type. Transcribe a necessary short scan passage and verify it, marking unclear boundaries provisional. State the rule before extracting results so a desired repetition does not lead to selecting three distant sentences and calling them successive.

## Keep all three original clauses complete

Our original passage says: “I am willing to preserve the draft, I am willing to verify the source, I am willing to correct my own misreading.” Each clause begins “I am willing to,” followed by preserving, verifying or correcting. Copy all three clauses and label positions 1, 2, 3 rather than saving three initial characters. The unit here is a repeated phrase, not an acrostic initial. These practice sentences are not a historical speech or famous poet’s words. Real works retain their own edition’s openings, not replacements with “I am willing to.” Anaphora’s definition establishes position and unit without proving every author’s intent.

## Separate repeated openings from new information

List three “I am willing to” openings in one column and the different actions and objects in another. Check exact wording before considering parallelism, progression or commitment. Repeated openings do not make the actions identical or establish their completion; preserve the distinction between willingness/intention and completed action. Order may matter, but later placement alone does not make correcting more important than preserving. Label interpretations as candidates supported by context rather than using a formal term as an automatic scale of emotion or moral merit. Separate observation, agreement and evaluation.

## Internal repetition does not inherit the opening pattern

For a control write: “I am willing to preserve the draft; verifying the source is what I am willing to do; misreading also needs correction.” The phrase still appears inside the second clause, but all three clauses no longer begin with it. Record the positional change, not an absence of every repetition or every literary effect. Number original and control separately instead of copying the original three-opening success mark. Studying repeated endings needs another location rule; do not mix openings, interiors and endings to preserve a preferred conclusion. Retain old and new rules whenever task scope changes.

## Retain the difference introduced by one negative variant

Change only the third clause in a labelled control to “I am unwilling to correct my own misreading.” Its opening no longer exactly matches “I am willing to,” and willingness changes direction; sharing “I” does not retain identical full openings. Variants in real works may create reversal or development, but record both shared wording and the added negative. A formal variant is not automatically poor writing, and removing the negative meaning for a tidy table is invalid. Preserve the negative in paraphrases and translations rather than turning refusal into commitment for smoother English.

## Compare refrains and translations under separate scopes

Anaphora concerns repeated words at the beginnings of successive clauses, sentences or lines; refrain checking often tracks a phrase or line returning at intervals. They can overlap but do not automatically share one count. These openings were not selected as stanza endings, and acrostics extract initials rather than whole phrases. For translations, retain each language’s clauses and repeated units without demanding equal word counts. Record changed repetition before alleging mistranslation. Compare both meaning and form; mark languages not actually checked as unchecked.

## Troubleshoot wrapping and duplicated records

A larger font can add display lines without adding clauses. Use punctuation and syntax to locate the source, then check whether three notes accidentally refer to one sentence. Matches in titles, notes or quoted regions do not automatically enter this paragraph’s count. Open exports and inspect openings, the negative variant, position numbers and alteration labels, not just three results. Retrieve unsearchable files by chapter and phrase before deleting and reimporting. Record changes to boundaries or units instead of overwriting results so nobody can explain why three matches became two.

## Exit and return to repeat the comparison from the full paragraph

Exit the reader and return to the complete three-clause passage. Cover markings, rebuild boundaries and read each opening before checking the three different actions. Review the internal-repetition and negative controls separately, identifying the changed layer without mixing them with the original. Save six items: edition/location, successive span, repeated unit, clause mapping, contextual interpretation and variants. Completion means another reader can repeat the positional comparison and see conditional interpretations, not automatic app judgements of sincerity, persuasiveness or a uniquely permissible rhetorical label.
