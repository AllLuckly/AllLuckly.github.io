Separate a character’s look, the displayed object and the supported viewpoint inference; a close-up alone is insufficient.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/icouldplay-subjective-shot-eyeline-object-evidence/>), translated on 2026-11-14. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A character looking offscreen followed by an object can suggest what they see, but an object close-up alone cannot establish whose viewpoint it is. Play an authorised short scene in iCouldPlay and manually record looks, views and reactions with retrievable evidence. This is a film-reading exercise, not automatic eyeline detection or shot analysis by the app.

## Fix one retrievable scene version

Choose an authorised local scene of about 20–40 seconds containing an offscreen look and subsequent object shot. Record filename, duration, source and playback entry; for a feature, add chapter and nearby visible action, not just poster title. Open that file in iCouldPlay and play from a few seconds earlier at original speed with the whole image visible, using controls actually present. Optionally create a look/table-cup/reaction sequence in external tools before import; a production plan is not an observed result.

## Record only visible actions on the first pass

Play once and list head turn, object appearance and character return in order, without assigning POV yet. Use approximate seconds plus anchors such as “looks up after lowering right hand”; do not invent frame precision without frame stepping. Replay from earlier if a cut is missed rather than using a scrub thumbnail as an action. Record whether an actual look is visible. If eyes are unclear, write “head direction visible; eyeline unconfirmed” instead of inferring unseen detail from the next shot.

## Separate the look from the object shot

On a second pass, use external notes with three columns: look direction, subsequent image and evidence connecting them. In the planned sample, looking down then a cup shot establishes that the cup is shown; viewpoint still depends on placement and sequence. A character facing away from the table does not establish POV merely because a cup follows. Separate visible facts from inference. No in-app table or annotation is assumed; playback, pause and replay simply permit inspection.

## Allow approximate viewpoint without claiming optical identity

Check whether object-shot height, direction and visible area broadly fit the character’s position, without measuring camera and eye coordinates from a finished film. A closer object shot may support approximate POV or simply insert a detail; retain surrounding look cues. Note a mismatch with strict eye position without declaring an error. Playback magnification establishes no real distance. Depth of field and focal-length measurement are unnecessary: first identify the proposed viewer and supporting connection.

## Add an uncertain-viewpoint counterexample

Find another object close-up or make a separate cup-only sample without a look. Record object, surrounding shots and eyeline evidence independently; do not borrow missing cues from the first sample. “Object insert; insufficient evidence of a character’s POV” is a valid conclusion. Keep distinct filenames instead of overwriting the original. Removing a look shot in external editing may alter duration, so rebuild the new time card rather than copying old positions.

## Use reactions as added evidence, not proof alone

Play through from before the object to after the reaction and note whether expression or action responds, including alternative objects. A reaction may strengthen continuity but proves neither every seen detail nor that the character knows all the audience knows. Record intervening shots before linking distant screenshots causally. Sound may be noted separately but is not required here. State “supported” or “insufficient” with time anchors rather than an automatically confirmed label.

## Separate playback obstructions from film evidence

If controls or captions obscure eyes, let hideable controls disappear or pause appropriately; record any persistent obstruction rather than claiming clarity. Restore an available whole-image display if cropped, then replay. Use earlier action anchors if seeking is imprecise, without claiming frame stepping. Verify edition and duration before comparing different cuts. Separate unclear eyeline, viewpoint-position mismatch and missing edit evidence instead of calling every interpretation problem a player fault.

## Reopen and recover the evidence chain

Reopen the same file without assuming identical resume behaviour across sources. Verify identity and recover look, view and reaction using time plus action anchors. If resume differs, enter manually from earlier and log actual recovery rather than changing old observations. Deliver three statements: visible action, object-shot features and support level with missing evidence. Without device execution, keep a verification checklist, not a completed test report. Success is retrievable fact/inference separation, not proof of intent or a unique psychological reading.
