English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-threaded-rod-helix-study/>), translated on 2026-11-06. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Several slanted marks can resemble a thread without tracing one continuous helix. Use a simplified wireframe rod, define axial advance per full turn and join front/rear half-turns. This manual teaching image specifies no standard profile, certified handedness, manufacturing dimensions or bolt fit, and assumes no automatic 3D thread generation.

## Define the axis and advance per turn

Create an 800 by 1000 pixel canvas with a vertical rod axis and downward advance. Start with parallel side references and leave explanatory space above/below. An invented pitch of 60 pixels means returning to the same side after one full turn 60 pixels lower, not one visible diagonal per turn. Mark left start A, left B 60 below, and an intermediate right point 30 below A. These are drawing coordinates, not mechanical specifications. Establish the three-point relationship before repeating so half/full turns remain distinct.

## Split one source turn into front and rear halves

Draw a gentle front-half curve from left A to the right midpoint, then a rear half from that point to left B. Join endpoints while keeping halves independently manageable. These curves explain depth, not an exact cylindrical projection represented by straight diagonals. Use temporary colours and trace A to B, verifying the path advances rather than closing at the same height. Keep it inside side references and preserve complete undashed sources before converting rear appearance to dashes.

## Repeat by full-turn pitch instead of filling gaps arbitrarily

Group one turn and copy downward by 60 pixels so each end meets the next start. Check three turns before extending through the image rather than inserting marks wherever gaps look empty. Preserve half-turn lengths/directions without an unexplained extra crossing. Temporary turn IDs should show starts and ends spaced 60 apart. Copied objects are not automatically topologically joined; inspect coincident endpoints for seams or heavy overlaps. Record any final partial cropped turn rather than counting it as another pitch.

## Use solid front and dashed rear lines for visibility

Use solid front halves and available dashes or manual short segments for rear halves with consistent rhythm. Dashes mean behind the rod, not absent material. Opaque rod fill may hide them, so distinguish a wireframe explanatory version from an exterior view and save separately. Rear lines should not dominate while being described as hidden. Preserve readable continuation at side joins instead of sparse dashes suggesting breaks. Naming a style does not make receiving tools recognise an engineering layer.

## Update both halves when changing pitch

In a copy, change full pitch to 80 pixels: the right midpoint is 40 below its left start and the next left point 80 below. Rebuild one turn before repeating by 80; simply increasing gaps while retaining old half-height is inconsistent. Keep rod width fixed and compare turn counts within one range without claiming the rod physically stretched. Retain the 60 version and update legends/filenames. This maintains the teaching definition, not strength, speed or fit relationships; real threads need applicable standards and professional processes.

## Separate end clipping from turn counts

A top/bottom mask may limit the displayed range while preserving full coils. Distinguish complete and partial turns, measuring between same-side points rather than counting solid marks alone. Optional heads/end faces must have explicit occlusion and not make the helix emerge from an unexplained middle point. For density, reduce turns or enlarge the image instead of deleting all rear halves while claiming continuity. Hide heads, trace end to end, then restore decoration so appearance cannot conceal breaks.

## Retain invented units and the teaching scope in output

Save one-turn sources, repeat groups and both pitch versions. Inspect solid/dashed lines, side joins and small text in the export. Title it a continuous-helix schematic, not a finished bolt engineering drawing. Test backgrounds and actual-size dashes; white backing remains until handled. Include 60- or 80-pixel pitch and revision in filenames and exclude obsolete explanations. Explain independent half-turn objects, including joint weight updates, for editable delivery. Keep the untested scope rather than treating a clear static image as approved manufacture or mechanism.

## Check turn endpoints when marks look like separate rings

If turns close as separate rings, check whether the rear half returns to A instead of the next left point. For gaps, inspect repeat displacement/endpoints before drawing arbitrary connectors. Repair dash continuity from the complete rear source rather than random fragment movement. Mixed 60/80 pitches suggest leftover groups/versions. Recheck turn definition, same-side advance, half joins and output so every path continues to the next turn rather than merely resembling threaded texture.
