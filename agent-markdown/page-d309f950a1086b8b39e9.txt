Avatar files are usually square but may display as circles or rounded squares. Looking only at the original corners can miss hair and shoulders cut off by a circular crop. Create two check versions of one avatar in VectorPaint, keeping key features intact in different containers.

## Start with an editable subject

Prepare a geometric portrait, animal motif, or simple lettermark and retain the original. Create or confirm a square 600×600 px canvas, placing the subject near its center with space around it. Do not fill the canvas immediately; leave room for later adjustments.

Identify the most important recognition features: hair, eyes, and collar, or a logo's main contour. Circular crops may intentionally remove secondary decoration, but key information should occupy stable areas. Decide what must remain before judging the crop rather than shrinking everything whenever a corner is lost.

## Check the uncropped square version first

On the full square canvas, compare subject size and whitespace above the head, beside the face, and below the shoulders. Slightly raised placement can work with a clear reason. If already tight against square edges, a circular crop is more likely to lose important contours.

Check edges against temporary backgrounds. Dark hair can disappear on dark backgrounds, as pale clothes can on white. Keep subject and background separately managed to change background colors for different uses without redrawing the person.

## Create a copy for checking through a circular window

On a copy, create a canvas-sized circle as the clipping window above the subject. Select the applicable circular path and subject content, create a mask, and check the visible area. Keep the original uncropped work to recover complete content.

Center the circular window correctly with equal width and height. If only its fill or an odd fragment remains, undo, check selection and stacking, and test simple objects. An opaque circle covering the avatar is not a completed crop. Avoid repeated experiments on the only original.

## Use key features to judge repositioning

Check the top of the head and hair tips, then ears, face sides, and shoulders. A circle is widest at its horizontal and vertical midpoints but cuts diagonal areas earlier, so decorations in square corners disappear most easily. Move the whole subject or scale it proportionally according to the missing area.

Change either position or size at a time. Shrinking and then moving down may restore the head while cropping shoulders. Keep a before preview and compare equally sized versions, selecting one with intact key features and a subject that is not too small.

## Compare recognition at small sizes

Reduce both square and circular versions to common avatar size and check eyes, mouth, contour, and decorations. If a delicate earring or fine text disappears, omitting it from the avatar version may be better than thickening it further.

If the background competes equally with the subject, simplify it first. Avatars prioritize rapid recognition, and complex textures consume limited pixels. Keep lettermark content short; place a long name in adjacent text instead of forcing it into the circle.

## Handle transparent and fixed-background needs

Some uses need transparent PNG, others a fixed background. Confirm genuine transparency outside the design without deleting white areas needed inside it. For the background version, fill the complete square and let the target platform handle the circular crop.

Understand the target platform's avatar treatment first to avoid double cropping and two edge rings. Provide a complete square version with background and a separate transparent circular presentation copy, distinguishing them in filenames. Their uses differ; one file need not cover every situation.

## Check again in the real container after export

Choose PNG, check pixel dimensions, and preview the file in its intended interface. The platform may resize, round corners, or crop automatically, so inspect the final display too. Verify the actual upload's dimensions and content rather than relying only on a screenshot sent to yourself.

For several uses, record which version suits circular or square display. Keep the same palette and identifying features so it remains one recognizable identity. After hair or text changes, recheck both containers; old safe positions may not suit the new contour.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must a circular avatar show all the shoulders?** Not necessarily. Cropping shoulders can intentionally create a close-up, while face and key features remain complete. The crop should look deliberate rather than almost revealing half a decoration.

**Is making the person very small the safest option?** It reduces cropping but may weaken recognition. Keep necessary breathing room, then compare at actual avatar size to balance a large enough subject with intact key features. Retain the complete original, square output, and circular preview for later adjustment.

## Let the background follow the container rather than the subject

When moving the portrait to check a circular crop, select only its related parts and keep the background covering the canvas. Moving both together can expose transparent gaps that look like mask problems. After adjusting the person, recheck the background coverage and whether the export is the intended transparent or background version. Separating subject positioning from background coverage makes square and circular output more reliable.
