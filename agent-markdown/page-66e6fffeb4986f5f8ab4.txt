A spiral notebook binding is not a row of circles pasted along a page edge. Draw a closed, single-spiral notebook with six illustrative holes, establishing how wire progresses between them, then cover, page stack and tabletop contact. Six is a practice simplification, not a universal product count. Check paths and occlusion manually in Aquarell without automatic coil generation or treating the image as a dimensioned binding plan.

## Establish the notebook plane and bound edge

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px landscape canvas with a pale cream-grey background. Angle the notebook lower left to upper right, binding on its left long edge. Establish a perspective cover quadrilateral and exposed right/lower page-stack thickness, not equal thickness everywhere. Separate sketch, pages, cover, holes, wire and shadow; use grey-blue cover and warm-white paper. Hide wire and test the closed-book form. Add appropriate side exposure if rug-like or reduce thickness if brick-like. Keep the bound edge on the cover plane rather than twisting it to expose holes; place six holes along it later.

## Locate six holes along one edge

Place six small elliptical holes inside the bound cover edge, temporarily numbered one through six. Widen near spacing slightly and compress far spacing with the page plane. Retain paper between holes and edge rather than cutting notches through it. Start with faint locations and count holes and gaps. Reduce dark area and expose rims if button-like. Correct perspective if distant holes bunch suddenly instead of imposing mechanical equal spacing. Hole count, wire path and real specifications are separate questions; this exercise checks only its six-hole correspondence.

## Trace one centreline between adjacent holes

Sketch one thin wire centreline emerging from near hole one, turning around the page edge and progressing to the next hole, continuing through all six. Each turn advances instead of becoming a separate closed ring. Use dashed hidden sections in the diagnostic sketch and remove them from the finish. Repair a fourth-to-third reversal rather than hiding it with highlights. Keep endpoints short without invented long spikes. Explain a continuous single spiral without claiming a manufacturer’s actual end-finishing process.

## Let pages conceal returning wire portions

Show wire arcs in front of the cover and conceal portions returning behind the page stack, exposing them again near appropriate holes. Establish full diagnostic paths, then occlude with cover and pages rather than drawing through paper. Hide holes to test depth order and restore them to check actual entry instead of a near miss. A row of tacks usually lacks turns around the paper edge. A floating spring needs repaired hole contact and distance. Not every hole requires a complete inner wall, but each visible wire segment needs an identifiable location.

## Keep wire width consistent but reflections broken

Use slender mid blue-grey wire with narrow grey-white upper-left highlights and darker rearward turns. Perspective may vary width slightly, but not from pipe-thick to broken hairline. Let highlights follow arcs and break at crossings or holes instead of six identical white crescents. Add restrained value jumps if plastic-like; reduce uniform dark edges and reopen cover gaps if fence-like. Treat the wire as a continuous slender cylinder, then reflect light over it. Material clarity cannot replace hole-by-hole continuity, and page texture must not cross metal arcs.

## Finish cover, page stack and ground contact separately

Use a narrow lower-right cover edge and only two or three faint page-stack lines rather than dense equal stripes. Cast shadow lower right, tightest beneath the notebook, with small soft wire shadows if useful. Avoid equal black borders around book and every coil. Reconnect cover and stack if the cover floats; reduce page lines and retain broad warm white if blind-like. An undecorated cover is fine. Titles, stickers and patterns cannot rescue silhouette or explain thickness and wire travel; each structural layer needs a definite position.

## Match hole numbers to wire segments

Keep a numbered one-to-six sketch with hidden paths. Read holes near to far, then trace wire back. Correct count does not excuse skipped holes, and continuity does not excuse wire through the cover. Change only the faulty arc or hole while holding perspective and light fixed. If coils merge at thumbnail size, modestly increase compositional spacing rather than deleting a hole while retaining a six-hole claim. Double-wire or separate binder-ring variants need new structural records; they cannot inherit a single-spiral explanation unchanged.

## Inspect hole entries and false joins after export

Save the layered original and inspect near entry, middle progression and far endpoint in a PNG at actual size. Repair broken wire locally instead of thickening all turns. Lighten hole shadows and retain paper strips if entries become a black chain. Prevent page texture joining an endpoint or background marks inventing a seventh turn. Record a numbered fault such as the front arc at hole five being incorrectly covered. Finish with six checkable holes, traceable wire and coherent occlusion, not a claim of manufacturability or physical page-turn testing.
