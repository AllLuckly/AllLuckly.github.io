## Establish one complete body route

Use an approximately 1900×1400 px canvas, head near left and tail farther right. Sketch a gently curving axis and body envelope tapering into the tail stock, leaving room for fins/flukes. Repair ball-and-string volume or an angular tail bend before detail. Pixels do not measure body length, and a foreshortened far end is not physically shorter. Keep the whale submerged without a surface boundary or blow plume that mixes different spatial states.

## Connect the broad head to the rostrum

Use a broad, somewhat flattened head connected to the trunk, not a dolphin-like pointed beak. Run the mouth line along the side without splitting detached shells. Place the near eye and omit an occluded far eye instead of putting both on one side. Revisit volume and mouth if fish-like; extra teeth are no remedy. Suggest selected tubercles along the surface, not multiple eyes. Facial details establish no age, sex or health.

## Extend paired flippers from the body sides

Mark a flipper root on each front-side body region: near flipper down/forward, far flipper partly visible up/back. Build broad roots tapering to tips, with clear basal overlap, not two fins from one belly point. Move wing-like dorsal attachments laterally. Shorten a far flipper resembling another tail and clarify its root. Long flippers are a referenced trait, but screen pixels give no body-length ratio. Keep attachment clearer than mottling.

## Keep the dorsal fin on the rear back

Place one small dorsal fin on the rear back, low or gently curved rather than flipper-sized. It may appear narrow in this view but stays dorsal, not a third lateral flipper. Reduce an oversized shark triangle and connect a floating root. Tone can separate it without an isolating black border. Shape varies; one invented silhouette is not a universal individual-identification feature. Add no hidden second dorsal fin.

## Join the tail stock to laterally spread flukes

Taper the stock into two laterally spread flukes with a central notch, not an upright fish tail. Unequal screen widths are possible; use a cross-stock checking direction before edges. Unite leaf-like separate halves at the base, and repair any half apparently growing from the dorsal fin. Use restrained irregularity, not identical sawteeth. Shade one coherent tail plane instead of incompatible halves later tied together with water marks.

## Keep markings and water colour within established forms

Use dark grey-blue with restrained invented pale flipper/belly markings, arranging visible fluke tones without copying an individual pattern. Let surrounding water fade without severing roots. Separate a hole-like white marking from ground and recover a lost pale flipper locally instead of black outlining everything. Suggest only visible lower-body grooves, not an exposed complete underside. Materials and water serve ownership of body, paired flippers, dorsal fin and flukes.

## Trace roots to tips to find extra fins

Hide markings/water and trace head to stock, then two flippers, one dorsal fin and two flukes. Correct counts still require correct roots; shadows and pale marks are not fins. Reveal a fully hidden far flipper through a revised invented view, not transparency. Repair a fish-like tail plane rather than labelling it horizontal. Restore detail without extra eye-like tubercles. This drawing check is no claim of underwater observation or tested swimming posture.
