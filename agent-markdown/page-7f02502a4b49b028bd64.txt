English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/nasbook-acrostic-line-initial-evidence/>), translated on 2026-11-10. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

When someone claims a poem hides a phrase, first check whether the extraction rule is stable. Use four original lines in Book Isle for a reproducible acrostic exercise based on character positions, without expecting automatic hidden-message detection. Read the full lines after extracting their initials; an accidental phrase is not an author’s secret declaration.

## Record the edition and evidence for line boundaries

Choose a permitted short poem or self-made document and record title, author, edition, chapter and neighbouring heading. Specify that this exercise takes the first body Han character of each authorial poetic line, not each displayed screen line. If the file cannot distinguish deliberate breaks from wrapping, mark line boundaries unresolved and consult reliable typography or the source file before extracting. Decide in advance whether titles, line numbers, bullets and headers belong to the poem; do not include one only when its character suits a desired result. Write the rule before the result and retain the original hypothesis.

## Use an explicitly original four-line sample

The original four lines are “春灯照旧纸。”; “风从半掩门来。”; “入夜仍听远雨。”; “画中一舟未归。”. They mean: spring lamplight falls on old paper; wind comes through a half-open door; distant rain is still heard at nightfall; a boat in the painting has not returned. Quotation marks delimit four lines here; retain four authorial breaks in your prepared document instead of merging them into prose. Number them one through four and copy each complete sentence. These invented practice lines are not an ancient quotation or a claim of correct metre or rhyme. A task on a real book must use that book’s actual lines, not attribute this sample to it.

## Extract and check the initial sequence line by line

Under the recorded rule, initials are 春, 风, 入, 画 in that order, combining as 春风入画, meaning “spring breeze enters the painting.” Keep each individual character paired with its line number before writing the combination, so every character remains traceable. Sentence-final stops and opening quotation marks do not count because the rule selects a body Han character. Taking final characters would be a separate task with a new rule and sequence; do not mix initial and final positions to manufacture an answer. Authorial line order determines extraction, not a semantic order preferred by the reader.

## Change reading width to test wrapping interference

For reflowable text, change only reading width or font size and record which of the four authorial lines wrap into two display lines. Extraction from the authorial four should still yield 春风入画. Selecting every visible screen-line initial may introduce internal characters such as 门 or 雨, contrary to this exercise’s rule. For fixed-layout files, observe zoom only; do not promise reflow. Save settings before and after with the authorial boundaries, without treating changed display-line counts as a revised poem. If the boundaries can no longer be identified after the setting change, stop reporting a definite extraction.

## Make a one-character control without editing the original

In a separately labelled manual control, replace the second line’s initial 风 with 雨 and change nothing else. The sequence becomes 春雨入画, “spring rain enters the painting.” This verifies dependence on the second initial, not literary superiority of either version. Change only that line’s final 来 to 过 in another control: the initial sequence remains unchanged, showing that the rule does not use that position. Never write controls back into the original book or quotation. Save position, old character, new character, expected sequence and observed sequence. If a control behaves unexpectedly, check which text was used before changing the rule to fit the result.

## Separate positional fact from inferred intention

In the fact field, state exactly what the four initials form. Discuss its relation to the full poem’s lamp, door, rain and boat in a separate interpretation field. It may reflect authorial design or a later arranger; without an author’s explanation, do not assert actual motivation. Do not select arbitrary lines from a long text, change their order and proclaim a hidden judgement about someone. If studying other positional patterns, define positions in advance and record the attempted scope instead of publishing only a successful attempt. A readable combination is evidence, not sufficient proof of any concealed accusation.

## Troubleshoot ornaments and variant characters at line starts

After copying an excerpt, check for added numbering, quotation marks, indentation or headers; exported ornaments are not authorial initials. For variant, traditional/simplified or handwritten characters, record the actual form and reading uncertainty rather than replacing it with a character that forms a more convenient phrase. A scan search failing to find an initial does not show its absence, and search results do not replace line boundaries on the page. If editions divide lines differently, make separate sequences and label editions; do not combine the first line of A with later lines of B. Give an exact location and permitted scope when seeking help deciphering text.

## Exit and return to repeat extraction from the boundaries

Exit the reader and return. Locate all four authorial lines first, then extract their initials again without looking at your combined result. Compare with 春风入画 and explain why changing the second initial affects it while changing the final character does not. Retain six items: text identity, line-boundary evidence, extraction rule, line-by-line mapping, controls and interpretation. Keep complete lines readable instead of saving only four characters and losing context. Completion means someone can reproduce the same sequence under the same rule and identify unverified intent; it does not mean the app detects every acrostic form or endorses hidden meanings.
