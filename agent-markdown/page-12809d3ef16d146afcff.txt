One person speaking at length is not automatically a soliloquy. Read a short scene in Book Isle, separating presence, spoken content and addressee cues. Beyond the selective hearing of an aside, examine voiced thinking and its limits. No automatic genre analysis is assumed, and a character’s expressed thoughts are not completed actions.

## Retain entrances, exits and directions

Choose a lawful play and record title, author, translator, edition, act/scene and phrase. Read surrounding entrances/exits, not only the long speech. Manually verify necessary names/directions from unselectable scans without automatic recognition claims. Use five fields: present characters, speaker, addressee, thought content and evidence, anchored by scene despite reflow. Missing directions mean missing evidence, not permission to invent exits from speech length. Offstage, concealed and reader-unknown presence are not automatically the same solitude.

## Use a definition without replacing evidence

The [Open University soliloquy lesson](<https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/literature/approaching-plays/content-section-7.1>) explains the convention of voicing thought for audience access. Solitude is common, but texts/productions may vary, so inspect specific directions. A long speech may address another character; word count is insufficient. Audience audibility does not require explicit audience address: voiced thought and direct address are separately checkable. Record differing translations of terms rather than forcing this edition’s labels into your preferred terminology.

## Record solitude only when established

In an invented scene B exits, and A alone indoors says, “I want to deliver the letter, but I have not decided yet.” Solitude comes from directions; intention/indecision comes from speech. Attribute this to no famous play. Removing B’s exit weakens solitude evidence, so retain less certainty. Record A as speaker and addressee unspecified, rather than inventing absent B as recipient because A stands alone. This practises externalized thought; applying a specific term still depends on edition and context.

## Separate intention from completed action

Wanting to deliver is not delivery, and undecided is not decided against delivery. Separate intention, decision state and action, marking action unseen. Apparent sincerity does not guarantee future fulfilment; characters can contradict themselves or change plans. If delivery occurs later, add that time/evidence without backdating it. If the letter is burned instead, retain the change rather than deleting the earlier intention. This tracks textual information without psychological diagnosis or automatic emotion/intention detection.

## Compare a long speech to another character

For a control, B remains while A gives a longer explanation to B about postponing delivery, then B replies. Length does not erase the explicit exchange or make A alone. Preserve speech/response links, without asserting that any responsive scene can never contain a brief shift to voiced thought. Segment explicit shifts. An aside may conventionally exclude some present characters and needs its own hearing-scope check. These frameworks prompt questions rather than replacing roles and addressee fields with one label.

## Separate text from production choices

For a permitted production record identity/time, separately observing orientation, spacing and audible delivery. Facing spectators alone does not establish direct textual address, and a close-up does not prove solitude because others may be offscreen. Retain script and production differences without automatically declaring one wrong. Without footage complete only the text task, inventing no device observation. The first-person voice remains the character’s, not automatically the playwright’s; external biography cannot override clear role attribution.

## Revisit the scene’s boundaries

Save an available bookmark or external scene record, leave and return before A’s speech/B’s exit, then read through the next entrance or exchange shift. Returning mid-speech can lose presence evidence. Open exported notes and inspect “not decided yet,” names and unseen-action labels rather than treating file creation as correct interpretation. Delivery searches may find another scene; verify scene/incipit. For unselectable text transcribe anchors instead of resetting whole-book progress. Necessary excerpts and precise positions better expose evidence than indiscriminate copying.

## Review presence, addressee, content and continuation

For all long speeches labelled soliloquy inspect addressees; for solitude equated with direct audience address inspect actual vocatives; for intended action treated as done inspect action boundaries; for speech attributed to the author inspect role labels. Retain old judgments and new directions without hiding missing evidence. Keep entrances/exits, invented thought, intention/decision/action distinctions, exchange control and production boundary. This bounded record neither detects real psychology nor guarantees honest soliloquies or one classification for every play. Rebuild presence lists for each new scene.
