Design an original two-color geometric picture, retain separate layers and registration marks, and compare displaced copies without treating screen color as an ink proof.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-two-color-screenprint-registration-proof/>), translated on 2026-11-20. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Design a two-color geometric picture with a blue circle and orange rectangle in separate groups, and two cross marks outside the finished frame. Use copies to inspect slight misregistration. MoMA's screenprinting material explains that multicolor work can use a separate screen for each color. This exercise makes a digital comparison only; it does not claim VectorPaint automatically separates inks, makes screens or accurately simulates physical ink.

## Plan the frame, colors and mark area

On a landscape canvas place a rectangle marking the intended finished area and leave equally wide mark regions on both sides. Group blue, orange, registration marks, finished frame and notes separately and save an original. Canvas and margins are exercise choices, not a printer's trim specification. Outside marks are for checking, not part of the intended image. Make sure the export canvas will not accidentally crop either mark region. Confirm actual bleed and trimming requirements separately with the service provider.

## Draw a blue circle and orange rectangle

Inside the frame draw a blue circle and an orange rectangle with a clear overlapping area. Retain both as editable objects rather than merging them into one indistinguishable shape. Pick two recognizable practice colors and record their values; these are not a brand's spot colors or physical ink formulas. Check both shapes fit within the finished area and remain recognizable as curve and straight edges when reduced, without requiring texture to explain their separation.

## Separate layer order from screen color conclusions

Hide orange and blue groups in turn to confirm only the intended shape remains. Restore both and record which is above. With two opaque objects the upper hides part of the lower; screen compositing does not automatically match physical ink mixing or transparency. To inspect both contours, temporarily reduce one layer or show outlines in a copy and identify that as a proof view. Do not describe a third color seen on screen as ink already printed.

## Place matching cross marks outside the frame

Draw one small cross in each outer region, each made of a horizontal and vertical line. Record center positions and ensure both color-proof copies carry marks at corresponding coordinates. Keep marks far enough from the finished frame not to be mistaken for artwork. You may put mark sets in their respective groups or copy a base set using controls actually available, but check matching centers; visually similar screenshots are insufficient. Without actual printing these crosses are digital registration references.

## Output two separate proof views

In copies hide the other color shape in turn while retaining each set of cross marks, giving blue-layer and orange-layer views. Record whether the finished frame is exported. Both views require the same canvas bounds and origin; separately cropping each to its colored shape will not preserve automatic mark alignment. Check filename, visible groups and canvas before output. SVG with an imported bitmap is not automatically all vector; PNG is raster. Record resolution and transparency actually selected.

## Create a control by shifting one color

Copy the full draft and move only the orange shape together with its corresponding marks a small fixed distance right, leaving blue unchanged. Record whether the distance uses canvas units or pixels. Inspect shape edges and relative mark positions; do not also scale or rotate. Marks should coincide in the original and show the same directional offset in this copy. Moving only the shape without its marks is a different error condition and cannot prove the marks reflect the whole layer's displacement.

Keep a third copy that moves only the orange shape, leaving marks fixed, and name it “shape and marks out of sync”. In the first control, separated marks expose the layer translation; in this one the marks still coincide while the shape is displaced. Marks must follow their corresponding content, but this exercise does not guarantee identical registration methods for every printing process.

## Check monochrome legibility and output scope

View each layer in black or another monochrome proof to check the circle, rectangle and crosses remain distinct. Label layers instead of relying solely on color. The composite does not need to be covered in text; place explanations alongside. Reduce the view to check the marks and enlarge to check that each cross has a continuous intersection rather than a gap caused by stroke endpoints or position. Actual printing depends on paper, screens, inks and process. This lesson gives no exposure, ink-mixing or printing-safety parameters.

## Save and inspect exported copies

Keep the editable original, two separate proof views and both displacement controls. Export needed SVG, PDF or PNG through an entry actually available in the installed version, then inspect canvas bounds and complete marks in a separate viewer. If color outputs have different coordinates, check cropping, object transforms and chosen files before redrawing. Mark unperformed export, reopening or actual printing pending. Describe the deliverable as a geometric registration exercise, not a color or press-specific production guarantee.
