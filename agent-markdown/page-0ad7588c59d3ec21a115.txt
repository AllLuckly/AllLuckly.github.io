Draw a small grid maze and audit its entrance, exit and dead ends, retaining separate prompt and solution outputs.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-static-maze/>), translated on 2026-11-03. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A maze can look intricate without offering a route from entrance to exit. Arrange walls manually on a small grid, first preserving a verified route and then adding branches. This is a static paper-puzzle exercise, without automatic pathfinding, random generation or interactive gameplay. It does not assume a unique solution.

## Define a small grid and its boundary

Use a 900 by 900 pixel canvas with margins and a six-by-six practice grid; that size is a chosen difficulty, not a tool limit. Record an entrance and exit by edge and cell, then label them outside the maze. Distinguish displayed grid guides from the ordinary paths that will become walls. Start with the outer boundary and openings before interior walls, checking that decoration does not obscure the entrance and the exit stays safely inside the export area.

## Mark one verified route first

Draw a cell-centre route on a separate draft layer, moving between adjacent cells without diagonal corner jumps. Record its cells and confirm it stays inside the boundary. Keep the first route short enough to verify. Design walls around the edges this route must cross. The drawn path is a human-designed reference, not a detected solution. Preserve a source containing it so later wall changes can be checked instead of relying on memory after deleting the only answer reference.

## Use consistent wall widths and joins

Draw walls along cell edges with uniform strokes and deliberate endpoints. Test one corner so tiny gaps do not resemble passages, and verify real junctions rather than approximate visual contact. Separate outer and inner walls. Build necessary walls around the route before extending elsewhere. Thin rectangles can also serve as walls, but use a consistent construction so filled thickness and stroked lines do not imply different passage widths accidentally.

## Add branches with identifiable endings

Add two or three branches from the main route, drafting their centre lines before walls. Give dead ends definite endings rather than accidental outer openings. A branch rejoining the main route may create another solution, so do not claim uniqueness without exhaustive checking. Record junction choices and inspect unintended isolated cells. Any decorative inaccessible area should be recognisable as outside the playable route rather than implying every blank cell must be visited.

## Hide the reference and solve independently

Hide the draft route and solve from the walls alone, marking a test copy in a different colour. At junctions, follow actual openings rather than forcing the original list. Record the last reachable cell and blocking edge when stuck. Compare the completed route with the baseline and exclude unintended outer-boundary shortcuts. This verifies one viable route only. Additional rules, such as no revisiting, need their own checks; ordinary connectivity does not establish every possible condition.

## Check walls and passages at real size

Inspect junctions, narrow passages and entrance markings at the intended paper or screen size. Thin walls may break; thick ones may close cells. Allow room for a handwritten answer and reduce grid complexity if needed. In monochrome, distinguish solution from walls through a clear convention rather than colour alone. Remove ordinary construction-grid objects before delivery so they cannot be mistaken for additional walls.

## Save a clean puzzle and its checked answer

Retain clearly separated walls, reference and test layers. Export a clean prompt with answers hidden and a labelled solution from the same wall revision. A moved wall requires a complete route recheck because it can change branch and exit relationships. Open both delivery files to catch draft lines or incomplete answers. Static images do not validate solvability; preserve the checklist and editable source as the basis for later expansion.

## Diagnose blocked routes by the exact edge

Locate the exact cell edge where progress stops and inspect duplicate walls or stroke joins that close an opening. A vanished exit may come from replacing the boundary with a closed rectangle. Distinguish actual endpoint gaps from rendering artefacts at normal size. Avoid accumulating white patches that conceal wall structure. Correct the relevant wall objects, solve again without the reference and recheck exports before retaining the shareable revision.
