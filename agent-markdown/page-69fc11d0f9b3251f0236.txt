Paint a monarch chrysalis hanging below a branch, using a green form, a few golden spots and a top attachment to explain its structure. The U.S. National Park Service describes a silk attachment made before pupation and a green chrysalis with golden spots that can become transparent later. This exercise chooses the green stage and invents the composition, light and background; a picture cannot establish an individual's health or emergence date.

## Prepare references and an editable draft

Read the source and study its photographs. Record suspension direction, silhouette, spot placement and supporting surfaces without copying an entire photograph's composition. Make an approximately 1500 × 1800 portrait canvas and save an editable draft. Separate sketch, branch, chrysalis and background layers; follow the layer and save controls visible in your installed version. Canvas dimensions organize the drawing, not the animal's actual size. Note the planned image and reference link on a record card so a sketch is not mistaken for a finished export.

## Draw the support and hanging direction

Place a gently slanted branch near the top and the chrysalis below, near the center. Sketch a faint vertical guide for suspension and a short horizontal mark for the branch underside. The axis may bend slightly, but its attachment must meet the support. If a gap remains between chrysalis and branch, correct the sketch instead of hiding the break with shadow. Let the branch extend beyond the frame to suggest a larger plant; the fragment does not identify a species.

## Build one complete chrysalis silhouette

Draw a short narrow transition below the attachment, then close a shape with a wider middle and slightly tapered lower end. The two sides need not be perfectly symmetric, but do not add adult wings. Fill with a single mid-green and hide the sketch to check that the silhouette remains complete. If it resembles a flat leaf, adjust width or the lower curve before adding shading; golden spots cannot repair an unclear outline. Do not invent a complete texture for the unseen side of a reference.

## Distinguish silk attachment from the body

Paint a small light attachment area touching the branch underside, then join a narrow connection to the top of the green body. Keep attachment, connection and body distinct without exaggerating this small structure into a thick rope. Darker green or gray-brown can mark each join, with a light background around it. At thumbnail size, can the eye follow branch, attachment and top of the chrysalis? If not, remove nearby background lines first so leaves are not mistaken for another connection.

## Model volume with values

Choose upper left as the exercise light source. Retain a broad lighter green on the left, add a lower-opacity darker green on the right and connect the bottom with an intermediate color. A soft narrow highlight is enough; avoid outlining the entire edge in white. Compare the three greens in small canvas-corner swatches before applying them and keep dark green distinguishable from black. If layers become muddy, undo the latest one, reduce contrast or opacity and retain an earlier version for comparison.

## Place a few gold spots

Study the general relationship of the spots to the upper body in the source. On a separate detail layer, add a small group of yellow-gold dots with varied spacing. Narrow dots near the contour can follow the curved form; avoid making every dot identical or arranging them as an absolutely horizontal screen line. Lighten one or two, keeping the rest quieter. If the highlights overpower the outline, hide the details and correct the body values first. The golden appearance is an observation of color, not a measurement of material composition.

## Arrange background and overlap

On the background layer, place two blurred green-gray leaf shapes with breathing room around the chrysalis. They need not identify a real plant, but do not run a vein through the attachment. If adding a foreground leaf, cover only a small portion of branch, not the whole chrysalis. Check depth order before simplifying detail. In a thumbnail view, the subject should separate from the background and the attachment remain legible. Avoid overwriting the original when experimenting with layer visibility.

## Make an attachment control

In a blank area draw two small diagrams. Connect the narrow attachment to the branch underside in one and deliberately leave a short gap in the other. Keep only branch, connection, green body and light background; compare thumbnails for the floating impression. Label the incorrect sample as a control so it is not confused with the final piece. In the finished image check the sequence of joins rather than copying the control's scale. If it disappears at small size, slightly widen the visible connecting color and verify it does not become a thick rope. This tests legibility, not a measurement of a real chrysalis.

## Check, export and record

Hide the gold spots, background and sketch in turn to check the complete body and connected support. Save the editable draft, export a separate image through a share or export control actually offered in your version, and open it elsewhere to inspect the attachment, spots and frame. Reopen the draft and check that body and background can be hidden independently. If output differs, first identify the file and visible layers. Deliver illustration, source link and observed steps, marking any unperformed export or reopening as pending. Drawing does not require collecting or touching wildlife; no physical-device or field observation test is claimed.
