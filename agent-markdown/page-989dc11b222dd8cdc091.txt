Check capacities, conservation and directed cuts in a four-vertex five-edge network, then reduce one capacity for a maximum-six control.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-maximum-flow-feasible-ten-cut-capacity-bound/>), translated on 2026-11-19. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Draw a fictional directed network with both a feasible flow and an upper bound for how much can be sent. Capacities are flow limits over one abstract period, not edge lengths or arrival times. One widest route is insufficient: flow may split, but intermediate vertices must conserve it and each edge must obey its own capacity.

## List capacities on four vertices and five edges

Use source S, sink T and intermediate A/B, with only S→A capacity seven, S→B five, A→B three, A→T four, B→T six. On about 1800 × 1400 pixels place S left, A upper-middle, B lower-middle and T right. Group arrows, capacities, flows and cuts, then save. Capacities are nonnegative integers here. A→B does not grant B→A capacity three. Layout length encodes no capacity, and unlisted connections are absent.

## Label each edge with flow slash capacity

Assign flows SA seven, SB three, AB three, AT four, BT six, labelled 7/7, 3/5, 3/3, 4/4, 6/6. These are flow/capacity labels, not probabilities or fractions to reduce to one. Check 0≤flow≤capacity on every edge. SB has two unused units, but that alone cannot increase total flow. Source output 7+3 and sink input 4+6 both equal ten. Check both totals, then intermediate vertices; matching endpoint totals alone can hide flow creation or loss inside.

## Check conservation separately at A and B

At A, inflow seven equals AB three plus AT four. At B, SB three plus AB three equals BT six. Put each equation beside its own vertex rather than replacing them with one global statement. Decompose into S–A–T four, S–A–B–T three and S–B–T three, totalling ten and adding contributions on shared edges. Filling each path independently would overuse BT; check shared edges before allocation. This network has no circulation and represents no pipe or communication test. The maximum value need not determine every edge flow uniquely. Another flow SA five, SB five, AB one, AT four, BT six respects all capacities, conserves five=one+four at A and five+one=six at B, and still has value ten. Save it as a distinct equal-value allocation without changing capacities or the minimum cut. The first solution’s SA seven does not mean every optimum saturates SA, and unused capacity on one edge does not prove more total flow is possible. Optimal value and its realisation are distinct.

## Sum only source-side to sink-side capacities in a cut

Take source side X={S,A,B} and sink side {T}. Outgoing crossing edges AT four and BT six give cut capacity ten. Every feasible flow value is bounded by every S–T cut, so the feasible ten attains this bound. Neither a bound alone nor a feasible flow alone proves optimality. A cut is a vertex partition, not an arbitrary drawn line touching edges. Do not add capacities of reverse-crossing edges to this directed cut capacity. State that X contains S and excludes T.

## Enumerate four source-side sets to verify the minimum cut

With S in and T out, A and B each have two memberships, giving four cuts. Capacities are {S}:7+5=12; {S,A}:5+3+4=12; {S,B}:7+6=13, excluding inward AB; {S,A,B}:4+6=10. The minimum ten matches the feasible flow, with every partition covered. In particular {S,B} is not seven plus three plus six: inward AB is not charged. Enumeration is practical for this small graph, not a promise that a larger drawn network is equally easy to calculate.

```text
capacity: SA=7; SB=5; AB=3; AT=4; BT=6
flow:     SA=7; SB=3; AB=3; AT=4; BT=6
A: 7=3+4; B: 3+3=6; value=7+3=4+6=10
cut capacities: {S}:12; {S,A}:12; {S,B}:13; {S,A,B}:10
control BT capacity=2
control flow SA=4; SB=2; AB=0; AT=4; BT=2
control value=cut({S,A,B})=6
```

## Change both flow and bound after reducing BT

Save a version changing only BT capacity from six to two. Old BT flow six is now invalid, so withdraw total ten. Use SA four, SB two, AB zero, AT four, BT two: A and B conserve flow and total six is feasible. Cut {S,A,B} now has capacity 4+2=6, proving maximum six. Source capacity remains twelve, showing why source outlets alone overestimate throughput. Keep nodes and edges fixed, updating capacity, flow and cut together rather than mixing old labels such as 7/7 into the control.

## Diagnose directions, conservation and cut capacity in order

If total twelve reaches a sink accepting only ten, check AT/BT capacity violations. If endpoints show ten but A fails conservation, repair that vertex instead of moving labels to hide a missing edge. For a cut of sixteen, check inward edges and duplicate capacities; cut capacity is not current flow. Keep the faulty version, read every flow/capacity label, trace paths and verify all four partitions. No costs are given, so maximum ten says nothing about cheapest allocation, shortest S–T route or minimum tree connecting every vertex.
