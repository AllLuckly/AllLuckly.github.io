The essential feature of a stone arch bridge is the space through its opening. Paint a fictional single-arch bridge with coherent deck, body and arch before selected stones and river cues. This is an illustration study, not structural or construction guidance.

## Align the deck with the scene

Use an approximately 1900×1300 px landscape canvas with a bridge showing some deck and side thickness. Establish a shallow box, river and two bank contacts. Separate structure and texture. Correct unsupported ends or a wall-like front through actual deck and side planes before stone marks.

## Reserve the arch within the main mass

Reserve a broad arched opening with upright lower ends and sufficient bridge mass above. Cut it with background colour and visible distant water or bank. Check it as an opening rather than a dark emblem, avoiding a pure-black fill that seals the space.

## Use the inner arch to show depth

Add an offset rear arch and connecting underside with darker upper interior and a lighter exit. Check one coherent tunnel before stones. Connect sticker-like layers or reduce a tyre-like uniform ring, preserving the relationship between the arch and the broader bridge mass.

## Turn the arch stones around the curve

Place a few radiating arch stones with joints turning around the curve, while quieter wall stones follow their own planes. Test a small set before adding more. Soften tooth-like outlines or correct arbitrary brick directions, letting texture reinforce the arch rather than replace it.

## Keep stone values within the broad planes

Use a lighter deck, middle-valued face and darker underside under one light. Vary stone colour within those masses rather than random extremes. Hide joints to test depth. Keep moss local and remove minor seams when the texture fragments the form, simplifying farther planes.

## Connect water and banks beneath the bridge

Continue the river through the opening with darker interior and lighter outer ripples. Ground the bridge feet on banks. Simplify reflection to bridge mass and arch gap with broken water edges, checking vertical correspondence rather than repeating every stone or creating a second competing bridge.

## Use rails and small objects only for scale

Choose a low rail or one small figure as a scale cue, aligned with the deck and subordinate to the bridge. Reduce dense rail lines. Review the opening small, tunnel thickness normally and stray seams close up, preserving background visibility through the arch before adding accessories.

## Study one arch-mouth corner

Enlarge one arch-foot corner and separate front face, inner wall and distant background with shared boundaries. Test pure planes before seams. Reduce a pipe-like ring and restore bridge mass. Use the study to understand connections, not to demand equal detail on every stone.

## Check deck width against the rail

Place rail feet on actual deck edges, keeping near/far spacing consistent with a usable roadway. Simplify distant rail segments. If large posts sit on an almost invisible deck, adjust deck width or rail scale. The traversable top should remain readable without a figure explaining it.

## Compare the untextured bridge

Keep structural, arch-depth and finished versions with separate seams. Next, turn the bridge to a more oblique view and rebuild deck width, front/rear arches and bank contacts. Do not simply squash the image; identify the edge most useful for tunnel depth before deciding stone count.
