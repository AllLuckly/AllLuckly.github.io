A handsaw with huge teeth on both edges becomes a decorative feather; a filled handle hole becomes a solid board. ## Separate handle and blade in a wide composition

Use an approximately 1900×1100 px canvas, handle left and tapered blade right, gently tilted but fully visible. Sketch blade envelopes, broad heel and narrower toe, with handle behind the heel. Add blade width and handle-hole space if dagger-like; avoid a double-edged needle. Adjust heel and back contour if ruler-like. Pixels are not inches and tilt is not a cutting angle. Reserve room below for teeth before detail.

## Keep one continuous blade face

Fill one continuous pale-grey blade with a smoother back and temporary tooth envelope. Connect its heel broadly to the handle rather than by a fine tether. A narrow thickness edge is enough, not a wooden wedge. Repair accidental reflection holes or crossing outer edges before teeth. Metal needs no complete mirrored environment; a few broad highlights stay within the blade without splitting it into separate silver strips.

## Enclose a genuine handle opening

Build a thick handle around a closed background opening, shaping the hole independently rather than a uniform inset. Keep upper bridge and lower grip continuous, without an open C-hook. Restore ground and restrained inner-wall shade if the hole looks like a sticker. Widen critical connections if the handle becomes a thin ring. Choose wood or plastic colours without branding. Opening size serves illustration, not hand fit or ergonomic measurement.

## Connect the blade heel into the handle

Overlap the broad heel with the handle front, deciding visible metal and occluded edges. Two optional invented fastener caps belong in solid handle material, not the hole. Move paper-like insertion out of the opening into its connection area. Support floating caps with material and simplify them. Two is an illustration choice, not universal. Omit exploded plates, hidden rods and transparent slots; exterior overlap must establish contact without invented internals.

## Run teeth along the chosen lower edge only

Hand-draw small teeth and gullets along the lower envelope, establishing heel/middle/toe rhythm before connecting them. Keep one coherent toothed side and a smoother back; symmetry does not justify a second row. Reduce square zipper-like forms, or shorten comb-like teeth and reconnect their roots. Abbreviated count establishes no teeth-per-inch, edge angle or suitability for any material.

## Keep highlights from erasing the gullets

Clarify gullets in mid-grey before restrained longitudinal highlights, without filling every notch. Keep upper-left lighting consistent and distinguish ground from handle inner wall. Shadows stay softer than the blade and must not form another saw; abbreviated tooth shadows should not become a second main row. Hide wear specks if busy and restore a broad light plane if too dark. ## Inspect three negative-space relationships first

Hide material and check closed handle hole, solid heel attachment and gullets opening to external ground. Repair internal tooth holes or accidental breaks in the handle bridge/grip. Trace heel to toe for stray separate strips. Adjust the invented view to reveal contact instead of transparent hidden metal. Record precise faults such as highlights filling rear gullets or a cap inside the hole, repairing them individually rather than darkening the whole saw.
