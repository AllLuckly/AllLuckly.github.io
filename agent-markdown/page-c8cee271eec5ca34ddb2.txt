The same dots may belong to the original or mark an omission by someone quoting it. Bracketed words may also come from someone other than the quoted author. Retrieve short quotations in Book Isle and separate original wording, omissions, additions and personal paraphrase. This does not impose one style on every publication or assume automatic source recognition, recovery of omitted wording or academic-style compliance checking.

## Choose a short quotation with a source

Find a short quotation traceable to its source. Record the quoting book’s title, edition, chapter and position, plus its cited source. Distinguish the quoting voice from the quoted author and inspect notices of abridgement or editorial brackets. Use material you may check without copying long restricted passages for practice. If the source is unavailable, practise with two invented sentences explicitly attributed to no author. Keep the real quotation pending rather than reconstructing a supposed complete original from memory. Fixed locations and editions provide the reference needed to explain later differences.

## Keep source wording and current quotation side by side

Invent this source: A put the paper hat on the table because the paint was not dry. B waited until evening to take it away. The practice quotation may read A put the paper hat on the table … B waited until evening to take it away, explicitly marking removal of the reason clause by the practice quoter. Preserve source and quotation separately rather than overwriting either. For real material, confirm edition and paragraph; different translations may explain differences without omission. Attach location, voice and completeness status to both columns. Similar words do not establish an exact source match.

## Do not confuse original dots with an omission

If the original says I thought … perhaps tomorrow, the dots may be authorial expression rather than a recoverable missing passage. Compare the source and label original ellipsis, added omission mark or unconfirmed. Publication styles differ in dots, spaces and brackets; this task tracks provenance rather than assigning a fixed number of omitted sentences to dot counts. Dots alone establish neither tampering nor omission length. Identifying what was removed requires original context or an explicit explanation, not punctuation appearance.

## Mark each bracketed addition and its author

In another invented example, expand he to he \[A\] as a reference clarification. A is added by the practice quoter; it did not suddenly appear as a name in the original. Real publications may use square brackets, other bracket shapes or another convention, so inspect editorial guidance first. Record added wording, contributor, reason and matching source position; mark an unknown contributor unknown. Clarification needs contextual support even when its bracketed wording sounds certain. Distinguish brackets already in the original from additions instead of classifying every bracketed passage as a later alteration.

## Check whether omission removed a limiting condition

Removing because the paint was not dry preserves the action but loses its reason. The shorter quotation no longer presents the author’s complete explanation of motive. Omitting a condition, negative, exception or time limit may matter still more; reread each against the original paragraph. State what the quotation still supports and what it no longer supports. Fluency alone does not establish complete fidelity. Retaining context does not require reproducing it all: lawful short excerpts and locators can suffice. Write interpretation separately; do not restore missing limits inside quotation marks through unlabelled paraphrase.

## Identify your own excerpt editing

If shortening a quotation yourself, first preserve the source scope needed for internal checking. In a shareable copy, mark omissions and clarifications according to the intended style. Identify your edits without claiming earlier quoter edits as yours or attributing your additions to the author. A study note may instead use an explicitly personal paraphrase, avoiding precise-looking quotation marks around mixed sources. Check the applicable style edition separately; one webpage example does not govern every genre. Excerpting or export in Book Isle preserves available material but does not perform source verification for you.

## Retain the gap when the original cannot be found

For missing search hits, check spelling, translator, edition and searchable-text scope, or locate a distinctive nearby phrase. Navigate an unselectable scan manually without claiming recovery of omitted content. If a reference names a chapter without a page, state what range you actually checked rather than pretending an exact match. If unresolved, mark unverifiable, neither accusing improper editing nor certifying completeness. For an app preservation or display issue, report a self-made minimal example separately from the real quotation dispute. A textual provenance gap is not automatically missing letters in the reader.

## Audit every edit’s provenance after returning

Leave the reader and return in order to quotation, source passage and editorial explanation. Check both locators and the provenance of every dot and addition. In the hat example, identify the omitted reason clause and explain that action remains while reason is missing. In the he \[A\] example, identify the name as the quoter’s clarification. Separate original punctuation, omitted scope, additions, personal interpretation and unchecked points in the summary. Reopen a separately saved note externally to ensure export retained its markers, preserving the book independently. Completion requires provenance or an unknown label for each alteration, not confidence based on attractive quotation formatting.
