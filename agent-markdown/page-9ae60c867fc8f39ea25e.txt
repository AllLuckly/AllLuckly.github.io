The inverse of seven modulo twenty-six is an integer giving remainder one after multiplication, not merely seven in a denominator. ## State the multiplication-to-remainder-one target

Seek integer r with 7r−1 an integer multiple of 26, written 7r≡1 mod 26. Choose representatives 0..25 if desired; negatives may represent the same class. Modulus 26 is fixed positive, not a quotient result. Manual conditions govern the diagram.

## Retain quotient and remainder in every division

Write 26=3×7+5,7=1×5+2,5=2×2+1,2=2×1+0. Each remainder is smaller than its divisor; the last nonzero remainder 1 gives gcd 1. Arrows pass divisor/remainder onward, not a factor tree. ## Back-substitute from one

## Multiply back and check remainder one

## Keep nonexistence when coprimality fails
