A lotus head’s many openings remain flat if identical black dots simply fill an ellipse. Draw a simplified dry seed head by establishing top direction and lower volume before selected recesses. Hole count is not species identification; shape and palette are original study choices. The focus is perspective, thickness and walls of openings on one tilted surface.

## Establish the top and tapering body

Use an approximately 1500×1600 px canvas with a top tilted slightly toward the viewer. Begin with a broad ellipse tapering into a short conical lower body and stem. Separate top, sidewall and stem layers, using a pale warm-grey background and an initial solid mid-brown body. Reduce an overhanging brim and restore continuous sides if it resembles a hat; taper the lower body more if bucket-like. Save a hole-free structural version. Every later opening belongs to this volume; complex dots cannot turn an unresolved flat surface into a completed form.

## Connect a narrow top rim to the sidewall

Give the near top edge a narrow thickness and gently uneven outline rather than insisting on a perfect geometric circle. Taper the sidewall downward, with mainly lengthwise marks rather than the same grid as the openings. Under upper-left light, the top is lighter and right/lower sides darker. Repair a separate-plate top through shared edge and thickness, not a heavy black ring. Reduce equally spaced deep grooves in a barrel-like sidewall and retain broad natural variation. Establish the planes before selected texture.

## Test hole placement with pale marks

Place six to nine provisional pale points, spaced slightly wider nearby and compressed farther away without a perfect checkerboard. Keep centres on the top rather than accidentally cutting into the side. Preserve a wider quiet patch instead of filling every corner. Correct a printed-fabric appearance through compression and irregular rhythm. Reduce crowded hole count before shrinking every opening into invisibility. Pale marks are movable placement guides; settle them before dark openings make repositioning harder to judge.

## Relate opening ellipses to the top plane

Establish each opening’s outer ellipse with its major axis approximately following the top plane, larger nearby and smaller farther away with some natural variation. Do not mix a frontal circle with a radically tilted neighbour unless the supporting plane changes. Resolve three near holes before extending backward. Check shared inner/outer directions in a button-like result before adding white lights. Walls and recesses come later; first make repeated openings belong to one plane and one reference.

## Use walls to turn the surface into a recess

A recess may have a lighter wall and deeper bottom, with visible near-wall width determined by viewpoint rather than an identical black ring. Use a small muted deep-brown area instead of pure black, retaining visible wall around it. Add a narrow sidewall and less uniform edge to ink dots, or reduce protruding lights and wall width in a row-of-bowls result. Test inner/outer ellipse relations enlarged, then retain only sufficient information in the main drawing. Not every opening needs its entire bottom visible at the same time.

## Vary recess values with position and light

A hole on the lit top may have a clearer lip without its entire cavity always being darkest. Quiet far holes and clarify one or two near ones. Keep shared top lighting between openings, not an independent light source for each. Test the complete top with fine holes hidden. Reduce equal contrast and enclosed contours in a honeycomb-icon result, or strengthen one near wall for depth rather than darken all holes. One clear example can explain the rest of the simplifications.

## Separate side texture from opening structure

Use selected short and long side marks converging from the rim toward the base, weaker than opening structure. Limit dry edge nicks rather than serrating the whole perimeter. Stop side texture before top openings: these are different surfaces. Connect a slightly bent stem continuously at the bottom rather than balancing it on a separate straight rod. Establish its plain form before a few marks. Direction, edges and restrained colour can explain dryness without mandatory random speckles.

## Choose one support state

Here the head leans on a table, contacting a low sidewall and a stem section, each with small contact shade and a softer lower-right cast shadow. Do not combine a hanging pose with black ground contacts around every lower edge. Review total shape small, top tilt normally and wall gaps close up. Repair floating through contact position, or reduce large darks and hard reflections in a heavy stone-like object. Dry material still has volume; weight depends on shape/shadow relationships rather than a pale-brown outline alone.

## Compare one, three and all openings

In copies show only one principal hole, then the three nearest, then all openings, comparing when volume starts becoming pattern. Quiet far holes if three already explain the structure; count should not sacrifice form. A temporary common pale grey tests near/far placement compression independently of colour. Restore intended colours and retain comparisons to distinguish arrangement, walls and values. Repair the relevant contribution rather than repaint all texture simultaneously.

## Rebuild walls for a changed top tilt

Keep plain mass, provisional hole placement and PNG, with an independent hole layer. Next, tilt the top farther edge-on and rebuild ellipse compression, near/far openings and visible inner walls while palette and light stay fixed. Uniformly flattening dots misses changing sidewalls and cavity exposure. Check that principal recesses survive at use size, simplifying fine texture if needed. Record a formerly wide wall becoming narrow and judge that spatial change before deciding hole count next time.
