English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/nasbook-flashback-story-order-log/>), translated on 2026-11-08. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A story can show an umbrella returned before describing its damage, separating reading order from event order. Select a short passage in Book Isle and record narration positions separately from story time, including the return from a recollection to the current scene. This differs from publication-date or historical chronology logs and assumes no automatic plot timeline, flashback detection or contradiction correction.

## Select a short passage and mark telling positions

Choose a permitted passage with one clear recollection or time shift and record book, edition, chapter and nearby wording. Label fragments A,B,C,D in reading order as personal note labels, not replacement chapter numbers. Give each a locator, retaining necessary time words through supported selection or manual page/description notes without claiming scan-text extraction. Restrict the task to one object or action chain instead of copying all characters or plot, reducing confusion between similar events.

## Define the two orders with four invented fragments

Write an external invented sequence: A, “On Friday, Lin returned the repaired umbrella to the shop”; B, “Three days earlier, its rib broke at the station”; C, “On Thursday, the shopkeeper replaced a part”; D, “The previous Sunday, a friend lent Lin the umbrella”. These are original examples, not quotations from the selected novel. Define B’s relative reference as A’s Friday, giving Tuesday of that week; D precedes that Tuesday. Telling order remains A,B,C,D. Do not reorder source fragments chronologically and then attribute that order to the author. Preserve the initial sequence for comparison.

## Order events only where evidence supports it

In a separate list, order D lending, B damage, C repair, A return. Preserve each original fragment label and time evidence so chronology leads back to reading positions. Do not invent lending time, return motive or repair cost absent from the four fragments. Where wording establishes only earlier/later, record that relation rather than assigning a date. This is personal organisation, not a replacement for narration. Keeping both orders reveals an outcome-before-background arrangement without “correcting” the novel into a supposedly mandatory chronological telling.

## Identify the scene anchoring each relative time

In the actual novel, anchor yesterday, that year or three days earlier to the current scene, a day inside a recollection or the character’s speaking moment, rather than always the chapter opening. Record the anchor, such as “relative to Friday in fragment A”, with supporting wording. Nested recollections may need another level, but keep this exercise short and mark unclear anchors unknown. Font and paging changes do not change story time or automatically reset the reference. Do not insert your real reading date into a fictional yesterday and invent a calendar the author never supplied.

## Separate remembered accounts from established events

If a character says “I remember it being repaired that day”, record a remembered claim rather than omniscient confirmation. If another remembers a later time, preserve both sources and wording, then inspect later corrections. Do not delete one for a tidy timeline or claim an import-order error. Dates are explicit in the invented model; a novel may be less certain. Label events as direct narration, memory, reported account or personal inference under your external convention, without implying an automatic Book Isle reliability score.

## Locate the return from recollection to the current scene

After the inserted passage, find renewed actions, setting, characters or time words that establish where narration returns. Link the return to the current scene near original A rather than assuming all subsequent material remains in the past. Without an explicit marker, note a possible return and reasons, then read slightly farther; paragraph indentation alone does not prove a time change. External arrows for narrative jumps need wording distinct from chronological arrows. App bookmarks can locate passages, not establish the interpretation of recollection boundaries.

## Retrieve the source through both lists and inspect export

Leave the reader, revisit four positions in telling order A,B,C,D and then the same positions in event order D,B,C,A, excluding another umbrella belonging to a same-named character. Use the actual work’s content rather than forcing the invented order. Export important notes through available means and inspect both label systems, anchors, source identities and uncertainties externally, preserving the book separately. Attach edition to necessary shared quotations, without presenting your ordering as the contents. Restore the prior reading position; progress changes during retrieval do not mean the story has reached a later event.

## Resolve disagreements through anchors, sources and identity

If Tuesday cannot be derived, check the reference day for three days earlier. For chronological conflict, check event identity or differing character accounts instead of editing source dates to fit. If only one order remains, restore narration positions rather than reducing the novel to an event summary. Preserve partial order and unknowns where evidence is insufficient; do not fill every gap through common sense. Verify original telling order, supported event order, time anchors and return location, each traceable to wording. This supports continued reading without claiming a complete authorial plan or universal narration reliability.
