## Fix the balanced equation and approximate molar masses

Write 2H2+O2→2H2O with specified approximate molar masses 2, 32, 18 g/mol. Start with 4g H2 and 16g O2, assuming the limiting reactant is completely consumed by this stoichiometry, without other reactions. Put conditions/mass bars on P1, moles on P2 and yield controls on P3; export PDF and retain source work. Check H2/O2 subscripts and bookmark P2. Coefficient 2 multiplies whole molecules/moles; do not alter subscripts to force a remainder.

Integer molar-mass approximations keep conversion transparent. More precise atomic masses require consistent recalculation of mass digits, not different balanced coefficients. Four grams do not become four moles through a more precise constant. Verify identity/units before significant figures; detailed calculation does not imply measured inputs.

## Divide masses by molar masses before comparison

4g H2 is 4/2=2mol; 16g O2 is 16/32=0.5mol. The available mole ratio 4:1 exceeds required 2:1, so hydrogen is excess and oxygen limiting. 16g being greater than 4g does not guarantee oxygen remains. Separate mass, molar mass, amount and multiply back 2mol×2g/mol,0.5mol×32g/mol. A gram-encoded bar is not simultaneously a mole bar; retain its legend.

## Compare stoichiometric reaction amounts supported by each reactant

One stoichiometric reaction amount consumes 2mol H2 and 1mol O2. Hydrogen supports 2/2=1mol reaction amount, oxygen 0.5/1=0.5mol; take 0.5. Compare n divided by its coefficient, not raw mole counts for every equation. Oxygen happens to have fewer moles here, but different coefficients can alter which is limiting. Ratio requirements and supported reaction amounts must agree, without deciding from a visually shortened arrow.

## Compute consumption before retaining excess hydrogen

A 0.5 mol reaction amount consumes 1mol H2=2g and 0.5mol O2=16g, forming 1mol water=18g. Hydrogen leaves 1mol=2g; oxygen leaves zero. Show water and unreacted hydrogen together; completion does not erase every reactant. Zero is ideal complete consumption, not an analytical detection limit. Mass balances 4+16=20g against 18+2=20g, not water alone against every initial gram.

Reverse 18g theoretical water: one mole requires 1mol H2=2g and 0.5mol O2=16g. But another input 2g H2/32g O2 also yields 18g water, with hydrogen limiting and 16g oxygen left. Equal product does not identify initial mixture or limiting reagent. Keep starting conditions instead of turning minimum consumption into exact initial supply.

```text
2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O
approx molar masses: H2=2, O2=32, H2O=18 g/mol
initial: H2=4 g=2 mol; O2=16 g=0.5 mol
reaction amounts: 2/2=1; 0.5/1=0.5 mol -> O2 limiting
consume H2=1 mol=2 g; O2=0.5 mol=16 g
water theoretical=1 mol=18 g; H2 remaining=1 mol=2 g
reported collected water=13.5 g -> percent yield=75%
```

## A theoretical ceiling is not a collected mass

P3 separately reports fictional collected water 13.5g. Percent yield=13.5/18×100%=75%. The report is a new input, not a measured result implied by balancing. The 4.5g gap does not identify a byproduct or evaporation loss without evidence, and 75% does not change stoichiometric coefficients. Collected mass alone does not separate conversion from collection losses, so infer no unique revised hydrogen remainder.

A report of 22.5g pure collected water gives 125% relative to 18g theoretical. The ratio is calculable, but does not establish extra matter or a valid breach of the specified pure-product bound. Check initial quantities, purity, collected mass and version identity. Moisture or other sources remain hypotheses, not established causes. Preserve the report and discrepancy rather than change the theoretical 18 to 22.5.

## Increasing only oxygen reaches the hydrogen bound

Change only oxygen 16g→64g while hydrogen stays 4g. Hydrogen is 2mol, oxygen 2mol, supporting 1 versus 2mol reaction amount; hydrogen now limits. Theoretical water 2mol=36g and leftover oxygen 1mol=32g balance 36+32=68g=4+64. Four times oxygen yields only twice the water, not 72g, because hydrogen becomes the bound. Keep approximate molar masses/full-reaction assumptions and do not simultaneously import 75% collection yield.

## Diagnose coefficients, units and theoretical versus actual status

Calling hydrogen limiting because 4g is smaller misses moles/coefficients; 20g water misses excess hydrogen; altering the equation after 75% confuses theoretical and collected values. Enlarge subscripts/g/mol, repair one field and balance mass. With one initial amount missing, calculate from it only if the other reactant is explicitly sufficient; otherwise limiting identity stays unknown. Coloured dots are legend-based symbols, not product-purity or reaction-rate evidence.
