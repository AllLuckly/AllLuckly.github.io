The great hammerhead’s cephalofoil belongs to its head, not another pectoral pair on a thin neck. Using adult Sphyrna mokarran as a structural reference, draw an obliquely elevated swimming pose, joining head and trunk before dorsal fins and unequal tail lobes. This records no measured speed and determines no field identity, size or behavioural safety.

## Place broad head and narrow tail on one action axis

Use an approximately 1900×1400 px canvas, head lower-left and trunk extending upper-right toward the tail root, leaving fin-tip space. Establish one action line and streamlined trunk, reserving a transverse head region before teeth or splashes. Taper posteriorly without reducing the head connection to a thin rod. Fix near side and dorsal surface for the oblique view instead of splicing overhead and pure side views. Pixels establish no body length or speed. First make head, trunk and tail continuously readable.

## Give the adult cephalofoil a near-straight notched front

Draw a broad flattened head with a nearly straight leading edge and shallow central notch, not repeated scallops. Return both ends into a substantial head-to-trunk connection rather than a pasted crossbar. This adult great-hammerhead simplification is not universal for juveniles or other hammerheads. Perspective can change apparent side lengths; do not force symmetry by breaking surface turns. If it resembles another wing pair, reduce exaggerated tips and restore thickness instead of adding a thin separate neck.

## Attach eyes laterally to the head ends

Place small lateral eye areas at the head ends, clearer near and only as exposed far. They are neither balls on antennae nor a central cartoon eye pair. Establish attachment on the flattened head sides before minimal highlights. Omit a complete ventral mouth, nostril and sensory-pore map without claiming absence. A concealed far eye is preferable to moving a large circle onto the dorsal face to force two visible eyes. Placement supplies no measured visual field or detection range.

## Separate paired pectorals from median dorsal fins

Attach paired pectorals to trunk sides behind the head, with the far one partly concealed. The tall recurved first dorsal rises from the median back rather than a pectoral tip. Check the posterior second dorsal against the reference instead of duplicating an equally huge triangle. Show other fins only where the view and reference support them; this is no exhaustive fin census. Check paired versus median attachments in pale colours before unifying. Random spikes need corrected roots, not darker paint.

## Keep unequal lobes in the vertical caudal fin

Taper the tail stock into one vertical caudal fin with a longer upper and shorter lower lobe. Oblique compression does not make it a horizontally symmetric pair. Trace stock-to-upper-lobe continuity before lower lobe and notch, without detached thread-like tips. Proportions are simplified rather than age measurements. Repair a pasted bow-tie tail through root transition and plane. Clarify the outline small without enlarging the upper lobe implausibly just to emphasise inequality.

## Turn darker back into a lighter underside

Use muted grey-brown, darker dorsally and lighter ventrally, with the transition following head and trunk volume rather than a rigid straight waistline. Separate pectoral planes modestly and keep dorsal highlights restrained instead of outlining every fin white. A subdued water ground supplies no measured depth, clarity or speed. Restore broad turning tones to a paper-flat body or reduce hard bands making it metallic. Grey colour neither universalises colouration nor diagnoses health.

## Audit head wings separately from pectorals

Hide water marks and highlights. Trace both eye areas to one broad head and both pectorals to trunk sides; these are not four equivalent fins. Check median first dorsal, vertical unequal tail and hidden-line stops. Repair repeated head scallops against the stated adult reference without universalising all hammerheads. Clarify a lost upper lobe locally instead of moving its root. Record part and viewpoint. These limited reference cues do not replace complete identification.

## Reopen and check head attachment and tail plane

Save native work, hide axis and checking colours and reopen PNG. Read broad head, tall dorsal and unequal tail small, then inspect eye attachment and fin/tail roots enlarged, keeping complete boundaries. Stop overly clear far-side roots showing through solid body. If head and pectorals merge, change broad tones before fine edges. Reopen editable regions and note adult reference, oblique view and omissions. Completion concerns readable structure and direction, not photography, measured results or automatic marine-animal recognition.
