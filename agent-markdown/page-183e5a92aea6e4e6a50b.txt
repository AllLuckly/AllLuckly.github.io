Draw a simplified bicycle side view with matched wheels, coherent frame joins and consistent line weight.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-bicycle-linework/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Two wheels and a few frame lines offer a useful exercise in shared references and connections. Draw a fictional side-view bicycle with recognisable structure. It is an illustration, not a manufacturing drawing, model identification or mechanical safety assessment.

## Start with matching wheels

Use an 1100 by 700 pixel canvas. Duplicate one unfilled wheel circle and place both centres on a common horizontal reference, leaving space for pedals and handlebars. Check spacing before spokes. If adding concentric tyre and rim circles, use the same construction for both. Mark centres temporarily; these are visual proportions, not measured bicycle geometry.

## Build the frame around two triangular relationships

Locate the pedal axis between wheels and establish the main frame triangle and rear connection. Start with unfilled paths of consistent weight. Inspect endpoint joins and avoid tiny disconnected gaps. Hide the wheels briefly to assess the frame itself, then restore them to check how it meets the wheel centres.

## Connect fork, saddle and handlebars

Connect the fork to the front wheel centre, saddle above the seat tube and handlebars through a short stem. Keep details generic. Ensure the saddle has a visible attachment and adjust saddle/handlebar height as a compositional relationship. Move stems with their associated parts and use separate component groups to preserve joins.

## Add selected spokes and pedals

Begin with four to six lighter spokes per wheel and omit density that becomes noise. Represent the crank with a small circle, opposing arms and simple pedals; optional fine chain paths should remain clear. Every detail needs an identifiable attachment. Prioritise the main frame before adding mechanical rhythm.

## Resolve crossings through deliberate stacking

Decide front/back relationships before resolving intersections with stacking or limited background-coloured support shapes. Preserve complete reference lines when segmenting copies. Keep frame weight consistent and avoid dark knots from overlapping translucent strokes. Toggle spokes to inspect frame joins and reduce detail contrast where it overwhelms the bicycle.

## Choose detail for the delivery size

Save the original and compare illustration and icon-sized exports for distinct wheels, frame triangle and handlebars. Reduce spokes and chain detail while preserving wheel geometry. Check background-coloured support shapes on dark backgrounds before transparent delivery. Name simplified versions separately and update their structural joins when revising the main illustration.

## Check a minimal structural version

Hide spokes, chain and decoration, retaining wheels, frame, saddle and bars. Confirm recognition at a small size. In a copy, move the rear wheel horizontally and maintain its frame joins; ordinary lines do not automatically follow a changed centre. Restore and recheck. Keep a simplified source so small exports do not require repeatedly deleting the same details.

## Diagnose unstable wheels

For elliptical wheels, restore aspect ratios before moving them. Diagnose the frame from pedal axis and wheel centres rather than shifting every node. Inspect cap and overlap geometry for spikes. Compare visible outer diameters, including tyres, when one wheel seems larger. Re-export and reopen the file after corrections.
