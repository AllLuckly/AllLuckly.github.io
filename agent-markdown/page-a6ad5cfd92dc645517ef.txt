Yellow-black markings can hide structural faults such as legs attached to the abdomen or four wings radiating from behind the head. Draw an original schematic honey bee in a resting oblique upper view with wings slightly spread. Check three body regions and appendage roots before texture, without copying private photographs or identifying a precise bee species.

## Establish three regions before stripes

Use an approximately 1700×1500 px canvas, head upper left and abdomen lower right. Connect head, thicker thorax and tapering longer abdomen as volumes, not loose beads. Check the pale-grey axis before hairs/stripes. Distinguish thorax/head in caterpillar-like forms and restore a separate head in spider-like two-region forms. Pixels and proportions measure neither body length nor sex/caste. Attach appendages later rather than using random short lines as six legs.

## Keep antennae and eyes separate from legs

Show visible compound-eye surfaces and a pair of bending antennae, rooted at the head with tapered ends. Occlude far parts instead of placing both on the near side. Antennae neither touch ground nor originate on the thorax. Exclude them from an eight-leg miscount and replace ear-like shapes with slender bent appendages. Abbreviate mouthparts rather than inventing a long extended apparatus. These features establish the head, not precise species identification.

## Attach three leg pairs to the thorax

Mark fore/middle/hind leg-pair roots under the thorax and trace six legs in temporary colours. Show near legs clearly and abbreviated far portions without moving roots to the abdomen. Bend joints rather than drawing needles. Recount antennae if eight legs appear, and move abdomen-rooted hind legs back to the thorax. Detailed segment terminology is unnecessary before direction/ownership. Six legs does not mean all bear weight simultaneously, and the pose is not a measured locomotion record.

## Separate fore- and hindwings before transparency

Place a fore- and hindwing on each thoracic side, larger forewings and partly overlapped smaller hindwings. Count four temporary shapes before occlusion. Do not merge each side into one while claiming two pairs, or root them on the head. Adjust the invented opening to reveal far hindwing clues without transparency through the thorax. Coupling is referenced but microscopic hooks are omitted; separated schematic wings prove no flight state. Add translucency only after attachment.

## Keep sparse veins within their own wings

Use very pale warm-grey wing fills and restrained root-to-edge veins, never crossing ground gaps between wings. Slightly darken overlaps without exposing all body patterns through them. Treat near wings first and soften far ones, avoiding four black fan blades. Remove web-like excess crossings and soften glass-knife edges. Vein counts are artistic simplification, not taxonomic venation or an assumed automatic mesh-generation/recognition feature.

## Let hairs and abdominal bands modify surfaces only

Use short thoracic hairs and stippling; curve abdominal bands around volume, compressing where the surface turns. Bands are not detached rings or body breaks. Remove stripes leaking into wings and reduce hair concealing leg roots. Pollen loads are optional, not identical balls on every leg. Invented bee colours/patterns neither reproduce a specimen nor establish species, health or real-world danger.

## Check appendage counts by body-region roots

Hide bands, veins and hairs. Identify head/thorax/abdomen, then three leg pairs and two wing pairs on the thorax, plus antennae on the head. Correct counts still need correct roots. Recover obscured attachments rather than adding wings. Keep left/right labels on guides and hidden contours hidden. Reduce to check the whole bee, then restore restrained surfaces. Record faults such as an unattached far hindwing or misclassified middle leg; yellow/black colouring alone proves no structural accuracy.

## Reopen and verify six legs and four wings without transparency

Save native work, hide temporary colours, export PNG and reopen. Check regions and paired wings small, then trace six roots, two antennae and fore/hind overlaps enlarged. Recover a lost hindwing through angle or local tone, not by making it larger than a forewing. Remove exported labels in the original and reopen editable groups. Note a resting schematic, not a specimen. Success concerns categories, counts and roots, not classification, microscopy, live-animal testing or automatic organ recognition.
