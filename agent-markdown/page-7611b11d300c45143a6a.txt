A padlock shackle needs two side runs, a shared curved top and two entries into the body, not just an arc floating above it. Draw a stationary closed padlock and inspect exterior connections without unlocking, disassembly or security-performance instruction. Arrange shapes and reflection manually in Aquarell. A closed-looking image does not establish real locking, and the keyway is a surface cue rather than an internal mechanism replica.

## Establish body depth and a shared upright direction

Use an approximately 1500×1800 px portrait canvas with pale blue-grey background. Turn the body slightly right, exposing front and right side. Establish a rounded rectangular block with room above for the shackle and below for shadow, using muted brass-brown. Separate sketch, body, shackle, entry darks and shadow. Keep corner and side-depth perspective consistent. Add modest side depth if icon-flat or reduce it if brick-like. Align shackle and body upright directions rather than combining a tilted body with a frontal arc solely for symmetry.

## Connect both shackle sides through one curved top

Locate two side runs and one rounded top in an inverted U, similar in width and directed into the body. Sketch its centreline, then add round metal width rather than a flat arch slab. Keep bends smooth without corners or a third branch. Connect endpoints if it resembles a ring floating above a box; narrow a plastic-handle-like shackle and retain the opening. Background must remain inside the U rather than body colour. A perfect semicircle is unnecessary, but the curve must read as one continuous metal piece.

## Place both entries on the body top

Mark two entries on the body top and conceal small lower shackle portions as they enter. A near opening may be clearer and a far one compressed, but neither endpoint belongs halfway down the front face. Hide the body to inspect the U, then restore and check real intersections. Move a near-missing endpoint rather than darkening a gap to fake insertion. Account for both sides in this closed pose; raising one end creates a different pose and invalidates the stated check. Omit hidden catches and internal details.

## Keep the keyway local to the front face

Place a small low-front keyway cue aligned to that face, using a narrow dark and a little rim rather than a hole through the whole body. Do not connect it visibly to shackle ports or add a duplicate side keyway for symmetry. Quiet contrast and suggest a recess if button-like; shrink exaggerated mouth-like shapes. This simplified exterior does not reproduce key bitting, cylinder mechanisms or operational information. Finish shackle entry first; a keyway cannot conceal disconnected upper and lower components.

## Differentiate brass body and silver shackle

Give the brass body soft yellow-brown upper-left light and grey-brown side values. Use cooler mid-grey shackle with narrow grey-white reflection. Shared lighting allows different reflection width and temperature. Avoid pure yellow covered in white dots or black-white-black wire stripes. Repair local value turns if plastic-like rather than adding dense scratches; reduce unsupported reflection masses if overly mirrored. Hide reflections and verify U shape and both entries, then restore without letting bright bands redraw concealed shackle ends through the body.

## Avoid a continuous crack around contact

Use short tight entry darks and tight body-ground contact, softening cast shadow lower right. Do not join ports, entire top and side seam into one black crack. Hide ground shadow to inspect completeness, then restore placement. Reconnect floating lower edge or narrow a sinking black band. A modest tilt still needs coherent support rather than half a bottom lying flat while the other rises inexplicably. Background inside the U differs from cast shadow; filling it dark for contrast would turn an open shackle into a solid handle.

## Audit two entries and one opening

Save a monochrome sketch with body, U centreline and both entries. Check two returns to the top and background-connected interior opening. Correct entry dots do not excuse a broken top curve. Use temporary near/far labels and remove them from the finish. Adjust one endpoint or arc while holding body perspective and light fixed. An open variant needs a separate pose with raised endpoint and new occlusion, not merely an erased entry shadow. Paths and positions, not captions alone, must explain its changed state.

## Judge visible closure only in the output

Save the layered original and inspect continuous top curve, two entries and keyway in a PNG at use size. Reduce dark edges and width if the U becomes a black mass rather than whitening all background. Separate metal/top values locally if entries disappear. Record a positional fault such as the far endpoint entering the side instead of top. The picture expresses a closed padlock, not confirmed locking or security rating. These manual layer/preview checks are not mechanical tests. A new view needs revised port ellipses, occlusion and body depth, not mirrored old highlights.
