Paint a side-view adult black skimmer resting on sand, bill pointing right and lower bill tip extending beyond the upper. Cornell's bird resource describes black upperparts, a white belly, short red-orange legs and a lower bill longer than the upper. This exercise designs its own pose and composition; the illustration cannot establish an individual's sex, age, behavior or actual size.

## Place the long bill and body on the canvas

Create an approximately 2000 × 1300 landscape canvas. Put the body slightly left of center, leave room for the right-pointing bill and reserve sand below. Sketch a horizontal body oval and smaller head, then mark upper and lower bills with two light lines; do not begin with dense feather marks. Separate sketch, body, wing, bill, legs and sand layers, and save the draft. Reduce the view to check head, body and bill direction. If the tip touches the edge, move the overall composition rather than shorten the bill.

## Start both bill parts at one mouth corner

From the front of the head draw a flat elongated upper bill, then a lower bill in a similar direction with its end extending farther forward. Both contours must trace back to the base rather than appear as two disconnected pointed sticks. The bird can have a closed bill; the two parts may be close while the longer lower tip remains legible. Check length in one color first, then make the base red-orange and the front black. If the parts look equally long, move the lower tip rather than create a false difference with color.

## Build adjacent dark head, back and pale belly planes

Connect the dark crown and back toward the tail, leaving the belly and lower face lighter. Bend the black–white boundary slightly with body volume rather than drawing a ruler-straight stripe. Place the near wing on its own layer over part of the body. The eye is a small dark point within the head, with enough boundary to read; do not enlarge it into a decorative circle. If dark wing and back merge, separate the adjacent shapes with a slight value difference rather than repainting the back bright blue.

## Fold the wing and retain the tail's place

This exercise uses a resting pose. Fold the long near wing backward along the body and taper the wingtip near the tail; do not combine an extended wing with a resting outline. Draw a gentle upper edge from shoulder to tip and one or two overlapping feather layers. The far wing need not be forced into view for counting. Keep the tail relatively short and straight, adding a slight fork only when actually visible from the chosen reference angle. If the tip looks like a second bill, check that it begins at the shoulder and points backward before adding feathers.

## Connect short legs and webbed feet to sand

Place two short legs below and somewhat toward the rear of the body. The near leg can read clearly while the far one is partly hidden by body or near leg. Color them red-orange and use a small connecting shape to suggest webbing between toes; a side view need not reveal every toe. Put visible soles on the same sand surface and interrupt the ground line where feet cover it. If the bird floats, first join soles to sand, then add narrow contact shadows beside the feet rather than floating ellipses below them.

First block both legs at equal temporary thickness, then hide the far leg and check that the near one still connects body to sole. Restore only the visible part of the far leg and check it does not suddenly emerge from outside the body. If webbing and sand have similar values, use one small light–dark boundary for the foot edge; do not outline the entire bird heavily or present the chosen number of visible toes as a count from a real bird.

## Add beach context without losing the bill

Use light warm gray or beige for sand and a low-contrast distant band for water, keeping horizon and bird distinct. Dot only a few grains in the foreground; keep the background quiet enough for the black tip to read. If that tip disappears against a dark water band, adjust local background value or the composition instead of outlining the entire bird in white. When pale belly and sand are close in value, use foot contact shadow and limited contour contrast rather than turning the whole belly dark gray.

## Hide details and recheck bills and overlaps

Hide feather texture, sand dots and highlights, leaving head and body, two bill portions, near wing, feet and background. Trace the upper bill to its tip and the lower bill to the farther tip, checking both return to one mouth corner. Ensure the wingtip does not pierce the tail contour and the soles meet the ground. If the bill portions are indistinguishable without color, adjust their slightly separated outlines or pose rather than adding a second mouth. Change one relationship at a time and retain the previous draft for comparison.

Save the native draft, export an image through a share or export control actually available in the installed version, and open it elsewhere. At reduced size check the longer lower bill; enlarged, inspect the red-orange base meeting the black front. Reopen the draft and hide only sand dots to ensure foot contact was not accidentally removed. If output differs, check file version and hidden layers first.
