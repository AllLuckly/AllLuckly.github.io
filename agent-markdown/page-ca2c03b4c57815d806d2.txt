## Separate the ellipse centre from its focus

Externally draw x²/25+y²/16=1 with O=(0,0), semimajor a=5, semiminor b=4 and c=√(25−16)=3. Put the dominant mass at S=(3,0); the other focus is a reference, not a second Sun. ## Measure near and far distances from S, not O

Right endpoint A=(5,0) is distance 2 from S, left B=(−5,0) distance 8. Both are five from O, but O is not the attracting focus, so those equal fives do not imply constant speed. Top P=(0,4) is five from S and lies on the ellipse. Draw radius lines from S and label model near/far points without converting abstract lengths to real Earth distances. Zoom changes no 2:8 ratio or stipulated a,b/focus values.

## Keep the curved orbital boundary in the area law

Kepler’s second law compares area swept by the line from S over equal time intervals. A region is bounded by two rays from S and the intervening actual orbit arc, not an endpoint chord substituted as an exact triangle. For an equal-time pair label equal Δt and equal curved swept area. The law requires neither equal arc lengths nor equal angles about O or S. Finite intervals need full curved regions; a narrow triangle is only a small-step approximation.

## Use the upper-half areas to refute equal-angle equal-time reading

Ellipse area πab is 20π and its upper half 10π. O-based ninety-degree regions O–A–P and O–P–B each have quarter-ellipse area 5π. Triangle SOP has area 3×4/2=6. Therefore S-based curved swept areas A to P and P to B are 5π−6 and 5π+6, unequal and requiring unequal times. Their sum returns 10π. Distinguish curved regions from the small straight triangle rather than moving S to O. This is a reproducible decomposition, not area inferred from shading darkness.

Starting a cycle at A, A-to-P time is (5π−6)/(20π) of a period, about 15.451%; P-to-B is (5π+6)/(20π), about 34.549%, totaling half a period. These percentages follow one constant area rate, not each arc divided by a common linear speed. They give concrete time fractions while leaving the actual period unspecified.

## Separate linear speed from angular rate at the apsides

At near/far apsides velocity is perpendicular to the focus-to-body radius. Angular momentum gives r\_near v\_near=r\_far v\_far, so near speed is four times far speed here. Instantaneous area rate is also (1/2)r²ω; with radii 1:4, near angular rate is sixteen times far. Four and sixteen answer different questions, not conflicting answers. This ratio is for the two apsides, not a universal inverse-distance rule for speed everywhere. Elsewhere retain the velocity component perpendicular to radius and its angle condition.

A circular orbit is a separate limiting control: a=b and zero focal distance make focus and centre coincide at fixed radius. Equal angles then do correspond to equal areas and times. The present ellipse-centre counterexample does not invalidate circular equal-angle divisions. Identify orbit/focus conditions before applying a graphical shortcut.
