English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/nasbook-focalization-information-voice-perception/>), translated on 2026-11-16. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Third-person she can occur under different information restrictions; person alone does not settle who knows what. Read three original doorway versions in Book Isle, separating voice, character perception and reader-accessible information. Focalization theories vary; this exercise emphasizes selection/restriction rather than treating a seeing verb as an automatic classifier or assuming app viewpoint recognition.

## Keep three versions separate rather than merging them

J1 A reads:“Mara stopped at the door and turned the key twice. Ivo did not turn around.” B reads:“Mara stopped at the door. Two clicks made her think the lock was broken; behind it was only a blur, and she guessed Ivo had gone.” C reads:“Mara feared the lock was broken. Ivo inwardly welcomed the misunderstanding because it bought him time; they had not spoken.” These are analytical versions, not successive plot paragraphs. Import them separately with subjects/punctuation, without making C’s extra information knowledge B’s Mara already possessed.

## Identify narrative voice and grammatical person separately

All three use third person; access to Mara’s mind does not turn narration into her direct first-person speech. Ask who narrates and whether it is character self-narration separately from whose experience limits information. Do not identify an unnamed narrator automatically with the real author. A first-person retrospective control would also distinguish experiencing-time from later narrating-time knowledge; I need not mean only immediate knowledge. Keep the present exercise grounded in these three third-person versions.

## For A record externally observable actions

A explicitly gives stopping, two key turns and Ivo not turning, without internal motives. Record externally presented behavior rather than adding Mara’s fear or Ivo’s pleasure. It supports an external-observation candidate locally, not an entire novel’s permanent restriction. Mentioned action does not name a perceiving character. First appearance alone does not make everything Mara’s perception, and reader access to Ivo’s action does not prove Mara sees him clearly.

## For B distinguish perception, inference and fact

B organizes clicks, blurred vision, thought and guess around Mara, supporting locally restricted access through her experience. Broken lock and departed Ivo remain her inferences, not unconditional story facts. Examine the passage’s information organization, not one seeing/hearing verb; another text can mention perception while providing information beyond that character. Retain uncertainty verbs and objects so an excerpt of Ivo had gone does not lose she guessed.

## For C note broader access without classifying the whole book

C explicitly accesses Mara’s fear and Ivo’s inward pleasure despite no conversation, exceeding sole access through Mara’s current experience. This can involve shifted internal focalization or a broader arrangement; two sentences cannot establish permanent zero focalization or omniscience for the whole book. Examine continued access, transitions and stable restrictions. Two minds do not prove universal knowledge of every fact. Establish which internal information appears and how it differs from B’s restriction.

## Build an information-ownership table instead of labels alone

Externally list sentence, whose perception/mind, statement versus character inference, and reader knowledge not necessarily shared by characters. Mark A’s inner access not provided, B’s broken lock Mara thinks, and C’s pleasure Ivo’s narrated mind. Unreported thought is not absent thought; unknown is not refuted. Highlight complete sentences where supported or use markers. The reader builds the table without an assumed information-access panel, and summaries must retain uncertainty qualifiers.

## Check person-based and single-verb shortcuts

Use B against third-person therefore omniscient, and inspect context against a seeing verb therefore internally focalized throughout. A reports actions, not Mara knowing all of Ivo’s mind. Theories organize focalization, perspective and focalizer differently; state this information-scope standard rather than mixing incompatible labels. Correct one attribution/interpretation at a time and retain sentences so new evidence can be distinguished from an excerpt that dropped guessed.

## Reopen and deliver local information boundaries

Reopen all three without labels and identify external behavior, Mara-centered experience and two-mind access. Explain why narrative voice and selected information are separate questions. Retain B’s two uncertain judgments and C’s inability alone to classify the whole book, rather than memorizing external/internal/zero. Restore version/context and reassess later chapters; a character’s knowledge claim is not automatically confirmed by the work. This is textual evidence/navigation, not automatic viewpoint recognition, mind reading or certain authorial intention.
