Use an axolotl retaining external gills to check three stalks per side, four limb roots and the continuous tail fin without counting each filament as a gill.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-axolotl-three-pairs-external-gills-limbs-tail-fin/>), translated on 2026-11-19. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Draw an axolotl facing left with a slight body turn, using its typical gill-retaining appearance. Three pairs of external gills mean six main stalks, not six filaments; each stalk bears many finer branches. Attach four limbs to the body and a thin fin along the tail rather than combining them into fish-like side fins. Use a grey-brown spotted reference, without assuming that all axolotls are pink-white.

## Join the broad flat head and long tail in one pose

Use a horizontal canvas of about 2100 × 1600 pixels. Place the broad head left-centre, extend the body rightwards and curve the tail within the frame. Establish a flat wide head, narrower trunk and tapering tail before adding feathery structures. Keep neck and head continuous and attach the tail to the body rather than a separate fish. Separate body, gills, limbs, tail fin and spots from the sketch on pale blue-grey. Save and inspect the head–body–tail silhouette small; pixels and pose measure neither an animal nor its health.

## Mark three gill stalks per side before their filaments

Lightly mark three main gill stalks, one, two and three, at each side behind the head: two sides times three equals six. Show the near three clearly and preserve head occlusion on the far side, with a separate checking diagram if needed. Stalks extend from their roots, not eyes, nose or tail. Check all six assignments before adding filaments. Three pairs is an anatomical total, not a requirement to spread all six fully into view, nor permission to label sixty short brush marks as sixty independent external gills.

## Branch filaments from the stalks without making leaf veins

Give each stalk a firmer mid-grey core, then add finer, softer branches on both sides, gathered near the root and looser towards the end. No fixed stroke count is required. Avoid identical combs or a closed leaf surface surrounding all filaments. Adjacent gills may overlap; stop rear filaments where a foreground stalk hides them. For six leaves, remove continuous enclosing edges and restore open branching. For hair, emphasise the six roots and stalks before adding more lines.

## Attach four limbs to the trunk with depth distinctions

Place the near forelimb behind the head and near hindlimb ahead of the tail root, then add exposed far-limb portions. Keep limbs short, slender and jointed, ending in small toe contours rather than solid triangular fins. This is not a full digit inventory: do not guess hidden toes by symmetry. Separate the total of four limbs from visible toe counts. Move limbs growing from gills back to the trunk; replace fish-like side fins with connected limb segments and feet. The pose describes appearance, not swimming thrust or walking ability.

## Extend the tail fin as a thin surface rather than spines

Draw the visible thin fin surface along the tail’s upper contour and tip, turning and tapering with it rather than attaching a separate fan-shaped fish tail over the root. Distinguish fin and solid tail by value, letting some background colour influence the fin while retaining a firmer tail axis. Stop hidden far edges instead of making the tail transparent. For dorsal spines, join isolated points into a continuous surface; for thick cloth, weaken heavy black edges. This reference-based simplification neither fixes every individual’s fin shape nor diagnoses development.

## Keep spots subordinate to organ boundaries

Use a grey-brown body with varied small dark spots and a lighter underside. Gills may be warmer without fluorescent red everywhere. Compress spots as surfaces turn; keep head spots from swallowing the small eye, and fin spots weaker than those on solid tail. Suggest the eye and wide mouth with minimal dark lines without interpreting a curved mouth as emotion. Hide spots to check gills, limbs and fin first. Colour forms vary; grey-brown is the selected reference, not a claim that pink is the only normal appearance or that darkness diagnoses health or genetics.

## Check three pairs of stalks, then limbs and tail fin

Hide spots and fine filaments, count three main stalks on each side in the checking layer, then trace four limbs to the trunk and the fin to the tail axis. For one fluffy mass, separate stalk depth; for all six forced onto the near side, restore left/right ownership and far occlusion. Move gill-like feet to correct roots. Retain the faulty version and recheck small after one organ group. Typical gill-retaining adult appearance does not mean metamorphosis is impossible under every condition. No hormone or regeneration experiment on real animals is part of this check.

## Reopen and inspect stalk counts and detail rhythm

Save layered native work, hide temporary numbers and reopen a PNG. At fit-to-window size identify broad head, limbs and long tail; enlarge to distinguish stalk roots from filaments and confirm a continuous thin tail fin. Reopen editable work and reduce only far filaments’ contrast without deleting near stalks. If gills become a black fringe when small, reduce dense tip lines rather than enlarging the eyes as a distraction. State a gill-retaining illustrative individual, grey-brown palette and visible occlusions; mark unperformed export/reopening as pending. This is no husbandry, medical, species-certification or automatic organ-recognition tool.
