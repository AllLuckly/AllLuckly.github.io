Mark two concepts and compare crossed and parallel controls, distinguishing chiasmus from identical-word reversal without treating symmetry as proof.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/nasbook-chiasmus-concepts-word-reversal-pattern/>), translated on 2026-11-10. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Similar words at both ends do not alone establish a crossed structure, and a crossed structure does not make claims in both directions equally valid. Read a self-made passage or trusted rhetoric explanation in Book Isle and check ABBA with concept labels tied to original wording. All labels are manual notes, not automatic rhetoric parsing or detection of authorial intent.

## Identify the terminology used by the source

Record the title, edition, chapter and location of the term’s definition. Some sources discuss identical-word reversal broadly with chiasmus; others, including the rhetoric source used here, distinguish conceptual or grammatical crossing from identical-word reversal, called antimetabole. Copy the book’s adopted definition first instead of declaring it wrong under another translation system. Here label conceptual order A-B-B-A as ABBA and record identical-word reversal separately as antimetabole, following the chosen source for their relationship. Labels are checking tools: they do not require equal-length parts or a symmetrical partner for every character.

## Label the four parts of an original passage

Our original passage says: “First admire his composure, then worry about his yielding; next be concerned about his silence, finally appreciate his calm.” Provisionally group admiration of composure and appreciation of calm as favourable evaluation A, and worry about yielding and concern about silence as reservation B. Their order is ABBA. Retain all four phrases rather than only the labels “favour” and “reservation.” These are contextual concept groups, not a claim that composure and calm are universally synonymous or that a named writer wrote the passage. A reader who disputes the grouping must be able to return to the actual words and discuss the reasons.

## Use a parallel control to check the ordering

For a control, order the four parts as admiration of composure, worry about yielding, appreciation of calm, concern about silence. The labels become ABAB. Almost the same vocabulary now forms alternating repetition instead of crossed order. Keep the original and control separate; your rearranged sentence is not another edition of the book. Check explicitly why the third part changes from B to A and the fourth from A to B, rather than deciding from a symmetrical sound. Retain conjunctions and negatives in real text: removing a word that changes evaluation merely to obtain a neat letter pattern is not valid analysis.

## Record identical-word reversal separately

A second original example is “Seek images for stories, and seek stories for images.” Label stories A and images B: their paired roles produce ABBA and the identical words recur in reversed order. Under this article’s terminology, also mark antimetabole; matching letters do not make this identical to the previous passage’s grouping of related concepts. Record both the actual word and its role in the phrase. If two names merely change positions without the relevant grammatical roles reversing, inspect the real structure again instead of using four letters to conceal how the classification was made.

## Crossed form does not prove either claim

Separate “seeking images to support stories” and “seeking stories to support images” as two action claims and ask what reasons the current passage gives for each. Suitability in one direction does not prove equal suitability in the other; rhetorical form cannot reverse causation either. A discussion of a creative method does not establish that all art must follow it. Keep formal observation, contextual interpretation and degree of agreement in three separate fields. You may confirm reversed wording yet find insufficient evidence, or accept an idea while reserving judgement about its rhetorical label. These judgements are compatible.

## Handle long sentences and alternative groupings

A long passage may contain several structural layers. First define which four phrases are being checked, then expand to neighbouring sentences; do not pick four arbitrary places across a chapter to construct a pattern. The same word used with different meanings need not share label A, while different words may represent one concept in this context if you explain the evidence. Retain two candidate groupings when appropriate, each with the context it needs. Stop where four items remain traceable and their relationships explainable. Not every repetition is chiasmus, and not every literary paragraph requires a unique final rhetorical label.

## Troubleshoot lost negatives and quotation boundaries

After copying notes, check the source page for words such as “not,” “no longer” and “yet”; a lost negative can reverse the favourable/reservation grouping. If scan text or complex layout cannot be selected reliably, transcribe short phrases with locations rather than claiming search recognised the structure. When an author quotes someone else’s crossed sentence, record its speaker and quotation boundaries so a criticised example is not mistaken for the author’s endorsement. For disputed terminology or translation, cite both definitions and state which scope you adopt. Do not rearrange original wording to make an example fit a preferred definition.

## Exit and return to verify wording-to-label mappings

Exit the reader and return. Read the full original passage, then group its four parts again without looking at letter prompts and compare the ABBA and ABAB records. Explain how the second example’s identical-word reversal differs from the first example’s conceptual grouping, and why form does not prove exchanged claims. Retain six items: edition/location, complete phrases, grouping reasons, order labels, terminology source and interpretive limits. Completion means another reader can inspect every grouping step and offer counterexamples. It neither establishes a unique authorial motive nor represents an app-generated rhetorical conclusion.
