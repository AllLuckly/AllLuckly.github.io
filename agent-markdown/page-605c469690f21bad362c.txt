Draw a transparent baseplate compass, separating three directional marks: the travel arrow on the baseplate, the bezel’s north mark and the independent magnetic needle. They need not coincide in the illustrative state. A single red arrow cannot represent all three parts. This is a static object drawing; the app measures neither map north nor magnetic north.

## Leave space ahead of the capsule in the top view

Use a canvas of about 1800 × 1800 pixels. Orient the long baseplate upwards and place the circular capsule slightly below centre, leaving room for the forward travel arrow. Follow the basic Silva Field structure without adding another model’s mirror lid or clinometer. Separate sketch, plate, bezel, needle and text on pale blue-grey, then save native work. This near-top view does not require a strongly flattened ellipse. Pixels and mark positions are a layout, not measured product dimensions.

## Show plate edges without making it opaque paper

Lay a very pale cool grey over the baseplate and add firmer highlights along selected edges, letting two fictional map lines show through. These background lines contain no real terrain or private location; the opaque needle must hide them. Keep the plate’s thickness consistent rather than alternating between glass and a thick-walled box. If transparency becomes merely a white outline, add a faint surface. If the background disappears, lighten the plate coverage without also making the needle and lettering transparent.

## Arrange the four cardinal marks on one bezel

Draw the bezel as a ring with a narrow side. In this illustrative state put N/0 above, E/90 right, S/180 below and W/270 left, increasing clockwise. Check these four pairs before adding a few intermediate ticks; dense numbering is unnecessary. All belong to the same ring, so 90 must not remain fixed on the plate while N turns. For a turned-bezel study, save a separate copy and rotate all four pairs together. Retain the stated orientation in the main drawing; screen positions do not determine a real bearing.

## Let the needle turn about its own centre

Draw a small central pivot and one elongated needle with a red end and pale-grey opposite end. Let the red end point upper-right, away from the upper N, explicitly as a hypothetical instant rather than a measurement of magnetic north. Both ends share the pivot instead of growing from the bezel. The red end denotes the reference compass’s north-seeking end, not a route arrow; the pale end is not another independently rotating needle. If they resemble separate blades, restore their common axis without adding a third red point.

## Keep travel and orienting marks separate from the needle

Place a fixed upward travel arrow on the front of the baseplate, aligned with its long axis and visually distinct from the needle. Simplify visible orienting marks on the capsule bottom, without attaching them physically to the red needle. Temporarily label plate, bezel and needle on a checking layer. A diagonal red needle and upward travel arrow are intentionally different here. If ownership is unclear, consult the manufacturer’s exterior and explanation. Aligning every pointed mark in a drawing does not establish real orientation or correct magnetic declination.

## Focus material cues on the capsule rim and centre

Use narrow dark grey at the bezel’s outer edge with broken lit highlights. Suggest the transparent capsule through slight value changes rather than a deep aquarium. Keep the pivot small and solid; a faint separation beneath the needle is enough, without raising it on a bridge outside the capsule. Use neutral ticks and make the red needle the focus, avoiding equally strong red background arrows. If it resembles a decorative compass rose, restore the long plate, capsule rim and independent pivot before adding directions. Reflections establish neither waterproof rating nor fluid composition.

## Trace each mark back to its component

Hide material detail and identify the fixed travel arrow, four bezel pairs and one double-ended needle. Check N0, E90, S180 and W270 in order, without mirrored east and west, then confirm that the pivot remains central. If the red needle reads as a route, detach it from background lines. If the ring has turned but numbers have not, correct their shared ownership in the copy. Save the faulty version and fix one relationship at a time. Do not append a phone-heading measurement: this static exercise calls no location, magnetometer or navigation service.

## Reopen and check transparent and opaque edges

Save layered native work, hide ownership notes and reopen an exported PNG. At fit-to-window size distinguish the long plate and circular capsule. Enlarge to check that map lines pass through the plate but not the solid needle, and that cardinal labels are not attached to the needle. Reopen editable work and change only the background lines, confirming that the object remains intact. Strengthen local edges rather than darkening everything. Label the hypothetical needle direction, top view and absence of measured bearings; mark unperformed export/reopening as pending. This explains components, not field navigation, actual readings or route safety.
