A preface can explain a book’s aims without establishing what the whole book delivers. Compare prefaces from two books on one topic, recording audience, scope and reading advice, then inspect small body-text samples. This does not automatically rank books or treat promotional wording or initial impressions as verified conclusions.

## Fix both editions and the comparison question

Choose two authorised books on the same topic and record their editions and sources. Ask a specific question, such as which to use for prerequisites, rather than seeking an absolute winner. Confirm body-text access. Identify each chosen preface’s type and range while focusing on its introduction to the book, not a separate taxonomy of commentary voices. Prefaces can change between editions; do not substitute newer marketing descriptions for the text in your actual files.

## Read the first preface fully before extracting fields

Read the entire first preface and extract explicit audience, assumed knowledge, coverage, exclusions and suggested reading method. Attach a locating quotation to each and mark absent information as unstated. Preserve qualifications around attractive claims. Separate source from personal reaction; “felt accessible” is not necessarily an author’s promise of no prerequisites. Use supported notes/highlights or manually verified external records where selection is unavailable, prioritising accuracy before formatting.

## Use the same fields for the second preface

Apply the same five fields to the second book rather than comparing one book’s scope with the other’s prose style. Preserve differing wording before proposing equivalence. Track unread portions of a long preface honestly. Explicit exclusion in one book versus silence in another does not prove the latter covers that material. Complete independent notes before juxtaposing them so the second reading is not merely a search for evidence favouring an expected choice.

## Separate common aims from different prerequisites

List explicitly shared aims separately from prerequisites, scope and sequence differences. Use notes for provenance and an external comparison if needed, without assuming a built-in comparison database. Page count does not prove depth, and a beginner audience does not make every topic simple. Preserve ambiguous terms pending body-text checks. Write “A explicitly requires this; B does not state it” rather than converting silence into a guarantee of no prerequisites. Keep unknown fields visible.

## Sample a body passage from each book

Choose a representative section from each book’s suggested entry or contents and read it. Record concrete prerequisites, examples and terminology obstacles without generalising a sample to the whole book’s fulfilment of promises. Compare sections serving similar purposes, not an introductory chapter against an appendix. Different tasks may mean no ranking is needed. Save sample locations alongside preface evidence so the expectation-to-text comparison remains traceable.

## Create a personal sequence without rewriting the authors’ aims

Choose a next book/chapter and a role for the companion, citing the fields and samples behind that decision. This is your plan, not a jointly recommended author sequence. Use available tags/statuses if helpful without automatic recommendation. If neither fits, seek prerequisites rather than force a winner. Preserve both records for changing goals. Keep the plan small enough to start in the next session instead of building an impractical full course.

## Keep the comparison and verify source return

Relocate both prefaces and body samples from the list after leaving the current pages. Verify editions and quotations. Export important notes where supported and reopen them externally, preserving the distinction between author statements and your plan. Keep originals separately and verify matching files on other devices independently of reading-data sync. Date the question and reassess relevant fields after a goal or edition changes rather than treating one useful sequence as a permanent universal recommendation.

## Repair uneven comparisons through fields and samples

If the result collapses into “good versus bad,” check equal reading coverage, field consistency and comparable samples. Verify that supposed promises did not come from cover copy. Diagnose wrong jumps through editions and sources before deleting records. Revise an impractical plan from observed obstacles rather than blaming the author for unfinished reading. Distinguish explicit source statements, sampled observations and personal decisions, each with its appropriate evidence.
