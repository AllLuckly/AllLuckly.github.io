Use a three-centimetre extension to check a three-newton restoring force and 0.045 joule stored energy within the stated Hooke range.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/manyue-hooke-spring-extension-restoring-force-elastic-energy-panels/>), translated on 2026-11-18. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A long-looking spring is neither its extension nor evidence of energy proportional to length. Prepare panels of natural length, endpoint, restoring force and energy, reading a stipulated linear spring in ManYue. Values support external calculation and static reading, without requiring physical stretching or in-app detection of an elastic limit.

## Define the natural position and linear range first

Externally draw a horizontal spring fixed at the left, natural length L0=0.10m. Set x=0 at its natural endpoint and right positive. Stipulate k=100N/m and Hooke behaviour only for |x|≤0.08m, ignoring spring mass and avoiding vertical gravitational equilibrium. Put lengths on P1, force/energy on P2 and compression/out-of-range controls on P3, exporting three PDF pages. Verify natural-length labels and markers after import and bookmark P2. The interval is an original model assumption, not material certification for real springs.

## Subtract natural length from final length

The main endpoint gives L=0.13m, so x=L−L0=0.03m, three centimetres rather than thirteen. Measure both lengths from the same fixed end, then bracket extension between natural and displaced endpoints. With 13cm and 10cm labels, subtract to 3cm and convert to 0.03m before using N/m. Neither 13×100 nor treating numeric three centimetres as three metres is valid. Zoom changes drawn length, not the labelled natural length or extension.

```text
L0=0.10 m; L=0.13 m; x=L-L0=0.03 m
k=100 N/m; stipulated range: abs(x)<=0.08 m
F_spring=-k*x=-3 N
U(x)-U(0)=0.5*k*x^2=0.045 J
x=-0.02 m: F_spring=+2 N; U=0.020 J
x=0.06 m: F_spring=-6 N; U=0.180 J
```

## Relate the restoring-force sign to natural position

F\_spring=−kx gives −3N, leftward toward the natural position. Draw the force on the object at the right endpoint, not its reaction force on the spring. If an external hand slowly holds this extension with other horizontal forces ignored, it supplies +3N, allowing zero net force without zero spring force. Label each force’s agent. A static drawing does not justify deleting restoring force or drawing both the hand’s force and the spring’s force rightward.

## Square extension and state the energy zero

Choose zero elastic potential energy at the natural position. U=kx²/2=100×0.0009/2=0.045J, without the restoring-force minus sign. Quasistatic external stretching from zero opposes a force growing from zero to 3N. Work is the triangular force–displacement area, (1/2)×3×0.03. Using final 3N over the whole distance wrongly gives 0.09J, twice as much. Place triangle and final-force rectangle side by side: force is not constant along the move.

For stretching only from x=0.02m to 0.03m, the energy increase is 100 times the difference of squared extensions divided by two, 0.025J. External force rises from 2N to 3N; mean 2.5N times 0.01m also gives 0.025J. Final 3N times that segment instead gives 0.03J. Compare all three to identify the error rather than prohibit every force-times-displacement calculation.

## Double extension and compare different scaling factors

A separate x=0.06m remains within the stipulated range: restoring force −6N and energy 0.18J. Force magnitude doubles but energy quadruples. At x=0.015m, force is −1.5N and energy 0.01125J, one quarter of the original. Hold k fixed and compare extensions, not total lengths. Energy increases by 0.135J from the main state to the doubled state, not the entire 0.18J. Subtract initial from final without silently moving the energy zero to the main panel.

Retain the energy zero with the table. Choosing x=0.03m as zero would give −0.045J at natural length, while the increase to the main state stays +0.045J and restoring force −3N. A negative potential-energy value does not imply negative extension. The main ledger retains its natural-position zero; never subtract values using different zeros.

## Keep compression and an out-of-range case distinct

For x=−0.02m, final length is 0.08m, force +2N rightward and energy +0.020J. Negative compression displacement does not make squared energy negative. An x=0.10m card exceeds the stated 0.08m range; the formula cannot predict actual force or energy there, nor prove permanent deformation. It only lacks model authorization and needs new material/experimental evidence. Do not alter the main stiffness to accommodate it, or treat all compression as excluded from Hooke behaviour.

For equal-magnitude compression x=−0.03m, final length 0.07m remains within the range, restoring force is +3N and stored energy 0.045J. Compared with the main extension, opposite force direction and equal energy independently check which quantity carries a sign.

## Troubleshoot reference, units and the force recipient

A result 13N suggests using final 0.13m as x; 300N suggests missing centimetre conversion. Negative 0.045J may transfer the restoring sign into stored energy, while 0.09J may use final force as constant. For a direction conflict, identify the force recipient and natural position before changing signs. Repair one fault-copy row and return to P1/bookmarked force page separately. If brackets hide the zero position, revise the external layout rather than guess extension.
