A river bending left on paper does not make the left bank permanently depositional or erosional. Read an original meander diagram in ManYue, establishing flow and each bend’s inner and outer sides before interpreting processes. This assesses no real riverbank safety or hazard prediction. The app supports reading; bank classification and change notes are manual.

## Identify plan view and flow arrows

Use an authorised page or draw an S-shaped channel in plan view, not cross-section. Label upstream A, downstream B and both bends, with flow arrows along the channel. North does not determine downstream. Record identity, location, legend and scale status. Without adequate evidence, leave upstream unknown instead of inferring flow solely from mountains drawn at the page top.

## Find inner and outer sides for each bend

Inspect bend one locally for inner and outer sides, then repeat independently for bend two. In an S, their page-side locations alternate; do not extend the first answer down the whole river. Label all four bank positions before adding process terms. With a wide channel, use bend geometry, not water colour, to identify sides, preventing a later interpretation from being inserted into initial location evidence.

## Associate processes under stated conditions

USGS outlines common outer-bend erosion and inner-bend point-bar deposition that changes meanders over time. Apply these as process labels in this general diagram, not absolute claims for every bank segment at every moment. Bed, engineering and flow conditions matter in real rivers. Retain stated walls or bedrock limits rather than override them with a general rule. This provides no advice about siting real homes.

## Name river-left and river-right downstream

In a separate column, imagine facing downstream: river-left is on that observer’s left and river-right on the right. Screen left is not necessarily river-left, and rotating the page does not change the downstream-based name. Check both bends with small downstream markers; one named bank can occupy inner and outer bends successively. If a source uses another convention, record its legend before comparing rather than silently replacing it.

Rotate the sketch by one hundred eighty degrees together with its flow arrows and re-identify downstream-based banks. Screen left becomes right while the same named bank remains tied to the same landmarks. This rotates a diagram, not a real river’s flow. Mirroring is a different operation that also needs fresh landmark correspondence.

## Require earlier and later evidence for change

For temporal change, make two diagrams of the same reach with fixed landmarks and scale. Describe stipulated outward bank movement or new inner deposits only from that matched pair. A sandy patch in one image supplies no annual deposition rate. Different rivers are not a time comparison, and an artist’s layout adjustment is not real migration. Rates need intervals and measurements not supplied here.

## Cross-check wording and shapes in ManYue

Read the full legend in ManYue, zoom bend one and return to the full page before finding bend two. If an outer-bank label points inward, retain both wording and target as an unresolved difference rather than silently move the leader. Dark water may be decorative, not measured depth or speed. Without contours or velocity data, leave these unknown. An external four-location list suffices; no automatic bank classifier is required.

## Check mirroring and mismatched river reaches

For reversed-looking banks, check mirrored reproduction, reversed flow arrows, another bend and source definitions. Preserve reach numbers and landmarks in checking sketches rather than stitch unrelated screenshots into a nonexistent continuous river. Change one judgment with a reason and retain the first version. An original S diagram is enough for support. Manual reading is neither hydrodynamic measurement nor real riverbank safety assessment or geolocation.

## Leave and return to relabel all four positions

Leave and return to the whole diagram and relabel both bends and downstream-based banks without consulting old answers. Compare records and retain identity, flow evidence, four positions, process assumptions, temporal evidence and unknowns. Explain page-side changes and why each bend needs its own check, noting missing time evidence. Success is reproducible reading, not memorising colours or judging every real river by a single simplified rule.
