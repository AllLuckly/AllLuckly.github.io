Use a simplified common shore-crab appearance with all limbs intact, rather than imposing one body plan on everything called a crab. Check two claw-bearing limbs plus eight walking legs, five pairs of thoracic appendages. Occlusion is allowed, but the sketch must account for each path. Organise connections beneath the shell manually in Aquarell, then develop texture. Extra legs do not make a livelier animal, and this illustration is not a species-identification plate.

## Establish the shell and the animal’s front

Use an approximately 1800×1500 px landscape canvas with a grey-sand background and a slightly elevated view, animal front toward the top. Establish a broad, somewhat flattened shell rather than a sphere, then locate two short front eyestalks. Separate sketch, far limbs, near limbs, shell, eyes and ground shadow; use muted ochre-red for the shell. Mark a front-to-back centreline and use the animal’s own left and right. Repair shell shape and thickness if it resembles a legged disc. Eyestalks do not count as legs; omitted antennae must not be reassigned to unrelated marks.

## Account for five limb pairs in the sketch

Mark the front claw-bearing pair, then four walking legs on each side: two claws and eight walking legs. Sketch each centreline emerging beneath the shell. Count roots and endpoints separately so a forked line is not counted as two limbs. Temporary left-one-to-five and right-one-to-five labels can help; remove them from the finish. If four near endpoints but only three far ones show, determine whether one is legitimately occluded and retain its hidden sketch path. Do not move rear legs beside the eyestalks merely to expose them; attachment must follow the body layout.

## Give each claw an arm and opposed tips

Extend or slightly raise both claw-bearing limbs. Draw short arms and joints from beneath the shell, then wider palms and two opposed pincer tips. A claw is not a pair of scissors glued to the shell. Modest asymmetry is allowed, but this is not a specified extreme-claw species. Both tips must face one opening, not form a three-pronged hand. Trace detached-looking claws back along their arms. Distinguish palm thickness and opening if heart-shaped. Use a little dark in the gap without filling it black to conceal incorrectly opposed tips.

## Bend eight walking legs toward the ground

Build each walking leg from tapering segments, locating root, outward bend and tip without bead-like equal joints. Vary the spread of four legs per side. Some may overlap, but their endpoints need plausible ground positions. If spider-like, recheck claws and shell layout rather than adding legs by intuition. Move back-growing roots beneath the shell and conceal them at its edge. Explain bending through silhouette and restrained joint shading, not black rings around every segment. Decorative simplification does not change the underlying count of eight walking legs.

## Separate shell volume from limb depth order

Light the upper-left shell and turn lower rim and right side toward deep brown, retaining one broad area rather than cutting everything into scales. Keep near contours clearer and far limbs slightly quieter while sharing one upper-left light. Hide texture to check shell-over-root occlusion, then hide the shell to inspect concealed connections. Spread roots if every leg radiates from one black rim dot rather than covering them with heavier lines. Use sparse quiet spots and avoid turning eyestalks or shell markings into extra joints. Volume, occlusion and texture require separate checks.

## Keep ground shadows distinct from extra legs

Use short contact accents near tips and a softer dark region under the shell, extending generally lower right. Make shadow edges softer and more grey-sand than real limbs, not equally thick black appendages. Hide shadow and count two claws plus eight walking legs; if restoring it apparently adds two legs, weaken or merge misleading shadow edges. Check lowest tips meeting ground shadow if floating rather than blackening the shell underside. Sand grains and foam are optional. Keep any background detail separate from real tips instead of hiding uncertain contacts in noise.

## Audit every root-to-tip path

Keep a numbered structural sketch and trace each root beneath the shell to pincer or toe, recording short sections hidden by other limbs. Correct total count does not excuse a limb attached to the wrong side. Hide eyes and texture and check whether claw placement and shell shape still indicate the front. Vary one or two leg angles if both sides look like mirrored stickers, then reread their numbers. Do not simplify anatomy by deleting a difficult limb. It may be occluded in the finish, but preserve its continuous sketch path and distinguish occlusion from an injured specimen.

## Retain readable gaps between limbs in the finish

Save the layered original and inspect claw openings, walking-leg gaps and shell rim in a PNG at use size. Separate neighbouring tips and local values if several merge, rather than outlining every leg heavily. Reduce eyestalk contrast if they dominate. Record a specific numbered fault such as the second left rear walking leg merging with shadow. Natural-history sources constrain basic form; palette, pixels and pose are drawing choices, not a complete scientific plate. A different species or injured specimen requires fresh reliable reference rather than wholesale reuse of this shape.
