A traffic cone uses few colours, but rectangular horizontal stripes can still flatten it. Draw an orange-and-white decorative cone, focusing on wrapped bands, the small top opening and support from its base. This is not guidance on selecting, placing or specifying real road equipment; colour widths are illustration choices. Separate these relationships with canvas, layers and hand-painted strokes in Aquarell without needing an automatic cone tool.

## Give the axis and base a common centre

Use an approximately 1500×1700 px canvas with a pale grey-blue background. Draw a vertical axis, a short narrow top and widening cone, then a flattened square base showing its top and a little side thickness. Place the axis sensibly on the base rather than at one corner. Separate sketch, base, cone, bands and details, inspecting one-colour masses first. If a paper hat seems placed beside an unrelated block, correct centre and contact arc instead of joining them with a large black shadow. Align near/far and side base edges within consistent perspective families. Exact measurement is unnecessary, but arbitrary skew is not.

## Keep a small top rather than a zero-width point

Choose a small top opening, leaving a short width between the upper sides and describing it with a shallow ellipse. Make the inner opening slightly smaller, keeping darkness inside rather than adding a floating black bead. Pass the axis through upper and lower centres, with top ellipse and bottom arc sharing orientation. If it resembles a pointed hat, truncate the top first. If the opening resembles a large cup rim, reduce it while maintaining downward-widening sides. A little thickness suffices without a pure-white highlight around the entire rim. Keep this one invented model consistent instead of starting with a solid tip and later adding a large hole without changing its outline.

## Wrap both boundaries of each band

Mark two pale bands on the cone, each with upper and lower boundaries dipping slightly at the front and turning behind at the sides. Their visible dimensions narrow with the cone, so an upper band should not inexplicably be wider overall than a lower one. Check boundary intersections with the silhouette in the sketch rather than curving one end and leaving the other straight like tape. Begin with warm grey-white before reflective accents. If it reads as a striped triangle, reduce heavy edge lines and strengthen the wrapping arcs. If two separate rings appear, weaken their lower shadows so they remain surface colour rather than thick protruding hoops.

## Light orange and white with one source

Use muted orange, lighter upper left and darker lower right, greying the bands slightly on the shadow side rather than making them glow white throughout. Hide bands to inspect cone shading, then restore them: the surface turn should continue uninterrupted. A little warm-red shadow is possible, but do not cut a black groove beneath every band. If it resembles stacked blocks, unify the value gradient and reduce individual shadows. These are colour regions on one surface, not proof of a real material’s reflective performance. If adding a highlight, use one short section and retain middle values so volume takes priority over colour contrast.

## Separate base top from side thickness

Use dark blue-grey for the base, with a slightly lighter top and darker lower-right thickness. Round corners gently while retaining a square reading. Seat the cone on the top, perhaps with a narrow near-side contact shadow, without piercing the base’s side thickness. Hide the cone to check the base independently, then restore it and inspect overlap. If floating, reduce the gap at contact and align centres. If it resembles a cushion, reduce swollen rounding and retain planar turns. Keep its thickness restrained relative to the cone rather than turning it into a deep black platform or adding side stripes in place of a correct top plane.

## Add wear only after structure works

Choose two or three low-contrast scuffs at the bottom or corners, following the surface instead of copying identical chips across every band. Orange wear and dirt on white regions must not obscure their wrapping boundaries. Compare with wear hidden and restored. If it looks cracked and leaking light, remove pure-white fissures and lower contrast. A clean new object can omit wear entirely. No fabricated use history is needed, and more spots do not establish realism. Prioritise the top opening, side band turns and base contact; texture only assists material and must not conceal unresolved contours.

## Check taper without coloured bands

In a copy, retain only the single-colour cone and base, checking both sides converge toward the top opening and the lower edge remains complete. Show bands separately and confirm they belong to that form rather than equal-width horizontal bars. Compare a deliberately straight-band version, changing curvature alone while retaining colour and lighting. If the original still looks flat, correct arcs and their silhouette intersections rather than concealing errors under stronger gradients. This is visual comparison, not automatic geometric certification or a source of real equipment dimensions or load conclusions. Readable surface and support are sufficient.

## Inspect the opening and narrow bands after export

Save the layered original and inspect the small opening, narrowest upper band and bottom contact in the PNG at use size. If a white band merges with the background into a cutout, adjust background or band locally instead of framing the entire cone in heavy black. If the base reduces to a thickness-free black line, lighten its top or simplify its outer shadow without inventing excessive thickness. Keep a texture-free structural preview. A new viewpoint requires redrawing base and band arcs rather than skewing the finished image. Record a precise issue, such as the upper band failing to turn into the right contour, to locate the edge needing correction.
