English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/manyue-plate-boundary-relative-motion-divergent-convergent-transform-panels/>), translated on 2026-11-18. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Two eastward map arrows need not mean no relative motion, and a sloping section line does not mean southward map travel. Read original coordinate cards for three boundary types in ManYue, checking normal and tangential components. Velocities are stipulated examples, not measured fault data or predictions of a particular earthquake.

## Give the local map view one coordinate system

Externally draw a locally planar north–south boundary, east as positive x and north as positive y, with left/right plate velocity arrows. Plates belong to the lithosphere; a continental coastline is not automatically a boundary. Make P1 three map views, P2 velocities/ten-year ledger and P3 a subduction section plus controls, exporting PDF and retaining source work. Check direction legend and P1–P3 markers, then bookmark P2. The local display does not imply centimetre-wide plates; the numbers describe motion rates.

## Check increasing separation as right minus left

For divergence stipulate left x velocity −2cm/yr and right +2cm/yr. Normal separation changes at right minus left, 4cm/yr. Over ten years of unchanged local rates, plate-attached reference markers separate by another 40cm=0.40m. Each plate does not move 40cm, and the relative rate is not zero. Seafloor spreading can accompany rising magma cooling into oceanic crust, but these velocities alone determine no magma volume, eruption date or ridge height. Separate kinematic arithmetic from geological interpretation.

```text
local boundary runs north-south; x east, y north
divergent: vL_x=-2, vR_x=+2 cm/yr -> relative_x=+4
convergent: vL_x=+3, vR_x=-1 cm/yr -> relative_x=-4
transform: vL_y=+3, vR_y=-2 cm/yr -> relative_y=-5
10 yr: normal separation +40 cm / -40 cm; sliding offset magnitude 50 cm
```

## Include both sides when checking convergence

For convergence, left moves east at +3cm/yr and right west at −1cm/yr. Right minus left is −4cm/yr, shortening normal separation by 40cm in ten years. Looking only at the right plate’s one centimetre misses the other side. Track reference markers rather than claim entire plates merge in ten years. Add +5 to both x velocities for a separate control: +8 and +4 are both eastward but retain relative −4. Classification depends on relative motion, not whether drawn arrows face each other.

## Compute sliding parallel to the boundary

For the transform panel set both x velocities to zero, left y=+3cm/yr northward and right y=−2cm/yr southward. Right relative to left is −5cm/yr in y, a 50cm southward offset in ten years with no normal separation change from these velocities. Parallel sliding means neither stationary plates nor no earthquakes. Draw an initially aligned imaginary marker across the boundary, then offset its halves. This is a teaching marker, not an actual photographed road rupture. Fifty centimetres is not a gap width.

## Do not mix subduction sections with map directions

Draw a separate vertical section with an oceanic plate subducting beneath another. Its axes are horizontal distance and depth, not the map’s north direction. Downward slope means increasing depth, not map-south travel. Continental collision is also convergence, so not every convergent boundary has the same simple trench/volcano section. Real boundaries can have broad deformation and mixed motion; these panels isolate ideal local types. Actual maps need their own legends/time windows, not precision inferred from schematic lines.

## Do not turn average rates into an earthquake timetable

The ten-year 40/50cm values are model relative displacements, not proof of uniform slip every second at one surface point. Real boundaries may lock, store strain and slip intermittently; a long-term average cannot predict an earthquake date. Typically limited transform volcanism is no absolute claim of no volcanoes or risk. Make no location-risk judgment here. Separate stipulated constant-rate extrapolation from geological unknowns; model years are not observation dates, and equal displacement values do not establish equal earthquake magnitudes.

A separate relative velocity with x=+4,y=−5cm/yr combines opening and boundary-parallel slip rather than one original pure type. Its ten-year displacement is (40,−50)cm, magnitude 10√41cm≈64.031cm, not the summed 90cm. This card illustrates local vector components; one resultant speed cannot uniquely determine a real fault mechanism.

## Troubleshoot relative differences, axes and dimensions

If same-direction arrows in the convergence control are called divergent, compute right minus left. If sliding becomes a 50cm-wide gap, check y versus x. 40cm over ten years is 0.40m, not 4m. After page rotation find east/north in the legend rather than use the phone’s top as north. Repair one copy item and recheck left/right identities. Missing frames or time units remain unknown; an unlabelled arrow is not an annually measured rate or permission to add earthquake magnitudes.
