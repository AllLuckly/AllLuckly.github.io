After learning an ending, an ordinary earlier detail can seem conspicuous. Practise rereading foreshadowing in Book Isle by separating available clues, your predictions and later events. These are original short scenes, not extracts from commercial novels. External notes are sufficient: the app supports reading and returning, not deciding authorial intention or guaranteeing correct plot predictions.

## Mark the current reading boundary

Record book, edition, chapter and visible reading boundary without consulting later pages or reviews. In the first scene an old ferry rope is frayed and Alan leaves a new rope ashore. The second only describes rising wind during supper. Treat these as the current information set; do not smuggle in scene three. If you already know the ending, label the record a rereading reconstruction rather than claim a successful first prediction. Use fresh material for a new first-pass exercise.

## Separate details from predictions

List fraying, the new rope ashore and stronger wind as source details. Predictions may include a broken rope, a later replacement or atmosphere alone. Possibility is not an accomplished event. Record no prediction honestly if none arose. Fraying is not an existing break, and no failure probability is supplied. Retain that evidential strength instead of inserting a new plot fact merely because general experience suggests it.

Add conditions for revising each candidate: replacement would undermine the old-rope-break prediction, and remembered rather than present wind would require rechecking timing. Recording these conditions before the ending limits hindsight certainty. This assigns no real probability; it preserves the alternative continuations you originally allowed.

## Read onward and check the same object chain

In continuation A, the same old rope breaks at night and the wind drives the ferry from shore. Scene four confirms that the new rope was never installed. Link earlier details to later events and verify the same ferry and rope rather than a similar object at another dock. Fraying and nonreplacement now support a payoff reading, while the original alternatives remain in the record. Do not erase unsuccessful predictions to suggest that your forecasting never failed.

## Compare a continuation without that payoff

In control B, the character replaces the rope in time and the ferry remains moored. Keep it separate from A’s story facts. Fraying still signals risk, but the specific prediction that the old rope breaks is not realised. Compare this clue’s later use without declaring B bad writing or every unfulfilled detail an authorial oversight. A hint may support prevention, misdirection or atmosphere; the full text determines the interpretation.

## Distinguish hints from explicit advance narration

An added sentence declaring that the rope will break three nights later explicitly states a future event, unlike the indirect fraying clue. Give advance narration or prediction its own column and check narrator reliability or whether it is merely a character’s prophecy. A later scene recalling an earlier broken rope concerns the past, not foreshadowing simply because it appears later in the book. Keep presentation order and story time separate to avoid confusing flashbacks with hints.

## Limit claims about a correspondence

State a bounded reading: earlier fraying and the unused replacement make the later break of the same rope traceable on rereading. Do not infer a maritime disaster in the author’s life or claim that readers must predict what a character misses. The university reference explains expectation, unease and possible misdirection; the specific rope relationship comes from this original sample. Separate conceptual authority from textual evidence instead of having a source endorse a novel it has not analysed.

## Check hindsight contamination and mixed versions

If first-pass notes contain names, dates or outcomes learned only in scene three, mark them as later additions. For missing clues, check revised editions, review summaries or a lost negation in an excerpt. Leave ordinary scenery without a payoff undecided instead of forcing events onto every detail. If notes or bookmarks are unavailable, use chapter and neighbouring wording externally; there is no need to delete reading records or invent automatic whole-book clue detection.

## Leave and return with both reading layers intact

Leave and return to scene one, reading the preserved first-pass card before the later correspondence card. Explain what was knowable then and what came later. Keep source locations, three details, initial predictions, A/B continuations, correspondence reasoning and unknowns. Success is auditable information timing and interpretation, not a higher guessing score. Create a new record for another work rather than assign this rope’s meaning to every description of wind or cord.
