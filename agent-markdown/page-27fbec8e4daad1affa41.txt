## Choose an angle showing front and rear crank relations

Use an approximately 1800×1500 px landscape canvas with pale cream-grey background, front opening upper left and crank emerging at the right rear. Establish a box showing top, near side and part of front, locating rear shaft in its perspective sketch. Separate housing, opening, shaft, arm, grip and shadow. Use muted blue-green. Do not display opposite faces frontally just to expose both ends. Repose the whole object if the shaft hides, rather than moving it beside the front opening. Establish width/depth without brands, buttons or motion arrows.

## Align the front opening with its face

Place a small elliptical front opening aligned to the face, with grey-silver lip and darker inner wall rather than a huge black hole. Add narrow depth if button-like or reduce layered protruding rings if camera-like. This entry does not expose rear shaft or cutters. No pencil is required; an optional short pencil must align to the hole rather than pass obliquely through the casing. This is not insertion or use instruction, and illustrated size relations do not establish physical fit or operating condition.

## Extend a short rear shaft from the housing

Draw a short rear metal shaft located consistently with the main-axis sketch, exposed only enough to connect the crank. It cannot grow randomly from base/top or appear through transparent housing. Hide the arm to inspect shaft/housing contact, then restore without a background gap. Remove unnecessary threading if screw-like or shorten a cannon-like shaft. Shaft location and arm are separate checks; darkening the crank cannot hide an unclear root. Omit invented internal bearings and drivetrain sections.

## Offset the crank arm and connect the grip

Lead an offset arm from the rear-shaft end to a shorter grip shaft. In this illustrative design, grip and main shafts are approximately parallel but displaced. Sketch both axes and connecting arm rather than one straight rod extending along the main axis. Reconnect to the rear shaft if door-handle-like, or repair corner continuity if broken-ruler-like. Choose one stationary angle without calling it the correct start position. Parallel shafts are this invented arrangement, not a universal statement about brands, gearing or motion range.

## Seat the grip on its short shaft

Draw a short dark-grey wooden or plastic cylindrical grip, its long direction following the short shaft entering one end. Keep end ellipse and side width in one view rather than a sphere. Trace rear shaft to arm to grip with housing hidden. Lengthen modestly and repair attachment if grape-like, or reduce a heavy crossbar. No hand is needed to prove grip, and pixels establish no ergonomics. Do not add a matching housing knob for symmetry and invent another function.

## Separate broad shell light from metal reflections

Light upper left: soft shell top, darker grey-green near side, narrow grey-white metal reflections and softer grip light. Avoid identical sharp white borders. Hide reflections to inspect joins and restore without lines through housing or grip end. Improve local temperature/turns if model-plastic-like rather than adding scratches. Check directions, offset and three joins separately: parallel axes do not excuse a detached arm, and an attractive crank does not excuse an incompatible front face. Change one axis or corner with housing perspective and light fixed. A new crank pose needs revised offset and occlusion, not merely a dragged grip.
