Clear ruler numbers in a video do not establish an accurate measurement. A raised scale and an oblique camera can shift apparent alignment through parallax. Use a self-made paper-strip demonstration with ordinary playback, pause and replay in iCouldPlay. The outcome is a traceable reading note, not precision measurement from screen pixels or automatic scale recognition.

## Prepare a paper-strip demonstration

Film a paper strip beside an intact ordinary ruler, first from above and then slightly obliquely. No powered equipment, blades or engineering task is involved. Lay both flat and record units and placement during filming. With an existing self-made clip, mark unseen zero points or endpoints missing rather than assuming a standard length. Preserve the original. Keep production notes separate from viewing notes so prior knowledge does not conceal inadequate visual evidence.

## Record units and the smallest visible division

Play the complete clip normally and locate a section showing ruler and strip together. Identify whether large numbers mark centimeters or another unit, then inspect the subdivisions. A printed 5 does not mean five millimeters, and ten small spaces need not have the same meaning on every ruler. Write readings only when units and divisions are legible; repeated enlargement cannot justify invented decimals. Use a content landmark and approximate playback position without requiring frame stepping or treating a pause as an exact acquisition time.

## Locate the strip start and the ruler zero separately

Pause to locate the strip’s left endpoint relative to a graduation. A ruler’s physical edge is not necessarily its zero line. If the endpoints align with two and seven centimeters, length is seven minus two: five centimeters, not seven. Copy start, end and units before calculating rather than filling readings with the known strip length. If a hand or shadow obscures either end, replay nearby sections. Leave it unreadable if necessary instead of substituting a different shot’s placement.

## Inspect the viewing direction at the reading point

The [NIST explanation of parallax](<https://www.nist.gov/document/07-lr-15-annual-annual-finalpdf>) describes apparent alignment changing with viewpoint when graduations and measurement point are separated. Do not calculate camera angles from the clip. Classify the view as visibly overhead, oblique or uncertain. Preserve conditions such as a thick ruler or a strip in a different plane from the marks. Legible endpoint numbers are visible readings, not automatic proof of accurate length; viewpoint and legibility are separate evidence.

## Verify placement before comparing viewpoints

Play through the viewpoint change and check whether ruler and strip remain still. If either moves or the shot cuts, do not attribute every reading difference to parallax. Record object movement and camera changes separately. A continuous section with unchanged placement is a useful comparison, though it establishes only visible conditions. Do not average an oblique reading with a production-note value to force agreement. Averaging does not remove an uncontrolled systematic error, and extra decimal places do not make it reliable.

## Track the same endpoint instead of screen coordinates

Camera movement can shift the right endpoint toward the screen center. Use the strip’s shape and neighboring graduations to identify the same endpoint instead of reusing a previous pause’s screen coordinate. Do not mistake the strip’s shadow for its edge. Draw an external sketch locating endpoints and marks without requiring an unverified measurement overlay in iCouldPlay. The sketch is a retrieval aid. Label obscured or blurred evidence and do not claim that reconstructed lines were visible in the recording.

## Check cropping and captions over the scale

If a ruler endpoint sits at the frame edge, check fill cropping and use an actually available fit control if appropriate. If optional captions cover graduations, temporarily disable them for comparison and record restoration; burned-in text cannot be removed that way. Display changes alter visibility without correcting filming parallax. If the source omitted an endpoint, stop the measurement and consult an existing trustworthy production record instead of extrapolating an unseen end.

## State the limits of reading precision

Record only precision supported by visible divisions. A player’s millisecond display does not imply millimeter measurement accuracy. If an endpoint lies between marks, give an approximate range and identify estimation rather than extracting three decimals from enlarged blur. The task does not calibrate instruments or certify product dimensions. Accurate physical measurement requires a separate appropriate process. Success means readings tied to their conditions, not an attractive value or perfect agreement with an answer.

## Save a replayable endpoint record

Save file identity, content landmark, units, both endpoint readings, difference, viewpoint conditions, obstructions and unknowns. Separate the known production length from viewing readings so another replay can independently locate the evidence. Restore captions, display mode and original position without overwriting the file. No automatic scale recognition, physical-device test or metrological certification is claimed. Recheck after a new shot, file or placement instead of reusing the old difference.
