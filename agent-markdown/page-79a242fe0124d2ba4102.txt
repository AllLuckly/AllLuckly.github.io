A thicker arrow deep in a tank still needs a label saying pressure or force. Read a self-made three-page PDF in ManYue to check depth, pressure type and area conditions, then deliver a reproducible ledger. Values belong to a stipulated ideal static-fluid model, not phone measurements or in-app physics computation.

## Put surface and gravity conditions in the opening panel

Externally draw stationary water with a horizontal free surface exposed to p0=100000Pa, densityρ=1000kg/m³ and stipulated g=10m/s². State nearly constant density and no flow. Put A and B0.20m and 0.50m below the same surface, measuring vertically rather than along a sloping wall. Use P1–P3 for conditions, ledger, counterexamples and retain an editable source. If an encountered diagram omits density or the reference surface, record unknowns instead of inferring all conditions from blue coloring.

## Separate depth units from graphic length

Write 20cm=0.20m and 50cm=0.50m, then use metres in the formula. Lines of 40 and 100 screen pixels would indicate a drawing scale of 200pixels/m, not physical water depth in pixels. Zoom changes displayed lengths, not labelled depths. Mark A/B precisely so h is not mistaken for height above the bottom or water below a point. For a perspective photograph, follow stipulated labels rather than derive exact metres from compressed screenshot lengths.

## Calculate the water-column gauge increment first

On paper use pg=ρgh: A gives 1000×10×0.20=2000Pa and B5000Pa, a difference of 3000Pa. These are increments relative to the same pressure above the surface, not total absolute pressures. Depth and gauge-pressure ratios are both 2.5. Density 1000 is not a thousand-litre volume to add to the formula. Retain units: kg/m³ times m/s² times m gives N/m². A result labelled 2000N suggests a missing area distinction before a wrong numerical value.

Independently check the difference: B is 0.30m deeper,so ρg times that difference gives 3000Pa too. This must agree with subtracting the two absolute pressures. A mismatch calls for checking centimetre conversion and decimals rather than silently assigning different densities.

```text
rho=1000 kg/m^3; g=10 m/s^2; p0=100000 Pa
A: h=0.20 m; pg=2000 Pa; p_abs=102000 Pa
B: h=0.50 m; pg=5000 Pa; p_abs=105000 Pa
pg(B)-pg(A)=3000 Pa
```

## Add surface pressure separately for absolute pressure

Calculate pabs=p0+pg: A102000Pa and B105000Pa. Their absolute-pressure ratio is not 2.5 because both include the 100000Pa baseline. A sealed gas above the liquid would require a new p0. At zero depth gauge pressure is zero but absolute pressure remains p0, catching omitted or repeated additions. The A-to-B pressure difference remains 3000Pa because their common baseline cancels; that does not make absolute and gauge pressure interchangeable everywhere.

## Compare same-depth points under matching conditions

Add a narrow straight tank and a wide sloping one with the same liquid, surface pressure, gravity and 0.50m measurement depth. Both gauge pressures are 5000Pa regardless of total liquid volume. This does not imply equal total bottom force, since area and pressure distribution may differ. One downward arrow also cannot describe every direction of static pressure. Force on a small surface is normal to it. Label this a local-pressure comparison, not a complete sidewall or whole-container equilibrium analysis.

## Introduce area only for a specified pressure-difference force

Take a horizontal patch S=0.010m², uniform depth, with the other side at the same p0. Net pressure-force magnitude pgS is 20N at A and 50N at B. Doubling area leaves pressure unchanged but doubles this force. This uses the stated pressure difference; absolute water-side pressure times area is not automatically net force. A vertical surface spanning appreciable depth needs a pressure-distribution treatment rather than deepest pressure times total area. The patch is an ideal model, not a constructed apparatus.

Check area units too:0.010m² is 100cm²,a square 10cm per side,not 0.010cm². Given a side length,square it and convert units before multiplying pressure difference. A horizontal patch shares one depth; a tall vertical surface lacks that uniformity even with the same area.

## Troubleshoot through conditions, ledger,counterexample pages

Import the PDF, check surface/depth on P1, pressure/force columns on P2 and counterexamples on P3. Bookmark P2 and use A/B and the surface as return anchors, not only page numbers. Lower pressure at B suggests reversed depths; absolute pressure 5000 suggests omitted p0; changed pressure after changing area suggests copying the force column. After one correction repeat the zero-depth check. Without actual opening or bookmarking record only paper results. App page numbers are neither depths nor sensor readings.

Add a pressure-reference column. An unlabeled pressure with no reference stays ambiguous rather than arbitrarily choosing gauge or absolute. Deep water in a caption supplies no exact h; all numerical results here come from specified points and surface labels,not adjectives.
