A balloon whisk needs both a coherent outer cage and traceable individual wires. Use a simplified four-U-wire example, each wire returning both ends to the neck. This count is compositional, not a universal product specification. Draw a stationary manual whisk in Aquarell without powered rotation or food preparation. Repair broken paths and crossings first, then add restrained metal reflection rather than filling its hollow interior with dense lines.

## Establish handle axis and cage envelope

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px landscape canvas with a pale blue-grey background. Point the handle lower left to upper right, cage at the right. Sketch a handle axis and elongated egg-shaped envelope, widest somewhat beyond the neck and rounded rather than spear-pointed. Separate sketch, rear wires, front wires, handle, ferrule and shadow; choose muted wood brown. Keep one shared axis. Remove solid interior if spoon-like or excessive parallel marks if shuttlecock-like, retaining four traceable U paths. The envelope guide is not part of the final image.

## Give each of four U wires two endpoints

Label wires A–D in a diagnostic sketch. Each travels from the neck around a rounded end and back, giving eight returning ends. Use four faint sketch colours, not eight independent floating needles. Slightly different spatial planes allow crossings and overlaps rather than eight equally spaced borders. Trace the assigned letter if an extra closed tip loop appears and remove an accidental turn or join. Four illustrative wires are not four detached circles: each must leave and return to the common neck. Endpoint accounting comes ahead of metallic brightness.

## Gather wire ends into the neck

Add a short silver-grey ferrule at the handle front, gathering and enclosing eight ends without exposed black cut faces. Hide the handle and check actual ferrule-to-wire connections rather than a cage beside a disc. Ferrule width may be slightly smaller than or close to the handle, but its axis must align. Smooth an abrupt bouquet-like spread. Reconnect cut-looking wires, then occlude. Hidden fastening methods are outside this exterior study; invented screws, welds or springs cannot rescue unclear visible connections.

## Treat crossings as occlusion rather than joints

Keep front wire continuous at a crossing and weaken or conceal the rear segment without adding rivets or welded nodes. Solve one near crossing, then trace letters through the rest. If woven-net-like, remove forced alternating over/under behaviour: projected wire planes need not weave like a basket. Separate local spacing or quiet rear contrast if wires knot, rather than whitening the whole cage. Background must remain visible through it. Crossing counts change with viewpoint and are not a fixed number copied from a frontal reference.

## Round slender wire without heavy outlines

Lay in mid-grey wire with fine grey-white upper-left highlights and a darker turning side. Keep roughly consistent width, slightly quieter at distance without broken hairlines. Break reflections rather than outlining every wire black around a white centre like thick hose. Add a few value transitions if plastic-like or reduce excessive white if glaring. Keep handle grain to two or three quiet axial cues. Grain must not cross the ferrule or bend the handle axis away from the cage.

## Keep tabletop shadow from filling the cage

Keep handle and neck near the table with cage slightly raised, casting softer lower-right shadow. Wire shadows may merge; duplicating four equally dark U shapes could suggest eight wires. Hide shadow and count four real wires, then restore and identify misleading shadow segments. Add necessary contact cues if floating; lighten interior shadow and recover background if the cage becomes a solid grey ball. Shadows describe placement, not real elasticity or load. Handle and cage supports need not all occupy one height.

## Audit the four-colour sketch for extra branches

Keep A–D paths and recite each neck-to-tip-to-neck journey and eight endpoint assignments. Eight endpoints do not excuse a wire switching onto another at a crossing. Hide three wires, inspect one smooth U, then restore crossings. Adjust one wire at a time with envelope and axis fixed. A separate three-wire version may help at small size if labelled accordingly; do not remove one while claiming four. Simplification may change an illustration choice, but not silently invalidate its stated checks.

## Inspect openness ahead of reflections in the output

Save the layered native work and inspect cage negative spaces, neck and rounded tip in a PNG at use size. Reduce distant contrast and stray highlights if lines merge instead of thickening them all. Repair ferrule occlusion if a black neck gap appears. Keep background stripes from inventing a fifth wire. Record a specific fault such as C joining D after a right-hand crossing and repair by letter. Finish with an open cage, four continuous paths and eight ends returning to the neck, without claims about whisking performance or kitchen/material testing.
