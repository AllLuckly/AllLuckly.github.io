Build a butterfly around one body axis and paired wing roots, distinguishing structural symmetry, pattern differences and text that should not be mirrored.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/vectorpaint-butterfly-wing-baseline/>), translated on 2026-11-05. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Paired butterfly wings seem to need only a flipped copy, but their roots, contours and markings each need separate correspondence checks. Draw an original decorative butterfly, first establishing a bilateral framework and then introducing a few deliberate differences. This is not a species-identification plate and assumes neither automatic symmetry detection nor generated markings; you check the relationships yourself.

## Fix the body axis and both pairs of wing roots

Create a 1000 by 800 pixel canvas with a slender central body. Mark four roots near the thorax for separate upper and lower wings. The body axis is a drawing reference, not an anatomically precise measurement. Leave margins outside the wings and above the antennae. Define left and right as image directions so explanations do not unexpectedly switch to the animal’s own sides. Before markings, inspect the body and all four attachments. Upper and lower wings need distinct contours rather than four copies of one shape explained only by labels.

## Draw two closed wing contours on one side

Use the pen to draw a broad left upper wing and a shorter, rounder lower wing, placing nodes at genuine direction changes. Keep roots near the body and remove sudden kinks or self-intersections. Inspect the silhouette in one fill colour with strokes temporarily disabled so heavy outlines cannot conceal gaps. Preserve independently selectable wings and define the upper wing in front at their overlap. Establish complete closed shapes before adding numerous veins. Unexpected fills call for checking node order rather than background-coloured patches concealing an open-path problem.

## Check attachments immediately after making the opposite side

Copy the left sources, use an actually available horizontal-flip method and position them on the right; if no suitable control exists, reconstruct from references instead of assuming a hidden shortcut. A flipped copy is not automatically symmetric about the body axis. Compare paired root distances, outermost points and upper/lower heights. The body may cover roots but should not be widened to hide broken attachments. Save the undecorated bilateral baseline. When altering a wing later, identify its partner explicitly; independent copies are not permanently linked.

## Organise markings by wing and inspect clipping

Start with two or three large markings per wing in a small, clear palette, assigning each to its own wing surface. Use a verified mask or draw within the contour where clipping is needed; white overlays do not delete excess paths. Hide the body to inspect markings across the upper/lower seam, then restore stacking. Copied markings still need orientation and size checks against their wing, not merely canvas alignment. Keep short IDs in an explanation area. Flipping text reverses it, so rebuild readable labels rather than expecting readers to decipher mirrored lettering.

## Record the differences you introduce deliberately

In a baseline copy, change only one marking’s size on the lower right wing and identify it as an invented decorative difference. Do not reclassify accidental displacement as natural realism. Compare outer contours to distinguish an unchanged framework from altered markings. A wing-edge change deserves a separate version and renewed bilateral review. Object names and a simple change list suffice; no database is needed. Real butterflies need not be perfectly symmetric, but that does not excuse every error. Explain which elements should match and which intentionally differ instead of inventing reasons afterward.

## Check body and four-wing identity at reduced size

At intended display size, check that the body remains distinct and antennae do not merge into a black mass, then identify four wings. Reduce dense markings in a simplified copy without deleting structural roots. Temporarily inspect a single colour for balanced visual weight instead of hiding a much larger side through colour differences. If lower wings become tiny slivers under the uppers, repair occlusion before deciding to resize. Every simplification needs renewed attachment, closure and stacking checks; success at large size does not establish success when small.

## Save editable sources and inspect the actual export

Retain one-side sources, the bilateral baseline and the decorated difference version with traceable names, not just a final preview. Check exported edges and masks against light and dark backgrounds; transparency does not remove background objects. Name the decorative butterfly, dimensions and revision and reopen the actual file to exclude draft axes and mirrored labels. A species illustration would require separate reliable morphological references; this geometry exercise cannot identify species. For editable delivery, explain which objects are independent copies so editing one side prompts checking the other.

## Troubleshoot from the axis and wing roots

For imbalance, compare axis, roots and extrema before scaling a whole side. Escaping markings need object/mask-scope checks; gaps revealed when moving the body indicate attachments that need repair. For reversed text, inspect the flip selection rather than flipping the entire image again. If upper and lower wings are ambiguous, remove decoration and inspect silhouette and stacking before adding shadows. Recheck framework, markings, difference log and output after repairs. Keep the design when each difference is an accountable choice in a maintainable source.
