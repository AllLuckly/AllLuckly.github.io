English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/manyue-resistor-power-fixed-voltage-versus-current-energy-ledger/>), translated on 2026-11-19. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Two cards saying “resistance increases” can have opposite power changes. Separate fixed-voltage and fixed-current ledgers, read their controlled quantities in ManYue and compare energy bars. No real wiring is required, and colour brightness does not diagnose resistor temperature.

## Declare two separate supply models at the top

Use ideal ohmic resistors of 3Ω or 6Ω, each resistance constant during its calculation, excluding temperature-induced changes. P1 fixes terminal voltage at 6V; P2 independently fixes current at 2A. They are not simultaneous conditions of one source experiment. Put units/controls on P3, export a PDF and retain editable work. After import, inspect each heading and bookmark P2. Draw a single resistor symbol rather than secretly change topology.

## Recover current from Ohm’s law on the voltage-fixed page

With 6V across each resistor, 3Ω gives I=6/3=2A and 6Ω gives 1A. Doubling resistance halves current; do not retain 2A in the second fixed-6V card. Check V=IR: 2×3=6 and 1×6=6. Voltage is a terminal potential difference, not an unreferenced value at one point. Without duration, proceed to power rather than claim a joule consumption.

Hide resistance labels for an inverse check: at 6V, 6W gives I=P/V=1A and R=6Ω; 12W gives 2A and 3Ω. Recover the original cards rather than assume larger power means larger resistance. The inversion retains nonzero voltage/ohmic conditions. Power alone without voltage/current does not uniquely determine R, and colour supplies no missing input.

## Substitute one consistent state into all power forms

P=VI gives 12W and 6W. V²/R gives 36/3 and 36/6, again 12 and 6; I²R gives 4×3 and 1×6. The formulas agree when V, I, R describe the same state. Keep all three inputs on each row instead of importing an incompatible value from another page. The R factor in I²R alone does not imply increasing power with resistance under fixed voltage.

Changing only the 3Ω card from 6V to 12V gives 4A and 48W, four times 12W. Check 12×4=144/3 instead of retaining old 2A. Resistance and duration stay fixed. This tests the square relation under its conditions, not a fourfold guarantee for nonlinear or temperature-changing devices.

## Allow voltage to change on the current-fixed page

On P2 fix I=2A: 3Ω requires 6V and 6Ω requires 12V. I²R gives 12W and 24W, doubling with resistance; VI agrees as 2×6 and 2×12. This does not claim a real source supplies unlimited voltage or power. A card retaining 6V,2A,6Ω violates Ohm’s relation. Mark inconsistent conditions before selecting a power answer that hides the conflict.

```text
R values: 3 ohm, 6 ohm; ideal ohmic, constant R
fixed V=6 V: I=(2,1) A; P=(12,6) W
fixed I=2 A: V=(6,12) V; P=(12,24) W
for 5 s: energies fixed-V=(60,30) J; fixed-I=(60,120) J
P=V*I=I^2*R=V^2/R for each consistent state
```

## Separate five-second energy bars from power columns

For 5s at unchanged states, E=Pt gives fixed-voltage energies 60J, 30J and fixed-current energies 60J, 120J. Watts are energy-transfer rates; joules are interval amounts. 12W does not mean 12J over five seconds. A separate 10s control for the 6W resistor gives 60J, matching 12W over 5s despite unequal power. Label duration instead of measuring it from a drawn bar.

For 3Ω at 2A over 2s then 1A over 4s, energy=12×2+3×4=36J and six-second average power=6W. Average current is 4/3A; its square times 3Ω is 16/3W, not the time-averaged power. Squaring is nonlinear. Keep this varying-current control separate from the constant-state five-second cards instead of extending one instantaneous power across a whole interval.

## Do not infer temperature or brightness from power alone

P describes electrical-energy conversion rate in ideal resistors. Heat capacity, cooling, area and environment are absent, so 24W supplies no Celsius temperature. Equal-power objects can have different temperatures under different cooling. Lamp brightness also depends on material and efficiency; fixed R is not every filament model. Separate larger power, greater interval energy and higher temperature. With equal duration the first two can be compared here; temperature remains unknown.

## Diagnose controlled quantities and incompatible inputs

If both pages say 24W, check whether a fixed-current answer migrated to fixed voltage. A 2 A result with 6V/6Ω requires division checked. “Consumption twelve” lacks watts, joules or time. Enlarge Ω and squares. Correct one controlled quantity or input, then verify with another power form. If source type is absent, record insufficient conditions rather than assume voltage/current control or App parameter recognition.

## Reopen and explain the opposite changes

Reopen at the fixed-current bookmark and write 12W→24W, then return to fixed voltage for 12W→6W, explaining why formulas agree. Check four five-second energies and the ten-second equal-energy control. Deliver controlled quantities, V/I/R and power/time ledgers, not merely a red-resistor image.
