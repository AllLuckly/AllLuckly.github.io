## Identify visible features

Choose a Black-capped Chickadee photo with a clear source and permission; record its angle and date. Cornell describes a black cap and bib, white cheeks and gray back. Similar species have different ranges and details, so one color alone does not establish identity. This exercise uses a perched profile; mark obscured or blurred areas unknown instead of inventing every feather from another image.

## Set up canvas and layers

Create an approximately 1600 by 1400 canvas in Aquarell and save an editable original; the dimensions are a choice for this exercise. Separate background, twig, bird color and sketch with the layer controls visible in your version. Leave room ahead of the bill and behind the tail. Let the twig cross the composition at a slight angle rather than touching the canvas edge, and block the layout lightly before adding detail.

## Compare head, body and tail

Block the body with an oval and attach a nearly round head, marking the short bill, tail base and tip. Compare head size to body in the reference, then compare tail length and its downward angle. Do not impose a fixed proportion on an unclear outline. View the canvas small to inspect the head-body transition and balance. If the head looks pasted on, revise the neck before adding feather marks.

## Place bill and eye

Compare the bill tip to the front of the cheek; draw the short pointed bill and indicate the meeting of upper and lower bill. Where the eye lies near a dark area, locate it relative to bill base and cap edge before using a tiny highlight or a slightly different dark value. Do not enlarge it until expression changes. If shadow hides the eye, record the uncertainty and focus on the silhouette and larger markings.

## Paint cap, cheeks and bib

Lay down the pale cheek first, then paint the dark cap from crown toward the bill according to the reference boundary. The bib beneath the throat is a separate area; in profile the white cheek may separate it from the cap. Observe the boundaries before filling instead of making the whole head black. Check edges enlarged and the cheek shape at thumbnail size. A dark gray can retain variation without losing the eye in a solid black area.

## Build wing and belly values

Block the back and folded wing in gray with a lighter belly. Lighting and photo white balance affect color, so a screen sample is not the sole standard for the species. Draw the wing overlap on the body first and suggest feather edges with a few marks rather than identical heavy outlines. Hide detail temporarily: head and body proportions should still work without feather texture.

## Draw twig and grip

Draw the twig direction and thickness so it passes continuously under the feet, stopping visible lines where toes cover it. Follow the visible reference for legs and toes around the twig: near toes appear in front, while the branch hides rear structure. Mark unseen toes as unknown. If the bird floats, align each leg-body connection, toe overlap and twig position rather than hiding the issue under a large shadow.

## Set background and inspect silhouette

Place the background below bird and twig. Muted gray-green shapes can suggest distant foliage without drawing every vein. Choose a value around crown and bill that separates the dark cap from its surroundings; also distinguish the pale belly and cheek from adjacent background. At a small size, check that head, wing, tail and twig direction are immediately readable before deciding which background marks to keep.

## Compare two head treatments

Duplicate the working copy. Keep only the cap, cheek and bib masses in one version; add a few feather edges and eye details in the other. Preserve the same outline and values in both and compare them at a small scale. If the detailed version reads less clearly, remove marks that interrupt the boundary instead of changing proportion and color together. Compare with the photo again so the white cheek does not shrink to a thin line.

## Troubleshoot and save

If the bill looks pasted on, check its connection to the head. If the bib is too large, recheck its boundaries with cheek and body. If the wing disappears, compare gray values before adding an outline. Save the editable original, then use an export control visible in the current version to save a separate PNG or JPEG. Reopen it to check crown, tail, twig and cropping, and return to the original to verify editable layers. Record the source and one overlap to improve; mark unperformed device steps pending.

## Recheck markings and exported copy

Place the photograph beside the canvas without tracing over it. Identify the boundaries of cap, cheek and bib in the photograph and then in the artwork. If one disappears at thumbnail size, adjust the neighboring values or boundary before changing a defining feature for clarity. Check the export filename and location and keep the source record. Reopening a flattened image checks its appearance; it does not prove the original layers remain in that image. If the printed cheek merges with the paper or background, adjust the surrounding value and print a small proof. First confirm that the printer did not change scale or color before revising the screen composition. Keep the first proof for comparison; one print does not predict every paper.
