## Use atomic number to determine proton identity

All cards have Z=8 and eight protons per nucleus, preserving oxygen identity. Nine protons would be another element, not repaired by changing O’s charge superscript. Each proton carries +e, but Z=8 is not net particle charge +8 without accounting for electrons. Put protons/neutrons in one nucleus box and electrons in a separate schematic area. This organizes counts without claiming true nuclear/electron-cloud scale.

## Subtract proton count from mass number for neutrons

A counts protons plus neutrons, not grams. Oxygen-16 has 16−8=8 neutrons; oxygen-18 has 18−8=10. Equal protons but different neutrons define isotopes. Eighteen is not eighteen neutrons plus eight protons, and a two-unit mass-number increase does not add charge +2. Write A=Z+N and check 8+8=16,8+10=18. Neutrons count as nucleons but contribute no net charge to the charge sum.

## Recover electron counts for neutral and two-minus states

Both neutral cards have eight electrons, balancing eight charges of +e with eight of −e. Oxygen-18 two-minus has 8 protons,10 neutrons,10 electrons and net (8−10)e=−2e. Two-minus means two more electrons than neutral, not two fewer or two extra neutrons. An oxygen-16 two-minus control still has 10 electrons but 8 neutrons. Isotope and ionic identities can change independently. Substitute counts into the charge sum rather than estimate dots in a ring.

```text
oxygen: Z=8; A=protons+neutrons
O-16 neutral: p=8; n=8; electrons=8; net charge=0
O-18 neutral: p=8; n=10; electrons=8; net charge=0
O-18 2-: p=8; n=10; electrons=10; net charge=-2e
O-16 2- control: p=8; n=8; electrons=10
charge/e=Z-electron_count
```

## Separate mass number from the periodic-table average

An element’s average relative atomic mass combines isotope abundances and actual isotopic atomic masses; it is not one particle’s integer mass number. An oxygen average near 16 must not be rounded into A for an oxygen-18 card. This task specifies nucleon counts 16/18, without natural abundance or precise atomic masses. Electrons have mass but are excluded from nucleon mass number. A=18 also does not assert an exact atomic mass of eighteen unified mass units.

A neutral neon-20 control has Z=10, A=20, thus 10 protons,10 neutrons,10 electrons. It shares 10 electrons and 10 neutrons with oxygen-18 two-minus, yet differs in element/charge: oxygen has 8 protons and charge −2, neon 10 protons and neutral. Electron count alone, even with this matching neutron count, does not identify an element. Keep proton number first instead of treating similar outer-dot diagrams as interchangeable symbols.

## Do not turn count diagrams into literal electron orbits

Use distinct shapes and p/n labels inside the nucleus and e labels externally, retaining grayscale readability. Ten electrons may be arranged in rows for counting, not ten classical orbital paths or a quantum-state simulation. The task verifies three counts, not permanent left/right electron positions. If a reference uses shell diagrams, read its model scope first; count-layout choices do not locate real particles in space.
