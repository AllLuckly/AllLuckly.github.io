Counting to a particular sonnet line does not establish a turn of thought. Confirm the edition’s poetic lines, then record before/after ideas and their evidence. This differs from rhyme,enjambment and pronoun-attribution checks, without automatic metre judgement,turn detection or a single author-intention answer.

## Establish poem identity and genuine line boundaries

Choose a permitted poem explicitly identified as a sonnet and record title,author,translator,edition and nearby wording. Distinguish poetic lines from display wraps: fourteen visible screen lines do not establish the form. For translations record whether original lineation is preserved, without inventing missing lines. Analyse thought progression without deriving mandatory rhyme/metre from fourteen alone. Use manual line/word locators when selection is unavailable, with no assumed OCR or structure extractor.

## Use conventional locations as clues, not conclusions

Use the [Poetry Foundation volta account](<https://www.poetryfoundation.org/education/glossary/volta>) for a turn in thought/argument, with familiar octave/sestet or pre-final-couplet locations in different traditions. These are clues, not permission to mark line9/13 without explaining change. Record the form documented for this poem, or unknown. Do not impose English traditions on arbitrary fourteen-line Chinese layout or demand exactly one equally strong turn in every poem. Specific wording must support the interpretation.

## State what the earlier section proposes

Read the earlier section and summarise its concern,question or judgement in one or two personal sentences with supporting lines. Preserve negatives,conditions and questions rather than treating rhetorical questions as settled answers. If lines extend one image, record progression without forcing opposed positions. Separate quotations from summaries; do not quote a paraphrase as the poet. Temporarily set aside whether a line is ninth and inspect how thought accumulates rather than substituting form labels for reading.

## Explain before/after differences at a candidate turn

Words such as but,yet,now may mark candidates, but inspect changes of target,attitude,question or argumentative direction. A connective with continuing content is not automatically the main turn; a shift can occur without one. State before/after propositions with decisive wording. If line7 already turns and line9 continues, do not force line9 to be the first turn for convention. Retain two candidates when evidence is incomplete instead of using reading progress to decide.

## Practise with an original fourteen-line outline

Use an original structural outline: first eight lines question why a lamp guards an empty house; line9 says “But what if I am the one returning?”; the final six shift from observing others to asking whether the speaker needs that lamp. This is a fourteen-line planning outline, not a finished metrical/rhymed poem. Target and personal involvement change, not merely the word but. Summarise both sections and test line9’s initiating role. Eight plus six is fourteen, not fifteen, and line9 is not also among the first eight. Keep this invented exercise separate from the book.

## Separate a turn interpretation from authorial intention

In the actual poem label a possible answer,objection,concession or renewed question with uncertainty rather than author-confirmed intention. For an added translated connective check translator/edition before consulting original/notes; do not attribute it automatically to the source. Multiple turns may retain separate evidence rather than being reduced to one. Append revisions instead of overwriting the initial summary to imply uninterrupted correctness. Highlights/notes locate evidence, not prove your argumentative segmentation.

## Preserve lineation and interpretation through retrieval

Leave and retrieve candidates by poem,edition and wording, then reread surrounding lines rather than matching a connective alone. Preserve genuine line identities after font changes and identify personally assigned numbering. Export through available means and inspect original excerpt,before/after summaries,candidates and limits separately, keeping the book elsewhere. Share necessary lawful quotations and label the invented outline without attaching a real poet’s name. Restore position; retrieval progress does not prove mastery of the whole poem.

## Check boundaries, thought and evidence in order

For a moving “ninth line”, inspect display wrapping. For an unexplained labelled turn, write before/after summaries. For but alone, inspect content rather than assigning a standard answer. Preserve unknown form information instead of deriving genre from personal layout. Retain line boundaries,earlier/later ideas and specific shift evidence, allowing no clear turn found. This is scoped formal reading, not automatic form identification,proven motivation or a claim that only one interpretation can be correct.
