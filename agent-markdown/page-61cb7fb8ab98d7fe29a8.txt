## 1. Build the thirty-second source externally

In an existing audio editor, place an owned fifteen-second passage from second five to second twenty, with five seconds of known silence before it and ten after it, producing thirty seconds. Use silence explicitly generated or verified by the tool; quiet room noise from a microphone is not automatically digital zero. The passage can contain pauses, so fifteen seconds denotes the selected interval, not uninterrupted speech. Retain the editing project and exported WAV and reread boundaries externally. This is a preparation plan, not a claim that assets have already been recorded. Repair an incorrect export before guessing inside the app.

## 2. Save a trimmed copy without altering the source

Externally copy the selected seconds five through twenty to time zero in a new project, or use an explicit selection-only export, producing the fifteen-second trim34.wav while keeping full34.wav untouched. An existing editor such as Audacity can perform its real selection-and-trim flow; the cited editing reference describes external work, not 调调 menus. Read the export back: the target is fifteen seconds with the selected content intact. Removing visible workspace without exporting audio is insufficient. If an edge cuts audible content, record it and adjust from the master instead of blaming a missing word on playback. Retain actual parameters, filenames and source paths for both outputs, checking clear words to identify the chosen interval.

## 3. Map the source regions to the trim

Use the half-open intervals below: source zero to five is leading silence, five to twenty the content region and twenty to thirty trailing silence. Trim zero to fifteen corresponds to the source content region; source second ten maps to trim second five. Subtracting five shifts the origin, not playback speed. Silence totals fifteen seconds and content-region duration fifteen, adding to thirty. Assigning a boundary to the following interval is a paper convention; actual edge samples require external verification. The ledger is manual analysis, not an importable chapter file or automatic editing script for 调调.

```

trim34.wav: [0,15) selected source content
source t = trim t + 5
```

## 5. Hear the source across both quiet boundaries

Confirm the full source starts normally at the beginning. During its intended first five quiet seconds, observe playback state and progress instead of immediately skipping. Identify the sound in the known content region, continue through about second twenty into trailing silence, and observe the normal ending around thirty. If content is not heard, inspect position, pause and output rather than filling observations from the ledger. Progress movement during silence proves display movement only, not the whole output chain; audible content still needs checking. Record approximate boundaries, not millisecond accuracy or gapless certification, and list real interruptions with their time and action.

## 6. Check the same content at the trim’s new origin

Open the trim separately and hear the same passage from its own beginning. The known word near source second ten should correspond to about trim second five, without the original trailing ten seconds. Verify that playback actually switched files rather than resuming the full source at second five. Similar sound alone can conceal a failed switch. The selected region may itself begin with a short pause, so lack of immediate sound does not prove trimming failed; consult the source selection. Listening checks identity and boundaries, not superior quality in a shorter file. Silence was removed externally, not automatically by 调调.

## 7. Separate intended silence from faults

Quiet only at the full source’s ends with audible content differs from both files being silent throughout. For the former consult intended boundaries; for the latter inspect pause, output and source. Missing words only in the trim call for external selection and export checks. If playback skips a region, record position and settings without inferring an internal silence-skip algorithm from one round. Change one asset or state per trial and keep the full source as a recovery reference rather than increasing volume, rerecording and reimporting together. Record room noise, digital silence and player pause separately; one vague “silence” label would obscure source properties and operating state.
