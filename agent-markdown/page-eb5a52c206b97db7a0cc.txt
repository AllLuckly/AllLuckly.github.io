English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-kelp-holdfast-stipe-blades-water/>), translated on 2026-11-20. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

Paint large brown algae attached to a rock, using bladder-bearing kelp as the example. A basal holdfast attaches to rock, flexible stipes extend upward and leaf-like blades sit at different heights. NOAA describes kelp as large brown algae and explains the holdfast, stipe, blade and gas bladders found in many species. This picture simplifies one structural choice and does not imply every kelp has bladders.

## Arrange surface, rock and growth direction

Create an approximately 1600 × 2000 portrait canvas, leaving a lighter water surface at top, a sloping rock at bottom and room for the algae to extend upward in the middle. Sketch two or three curved main directions lightly; they are not finished blade veins. Separate background water, rock, holdfast, stipes, blades and gas bladders into layers and save the draft. Proportions serve the composition, not measured depth or length. If the top looks crowded when reduced, adjust stipe directions rather than moving the holdfast to the surface.

## Attach the holdfast to the rock

Near the rock's upper part draw a small group of branching attachment structures whose ends meet the rock surface, then connect the stipe bases to their common center. Do not leave the holdfast as a floating black dot or treat it as a land plant root and assume the same absorption function. Temporarily hide the stipes and check the branches contact the rock. Restore them and trace one main stipe down to the base. If contact floats, fix contour and overlap before adding a little contact shadow; do not cover the rock in solid black.

## Curve flexible stipes with the current

From the base draw several slender stipes upward in distinct but coordinated curves, varying apparent width slightly for perspective. Near stipes may be clearer and distant ones paler. Each follows a continuous path; indicate crossings by local overlap instead of making every crossing a new branch. If many blades gather near the top, mark which stipe each joins in the sketch before cleaning lines. Current direction is an artistic choice, not proof of actual speed; avoid making every stipe a rigid parallel line.

## Place blades and bladders beside stipes

For this bladder-bearing example, draw small round or oval floats near the stipe at some blade bases, with each blade extending from nearby. Establish each blade's width and bend first, then add modest edge variation. Near-large and far-small is a compositional choice, not a judgment of development. Check a blade, its bladder and the connected stipe for unintended gaps. Do not demand the same number on every blade: the source says many species have bladders, not that all kelp share them.

## Separate overlapping blades with brown values

Block the stipes in dark brown, use warm brown for near blades and cooler paler brown-green behind; give bladders some value separation from adjacent blades. Establish broad planes before adding one or two light marks that follow form. Do not duplicate the same pattern on every blade. If they resemble stiff cards, vary a few bends and overlaps; if crossings are unreadable, reduce the number and leave gaps rather than outlining everything heavily. With texture hidden, each blade should still connect to its stipe.

## Suggest underwater depth without replacing structure

Use blue-green background water, darker below and lighter above, adding a little scattered light near the top without outlining every blade white. A few blurred vertical shapes can suggest distant kelp, but must not accidentally connect to the main stipes. Keep the holdfast readable above the rock; where surface light and upper blades meet, maintain clear front–back order. These colors establish mood and do not diagnose water quality, pollution or local species distribution.

Put one near blade in front of a distant stipe, and let another distant blade be partly hidden by a near stipe: the two different overlaps test depth. If an accidental break appears at a crossing, decide which part is in front and repair the wrongly erased segment. Do not let all crossing objects pass through one another. Keep a draft with fewer blades and compare readability; remove decoration if needed rather than deleting the holdfast or base.

## Trace connected parts and repair gaps

Hide distant background and blade texture. Starting at the holdfast on the rock, trace one stipe, a bladder and a blade; repeat with another differently curved stipe. If the bladder resembles a floating bead, inspect its contacts with blade and stipe. If a blade seems pasted on the background, recover its connection. Contact shadows only suggest where holdfast meets rock; branches need not all have identical thick outlines. This source supports drawing structure, not a diagnosis of health or ecological abundance.

## Save the original and inspect output layers

Save an editable draft, export an image using a control actually available in the installed version, and open it elsewhere. Reduce the view to check continuity from rock to surface; enlarge it to inspect gaps at fine stipes, bladders and blade junctions. Reopen the original and hide the background to make sure the subject stays visible. If it differs, check the chosen file and layer visibility first and preserve the original copy. State that this is a simplified bladder-bearing example; leave unperformed export, reopening and device steps pending. It is neither species identification nor a field survey.
