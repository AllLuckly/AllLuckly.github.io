Observe a round radish’s root, leaf stalks and fine root tail in a specimen or permitted photograph.

## Choose a round reference

Choose a round radish with leaves as a specimen or permitted photograph and record its source and view. The Royal Horticultural Society describes round and elongated roots in several colors. This exercise uses a red round root, not a universal radish shape. Choose a reference showing root, leaf stalks and fine root tail when possible; mark missing or obscured details unknown rather than combining different varieties.

## Set up canvas and layers

Create an approximately 1400 by 1800 portrait canvas in Aquarell and save an editable original; the dimensions are choices. Use visible layer controls to separate sketch, root, leaves and background. Place the round root in the lower half with space above the leaves and below the root tail. Draw a light axis and observe any tilt in the reference rather than assuming it is exactly vertical.

## Block the root

Use a slightly irregular round form for the swollen root. Compare width, height and the point where leaf stalks gather at the top. A round radish need not be a perfect geometric circle; highlights and small surface changes affect its appearance. Establish the whole before texture. Mark the fine tail’s origin at the lower root and connect it rather than leaving a floating white line. Check balance and margins small.

## Connect the leaf stalks

Observe where visible leaf stalks emerge at the root top and draw them spreading upward, with near stalks able to hide far ones. Do not attach leaves arbitrarily to the sides of the round root or force all stalks into one unreadable point. Start with two or three clearly visible stalks and broad leaf shapes, verify their connections, then add other visible leaves while preserving occlusions.

## Draw leaf edges and midribs

Block each leaf outline from the photograph, observing its tip, edge curves and midrib direction. Differences in apparent size reflect the chosen view, not a fixed leaf formula. Connect stalk to tip with a midrib, then add only a few side veins; avoid giving every leaf identical teeth. Draw only visible edges of a rear leaf and stop lines at the foreground leaf rather than drawing through it.

## Build volume in red

Lay a mid-value red on the root, then distinguish lit, shaded and lower regions according to the reference light. Red roots may contain lighter and darker areas, but the pale root tail and highlights are different features. Highlight position changes with light; do not put the same white dot on every radish. Hide leaves to inspect root volume, then restore them and check that stalks meet the top.

## Separate leaf values

Use slightly different green values for front and rear leaves, with a few darker marks at overlaps. Avoid pure-black veins everywhere and do not distinguish leaves by changing their shapes arbitrarily; stalk-to-leaf connections matter more than texture. At thumbnail size, if leaves merge into a mass, adjust gaps, values and stacking before adding more texture.

## Draw the root tail and contact

Follow the reference from the bottom of the round root into a tapering fine tail; use the observed curve and visible length rather than a rigid plastic-looking rod. If a specimen rests on a table, observe contact and add a modest shadow. If the photo shows it suspended, do not force a ground shadow. A separate root-tail layer helps reveal whether it merges with a shadow or table texture.

## Check outline and overlaps

Temporarily hide color and inspect connections between root, stalks and leaves in the sketch. If leaves look pasted beside the round root, revisit their origins at the top. If the fine root tail appears disconnected, zoom in to check color coverage and layer order. Restore color and shrink to phone size, confirming the red root, leaves and tail remain readable without relying only on the title.

## Troubleshoot and export

If the root looks like a flat red circle, inspect its shape and side values rather than adding white lines to force volume. If veins are crowded, remove some and recheck stalk directions. Save the editable original and export a separate PNG or JPEG using a control visible in the installed version. Reopen it to inspect leaf tips, root tail and any table shadow for cropping; return to the original to verify layers. Record the source and unperformed device or print steps.

## Compare two backgrounds

Duplicate the original, using a light tabletop background in one copy and a darker neutral background in the other while keeping root, leaves and fine tail unchanged. Compare them at the same size, especially whether a pale root tail disappears against the light background. If adjustment is needed, change local background value first, then recheck stalk-root connections. Keep both copies and the source record for future composition work. Compare dark leaf edges against the background too: if values are close, separate the background value rather than outlining every leaf heavily. Finally inspect both exports at the same zoom to ensure the change does not hide the root-tail origin, stalk junctions, or margins.
