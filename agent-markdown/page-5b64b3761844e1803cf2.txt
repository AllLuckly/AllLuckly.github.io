Plan a fictional walking-day journal spread with a central gutter, sketch hierarchy, hand-lettered title and usable writing space.

English translation of the [Chinese original](<https://allluckly.cn/guides/aquarell-travel-journal-spread/>), translated on 2026-10-26. Original publication dates, attribution, code and version notes are retained.

A travel journal need not give every stop equal space. Design a fictional walk as one main street sketch, two small objects, a route and writing areas. Plan the spread on one canvas; this is a layout study rather than an automatic book-binding or multi-page feature.

## Set up a separate study

Create an approximately 2400×1500 px spread with faint page and gutter guides. Separate street sketch, route, objects and title. Test the longest heading and a note at the intended display size. Treat it as one illustration; check actual paper, binding and bleed separately for print.

Block in four content areas and check the gutter and balance between pages. Save a decoration-free layout. Test a note of realistic length now rather than squeezing writing into leftover space after tickets and cups are finished.

## Reserve two pages and a centre gutter

Divide the wide canvas into two pages with a small central reserve. Check dimensions separately for real printing or binding. Keep essential letters, faces and route turns away from the gutter. Establish content regions and consistent outer margins before decoration.

List four items: the main street sketch, a ticket-shaped note, a travel cup and a three-stop route. Use fictional places and times until replacing them with your own. Estimate writing length before painting so notes do not become tiny crowded text. Use area and spacing to establish hierarchy.

## Give the main sketch the largest visual area

Place the street sketch in the upper left, using roughly one third to half the spread. Concentrate detail around one doorway or junction and simplify the surroundings. Avoid crowding the gutter. Let cropped forms clearly enter and leave the edge instead of barely touching it.

Limit the main palette and repeat one or two colours in the small objects. Soften image edges near writing areas. At small size, the main sketch should lead to title and route rather than lose attention to a vivid ticket. Resolve large masses before paper textures or borders.

## Use the route for sequence rather than geographic precision

Connect three small stops on the right page with a simple curve. Treat it as an itinerary diagram, omitting a precise-looking scale when distance and direction are fictional. Keep stop names short and place explanations beside them. Establish sequence before adding footprints or arrows.

Repeat an accent colour from the main sketch and keep the route line thinner than the title. Leave usable space beside stops for a time or observation. If crowded, shorten labels or enlarge the route area before moving icons. Preserve the journal’s writing function before adding decoration.

## Use small objects as pauses rather than new focal points

Give the ticket and cup small, balanced areas. The ticket needs only an outline, date and short sample text. Describe the cup with an ellipse, shadow and a few identifying features. They should provide visual pauses without matching the street sketch’s complexity.

Use a faint local shadow for a lightly overlapping paper if helpful, rather than identical heavy shadows under every element. Leave clear gaps and avoid cup handles touching route lines or ticket corners colliding with titles. Check tangencies close up and hierarchy at small size.

## Proof the title and writing areas together

Hand-letter a short two-line title over faint baselines, larger than stop labels. If using text tools, check the available font’s glyphs and wrapping. Keep writing guides faint. Test an actual paragraph length to verify that the reserved area is usable rather than merely attractive.

Before sharing, verify place names, dates and sequence and replace fictional examples as needed. A decorative route is not a navigation guide. Save blank and filled versions. Next, retain the margins and three-stop structure while changing the main illustration to test the layout’s flexibility.

## Review and plan the next study

Review main image and space small, read route stops at normal size and inspect lettering close up. Imagine covering the street sketch: the right page should retain a clear sequence. Restore it as the focus, keeping vivid small objects and the reserved gutter from disrupting the hierarchy.

Keep blank and filled PNGs with editable title and route. Next, replace only the main sketch with a vertical doorway while retaining margins and three stops. If balance fails, move content regions before redrawing details instead of relying on decorative polish to support a weak spread.
