## Keep the two dictionary labels distinct

RPO describes archaism as obsolete/archaic diction with current alternatives. Merriam-Webster labels ere old-fashioned and literary, meaning before, but oft chiefly literary, meaning often. Literary does not mean entirely vanished, and the two entries do not establish no present users. Record form, meaning and original register labels separately before proposing archaic colouring in context. A word’s earliest-use history is not the writing date of the current lines.

## Return ere to its complete time clause

A’s Ere introduces the lamps went out: the speaker left the gate before the lamps went out. This orders departure before extinguishing, not here meaning this place or ear as a hearing noun. B’s Before retains past-tense went/left and the same ordering. Record departure before extinguishing externally, then verify the complete sentence. The conjunction use fits a full following clause, rather than mechanically applying a dictionary’s first listed sense everywhere.

## Use oft for frequency without changing tense

I oft return means the speaker often returns to hear the river. Oft modifies frequency, not one visit or a completed past event replacing the habitual present. B’s often retains that basic frequency. A has nine/seven words by line; B also nine/seven. Equal word count does not guarantee identical sound rhythm. Common pronunciations of ere/before and oft/often may affect rhythm, but two words cannot prove a whole metre or establish automatic in-app narration/syllable measurement.

## Separate register effects from historical facts
