## Declare two operations and the starting point

Define S(x,y)=(x+y,y) and D(x,y)=(x,2y) on paper. Start P=(2,1), declaring that “S then D” means calculate S first and pass its output to D. ## Pass the sheared result to height scaling

S sends (2,1) to (3,1), since 2+1=3. D then acts on that intermediate point, doubling its y to produce (3,2). Label P→P1→P2 with S and D and record both input/output pairs. ## Restart the reversed chain from the original point

Restart at original P=(2,1). D gives (2,2), then S adds the new y=2 to x=2, producing (4,2). Final points (3,2)/(4,2) share y but differ in x by 1. ## Explain the difference with general coordinates

For arbitrary (x,y), S then D yields (x+y,2y), whereas D then S yields (x+2y,2y), differing in x by y. Points with original y=0 therefore agree, without establishing equality for all points. Add Q=(3,0), which both chains leave at (3,0), then retain nonzero-y P as a counterexample. Separate agreement at one point from equality of composite functions. One axis point cannot prove commutation, and P’s disagreement does not imply no points can agree.

## Reverse operation order for the inverse

Invert the first chain from (3,2): halve y to obtain (3,1), then subtract current y=1 from x to recover (2,1). Subtracting y first gives (1,2), then halving gives (1,1), failing. For the second chain, invert (4,2) by undoing shear first to (2,2), then height scaling to (2,1). Name correct and incorrect inverse chains separately.
