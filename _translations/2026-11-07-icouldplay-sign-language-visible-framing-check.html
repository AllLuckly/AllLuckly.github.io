Signing footage requires more than a visible person: face and hands must remain usable through movement. Following W3C guidance, inspect framing and caption occlusion in iCouldPlay. A non-signer can record visibility but cannot establish interpretation accuracy. No gesture recognition, sign translation or detachable signing window is assumed.

Choose permitted material and define scope

Use permitted signing material, recording title, version, source notes and duration without redistributing restricted content. An ordinary self-recorded hand wave can test controls but is not a sign-language demonstration. Scope this task to visible face, hands and movement space; identification of the sign language, completeness of interpretation and grammatical accuracy require appropriate users of that language or specialists. State those exclusions before any pass result.

Begin with the complete image

Use an available mode that retains the complete image at normal orientation and distance, then play from before signing begins. Establish whether the signer fills the main image or is embedded in a smaller panel. A baked-in panel is not necessarily independently movable or resizable; enlarging everything can crop other regions. Preserve this baseline and judge the whole movement, not one opening still.

Observe the face through movement

Watch a short interval for face visibility, titles and head turns leaving frame. One front-facing pause cannot establish continuous visibility. Record time and obscured region without interpreting expressions beyond your competence. If too small, report insufficient visibility under these conditions rather than fill the gap from audio or captions. Keep semantics separate so dialogue text is not mistaken for verified signing correspondence.

Track both hands to movement extremes

Replay the interval following both hands through extension, crossing and near-face movement, noting cropping and overlay occlusion. Hands naturally overlap; record it without automatically calling it a filming error. A user of that sign language must assess information loss. Mark unseen intervals rather than guess an offscreen hand’s position. Include maximum movement extent, not only hands resting centrally.

Distinguish baked-in and selectable captions

If text covers hands, record time and selected captions, temporarily disable the selectable layer, then restore it. Remaining text may be baked in or another layer, not proof of a failed switch. Do not repeatedly delete subtitle files. Use visible positioning options when supported and retain original values, without promising control over every external subtitle format or burned-in lettering. Recheck that a moved caption does not cover the face or other essential information.

Compare fit and crop with all references

Compare another available fit mode while tracking face, both hands and embedded-panel edges. Record gained size and lost scope separately. Filling the screen may enlarge the face while cropping movement. If one mode reveals the face but loses hands, report the tradeoff instead of choosing a favourable screenshot as a solution. Restore the most usable complete-view setting.

Separate paused visibility from continuous usability

Pausing locates occlusion but does not establish usability at normal speed. Watch continuously again and note frequent pauses needed to find a person or movement, without generalising one viewer’s experience. Log slower viewing separately and return to normal speed; changing speed is not interpretation correction. Audio or readable captions do not prove the signing presentation meets its audience’s needs.

Leave language assessment to appropriate users

For interpretation or language assessment, prepare version, intervals, visibility issues and unknowns for someone who uses the relevant sign language. A generic sign-language label does not make languages interchangeable. Prepare notes without contacting anyone automatically. W3C G54 informs signing-video and display-size concerns; this limited check is not full WCAG conformance certification.

Save issues and restore viewing state

Each issue should identify time, region and conditions, distinguishing source cropping, overlays and size. Preserve original media, captions and semantic unknowns; reopen an interval to verify the same version. Restore useful caption and fit settings without deleting material to hide limits. This is viewing and logging, not interpretation assessment, all-device accessibility testing or validation of automatic app recognition.
