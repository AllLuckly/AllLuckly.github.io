Metal can contain abrupt value changes, but arbitrary black-and-white stripes do not make a kettle convincing. Build a simplified round kettle with coherent lid, spout and handle connections, then paint a few varied reflection bands by hand.

Set up a separate study

Use an approximately 1400×1600 px canvas with space before the spout. Separate body, attachments, reflection bands and table shadow. Test three greys and two brush widths before near-white accents. Strong contrast makes area control important; avoid fragmenting the kettle into black and white immediately.

Check the lid ellipse, spout root and handle joints in a grey structural version before strong reflections. Keep attachments separate from the belly so a spout-angle correction does not require repainting the main window reflection. Judge structure and surface in distinct passes.

Place the body and attachments in one space

Sketch a slightly flattened round body, an elliptical lid and a base. Build the spout as a curved tapering tube, aligning its opening with the view. Leave clear negative space inside the opposite handle. Establish attachment points so the parts connect rather than sit beside the silhouette.

Align the lid and base ellipse axes and check the grey silhouette. Broaden a stick-like spout at its root and show opening thickness. Give the handle two clear attachment points. Establish these before reflections so highlights do not conceal structural errors.

Establish three main reflection areas

Use a light warm-grey background and slightly darker table. On the mid-grey body, place one broad light-grey band, one narrow dark-grey band and remaining middle values. Curve and taper the bands around the body. Avoid equal widths or immediate pure black and white.

Let a broad left reflection suggest a window and a narrow right dark suggest a shaded environment without depicting the entire room. Vary edge softness. Concentrate the strongest contrast centrally and reduce it near selected edges, preserving the round volume before adding bright accents.

Turn reflections across the attachments

Turn the light band along the curved spout rather than copying the body’s vertical stripe. Vary its width through the bend. On the lid, reshape the reflection into curved planes and add a broken narrow rim shadow so it reads as a separate covering.

Use subdued brown-grey for a wooden or coated handle, reserving metal highlights for its fittings. Material contrast comes from different value organisation, not identical white outlines. Quiet the handle and base if everything sparkles, allowing the reflective body to remain the focus.

Restrict the brightest and darkest accents

Place a narrower near-white accent inside the broad light band, with one harder and one softer edge. Reserve tiny deepest darks for the spout opening, lid edge and handle joints. Avoid tracing the entire kettle in white; selected edges can disappear into the pale background.

A white-tape reflection is often too uniform and ignores the body curvature. Narrow it near the lid and spread it over the belly. If the dark band resembles dirt, clarify its surface direction and simplify its edge. Keep only reflections that help explain the volume.

Ground the kettle with shadow and reflected colour

Add a narrow contact shadow and a broader cast shadow to the lower right. Suggest a little warm table colour on the lower metal, keeping it muted. Contact shadow and reflected dark bands serve different purposes; do not join them into one equally dark outline.

Hide the brightest accents to assess the broader bands. If the kettle resembles grey ceramic, strengthen selected abrupt value changes. If it resembles a collage of mirrors, remove small reflections and restore broad curved areas. A three-value thumbnail can reveal which contrasts are essential.

Review and plan the next study

Hide the whitest accents and check whether broader bands still suggest hard reflective metal. Restore them to judge concentration. Review the silhouette small and accidental band gaps close up. Remove one side’s white outline if everything sparkles; a few accurate value changes are more effective.

Retain grey structure, reflection and final PNG versions. Next, change the background from warm to cool grey and adjust the reflected environment while keeping the attachments fixed. Study that response rather than simply recolouring the entire kettle blue, preserving shadow and joint logic.
