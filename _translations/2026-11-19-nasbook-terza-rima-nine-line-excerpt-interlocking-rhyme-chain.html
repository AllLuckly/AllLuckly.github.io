Terza rima requires more than matching the first and third lines of each tercet: a middle rhyme supplies the next tercet’s outer lines. Read an original nine-line English excerpt in Book Isle and build a sound ledger with connecting arrows. This is an excerpt exercise, not a claim that nine lines form a finished longer poem.

Preserve authored lines and three tercets

Externally divide the nine lines into groups of three, label L1–L9 and reserve another page for rhyme records. Export PDF or retain EPUB source work. After import check nine authored line numbers and two stanza gaps. Display wrapping does not create a tenth line. Use the source IDs if a small screen wraps a sentence. Bookmark the poem page; select end words where possible or record them externally for scans, without automatic form recognition.

Assign letters after checking end-word sounds

List night, glow, light, slow, stone, go, alone, rain, known. In common English pronunciations night/light share /aɪt/; glow/slow/go share /oʊ/, often /əʊ/ in British English; stone/alone/known share /oʊn/; rain has /eɪn/. Label the first three sound groups A, B, C and rain D. Letters record compared sounds rather than spelling identity: alone and known can rhyme despite different lengths.

L1 The keeper lifts a lantern into night.
L2 The windows catch a narrow amber glow.
L3 Along the wall, the rain returns its light.

L4 The square gives back the footsteps, soft and slow.
L5 A cart stands empty near a broken stone.
L6 He sets the lantern where the children go.

L7 Beyond the gate, a bell begins alone.
L8 Its echo sends the pigeons through the rain.
L9 The keeper turns toward streets he has not known.

end words: night glow light / slow stone go / alone rain known
scheme: A B A / B C B / C D C
links: L2 -> L4,L6; L5 -> L7,L9
counter: change only L5 stone to brick

Trace both links across stanza boundaries

The first tercet is ABA. Its middle B at L2 is taken up by L4 and L6, giving BCB. The C at L5 passes to L7 and L9, giving CDC. Record two complete arrow sets: 2→4, 6 and 5→7, 9. Checking only each tercet’s outer pair misses the linking requirement; separate internally paired tercets do not establish an interlocking chain when their sounds are unrelated.

Check group sizes: A has two lines, B three, C three and D one, totalling nine. Omitting rain leaves an incomplete ledger even if both earlier links were drawn correctly. A new middle rhyme may await continuation but must remain recorded; unknown continuation is not absent sound evidence.

Do not mistake an unfinished middle rhyme for closure

Rain at L8 introduces D, with no following DED tercet in this excerpt. Mark the continuation unknown. This neither invalidates the completed links nor makes L9 unrhymed: L9 already matches L7 as C. Terza rima has no single total line count and has used different endings, including an added single line or couplet. Do not invent one here. Nine excerpt lines, three complete tercets and a completed whole poem are different claims.

Change one middle end word to test a broken link

In a control copy change only stone at L5 to brick. L4/L6 still rhyme as B, but brick no longer supplies the selected rhyme of alone/known at L7/L9, breaking the second link. Relabelling brick C does not repair its sound. This control uses the worksheet’s clear matching rhymes; modern poems may employ slant rhyme or deliberate variation, so it does not disqualify every variant practice.

The brick control breaks only the second arrow set: L2 still links to L4/L6, and L7/L9 still rhyme with each other. Record a local linking failure rather than loss of every rhyme. This distinguishes the chain requirement from an internal outer pair. Any creative repair belongs in a separate version rather than editing the source until the ledger always passes.

Separate sound form from interpretation of the scene

The excerpt presents a lantern, square, footsteps, empty cart and bell. Linking sounds may let readers hear a prior stanza as they enter the next; that is a proposed effect, not proof of a sole intention of loneliness. Preserve the English poem unchanged on the Chinese page so its rhymes remain checkable. Chinese explanation does not automatically reproduce ABA BCB CDC. English terza rima often uses iambic pentameter, but this worksheet has not scanned every foot and must not certify strict meter.

Diagnose wrapping and arbitrary rhyme letters

If the ledger has ten lines, check display wrapping. If stone and known get separate groups, compare their rhyme endings rather than whole spellings. A claim that every middle line already has a following stanza overlooks L8. If the control passes, verify the actual L5 substitution. Search end words and return manually to tercets; search does not hear rhyme. Preserve the source and keep erroneous labels, sound evidence and corrections in a separate versioned ledger.

Reopen and verify links plus the unknown continuation

Without the answer ledger reconstruct four sound groups, three tercets and two links from the nine end words, then explain the uncontinued D at L8. State the excerpt scope, brick control, untested meter and lack of guaranteed translated rhyme. Retain editable source, reading file, bookmark and ledger; mark unperformed highlighting/reopening pending rather than report device success. Deliver a chain tied to specific lines, not an unsupported form label.
