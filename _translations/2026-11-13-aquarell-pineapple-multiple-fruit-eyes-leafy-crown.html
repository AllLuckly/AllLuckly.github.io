Pineapple eyes drawn as square stickers flatten the fruit, while raised scales can make it look like a cone. Reference Ananas comosus for an invented multiple-fruit still life in Aquarell. Wrap visible eyes over the surface and return crown leaves to their top base. Patterns measure no original flower count and are not food-quality or ripeness tests.

Set fruit body and crown proportions

Use an approximately 1600×1900 px portrait canvas, warm-grey background, fruit low-centre and crown spreading upward with margin. Establish a long-oval envelope, crown base and ground contact before the surface grid. Separate sketch, body, eyes, crown and shadow in yellow-green-grey. Elongate a sphere without making corn; repair the crown attachment if floating. Proportions are invented, not copied real dimensions or evidence of ripeness/freshness from crown height.

Wrap diagonal eye guides over the surface

Lay two diagonal curved guide families over the visible surface. Keep central cells broader and narrow them toward edges rather than using equal screen-space diamonds. Do not show rear guides through the body. Establish broad zones before smaller eyes, without equal counts per row. Repair edge compression if flat-net-like and revisit tapering if top/bottom cells break into bands. Guides locate drawing shapes, not measured eye counts, fixed numerical sequences or universal cultivar constants.

Give eyes shallow relief rather than separate scales

Model low relief and a small central hint inside each eye with modest darker boundaries, not thick lifted scales. Maintain one light direction and whole-body shade before detail. Reduce pointed lifted edges if cone-like and soften uniform black lines if chessboard-like. The multiple fruit fuses many constituent fruits; the exercise translates whole/unit relationships rather than depicting detachable complete fruits. Surface centre marks are not directly labelled seeds.

Root crown leaves in the top region

Group far, central and near crown leaves, all emerging from the top region rather than individual eyes. Give each a narrow base, broader middle and tip with traceable roots/occlusion, not a uniform fence of tips. Establish lengths in one tone, allowing far roots to hide. Repair a floating arrow-bundle attachment and narrow petal-like leaves with longitudinal curves. Visible count is simplified, not copied crown counts or a claim that real leaves share length, direction or condition.

Align leaf facets with each leaf axis

Give two or three near leaves gentle longitudinal facets continuing along their curved axes. Simplify others rather than black midlines/white edges on every leaf like plastic comb teeth. Show only visible portions at crossings without transparency. Repair broken light bands and thin belt-like edges. Omit and record marginal teeth if necessary rather than inventing large hooks. Colours distinguish layers without conclusions about planting, rooting or edibility, and the drawing is not horticultural instruction.

Separate ground contact from surface pattern

Place a broad soft shadow under the fruit with firmer contact, not separate circles for each bottom eye. Mix yellow and green modestly while preserving whole-volume shade instead of many little spheres. Hide eye detail and ground the silhouette before restoring it; repair contact/position if lantern-floating. Eye boundaries are not flesh cutaways. No interior seeds, juice or decay is claimed from surface colour blocks, and invented colours are not food-selection criteria.

Check surface curvature and crown ownership at reduced size

At roughly quarter size, inspect the outline, then trace central eyes toward the sides for progressive compression, not equal-width stickers. Trace main leaf tips back to the crown base instead of side eyes. Repair broad shade/curves on a flat diamond-board body rather than adding detail. Reduce far-leaf contrast and retain key gaps in a crowded crown without severing bases. Hide full guide grids, rear cells and numbers before export. Visible eye counts are not full-fruit totals or flowering-event counts.

Reopen the export and preserve a multiple-fruit reading

Save the layered native work, export PNG and reopen it. Check curved eyes, continuous rind, attached crown and whole shadow. Thin local dark boundaries or enlarge the subject if the preview becomes a black net, without whitening every eye equally. Repair crown attachment in the source instead of a title arrow. Record a pineapple multiple-fruit reference with invented pose, visible units and colours. Success relates whole and units, not counting original flowers or judging ripeness, flavour or quality, and no specimen/device test is claimed.
