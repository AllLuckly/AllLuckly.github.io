Wicker patterns can become a dense checkerboard. Paint an open container with thickness first, then wrap the weave around its form. Keep the centre clearer, compress the sides and omit selected shadow detail so the pattern explains how the basket turns.

Set up a separate study

Create an approximately 1400×1600 px canvas with room above the handle and below the base. Separate body, opening, handle and weave. Test broad strips and short-shadow marks. Establish the pale-brown container and darker interior so it reads as a basket before any grid is added.

Check aligned rim, base and handle, especially the front and rear attachments. Save an empty structural version before laying out the supports and bands. Keep the weave hideable so you can determine whether later flattening comes from the body or overemphasised surface detail.

Align the rim and base of one container

Draw a broad elliptical opening and taper the sides toward a narrower base, keeping both curves aligned. Separate a darker interior from the pale-brown outside before weaving. If it looks sealed, enlarge the opening and reveal the back wall inside the rim rather than relying on pattern.

Build the handle as an arched strip with inner and outer edges, attached to the basket sides. Establish the rear and front connections through overlap. Interrupt the rim where the handle covers it; showing every line through every part would confuse the space.

Lay in vertical supports before horizontal bands

Place six to eight vertical supports, spreading them centrally and compressing them at the sides as they taper toward the base. Add three to five horizontal bands wrapping around the wall. Their curvature and side compression should agree with the rim ellipse rather than form a flat grid.

Resolve a small frontal patch first. Adjust side compression if the cells are too square and band curvature if they look pasted on. Extend the pattern only after this patch reads correctly, rather than filling the whole surface with a flat regular weave and trying to repair it afterward.

Use short shadows to show over-and-under weaving

At intersections, alternate short shadows to suggest over-and-under weaving, offsetting the next row. Place each small shadow beside the overlapping strip rather than adding identical black dots everywhere. A pale edge can suggest thickness. Establish the rule at a few intersections, then omit selected distant or shaded details.

Use light ochre, grey-brown and restrained yellow lights within one palette. Avoid changing to bright unrelated colours for every strip. Even pale wicker on the shaded side should remain darker than the lit front. Hide the pattern to confirm the basket’s broad lighting is intact.

Simplify the rim and handle weave

Use a thicker light-brown rim with a few diagonal wrapping marks. Clarify the front edge and soften the back into the interior. Let handle marks follow the arch, leaving the top cleaner. Keep their scale related to the wall weave rather than switching abruptly to unrelated thicknesses.

If the handle droops, check its continuous arch and attachments before adding root shadows. Narrow an oversized tyre-like rim and break some texture. Choose one frontal weave patch as the brightest detailed focus, keeping the handle and rim structurally clear but quieter.

Use omitted texture to preserve viewing distance

Merge side and lower weave into a few colour passages while preserving clear central intersections. At small size, the container should read before its weave. If a grid dominates, hide the texture, restore opening and values, and remove less useful marks instead of adding highlights.

Add a narrow base contact and one broader cast shadow instead of shadows beneath every strip. Save an empty-basket version before testing one or two fruit shapes. Fruit hides the rear rim while the front rim overlaps its lower edges. Simple inserts are a useful check of interior space.

Review and plan the next study

At normal size, check a usable opening and clear frontal weaving; small, look for rhythm rather than countable cells. Repair stray lines through the handle close up. Compress far-side spacing when needed instead of merely fading a pattern that lacks perspective.

Keep empty and finished versions, plus a simple fruit-filled test of rim overlap. Next, change only the viewing angle and rebuild the visible interior and band curvature while retaining wicker colours. Compare how the weave follows the container rather than how many marks each version contains.
