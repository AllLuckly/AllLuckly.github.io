A horizontal heating-curve segment does not mean no energy enters. Read two stages—melting pure ice and warming water—in ManYue, checking phase, temperature and heat page by page. Constants are explicitly stipulated teaching approximations, not sensor records or a claimed physical melting experiment.

State purity and pressure in the opening panel

Externally set m=0.020kg=20g of pure ice initially at 0°C, at about one standard atmosphere, ending as liquid water at 10°C. Stipulate Lf=334000J/kg and liquid c=4200J/(kg·°C), neglecting vessel heat capacity, heat loss and evaporation. Make P1 assumptions, P2 an energy-axis curve and P3 stage ledger/controls, retaining editable work. Check units/markers on import and bookmark P2. There is no below-zero ice-warming stage; do not add one silently.

Separate accumulated heat on x from temperature on y

Label P2’s horizontal axis accumulated input Q in joules and vertical axis T in °C. The first segment starts at Q=0 along T=0°C; show mixed ice/water panels with changing phase fractions. A plateau means nearly constant temperature, not zero heat or motionless particles. Zoom axis labels if line weight hides them. Without the horizontal-axis identity its length cannot be read as seconds. Include that identity in the curve title so it survives a page return.

Compute latent heat to melt all the ice first

Qm=mLf=0.020×334000=6680J melts all ice at its melting point, leaving water at 0°C. It does not also warm it by ten degrees. Mark the phase boundary at (6680J,0°C), just fully melted. Units kg×J/kg give J. Treating numeric 20g as 20kg gives the wrong 6680000J, a thousandfold error. Keep 334000 as a per-kilogram constant, not this sample’s total heat.

m=0.020 kg; Lf=334000 J/kg; c_water=4200 J/(kg*degC)
melting: Qm=m*Lf=6680 J; T remains 0 degC
Q=3340 J: melted_mass=0.010 kg; ice_remaining=0.010 kg
warming: Qw=m*c_water*10=840 J
total to liquid water at 10 degC: Q=7520 J
ideal constant input 100 W: time=75.2 s

Use phase fraction, not averaged temperature, halfway along the plateau

At input 3340J, melted mass Q/Lf is 0.010kg and remaining ice 0.010kg, still coexisting at 0°C under these assumptions. Half melted does not mean half the final 10°C, nor opposite ice/water temperatures averaging to zero. Distinguish plateau positions by phase fraction instead of declaring their entire states identical because temperature matches. A different initial mixture or pressure needs new phase data, not a guaranteed reuse of the 0°C plateau.

Warm liquid water with specific heat only after melting

The second stage retains 0.020kg and raises liquid from 0 to 10°C. Qw=mcΔT=0.020×4200×10=840J. Final accumulated input is 6680+840=7520J, not 840J alone. Connect (6680,0) to (7520,10), with slope ΔT/ΔQ=10/840°C/J. A ten-degree Celsius difference equals a ten-kelvin difference numerically, but 0°C is not zero kelvin. This formula uses the difference, not 283.15 substituted for ten.

At cumulative input 7000J, subtract 6680J melting first, leaving 320J for liquid warming. Divide by 0.020×4200 for rise 80/21°C≈3.810°C, with all water liquid. Directly dividing 7000 by heat capacity falsely assigns latent heat to warming. Classify the stage before choosing the accumulation origin.

At liquid-water temperature 5°C, cumulative input is 6680+420=7100J, not half of 7520. Half the final temperature does not mean half the heat accumulated from initial ice; latent heat remains counted. Add this P3 blind check, requesting both all-liquid phase and cumulative energy.

Convert energy to time only with constant power specified

Add an ideal constant 100W input entirely entering the sample: W here means watts, J/s. Melting takes 66.8s, warming 8.4s, total 75.2s. These time labels require this added assumption; they cannot be read directly from the original energy axis. At 50W with other assumptions unchanged, total time is 150.4s while input remains 7520J. Losses or varying power require a new energy-flow record; longer actual waiting does not prove a changed latent-heat constant.

For a reverse control, start with 20g liquid at 0°C under the same pressure. Just fully freezing it releases 6680J. With heat entering the sample positive, this stage has Q=−6680J while temperature stays at the transition, not negative 6680 degrees. Separate released-heat magnitude from signed input; a minus sign does not violate energy conservation.

Troubleshoot phase, units and the accumulation origin

An 840J final total may omit the plateau. Five degrees halfway along melting confuses phase fraction with temperature fraction. A thousandfold total suggests grams versus kilograms; 75.2J for total time mixes units. Repair one value in an owned copy and return to P1 for initial phase. If full melting is unmarked, revise the external source rather than invent automatic phase analysis in the reader. A smooth displayed curve does not establish actual phase equilibrium.

Reopen with both energy and state ledgers

Reopen at the curve bookmark and predict phase/temperature at 3340,6680,7520J: half melted at zero, just fully melted at zero, then liquid at ten degrees. Check 100W versus 50W times, explaining why equal heat need not mean equal duration. Deliver original diagrams, two calculations and controls; leave unperformed reader-return actions pending. Results do not claim ManYue thermometry, heater control or an experiment, nor universal measured constants for all pressures and materials.
