Draw a hand-operated can opener without a can, its working head nearer the viewer and both handles extending down-right. Identify the wheel plane, turning-knob connection and handle junction instead of producing a pair of scissors. This exterior study performs no cutting or product test.

Arrange head and handles as three main masses

On an approximately 1800×1400 px canvas place a pale working-head mass upper left, with two handles opening slightly down-right. They need not be identical; distinguish fixed and moving parts according to the chosen conventional hand-operated design without mixing in side-cutting or electric models. Separate sketch, metal, dark grips and reflections, leaving space around the head. Save native work and reduce the view. If the masses resemble a hammer, restore the handle gap before adding rivets as identification clues.

Build the cutting wheel as a thin disc

Place one obliquely viewed cutting wheel on the visible head side. Establish its compressed face ellipse, then a short second edge for thickness. It is an edged metal disc, not a toothed sun or decorative dot. Its centre belongs within the supporting head, not on a long pin hanging in empty space. Preserve partial occlusion if the reference reveals only part of its rim. One explicitly shown cutting wheel is not a claim that the real head contains only one wheel or that all drive parts are illustrated.

Join the turning knob to its shaft, not the blade rim

Draw a short substantial shaft and turning knob at the corresponding working-head area. Simplify the knob as two rounded wings connected through one centre, not two separate blades. No internal gears are shown, so do not invent a precise drive path from the knob’s orientation. Conceal the shaft section behind the head. Give the knob graspable thickness while retaining the wheel as a thin disc. Their projections may overlap, but contact edges and short shadows must separate their roles.

Meet the handles at the hinge while preserving clearance

Narrow both metal handle cores toward the head, turning the moving handle through its visible hinge region rather than branching like soft twigs. Keep a background-coloured gap between grips. The nearer handle can obscure a short far section; never let the handles interpenetrate merely to show both. A small solid circle or narrow ring suffices for the pivot, not a third oversized wheel. Trace each handle continuously from its end to the head, then remove concealed guides. Check the reference when an endpoint is unclear instead of imposing symmetry.

Wrap colour blocks around the metal handle cores

Block both grips in related dark greys or chosen colours, with gently turning edges and rounded ends. Expose metal near the head where appropriate, not inexplicably through the covering’s middle. Give the near grip a broader light plane and subdue the far one so they do not resemble flat text bars. Use two or three texture marks rather than branding or an invented grip rating. Hide metal briefly to inspect grip shapes, then restore it and check their correct cores. Colour blocks do not identify components automatically.

Use local highlights to distinguish discs from curved grips

With light from upper left, brighten a thin light-facing wheel rim while retaining mid-grey on its face. Separate the knob’s broad plane and narrow side without equal white borders everywhere. Confine deepest head values to shaft root, hinge seam and contacts, preserving the long negative space. Cast the table shadow down-right, firmer near a grip contact and softer farther away. If metal resembles white plastic, restore tonal turning and broken reflections before outlining everything black. Appearance does not establish a steel grade.

Remove scissor-like and decorative-wheel errors

Hide highlights and check wheel support, knob-to-head shaft connection and the handles’ common hinge region. Two long blades call for restoring covered handles with a small head wheel. Move an unsupported wheel centre back into its support, and reopen a dark-filled handle gap. Preserve the earlier version and correct one issue before reducing the view. With no can rim shown, invent neither a cut seam nor a shadow implying completed opening. Exterior connectivity and actual mechanical operation are different checks.

Reopen and deliver three connections and one gap

Save native work, hide guide labels and reopen a PNG. Identify head, knob and two handles at fit-to-window size, then inspect wheel thickness and pivot enlarged. Reopen editable metal reflections and grip colours separately instead of repairing everything only in a flattened image. Document a conventional hand-operated model, visible exterior and omitted internal wheel assembly; do not import side-cutting instructions from another design. Leave unperformed exports/reopening pending. Deliver three connection types and the handle gap, not a cutting-safety test or automatic App model.
