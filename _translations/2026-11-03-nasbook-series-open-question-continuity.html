Correct volume order answers what to read next, but continuity also requires knowing which earlier questions remain open and what later text actually resolves. Keep a small question-and-evidence notebook with historical knowledge boundaries. This is neither a character network nor a filename guide, and it assumes no automatic series-plot understanding.

Verify the two editions and their reading order

Record series, volume, author, translator and edition and verify that both are consecutive main volumes. Extras, revisions and omnibuses can alter coverage; sequential filenames or publication dates are insufficient. Inspect contents and opening notes. State what you have actually read, including whether the later volume is already familiar. Label a retrospective exercise honestly instead of presenting remembered hindsight as a genuine first-read prediction.

Choose a few genuinely open questions

Select two to four evidence-answerable questions, such as who took a particular letter. Attach chapter and short phrase and verify that the earlier text really leaves it open. Correct a missed answer rather than calling it an unresolved authorial thread. Distinguish character guesses, narrator statements and shown events. A small set is actionable; cataloguing every possible foreshadowing detail is unnecessary.

Preserve what was known at the ending

Record prior facts, your prediction and the missing evidence for each question, dated to its volume. Add later answers separately rather than rewriting old knowledge. Use ordinary notes or an external list, not an assumed cross-volume database. Record distinct file sources. This boundary makes gradual disclosure visible on rereading and prevents reader knowledge from being attributed prematurely to a character.

Check the voice behind a later recap

Identify whether a recap is editorial, authorial, a character’s memory or advancing prose. Omissions do not necessarily contradict the earlier volume, and memory can be incomplete. Label the passage’s voice and revisit the original evidence. Do not let the first later paragraph overwrite all prior evidence or infer changed plot solely from translation wording. Use the established edition record to interpret differences.

Record responses without forcing matches

Mark answered, partly addressed, not yet mentioned or based on a rejected assumption only when relevant evidence appears. State chapter, phrase and whether it is fact or another character claim. Matching vocabulary does not establish the same question. Leave unmatched items open rather than inventing an answer for a tidy table. Explain narrowed or reformulated questions instead of automatically completing the narrative’s causal links.

Test return references in both volumes

Save and test separate locators for the earlier question and later evidence. Use chapter/phrase or clearly labelled PDF numbering instead of shared percentages. Verify content after source/import changes. A clear book title and list work without an assumed cross-book deep-link feature. Leave the current pages before testing so memory does not conceal an unusable reference.

Write a continuity conclusion from evidence

For one question explain prior knowledge, later evidence and remaining limits in three sentences. Do not reduce the entire series theme to one answer or assume an unmentioned thread was forgotten. Export useful records where supported and preserve both sources/files separately. An export is not an automatic two-book package or complete app backup. Continue with a third volume under the same question IDs without erasing the earlier knowledge history.

Acceptance and mistaken continuity claims

Finish with recoverable questions, evidence-based statuses and separate predictions/confirmations. Verify coverage before trusting consecutive filenames and context before calling a shortened recap contradictory. Correct a previously answered question openly. Limit the notebook to selected threads instead of claiming automatic tracking of every character and unresolved detail.
