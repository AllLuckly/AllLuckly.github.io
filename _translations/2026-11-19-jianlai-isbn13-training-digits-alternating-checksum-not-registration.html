Declare the practice string independent of publication data

Establish ordinary Jianlai input and preserve target 9781234567897 externally. This constructed arithmetic input supplies no title, author, publisher or allocation evidence and performs no book lookup. Passing a digit check neither obtains an ISBN nor proves a book exists. Preserve all thirteen digits without invented hyphen grouping because registration/publisher boundaries have not been established. Separate the first twelve digits from the final digit

The International ISBN Agency describes a final mathematical check digit using modulus ten and alternating weights one/three. Use 978123456789 as the first twelve digits and retain final 7 separately, outside the initial sum. The current public rule uses prefix 978 or 979. Number positions from the left and alternate 1, 3 through position twelve; do not arbitrarily restart weights from the right or multiply position indices instead of digit values. The external arithmetic table is no importable Jianlai configuration or evidence of automatic ISBN recognition.

Type thirteen consecutive digits and inspect their identity

In an identifiable English/numeric state enter portions 978, 123, 456, 789 and 7 without separators. Inspect each committed portion and the complete thirteen digits to avoid duplicating 789 or dropping the final 7. Targets are ASCII digits; similar I, l or O are not substitutes even though no zero occurs here. Follow actual numeric controls without a prescribed gesture. Record pasted/other-keyboard input separately. Preserve automatic grouping/fullwidth replacements before a one-point corrected draft rather than claim initial equality after cleanup.

List twelve weighted products and sum them

Externally list products 9, 21, 8, 3, 2, 9, 4, 15, 6, 21, 8 and 27, summing to 133. Bind products to positions before summing rather than copy an answer. Prefix 978 establishes no allocation here. Derive final seven from remainder three and close the sum

133 leaves remainder 3 modulo ten; final 7 makes 140 divisible by ten. Only one character belongs in the check-digit position; keep division steps and product tables outside the thirteen-digit draft. Do not multiply this final digit by three: its closing weight is one. In general use ten minus remainder, modulo ten; remainder zero gives digit zero, not two-character 10. For this target check final 7 and closure only, proving no registration boundaries, allocation or bibliographic identity. Verify characters before arithmetic if the host changes digit forms; a seven-like glyph is not automatically ASCII 7.

Use final-digit and transposition controls to limit the check

One external control changes final 7 to 8, producing 141 rather than 140, which fails divisibility despite thirteen digits. Another swaps the first 9 and third 8 in the first twelve positions. Both have weight one, preserving 133, so final 7 can still pass while the string differs. The resulting prefix 879 also fails the public ISBN prefix condition; this control tests only the weighted checksum. The checksum does not detect every error or replace a position-by-position transcription check. Controls are external, not two app detections.
