A spherical triangle can have three right angles when its sides are great-circle arcs, not planar segments. Use the positive-coordinate octant to compute excess and area, then draw a declared oblique projection manually. The projection does not preserve angles; screen measurements cannot replace its three-dimensional definition.

Fix sphere and minor great-circle boundaries

Set O=(0,0,0), R=2 and A=(2,0,0), B=(0,2,0), C=(0,0,2), each on x²+y²+z²=4. Boundaries follow minor quarter-circle arcs in planes z=0,x=0,y=0, all through O and therefore great circles. Not every surface curve is a great-circle edge; choosing the long three-quarter route changes the region. Preserve 3D definitions, projection and curved boundary separately. This positive octant is not a country or surveyed location.

Define all three angles in tangent planes

A spherical angle is between edge tangents at its vertex. At A, tangents toward B/C point along positive y/z and are perpendicular, so angle A is π/2; similarly at B/C. Sum is 3π/2, not subject to a planar triangle’s π rule. Label any angle symbols as spherical right angles, not measured projection squares. Straight spatial chords instead form another, planar equilateral triangle with π/3 angles. Different boundary definitions explain the difference without contradiction.

Compute area from radian excess and check eight copies

Excess E=angle A+angle B+angle C−π=π/2. Area R²E is 4×π/2=2π square units, not ninety degrees multiplied by four. Whole sphere area 4πR²=16π equals eight octants of 2π. Separate angular and area units and retain R squared. This uses the convex small region bounded by minor arcs; selecting the large complementary region requires new interior/angle conventions despite similar boundary curves. With radius four and the same angles, area becomes 8π, four times the original, while arc lengths only double. Keep this separate scale control and main R=2 labels distinct; it tests area versus length scaling.

R=2; A=(2,0,0); B=(0,2,0); C=(0,0,2)
angles=(pi/2,pi/2,pi/2)
excess=3*pi/2-pi=pi/2
spherical area=R^2*excess=2*pi
sphere area=4*pi*R^2=16*pi=8*(2*pi)
minor arc AB=pi; chord AB=2*sqrt(2)
AB arc midpoint=(sqrt(2),sqrt(2),0)
AB chord midpoint=(1,1,0)

Specify reproducible positions for an oblique projection

Use a suggested 1800×1700 canvas and X=850+180(x−y), Y=1000+90(x+y)−180z. A/B/C map to (1210,1180),(490,1180),(850,640), O to (850,1000). This illustrative oblique projection preserves neither lengths, areas nor angles; labels retain 3D values. Plot guides first. The projected sphere envelope is an ellipse centred at O with semiaxes 360√2 and 180√6 pixels. Place approximately if numeric controls are absent; an ellipse tool does not create a precision spherical model.

Use arc midpoints to distinguish curves from chords

Arc midpoints are (√2,√2,0),(√2,0,√2),(0,√2,√2), also on the sphere. Project them with the same rule and approximate curved edges through their vertices. Do not substitute endpoint averages: chord midpoint (1,1,0) has squared norm two, inside the sphere, not four. Use translucent region fill and subdued/dashed hemisphere guides. Finite Bézier paths only approximate projected arcs; sphere and great-circle planes still define exact edges. Projected AB arc midpoint is approximately (850,1254.558), versus chord midpoint (850,1180). AC/BC arc midpoints are about (1104.558,872.721),(595.442,872.721). Check them before handles; decimals are placement aids, not replacements for exact 3D radicals.

Compare arc and chord lengths between the same endpoints

AB’s minor arc subtends π/2 and has length Rπ/2=π, whereas chord length is √(2²+2²)=2√2. Total curved perimeter is 3π, distinct in quantity and units from area 2π. An inset dashed chord is allowed, but the main region stays spherical; planar chord-triangle area 2√3 cannot replace it. Connecting the same three points still requires a path definition. Similar projected lines at small size cannot erase that difference or change the calculation.

Troubleshoot surface points, edges and region selection

An area of 360 suggests degrees inserted into a radian formula. An interior midpoint suggests coordinate averaging. Straight-edged filling calls for checking the three arc midpoints. Do not move vertices to create screen right angles in a non-angle-preserving projection. Repair one copy condition and recheck squared norm four, great-circle plane and positive-octant region. Fix approximate paths or occlusion if fill exceeds the envelope, not theoretical area. Transparency computes neither area nor geodesics automatically.
