Draw a steel-string acoustic guitar in a mild three-quarter view, arranging body and neck before tracing six strings. The sound hole opens through the top, while the bridge is attached to it; they must not merge into one black patch. This invented still life establishes neither tuning nor tone.

Join body and neck around a centreline

Use an approximately 1600×2200 px portrait canvas and a tilted centreline. Reserve a narrower upper bout, broader lower bout, neck and headstock. Draw an inward waist without splitting the body into two disconnected circles. Connect the neck physically, not a head suspended by six strings. Show a little near-side thickness while retaining one consistent view. Separate sketch, wood, strings/frets and highlights; save editable work. Pixels and tilt are compositional, not manufacturing dimensions. Check the silhouette before grain or labels.

Keep sound hole and bridge distinct on the top

Place the sound hole near the neck’s continuation as an ellipse consistent with the top, not an invariably circular sticker. Leave wood around it and a separate low bridge below, with top surface between. The pale saddle is a narrow part on the bridge, not an entirely white bridge. Choose six bridge pins, one attachment per string. Keep the cavity darkness separate from bridge shadow instead of forming a black gourd. Establish both locations before colour so contrast does not enlarge the hole into the waist.

Run frets across the neck with decreasing spacing

Draw a fingerboard and nut near the headstock. Frets cross the board rather than run lengthwise like strings. Show visible portions of the first twelve frets illustratively, with spacing decreasing toward the body rather than copied equally. Twelve is the represented range, not the guitar’s complete fret count; exact positions require string-length ratios not measured from this screen. Stop frets at board edges, softening the far edge. Any selected position markers must remain distinct from strings or sound holes.

Trace six strings over nut and saddle

Mark six positions at the nut and six at the saddle, then trace strings one through six over fingerboard and sound hole to their bridge pins. Strings continue above the cavity rather than stop at its edge or bend inside. Perspective may alter spacing but must preserve order without crossings. Selected thickness differences need not imply named pitches. Count strings with frets hidden, then restore frets to inspect crossings. Six describes this selected steel-string configuration, not every guitar.

Assign headstock tuners and string paths

Choose a three-per-side headstock. Beyond the nut, assign each string to one tuning post instead of merging six onto a central pin. A far post may be concealed by the headstock; explain occlusion rather than rotate every tuner toward the viewer. Buttons project at the sides and posts occupy their mounting positions, not six stemless leaves. Temporarily label six assignments, ensuring none branches to two tuners, then fade labels. Document simplification and avoid treating this as the only steel-string headstock arrangement.

Keep wood and string highlights subordinate to structure

Use a warm pale top, darker sides and dark fingerboard. Keep grain restrained along the chosen surface rather than concentric end-grain rings. A narrow near-side shadow shows thickness; retain hole-edge thickness around the cavity. String highlights may weaken intermittently without deleting their traceable paths. Distinguish pins, frets and strings by small shapes and tone. Reduce grain and rosette decoration before sacrificing hole, bridge or six strings. Invented lighting and wood colours establish neither warmer tone nor greater value.

Hide decoration and inspect strings and represented frets

Trace every tuner through nut, fingerboard, above sound hole, saddle and pin, checking all six paths. A missing string above the hole may lie behind its layer; string-like frets may run in the wrong direction. Correct body/neck alignment in the sketch before strings instead of moving the bridge to fit a faulty axis. Count twelve illustrative frets without including nut or board edge. Preserve a faulty copy and recount six paths after each repair rather than add a short highlight to restore numbers. Guide labels are a manual ledger, not automatic detection.

Reopen and inspect whole guitar before fine strings

Save native work, hide guides and reopen a PNG. Read neck attachment, waist and distinct hole/bridge at fit-to-window size, then inspect six strings, twelve represented frets and three-plus-three tuner assignments enlarged. If strings merge small, lower fret contrast or vary string tones rather than draw huge black bands. Reopen editable wood and string layers. Deliver configuration and omissions, leaving unperformed actions pending. This still life is not a string-clearance measurement, tuning test or automatically generated playing model.
