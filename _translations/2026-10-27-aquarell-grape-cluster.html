A grape cluster is not a flat arrangement of identical circles. Build a broad-topped, tapered purple bunch in near and far groups, using overlap and shared values. Fewer grapes can still convey one weighted cluster more clearly than many independent dots.

Begin with the whole cluster silhouette

Use an approximately 1500×1700 px portrait canvas with a broad upper mass tapering to smaller lower grapes. Establish the purple-grey silhouette and three groups before individual fruit. Separate near, far, stalk and highlight layers, correcting a rectangular or overly symmetrical cluster before detail.

Vary how much of each grape is visible

Clarify four or five near grapes and reveal only partial arcs behind them. Vary size modestly and interrupt hidden contours. Break regular rows if the cluster resembles balloons; several incomplete forms help the viewer read stacking rather than count circles. Establish depth through overlap before colour.

Unify the bunch through a shared light mass

Under upper-left light, keep the bunch lighter above left and deeper internally and lower right. Model individual curves within these group values. Hide small highlights to check the shared light/dark organisation. Soften independent black borders if every grape becomes a bead.

Use contact darks only at real overlaps

Place narrow shadows on lower grapes at genuine overlaps, reserving deepest colour for a few gaps. Resolve one pair before repeating. Avoid identical black star-shaped junctions. Reduce hole-like gaps and restore nearby curvature, or clarify contour interruption before adding more darkness.

Keep skin texture restrained

Suggest bloom with muted blue-grey lights rather than dots everywhere. Clarify reflections on one near grape and keep broader lights on the rest. Soften plastic-like white points or restore warm purple middle values to stone-like fruit instead of fading the whole cluster.

Use stems to establish a bunch rather than a pile

Use a short top stem with a few branches entering and disappearing among grapes. A few clear attachments establish a bunch without visible stalks on every fruit. Keep muted brown-green stems subordinate; do not repair a loose-pile appearance by giving every grape an exposed handle.

Distinguish resting from hanging

Here the lowest grapes rest on a table with narrow contacts. A hanging variant requires a different stalk and gravity arrangement, not merely removal of the ground shadow. Review grounding and total tilt, using one broad cast shadow instead of a separate circular shadow for every grape.

Check shadow ownership with two grapes

Study one grape overlapping another without highlights. Place the contact dark on the lower surface rather than around the entire front fruit. Reverse the order in a second study with unchanged colours, comparing both contour breaks and shadow ownership before correcting the main bunch selectively.

Test depth without colour coding

In a copy, bring near and far groups to similar purple while retaining contour breaks and contacts. If depth vanishes, repair overlap before restoring hue differences. Colour should enhance rather than rescue space, avoiding unrelated colours used merely as group labels.

Make a version with half as many grapes

Export the finish and a simplified version with only eight or nine principal grapes. Compare silhouette and shared lighting to identify useful overlaps. Next, move only the near group and rebuild occlusion, retaining separate near/far layers rather than merging everything and repairing depth with repeated outlines.
