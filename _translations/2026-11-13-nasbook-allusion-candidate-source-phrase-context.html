A familiar phrase does not establish its allusive source. Practise allusion checking in Book Isle by preserving the passage location, then reading a candidate source in context. The old song below is an original teaching text, not a historical poem. Reading and return navigation may use the app, while research and notes may be external. No automatic allusion detector or unquestioned first search result is required.

Preserve the phrase before assigning a source

Record novel, edition, chapter and neighbouring sentences. In the original sample, Alan waits at a ferry, touches the third knot of a red cord and swallows words about returning. Initially record the phrase, ferry and waiting without assigning a historical poet or legend. It could be ordinary description or a reference elsewhere. Keep candidates open rather than attach a preferred name first and then search only for support.

Read a clearly identified candidate source

The separate teaching source is the newly written “Old Ferry Song”: a red cord is tied at the ferry, and three knots mark the promised return. It is created for this exercise, not copied from an ancient text. Record its identity and both complete lines. Cord, knots, ferry and return form several correspondences with the prose, stronger than a shared colour alone. In real research consult a verifiable edition and surrounding lines, leaving unknown author or date explicit instead of inventing antiquity.

Compare matching and changed elements

Record the song’s three knots marking return, the prose’s finger on the third knot, their shared ferry and cord, and the new swallowed speech. Do not silently rewrite variants into an exact quotation or reject any reference merely because wording changes. An allusion can evoke another text indirectly without verbatim reproduction. These correspondences establish a candidate; a separate teaching note below confirms borrowing here. Real works without such evidence require a weaker conclusion.

You may label this manual ledger unlocated, lexical similarity only, several contextual matches or separately confirmed. These are working labels, not a universal scoring scale. Strengthen a judgment only with new evidence, not repeated viewing of one search result. If a revision removes the editorial note, retain its old-edition identity instead of transferring confirmation without qualification.

Distinguish textual quotation from a confirming note

A teaching note explicitly states that the prose borrows the original song’s knots-and-return motif, allowing confirmation within this exercise. It is not character dialogue; record it as editorial instruction. A version quoting the two song lines verbatim needs separate quotation boundaries, wording and attribution, not just an allusion label. Never fabricate a real author’s statement or interview to imitate the confirmation supplied in this controlled example.

Explain how the current context reuses the source

The song links knots to an expected return, while the prose combines touch with withheld speech. Blocked expectation or hesitation is a possible reading. State the action before the interpretation and source link, without declaring that the character broke a promise. Later learning that he merely feared waking someone may revise the interpretation without necessarily erasing the reference. Identifying a source and explaining its function are different evidential tasks.

Create a shared-word control without provenance

A second original sentence describes three cartons tied with red cord for an afternoon departure. Shared cord and three do not establish the same allusion without ferry, return or borrowing evidence. Other context might matter, but these materials are insufficient. This does not prove that no writer could associate it with the song; it limits the present evidence. Unconfirmed and disproved are different states.

Check variants and search-word coincidences

For source variants, retain edition, wording and link instead of assembling a conveniently matching version that never existed. Search context and background when needed, but preserve unknowns if unsupported. Do not keep reformulating keywords merely to obtain a result resembling a preferred answer. Label paraphrase, translation and original separately: similar translator wording alone does not prove the original author’s allusion. Use authorised public sources and record what was actually accessed, without claiming a complete work was read from a search snippet.

Leave and return with an auditable source chain

Leave and return to the prose and song locations, reading phrase, candidate source, confirmation and interpretation in order. Preserve source identity, both contexts, correspondences and differences, any quotation boundary, confidence and unknowns. Another reader should distinguish borrowing stipulated here from an unresolved association in real reading, without trusting an unsupported source name. Success is traceable provenance with revisable interpretation, not assigning an ancient author to every common colour and number.
