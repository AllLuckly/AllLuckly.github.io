A pair of verse lines may stand as a stanza or form part of a longer stanza; blank spacing and rhyme answer different questions. Use two successive verse lines as the basic unit

The Academy of American Poets describes a couplet as two successive lines, often rhymed, which may belong to a larger stanza. No middle blank does not automatically remove couplets, and rhyme is not an exceptionless requirement in every usage. A has two-line stanzas and rhyming pairs. B retains both pairs within one four-line stanza. Record line count, stanza count and rhyme groups separately under the chosen definition, not as one vague two-part structure.

Check rhyme from the final stressed vowel

Endings are gate, wait, bend, end. In common English pronunciation gate/wait share /eɪt/ and bend/end /ɛnd/ from the stressed vowel; initial consonants can differ. Mark AABB across four lines, pairing L1/L2 and L3/L4. Different spelling does not defeat rhyme, and a shared final t does not pair arbitrary different vowels. Check closure at the second line

A’s L2/L4 end with clear syntactic and semantic closure, supporting closed two-line units. Full stops are clues, but grammar matters rather than every dot certifying completion. Reserve heroic for an additional metrical test

A heroic couplet additionally requires rhyming iambic pentameter, not merely two-line grouping or similar visual length. Even ten candidate syllables still need stress organization and permissible substitutions; word count is not foot count. This lesson establishes successive lines, AABB and closure. Leave heroic unverified without foot-by-foot scansion. An unproved narrower label does not undo a supported basic couplet; familiar terminology cannot replace its additional conditions.
