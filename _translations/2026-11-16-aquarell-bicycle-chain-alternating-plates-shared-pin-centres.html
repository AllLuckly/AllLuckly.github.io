A chain is more than repeated rings. Draw an invented bicycle roller-chain segment in Aquarell from a slightly elevated side view, separating pin centres, alternating plates and transverse depth. It is an isolated exterior study without sprockets, disassembly or repair instructions. Six pin centres and five spans are this drawing’s ledger.

Set six centres before repeated shapes

Create an approximately 1800×1200 px canvas. On a sketch layer draw a gently rising baseline and six equally spaced paper centres A–F, making five spans before metal outlines. Equality belongs to this near-side-view approximation, not a millimetre measurement. Leave room above and below for plate waists and rollers, and beyond both endpoints. Repair a missing centre before detail. If the last span hits the edge, reduce the whole composition rather than compress only that span and break the rhythm.

Assign outer and inner spans alternately

Temporarily assign A–B, C–D and E–F to outer links, and B–C/D–E to inner links. Check the alternation in two pale colours. Each near plate has rounded ends around two centres and a narrower waist, not a detached doughnut. Adjacent ends overlap at one pin centre instead of being joined by an improvised black bridge. Guide colours are manual assignments and will be hidden. Three outer and two inner spans follow this cutout’s starting point; they do not imply an extra outer link in a complete closed chain.

Add far plates to establish depth

Behind each near plate add a narrow offset for the far plate of the same link. It is not a second parallel chain: both plates share the same two transverse pin axes. Establish depth on one link, then keep that viewing direction across the other four rather than alternating offsets upward and downward. Stop concealed inner-plate contours behind outer plates instead of drawing transparent figure eights. If the result resembles two zippers, reduce far-side contrast and trace every plate back to its two centres.

Keep rollers between the inner plates

Place short cylindrical rollers in the transverse space between inner plates, on the established pin axes. A side view shows a round side or narrow curved surface without requiring a full internal section. Rollers should not sit outside near plates like buttons or merge with pin heads into oversized balls. Distinguish plate ends, roller rims and pin ends, leaving hidden boundaries concealed. With no sprocket in this drawing, add no invented tooth in every circle and no guessed internal contact details.

Use selective metal highlights

Use a cool mid-grey base, short bright upper bands on near plates, darker turning waists and subdued far plates. Small tonal divisions describe flat pin ends; avoid identical thick white outlines on every circle. Test one span, check centres at small size, then manually repeat the brush rhythm. No automatic array or calculated metal reflection is required. Modest surface variation is useful, but random rust marks should not cover every joint and obscure the connection evidence.

Bend a copy by changing the centre path

Save a copy with a shallow sag while retaining six centres and five spans. Each plate follows its adjacent centres; a curved shadow under unchanged straight plates does not bend the chain. Revise the sketch and shared ends before far-side depth. Projected screen spacing need not remain equal after viewing directions change. The curve studies continuity rather than force or allowable bending. Keep the straight reference so every directional change remains identifiable.

Audit alternation and hidden boundaries

Hide materials and read outer/inner/outer/inner/outer from A to F, then inspect shared centres at every joint. Move a misaligned plate end rather than stretch one pin into an ellipse enclosing two centres. Remove a far plate wrongly connected to a third point. Preserve real gaps instead of painting holes and shadows identically black. Name each faulty span and side, repair structure, then restore scratches. Added texture should not make a misconnection merely more elaborate.

Reopen and retrace endpoints and spans

Save native work, hide letters and checking colours, export PNG and reopen it. First trace all five spans end to end small, then inspect plate overlap and rollers enlarged. If pins merge into a dark band, reduce competing contours without changing counts. Restore the retained reference if the curved copy replaced it. Reopen editable sketch, metal base and highlights separately and record each view’s scope. Completion establishes readable structure and continued editing, not correct installation, acceptable wear or automatic mechanical inspection.
