Use clear filenames, original-file copies and restore checks to record a project, distinguishing undo, versions, recently deleted items and independent backups.

Separate working files and deliverables first

A design has at least an editable original and files others view. PNG previews cannot replace objects, paths and text structure. Use clear names such as “mountain_icon_original_v01” and corresponding previews rather than many indistinguishable Untitled projects.

Keep clear records at important stages

Use sketch, shape finalisation, colour and delivery as milestones instead of duplicating after every move. Confirm saving, then export editable copies as needed. Names like “shape final” or “dark palette” describe changes better than “final”, “final two” and “really final”. Use the app’s actual native extension.

Create an independent copy by exporting the original

Choose the editable original in Export & Share and save somewhere locatable, confirming it is not PNG or PDF. Keep important copies independently and understand the storage service’s sync behaviour. Do not assume automatic cloud sync or treat export success as verification on another device.

Practise recovery without overwriting existing work

Reopen or import a copied practice original as a separate project and check title, paths, text and layers. Never test deletion/restoration on the sole important original or empty the gallery to verify a backup. Check the content you actually need editable, not just thumbnails. Name the test clearly afterwards.

Distinguish undo, versions and Recently Deleted

Undo reverses edits; versions inspect or restore saved stages; Recently Deleted recovers removed projects. None is synonymous with an independent backup. Public information includes versions, deletion recovery and backup, but check current controls and scope. Do not assume unlimited automatic history or a fixed unverified backup interval.

Document deliverables and their sources

Include the original version in export names, such as “mountain_icon_v03_512_transparent.png”, and record its source. Update outputs after colour changes so old previews are not sent. A short project note can explain original, SVG interchange and PNG preview roles; keep it simple but understandable later.

What a useful recovery check verifies

Open the backup and verify a specific last change, such as new title text or circle colour. General resemblance does not prove recency. Move one path slightly and undo, then test expected text editing to distinguish a usable original from an identical-looking preview.

Give test copies separate names without overwriting formal work. Record time, location and result briefly. Reuse that practice file when changing devices or storage, avoiding important-project risk.

If restoration fails, retain the original rather than repeatedly overwriting it with other tools. Check extension, size and download completeness, then try another verified copy. A specific file-and-operation investigation preserves options better than clearing caches or deleting the gallery.

Final checks and long-term maintenance

Periodically open an important original, inspect editable paths and required text. Before cleanup, distinguish test copies, old deliveries and retained sources; begin with clearly unnecessary copies. A PNG does not make the original disposable. A useful backup can be located and restored when needed, not merely seen as an icon.

Further exercise: a minimal recoverable project package

  1. Save an exercise as original, SVG and PNG, with a short explanation of each purpose.

  2. Reimport a native copy and inspect a path, text and layer, not just a similar-looking preview.

  3. Change a colour and save a new version. Verify the old copy remains distinct and the new export does not retain a misleading old filename.

Frequently asked questions

Can Recently Deleted replace backups?

Not as the sole protection. It mainly recovers deleted work, whereas independent backups should be clearly locatable and verified. Store important files separately and test restoration.

Is exporting only PDF enough for long-term editing?

PDF suits document delivery but does not guarantee every original editing relationship. Keep an editable original for layers, paths and text.

Does version history record every step automatically?

Do not assume so. Check actual generation conditions and scope in your version. Save important stages and independent originals deliberately rather than relying on unverified automation.
