English verse without end rhyme can still follow a fixed five-foot meter; unrhymed does not automatically mean free verse. Fix authorial lines for two original examples in Book Isle, scan syllables and check end sounds separately. Chinese glosses aid meaning without substituting character count for English syllables. Reading/returning does not imply automatic stress analysis or one mandatory performance.

Keep two authorial lines and their meaning

Card P1 has these original lines, about a clock waking a town at dawn and a gate keeping rain outside:

The clock will strike and wake the town at dawn.
The gate will close and keep the rain away.

Preserve two authorial lines externally and verify P1/dawn/away after PDF/EPUB import. Narrow screens can wrap one line into two visual segments; use source/line numbers rather than making four poetic lines. These are original exercises, and semantic translation does not determine English meter.

Count ten syllables rather than ten words

Line one has ten monosyllabic words. Line two has eight monosyllables plus two-syllable away, so nine words still yield ten syllables. Separate word and syllable counts. Real poems can have contractions, dialects or alternative pronunciations: declare the reading rather than swallowing sounds to force ten. Chinese character counts do not map one-to-one to this English table; changing a translation does not alter source syllables.

Another original ten-monosyllable sentence is“Clocks strike; rain falls; gates slam; dogs bark; lights fade.” Its ordinary phrasing has denser content-word stress, so total ten alone does not establish five iambs. An alternative scan needs phonetic/contextual reasons, not alternating colors.

Mark five weak–strong candidate feet

One conventional weak–strong candidate uses capitals for relative stress and slashes for feet:

the CLOCK / will STRIKE / and WAKE / the TOWN / at DAWN
the GATE / will CLOSE / and KEEP / the RAIN / aWAY

Each line has five iambs, a weaker followed by stronger syllable. Away is one word whose weaker a and stronger way form a whole foot; do not confuse word count with syllable count or lose the final foot. Capitals prescribe no fixed pitch/volume. Performance can vary, and scansion is an argued candidate, not determined automatically by reader typography.

Check terminal sounds independently of spelling

Dawn/away do not form the usual end rhyme here. Combine unrhymed endings with the five-foot pattern to support this blank-verse exercise. Not every stressed syllable is a rhyme position, and final letters alone do not replace sound. Rhythm need not entail end rhyme, nor rhyme five iambs. A dawn/lawn control with actual rhyme changes that pair’s arrangement even if counts remain ten; an isolated coincidence still does not classify a whole poem.

For free verse ask whether a fixed plan governs

For P2 use two lines,“Rain.” and“The people listen behind the gate.” Its exercise note explicitly disclaims a fixed five-iamb plan. Discuss speech rhythms/length changes without calling free verse rhythm-free, formless or arbitrary lineation. Local regularity or one ten-syllable line does not reclassify a whole free-verse poem as blank verse. Keep P1/P2 lineation distinct despite unrhymed endings, and examine wider evidence for real long poems.

Remove one syllable without overgeneralizing

In analytical P3 delete initial The from line one, leaving nine monosyllables and breaking this exercise’s regular ten-syllable scan. Identify the edit instead of treating P3 as the original. Do not infer that every nine-syllable real line is excluded from blank verse: omission, substitution and variation require broader metrical/performance evidence. Separate the regular model from variants and rescan after editing rather than changing ten to nine while keeping identical five weak–strong groups.

Save original lines beside candidate scansion

Keep P1’s source location and excerpt the original where selectable. Put scansion in your own note rather than changing source capitals. Record words, syllables, five candidate feet and end sounds, appending alternate readings with reasons. Whole-line markers suffice if slashes are not selectable; no poetry-meter editor is assumed. Font-size wrapping does not change the two authorial lines or their feet. Image pages remain readable, but search/selection scope needs an actual check.

Weak/strong rhythm is neither Chinese tone-category patterning nor silence on every weak syllable. Preserve this as English scansion rather than another character encoding or a meter changed by bold type. The analyst adds marks; original typography remains separately preserved.

Reopen and count before stating classification scope

Reopen P1 without the scan, recover ten versus nine words, ten syllables each and five weak–strong feet, then explain why spelling alone does not settle dawn/away rhyme. Contrast P2’s free arrangement and P3’s edit without classifying by unrhymed alone. Retain candidate/variation limits and reanalyze changed language, translation or lineation. This checks text, sound conventions and notes, not app speech synthesis, stress detection or testing of every pronunciation environment.
