Literary apostrophe turns speech toward an absent or unresponsive addressee. Preserve that relation while reading three short passage groups in Book Isle. Distinguish direction of address, listeners present in the story and an actual reply. Lack of a reply does not erase the line’s expressive role.

Fix who addresses whom in an original passage

Use passage A: “Only Ahe remained on the shore. She looked at the old lighthouse and said: O lighthouse, do you remember yesterday’s returning boat? The sea gave no answer.” Save and import an owned readable file, noting chapter and opening words. Identify Ahe, lighthouse and boat; the boat is discussed rather than automatically addressed. Silence from the sea does not prove the lighthouse understood. Keep the opening solitude condition with excerpts so an isolated “you” is not mistaken for a two-person exchange; opening words remain recoverable after repagination.

Look for the act of direct address

The Harvard guide’s account of apostrophe concerns direct address to an absent or otherwise unresponsive entity. “O lighthouse” and “you” establish the direction of A’s speech; the building need not first become a human character. The definition does not require an “O” in every instance and is distinct from the punctuation mark called an apostrophe. Record the literary term and conditions instead of collecting every English possessive punctuation mark into the same rhetorical list.

Separate the addressee from present listeners

Make speaker, addressee, present listeners and response-evidence columns. For A, enter Ahe, lighthouse, no other person present and no lighthouse reply is stated, staying within textual evidence. No other person in the story does not eliminate real readers: reading a text differs from characters hearing speech. If a version adds an observer, put that person in the listener field without replacing the explicitly addressed lighthouse. Only then propose effects such as loneliness or remembrance; interpretations must not alter the original cast list. Replacing Ahe’s address with narration describing the lighthouse changes the address relation. Unchanged names do not preserve speech direction; record it again from the actions.

Add an address to an absent friend

Write B separately: “Ahe put away the unsent letter and addressed Mingning, far away in North City: If you could see this sea, you would understand my pause.” They are explicitly apart, supporting an absent-addressee reading, but no telephone, recording or delivery is stated. “You” does not mean Mingning has received the message, and an unsent letter is not necessarily one that will never be sent. Record location, unsent status and address separately. Compare an absent human with A’s building rather than treating both objects as identical.

Use actual dialogue as a response control

For C write: “Mingning stood beside Ahe. Ahe asked: Have you seen that boat? Mingning answered: Yes.” Presence and a reply make this a clear live-dialogue control. Its “you” does not justify mechanically applying the account of A and B. Identify responder and response and check that the answer addresses the question rather than taking any nearby sentence as a reply. Names recur across independent situations; label A, B and C so C’s answer is not inserted into A’s solitary shore scene.

Do not require personification in every address

Return to A and ask whether “remember” attributes human-like memory to the building, a separate possible personification cue. The apostrophe field records direct address. In the rewrite “O lighthouse, I have returned to your foot”, address remains while explicit memory or replying behaviour need not. The analyses can overlap but have different evidence; one label does not automatically establish the other. Keep this as your own labelled control without altering A, identifying the removed wording so another reader can test why the analysis changes.

Troubleshoot pronoun reference and omitted context

If a page shows “you” without its addressee, recover the preceding vocative or name and check whether chapter navigation skipped opening conditions. If the boat became the listener in your notes, reread the direction of speech; the nearest noun does not automatically determine reference. Missing quotation marks in an export require comparison with the original and a labelled explanatory note, not fabricated authorial punctuation. Without a recoverable passage, leave reference unresolved instead of identifying the current version from memory of A.

Reopen and check address and reply independently

Leave reading, reopen the exercise and locate A, B and C before consulting old labels. Refill the four fields independently, then compare address to a building, address to an absent friend and live dialogue. Retain supporting sentences separately from proposed effects. Check that your rewrite remains labelled and originals, excerpts and situations have not been merged. A reader should identify whom “you” addresses, who actually replies and why silence proves no receipt, rather than classifying every exclamation or second-person sentence as apostrophe.
