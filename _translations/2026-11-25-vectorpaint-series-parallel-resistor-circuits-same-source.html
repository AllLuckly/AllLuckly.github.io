Compare two ideal resistor circuits on the same source using nodes, branches, voltage and current calculations.

Define the diagram model

This exercise draws ideal circuit diagrams only; it does not build a circuit. Use a 6 V DC source and ideal resistors of 2 Ω and 3 Ω, comparing series and parallel arrangements with the same source. Ignore wire and source internal resistance, power limits and real tolerances. This is a calculation model, not wiring or component-selection advice. OpenStax explains that series elements share current while parallel elements share voltage.

Calculate the series result

In series, equivalent resistance is 2+3=5 Ω. Total current is 6÷5=1.2 A and is the same through both resistors. Ideal voltage drops are 1.2×2=2.4 V and 1.2×3=3.6 V, which add to 6 V. Record units and arithmetic. Do not add resistance values as though they were current or label each series resistor with the full 6 V.

Calculate the parallel result

In parallel each resistor has 6 V across it. Current is 6÷2=3 A in the 2 Ω branch and 6÷3=2 A in the 3 Ω branch, totaling 5 A. Equivalent resistance is 6÷5=1.2 Ω as a cross-check. These are comparison models and do not imply a real source can supply such current. Record the total separately rather than changing resistor labels.

Create canvas and objects

Create a landscape VectorPaint canvas, for example 1800 by 1100 pixels, and save an editable original. Reserve equal left and right regions for series and parallel diagrams. Keep wire paths, source symbols, resistor symbols, nodes, arrows and labels independently manageable. Use drawing and object controls actually available; do not assume automatic circuit simulation or a symbol library.

Draw a closed series path

On the left draw a voltage-source symbol. From one terminal, connect wires through a 2 Ω resistor and then a 3 Ω resistor before returning to the other source terminal, forming one closed loop. Clear rectangles may represent resistors with values beside them. Do not place them on separate parallel branches. Trace from the source around the entire path, checking for gaps or extra wires bypassing a resistor.

Label series current and drops

Beside both series resistors write I=1.2 A; label their respective voltage drops 2.4 V and 3.6 V. Choose a consistent current-arrow direction around the loop. Arrows represent a diagram convention, not separate wires. Check that the two drops sum to the 6 V source. Labels must not obscure component connections; adjust whitespace when placement is difficult.

Draw parallel nodes and branches

On the right draw the same ideal 6 V source and two clear nodes, upper and lower. Connect one end of the 2 Ω resistor to the upper node and its other end to the lower; connect the 3 Ω resistor to that same pair, forming two branches. Junction dots or meeting lines must make connections unambiguous, with no floating branch. Use a connection point only at an actual junction and distinguish any crossing that is not connected.

Label parallel voltages and currents

Label both branch voltages 6 V, the 2 Ω branch current 3 A and the 3 Ω branch current 2 A. Mark total current 5 A before the split and after recombination, not on either branch alone. Distinguish branches by text or position rather than color alone so the result works in monochrome and a text alternative.

Check connectivity node by node

In the series diagram trace from source through 2 Ω and 3 Ω and back, confirming no branch. In the parallel diagram identify the upper node and trace two independent paths to the lower node, confirming both resistors connect to the same pair. Lines that look parallel do not alone establish an electrical parallel connection; the endpoints determine topology. Move labels or paths to remove obscured or ambiguous crossings.

Compare the numerical results

Place calculations side by side: series equivalent resistance 5 Ω and total current 1.2 A; parallel equivalent resistance 1.2 Ω and total current 5 A. Check series drops 2.4+3.6=6 V and parallel currents 3+2=5 A. The same-source comparison applies only to this ideal model. Real source capacity, heating and protection require independent assessment; the figure is not a real-circuit design or safety test.

Troubleshoot, export and describe

If the series diagram has two paths leaving one node, redraw the topology. If parallel branches show 2.4 V or 3.6 V, check whether series calculations were copied by mistake. Provide a text alternative listing source, resistors, connections and results. Save the editable original and use an available export control to create a separate SVG or PNG. Reopen it to check nodes, units and labels; mark device steps not actually performed as pending.

Check using reciprocal resistance

For parallel resistors, 1/R equivalent = 1/2+1/3=5/6, so R equivalent = 6/5=1.2 Ω. This matches 6÷5 after calculating total current of 5 A. The equivalent is below the smaller 2 Ω branch resistance, another check for this example. In series the sum is 5 Ω, above either individual value. If methods disagree, inspect units and fractions before moving resistor symbols to hide an arithmetic error.

Preserve the ideal-model scope

Label both figures “ideal model, illustration only” and include the 6 V, 2 Ω, 3 Ω and negligible source and wire resistance assumptions in the text alternative. The example yields 5 A total in parallel; a real source’s capability, component power, wiring and protection cannot be guaranteed by this exercise. Do not use it as a physical wiring instruction. Before export, compare the calculations with the diagrams: both parallel branches connect to the same two nodes, while the series circuit has one path. Labels must match their respective calculations.
