Use a permitted reference to draw the disc in perspective and connect the soundbox, pivoting arm and horn.

Check the reference and structure

Choose a permitted photograph of an early gramophone and record its source, viewpoint and access date. The Science Museum Group describes its object as including a hand-turned turntable, soundbox and cardboard horn, with the soundbox on a pivoting arm. Machines vary. Draw visible connections without claiming from a static photo that the turntable works or the stylus is in good condition.

Create canvas and sketch

Create a landscape canvas in Aquarell, for example 1800 by 1400 pixels, and save an editable original. Use visible layer controls to separate background, base, turntable, arm and horn, and sketch. Leave space above the horn and in front of the disc rather than cropping tightly around the base and cutting off the horn or handle. The size is an exercise choice; use controls actually shown in the installed version.

Establish base perspective

Lightly sketch the top, front and visible side of the base, first deciding which side the viewer sees. Compare front and rear top edges and keep the base edges consistent in perspective. The turntable sits on the top, so that surface must have visible depth. At thumbnail size check that the base appears stable; follow the reference for any tilted table or camera angle.

Draw the turntable ellipse

Draw the turntable’s outer ellipse on the base and mark its center and major and minor axes. If thickness is visible, suggest its side along the near edge. The record label and center hole should share the same center rather than using unrelated ellipses. Compare left and right margins against the base so the disc stays on its top surface. In perspective a circular disc generally does not appear as a perfect circle.

Place the record and center

Draw a slightly smaller record ellipse on the turntable with the same orientation; its hole, label and visible edge belong together. Suggest grooves with a few light marks rather than many misaligned circles. Omit distant grooves if they merge with horn and arm. If the ellipse looks egg-shaped, balance its upper, lower, left and right contours before adding fine lines.

Connect the pivoting arm and soundbox

Observe the arm’s fixed end and soundbox in the reference. The fixed end joins the base and the other end comes near the record; draw the stylus contact only if visible. Give the arm continuous thickness so the soundbox does not float above the disc. If the stylus is hidden, do not invent a long needle passing through the record or infer from the drawing that it is correctly adjusted.

Draw the horn and tube

Block the horn opening with an ellipse or visible contour, establish its direction, then taper it toward the tube and soundbox. Suggest thickness with inner and outer edges, and use different values inside and outside the opening. Stop lines where a connection is obscured; do not force a tube through the soundbox to make the parts connect.

Block color and reflections

Give base, record and horn midtone colors from the reference, first checking that their values are distinct in monochrome. Place highlights consistently with one light source: a few arcs can suggest record reflection, while the inside of the horn need not be pure black. Follow the photograph for a cardboard horn instead of automatically adding metallic mirror reflections. Simplify material where uncertain.

Check the connections and overlaps

Hide surface texture and inspect whether the disc ellipse, arm pivot, soundbox and horn still form a connected structure. The disc hole and label should share a center; the arm should not pass inexplicably through the record edge or base. Restore texture and inspect what lies in front and what is hidden. If lines cross both foreground and background objects, correct layer order before darkening outlines.

Check disc and soundbox positions

Look outward from the disc center and check that the label and record edge have not drifted apart. If perspective makes an ellipse appear offset, compare visible edges in the reference rather than forcing every ellipse into one rectangle. At the joint between soundbox and arm, determine which part is in front, behind or above, and stop hidden contours at the overlap. The horn opening has its own orientation and need not match the horizontal turntable ellipse. Keep the construction sketch for a side-by-side comparison of revisions. If a tube is unclear in the photograph, record that uncertainty rather than treating a museum description as a complete depiction of the chosen image.

Compare proportions and save

Reduce the image and compare the horn opening, disc and base. If the horn dominates the entire image, check reference proportions and position before shrinking every component. Save the editable original, then use the visible export or share control to save a separate PNG or JPEG. Reopen it to check horn, arm, disc and base for clipping. Return to the original to verify editable layers and record permissions and unperformed steps.

Recheck connections

Trace the arm from its pivot to the soundbox, then the tube to the horn, identifying front and back at each overlap. Keep the visible turntable edge above the base. If the arm covers the center hole, do not draw that hole in front of it. Record one unclear joint in the photograph.
