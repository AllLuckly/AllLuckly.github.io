Travel videos often mix landscape, portrait, slow-motion, and footage recorded on different devices. Before adding them to a player, choose three recognizable samples to catch incomplete downloads, orientation problems, or duplicate names early. This guide uses three clips—departure, the seaside, and a night market—to establish a small-batch workflow without moving the entire photo library into the app at once.

Check the original clip in Photos first

Choose landscape, portrait and clearly audible clips, each around fifteen seconds to one minute. Play them fully in Photos first to verify direction, duration and sound. Wait for cloud originals to download before importing; thumbnails do not prove complete local files.

Record each clip’s identity, such as “seaside portrait, about thirty seconds”. Display dates and filenames may differ, so do not rely on names alone. For slow-motion, time-lapse or edits, choose the desired edited result or original through supported export/share controls; not every entry point returns the same edition.

Use Photos import and inspect the system picker

In the iCouldPlay media library, open “Import Video” and choose “Import from Photos.” The app hands this over to the system video picker; its appearance and available locations may vary by system and file provider. First confirm that the target video is actually visible, then select one clip as a test. Do not assume that every Photos album will appear unchanged in the app simply because of the entry's name.

If the current picker does not show the target video from Photos, first save the required clip from system Photos to Files, then use “Import from Files” to reach the same goal. This alternative still uses the normal file workflow and does not require changing internal settings beyond Photos permissions. For the basic import steps, see Getting started with iCouldPlay.

Check the content after importing a small batch

After the first clip succeeds, import the other two. Compare the success count with the items actually added, return to the local video source, and start with the “All” category. Open each clip and check its opening frame and total duration. The capture date in Photos, the date added in the app, and the file modification date may differ, so list order alone does not prove that the wrong file was imported.

For matching clip names, compare duration and picture before declaring duplicates. Import may detect an existing filename, so repeated selection may not create a copy. To keep editions, save to Files, give clear date/location/sequence names, then import rather than tapping the same entry repeatedly.

Distinguish video orientation from screen orientation

For a portrait clip, start with a fit-to-screen display mode and check that the top and bottom are visible. Side margins on a landscape device usually reflect a different aspect ratio, not incorrect rotation. Filling the screen may crop some edges; restore fit mode before judging the source orientation. Do not crop people's heads or subtitles just to remove black bars.

Rotate the device and watch again, checking how the player layout and the video's own orientation change separately. If the video looks correct in system Photos but remains sideways in iCouldPlay, note its source, container, and the current app version, then compare it with an ordinary clip. Reimporting the same file generally does not change its rotation metadata, so repeated copying need not be your first fix.

Check slow motion, audio, and pacing

Check ordinary videos at beginning, middle and end, and slow-motion speed-change points. Photos edits may depend on system processing, so the player’s file may differ from the Photos preview. Export the intended edited result through system controls and verify that file rather than assuming a progress error.

For sound tests, choose speech or clear ambient audio. Check volume and output, then another clip. One silent clip may be silent originally or exported without sound; if every clip is silent, follow Audio tracks and sound troubleshooting to isolate each possibility.

Manage duplicate copies and storage space

The original in Photos, an intermediate copy saved in Files, and the local copy in iCouldPlay may all use device storage. Before tidying up, identify each copy's purpose: Photos handles capture and editing, the file copy helps with naming and transfer, and the iCouldPlay copy supports playback and library management. Do not immediately delete two copies just because the same content appears in three places; first confirm which one is the complete original.

iCouldPlay local-deletion confirmation may include deleting video files, not just posters. Play and identify duplicate targets before reading confirmation. Keep independent travel originals; retain Photos editing history, people categories and capture metadata in original materials and Photos, not solely the media library.

Final checks before expanding the batch

For each of the three clips, check its name or content, duration, orientation, sound, and whether you can find it again after returning. Once all pass, expand the import in batches grouped by travel date or location, so a failure can be traced to a particular clip. Around ten short clips per batch is usually easier to check manually than hundreds of mixed videos, though the right number still depends on file sizes and available device storage.

Do Photos favourites and albums transfer too? This guide imports video files. Do not expect the photo library's album structure, favorites, or person recognition to turn automatically into iCouldPlay categories. To organize by trip, start with clear filenames or a separate source directory, then use library browsing alongside them.

Why do imported clips lack movie descriptions? Personal travel footage is not a movie catalog entry. Keeping the original content and clear names is more useful. If you later put family videos on a NAS, continue with Folder-scanning tutorial, organize it by directory first, then decide whether automatic matching is needed.
