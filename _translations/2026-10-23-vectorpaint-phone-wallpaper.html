Phone wallpaper is ultimately displayed alongside time, notifications, or app icons. A centered subject on the canvas may sit directly behind the clock on the phone. This article creates a simple geometric wallpaper in VectorPaint, treating system UI as a composition constraint and checking lock-screen and home-screen results separately.

Identify the target device and screen use first

Check your phone's screen ratio or intended image dimensions and create a portrait canvas with that ratio. You can practice at 1080×2400 px, but this is not a universal phone specification. For actual output, confirm cropping with the target device and system preview.

Decide whether the primary destination is the lock screen or home screen. The lock screen usually has large time and notification areas, while the home screen has rows of app icons. Their empty-space requirements differ. Two layouts are usually clearer than forcing one image to serve both.

Mark system content with temporary regions

Place a pale reference rectangle near the top for the clock's approximate area and leave space below for common controls or notifications. For the home-screen version, use simple rows of small squares to represent icons. These are manual composition guides that need rechecking when system versions or personal settings change.

Put reference objects on a separately named layer and hide it before final export. Do not publish a screenshot containing private notifications as source material. Approximate placeholders are enough to understand which regions the subject should avoid.

Establish the subject on a quiet background

Start with a solid background and build a simple subject from circles, rectangles, or pen paths. Choose an abstract moon, a small mountain, or a geometric portrait, placing it in the lower-middle area rather than immediately against the clock. Limit colors to three or four initially to reduce competition with system icons.

Leave enough space around the subject, especially around important details near the canvas edge. Wallpaper settings may zoom or crop the image, making edge text and fine lines easy to lose. Establish the main silhouette within a spacious composition before adding local texture.

Use value relationships to support the clock and icons

Check whether brightness is stable in the upper reference area. A large gradient or high-contrast texture crossing the clock can leave some digits clear and others hard to read. Keep the top color region simpler and reserve richer graphics for the subject.

Home-screen icons already contain many colors. A fragmented background makes the whole screen crowded. Remove decoration behind icons and concentrate detail in open areas. Opacity is not the only solution: moving shapes, reducing their area, or simplifying background values is often more direct.

Export a preview and inspect it in phone settings

Choose PNG in Export and Share, check width, height, and ratio, and save. Use the image in the phone's wallpaper settings and inspect the actual scaling, positioning, and preview options. Follow your own system version's entry points; this article requires no specific wallpaper effect.

Check whether the time overlaps the subject first, then inspect cropping on all four sides and the lower content. Note the direction and approximate distance of needed moves, then adjust the original. Opening the image in Photos is not enough to declare it complete, because full-image viewing differs from wallpaper filling a screen.

Organize separate lock-screen and home-screen versions

The lock-screen version can retain a fuller subject while keeping the clock area quiet. The home-screen version should reduce detail behind icons, perhaps retaining only the background and a smaller subject. Shared colors and shapes can keep the two versions cohesive.

Name the two editable originals and outputs clearly, such as Moonlight_Lock and Moonlight_Home. Update both when changing colors later, but do not force their compositions to be identical. After using them for a while, inspect the actual icon arrangement again; you may notice obstructions missed in the first preview.

Check edges, text, and output quality

Compare the phone's actual appearance with the original. Confirm that circles did not stretch into ellipses and cropping did not remove important parts. If you add a short phrase, ensure it does not mingle with the system clock or notifications. Wallpaper usually needs little text; one clear phrase works better than a long explanation.

PNG has fixed pixels. Higher numbers do not add detail absent from a small imported image. A purely vector subject can be rerendered at appropriate dimensions, while photos need sufficiently clear sources. Keep the original and verified device dimensions so a phone change allows a new layout rather than repeated compression of an old image.

Frequently asked questions

Why did the subject enlarge after setting the wallpaper? The system may crop or scale to fill the screen. Check positioning in its preview, then add space in the original. Merely increasing export pixels does not solve ratio or composition problems.

Can one wallpaper be guaranteed to fit every phone? Screen ratios, system interfaces, and personal layouts change the obscured regions. Make a spacious base version, then adapt it for primary devices. When sharing, state image dimensions and suggest checking subject placement in wallpaper preview to reduce misunderstandings.

Leave edge margins for repositioning

Make a spacious version with a smaller subject and a comparison version with a larger one, then inspect both in system preview. The spacious version adapts more easily to cropping; the larger subject has more impact. Choose by whether key contours remain complete, not merely which fills more space. Keep the spacious original when sharing across devices; adapting its ratio is easier than extending a finished edge-to-edge design.
