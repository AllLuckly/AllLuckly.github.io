A desk, floor and window may each seem plausible but disagree when combined. Paint a near-frontal room organised around one vanishing point. Limit the scene to one desk and one window, establishing their shared space before adding everyday details.

Set up a separate study

Use an approximately 1800×1300 px landscape canvas with eye level above centre. Separate room planes, furniture, window and light. Use a few hand-drawn depth guides and broader colour planes; an automatic grid is unnecessary for checking shared convergence.

Establish back wall, sides and floor before placing the desk. Save this broad space before panes or floor seams. Keep guides separately available for later checks instead of turning every construction edge into a heavy final outline that is difficult to revise.

Fix the eye level and back wall first

Draw a faint eye-level line slightly above centre and mark a vanishing point to the right. Establish the rectangular back wall and extend its corners outward to define side walls, ceiling and floor. Leave desk space without an exaggerated wide-angle effect.

Keep frontal edges approximately horizontal, verticals upright and depth edges converging on the shared point. Use a few guides rather than a dense grid. Separate structure, furniture and light, hiding construction lines later instead of turning all of them into black final outlines.

Compress floor cues into the distance

Lay in warm-grey flooring, then run a few seams toward the vanishing point. Compress crosswise seams into the distance rather than spacing them evenly. Begin with only a few. Check the rear wall contact and convergence if the space resembles a flat stage backdrop.

Keep the floor unified, adding subtle variation in only a few near boards. Use lighter walls with a modest lighting difference between sides. Establish an inhabitable room through broad planes before adding window and desk; accessories cannot repair an incoherent structure.

Align the window with its wall

Place the window on the left wall with upright near and far sides and top and bottom edges converging toward the point. Avoid pasting a frontal rectangle onto a receding wall. Establish the frame before panes, with a narrow lit and shaded sill to show thickness.

Simplify the exterior into pale masses and keep the inner frame darker. Hide the light patch to check the window geometry rather than covering errors with thicker framing. A simplified floor light should relate to the window position, not appear as an unrelated bright square elsewhere.

Give the desk the room’s perspective

Place the desk near the back wall, with a roughly horizontal front and receding sides aimed at the same point. Mark all tabletop corners before adding upright legs, allowing partial rear-leg occlusion. Relate its height to the sill, back wall and plausible human scale.

Use restrained warm wood against grey walls. Add small leg contacts and a darker area beneath the desk. If it tips, check corner convergence. If it seems attached flat to the wall, restore tabletop thickness and its projection into the room before strengthening outlines.

Add a few objects and check them individually

Add a book and cup as a simple box and ellipse, matching the desk orientation and scale. Any wall frame should belong clearly to its wall and compress with it. Zoom out after each object so small details do not overwhelm the room’s space.

Hide the guides and assess whether colour planes distinguish walls, floor, desk and window. Restore them to check a few major depth edges. Keep minor hand-drawn variation when the larger space works, but correct conflicting broad directions. Shared spatial logic is the goal, not mathematical perfection in every small mark.

Review and plan the next study

Check the desk direction at normal size, room continuity small and selected window and desk corners close up. Hide floor seams to test the broad planes. Correct conflicting long edges first while allowing small hand-drawn variation, rather than rebuilding the entire grid for a cup-handle error.

Keep perspective, lighting and final PNG stages, checking exported fine lines for noise. Next, move only the desk nearer with walls and window fixed, rebuilding its apparent size and contacts. This tests understanding of shared space better than adding dark outlines to every object.
