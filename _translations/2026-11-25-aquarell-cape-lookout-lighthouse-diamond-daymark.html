Use a permitted reference to study the taper, lantern room and diamond daymark of a coastal lighthouse.

Check the reference

Choose a permitted photograph of Cape Lookout Lighthouse and record creator, viewpoint and date. The National Park Service historic structures report describes a tall slender tower with a black-and-white diamond pattern and a lantern room. Viewpoint, distance and weather change the pattern’s appearance. Work from one image for visible structure instead of inventing hidden portions from another photograph.

Create an editable canvas

Create a portrait canvas in Aquarell, for example 1400 by 1800 pixels; this is an exercise size, not a printing standard. Save the original and use available layer controls to separate sky, ground, tower, diamonds and sketch. Place the lighthouse slightly off center with room above and below. If control names differ, use those visible in the installed version.

Set the horizon and tower axis

Observe the distant sea or ground in the photograph and draw a light horizontal guide, avoiding a sloped foreground mistaken for the horizon. Add a light axis through the tower center and compare the positions of top and base. If perspective or camera tilt is visible, record it rather than forcing a perfectly vertical tower. At thumbnail size check that the tower sits on the ground.

Draw the tapered body

Use two light lines for the tower edges and compare top width, base width and height. Establish the large continuous silhouette before adjusting its taper to the reference; avoid parallel edges that ignore visible changes. Keep the guide axis between the edges and check whether the top drifts excessively. If details later look wrong, return to this outline instead of hiding a proportion problem under diamonds.

Place the lantern room

Mark the outer shapes of platform, lantern room and roof at the top, comparing their widths with the tower. Block the visible shapes first, then use the photograph to add the platform edge and a few vertical divisions. Do not force hidden railing sections into view. If the lantern resembles a box pasted on the tower, check its connection and symmetry before adding many fine lines.

Mark the diamond transitions

On a separate diamond layer draw light diagonal guides across the tower. Observe where black and white shapes meet and how their apparent size changes. The tower has volume, so markings near the edges may appear narrower; do not paste a flat grid beyond the silhouette. Place two or three visible dark diamonds, compare them with the photo, then continue without guessing how many are hidden behind the tower.

Fill the black and white shapes

Keep a light tower base and fill the dark diamonds with a near-black gray on their own layer. Stop each shape at the tower silhouette. If the current canvas provides a visible clipping method, use it as available; otherwise erase outside portions manually. The light areas need enough value contrast against the sky for the edge to remain visible. Near-black gray leaves room for shadow detail.

Suggest tower volume

Use the same photograph to identify light direction and add a gentle shadow on the unlit side without destroying the black-and-white pattern. Keep the shadow continuous along the tower rather than giving each diamond a different light source. Hide and show the shadow layer to compare the round form. If the markings become muddy, reduce the shadow strength or adjust neighboring values.

Paint coastal ground and sky

Use muted colors for visible sand, grasses or sea, simplifying the reference rather than inventing detail. Keep distant features quieter than the tower. A soft contact shadow where ground meets base can help, but it must not make the base float. Check sky value so both pale diamonds and the lantern roof remain distinct without darkening the entire sky.

Compare pattern and silhouette

Hide the diamond layer and inspect only the tower and lantern silhouette. If the relationships among top, base and ground fail, correct the large shapes first. Restore the diamonds and compare the black-white boundaries in the center and at both edges with the reference. Reduce the image to phone size and check that tower and top remain recognizable; do not change the basic pattern just to make a thumbnail louder.

Troubleshoot and record changes

If the tower appears to lean, compare its axis with the photograph’s horizon rather than rotating diamonds independently. If a diamond appears to float, check for color outside the silhouette and whether the edge pattern narrows with the curved surface. Duplicate the original for a revision and record the before-and-after issue. Retain source, access date and permission information, and recheck the license before sharing.

Save and inspect the export

Save the editable original and use the visible share or export control to save a separate PNG or JPEG without replacing it. Reopen the export to inspect the tower top, base, pattern and sky for cropping. Return to the original and confirm that diamonds, tower and background remain editable. If the pattern is missing, check layer visibility, file location and canvas boundaries. Mark device steps not actually performed as pending.

Check tower top and base

Return the image to its normal orientation and compare tower top, base and guide axis to ensure the lantern room has not drifted sideways. Do not complete a base edge hidden by ground with a dark line; leave unclear doors or windows unknown.
