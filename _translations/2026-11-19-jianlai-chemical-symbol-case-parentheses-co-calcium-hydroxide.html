Enter two case-sensitive symbols and a parenthesized formula in an ordinary draft, then check symbol boundaries and atom counts externally. This is text checking, without preparing, handling or identifying chemicals. Jianlai is not an element recognizer, and visual subscripts do not establish identical source text.

Fix three ASCII targets and the scope

Create a private ordinary English/symbol draft with Jianlai identified and retain the current scheme/appearance. Three literal lines are Co, CO and Ca(OH)2, using ASCII letters, parentheses and digits without outer spaces or punctuation. These are text exercises, not labels, recipes or reactions to perform; do not seek household chemicals. Preserve external targets/references separately from the draft rather than edit them to match errors. Record host capitalization behavior and inspect committed characters; a fortunate correction does not establish correct original entry.

A lowercase second letter matters in an element symbol

An element symbol starts with an uppercase letter; a second letter is lowercase. Co is the cobalt symbol, joining C with lowercase o. CO instead contains uppercase C and O and is carbon monoxide’s molecular formula. Similar sound or width does not make them interchangeable. Mark Co as one symbol and split CO into two externally, then inspect the committed second character. A font change does not replace characters, and digit zero is neither letter. Record appearance, symbol segmentation and formula meaning separately rather than treating a candidate hint as chemical identification. The third line has C, a, left parenthesis, O, H, right parenthesis and 2: seven ASCII characters. Co and CO have two each. Counting each logical line break once gives thirteen items overall. Keep indices outside the draft; seven positions are not seven elements, and case state adds no character.

Enter each line and distinguish case state from text

Enter uppercase C then lowercase o, verify Co, then a real newline and uppercase CO. Follow identifiable installed case controls without assuming one Shift action affects exactly one character or locks automatically. Pause to inspect text. On line three enter Ca, ASCII left parenthesis, OH, right parenthesis and digit 2, without final punctuation. Ca uses lowercase a; both letters inside the parentheses are uppercase. Candidate text, keycap state and committed characters differ. Record any line completed with another keyboard separately from Jianlai entry.

Apply the trailing two to the entire OH group

Segment Ca(OH)2 as Ca plus two OH groups. Each OH contributes one O and one H, giving Ca=1, O=2, H=2, five atoms per formula unit. For this ionic compound, the formula describes composition rather than a photographed independent five-ball molecule. The two applies neither only to H nor also to Ca. Establish scope before counting. Preserve ASCII 2 for literal checking instead of silently replacing it with Unicode subscript ₂. Save a separate presentation version if desired; similar meaning does not establish identical characters.

A copy without parentheses has different counts

Remove only both parentheses in an external control to obtain CaOH2. Under the stated symbol/count rules it gives Ca=1, O=1 and H=2, four atoms, differing from Ca(OH)2. This tests textual scope, not the existence or stability of a real substance with that formula. Put no erroneous formula on physical items and do not enlarge letters to disguise missing parentheses. Both strings contain 2, but its scope differs. Length, digit identity and composition require separate checks. An untyped control is external analysis, not an automatic keyboard error report.

Ca(OH)2 -> Ca:1 O:2 H:2; total 5
CaOH2   -> Ca:1 O:1 H:2; total 4

Distinguish a leading coefficient from an internal count

Use 2Ca(OH)2 as a separate mathematical control. Its leading coefficient means two original formula units: Ca=2, O=4, H=4, ten atoms total. It does not rename a symbol or add one calcium to a single unchanged unit. Expand two copies and compare with five times two rather than merge both twos into a final four while leaving Ca at one. This is composition counting without mass, rate or purity inputs. The coefficient control is outside the three-line target and must not be appended while claiming exact equality.

2Ca(OH)2 -> Ca:2 O:4 H:4; total 10
Leading coefficient applies to the complete formula unit.

Troubleshoot case, parentheses and character kinds

For Co/CO inspect the second character and host capitalization. CA may reflect a locked uppercase state. Missing O count suggests lost parentheses; doubled totals suggest an unintended leading coefficient. Full-width and ASCII parentheses may look alike but differ under this literal contract. Do not normalize silently before passing the initial draft. Revise one point with before/after copies, then segment/count again. Without reliable text state limits rather than report exact code points from a small screenshot. External chemistry is not real-time keyboard recognition, balancing or label correction.

Reopen and deliver literal text and composition checks

Reopen the draft and check Co, CO and Ca(OH)2 with two logical breaks and no numbering. Deliver case equality, symbol segmentation, group counts and two external controls separately, marking unperformed steps. Restore ordinary input and keep the draft private and off physical items. Explain from characters why Co differs from CO and why the group count affects two elements; the keyboard need not understand chemistry. Charges, hydrates, isotopes or deeper parentheses require new notation checks, not universal formula parsing, substance identification or host/font certification.
