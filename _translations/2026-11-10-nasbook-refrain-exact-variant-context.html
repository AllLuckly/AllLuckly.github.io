A recurring poetic line can keep its wording while changing its effect in context. In Book Isle retain stanza boundaries and occurrence locations, separating the exact line, neighbouring context and personal response, then test a negative variant. The task examines returns of a refrain rather than rhyme identification or enjambment, and requires neither automatic verse classification nor generated literary judgments.

Establish three locations in a complete short poem

Choose a lawful short poem and record title, author, translator, edition, stanza and original line location. Read the whole poem rather than searching only for repeated words. This invented three-stanza exercise ends each stanza with “灯还亮着。” (“The lamp is still lit.”); preceding contexts are waiting, the other person’s absence and eventual return. Attribute it to no real poet. Keep location, original line, nearby content, wording changes and interpretation as five fields. Manually transcribe unselectable scans and compare with the image; failed extraction does not establish absent repetition. Mark uncertain original stanza boundaries unresolved.

Use the refrain concept rather than end rhyme

Use the refrain entry in the Academy of American Poets glossary for recurrence of a phrase or line at intervals. Identical final characters alone do not establish a full refrain. Compare the entire “灯还亮着” line, initially preserving punctuation, rather than only its final 着. Record all three locations and intervals. Three search matches do not automatically mean once per stanza. Identify matches in titles, epigraphs or notes separately from body-text returns. The glossary defines the task without imposing one compulsory classification on the actual poem.

Compare every occurrence including negatives and tense

Place the three occurrences side by side and compare 灯, 还, 亮, 着 and the full stop, deciding exact equality before discussing effect. All are identical here; do not alter the third to justify a different interpretation. Preserve actual changes such as negatives or words meaning still, formerly or not yet rather than deleting small words for a tidy table. Wording and punctuation equality can be separate checks. A comma/full-stop change may affect syntax or pause cues but alone establishes no authorial psychology. Retain each original location so copying one occurrence three times cannot create false repetition.

Record the distinct context around each return

Before the first return, waiting can support an interpretation of the light accompanying anticipation. Absence before the second may suggest persistence or disappointment. Return before the third may suggest welcome. Label these as evidence-based possibilities tied to neighbouring information, not a changed physical state of the lamp. For other plausible readings retain alternatives and supporting words rather than treating repetition as proof of identical emotion. Read the next line or stanza opening where necessary to see whether the return also opens a new development; one preceding line cannot establish a whole-poem conclusion.

Test the variant meaning no longer lit

Write “灯不再亮着。” (“The lamp is no longer lit.”) as your own variant, without replacing the third original occurrence. 不再 changes continued illumination to no longer being lit, so the literal condition differs and must not retain an exact-equality mark. Discussing its possible effect is a separate exercise, not a claim that the original poem used it. Variant refrains in actual poems can have formal roles: identify unchanged and changed parts in the whole work. This exercise does not ban discussion of all nonidentical refrains; it requires acknowledging actual changes.

Preserve line structure and translation separately

Use a reliable edition to determine whether the phrase occupies a whole verse line or appears within one; narrow-screen wrapping does not create extra repetitions. For translation comparisons retain all three occurrences in each language, translator and edition. Identical Chinese does not guarantee identical English wording, and an English variant does not automatically establish mistranslation. The invented original may consistently read “The lamp is still lit,” while the variant means “no longer lit”; still must not become already extinguished. Complete the formal comparison before interpretation. Do not remove 还 or stanza spaces merely for fluency.

Return to all three locations beyond search counts

Save necessary positions through available bookmarks or notes, or number occurrences 1–3 externally. Leave and return to all three in any order, reading each line and its context to ensure three records do not point to one passage. Exclude title/note search matches from the body count while retaining the reason. For unsearchable scans retrieve by stanza before deleting and reimporting a book. Open exported records and inspect negatives such as 不再, location numbers and alteration labels, not just file creation. Use necessary short excerpts rather than copying another person’s whole poem to fill a table.

Troubleshoot wording, locations and context

For count discrepancies inspect text regions and duplicate records of one location. For conflicting interpretations check small words and surrounding sentences before blaming layout. Preserve earlier readings and new evidence, specifying whether a revision changes wording equality or contextual interpretation. Retain three exact occurrences and locations, equality/changes, three contextual possibilities and the negative variant, all traceable to the source. The result is a discussable refrain-reading record, not automatic emotion detection, a judgment of poetic quality or guaranteed symbolic meaning.
