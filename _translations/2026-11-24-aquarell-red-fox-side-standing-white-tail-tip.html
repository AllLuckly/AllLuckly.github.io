Use a permitted side-view photograph to study a red fox’s proportions, overlapping legs and bushy tail.

Confirm reference and color limits

Choose a permitted side-view photo of an adult red fox and record angle, season and lighting. The National Park Service describes a common red form with dark legs and ears and a pale belly and white tail tip, while other coat colors occur. This exercise uses one photo rather than treating red as the only appearance. Observe the image without approaching, feeding or disturbing wildlife.

Set up canvas and support line

Create an approximately 1800 by 1400 landscape canvas in Aquarell and save an editable original; dimensions are exercise choices. Separate sketch, body, background and ground shadow with available layer controls. Mark the ground and body axis lightly and leave space ahead of the head and behind the tail. Compare back slope and visible foot heights with the photograph instead of putting every foot on one heavy line regardless of perspective.

Block head and body proportions

Use an elongated oval for the torso and mark head, muzzle tip, rump, tail base and tip separately. Compare head-to-body proportion and then tail length against body length. Follow the individual and pose rather than a fixed number. Connect the head through the neck into the back and continue chest into belly. Inspect the silhouette small; if the head looks pasted on, revise the neck transition first.

Locate pointed ears and muzzle

Observe the two ears: in profile the far ear may be partly hidden by the near ear or head, so avoid two identical triangles side by side. Extend pointed ears from the head silhouette, then draw the slender muzzle and nose. Judge eye-to-nose distance from the photo. The slender muzzle should connect naturally to the head rather than look like a pasted-on triangle. Use simple values for eye detail that cannot be clearly seen.

Distinguish front and rear legs

Mark visible front- and hind-leg connections beneath the torso, following the photograph for joint bends and feet. Where a near leg overlaps a far leg, stop the hidden contour. Omit a leg that cannot be seen rather than lining up four complete legs merely to show a count. Separate dark leg fur from the ground shadow so contact points remain legible instead of becoming one black mass beneath the belly.

Connect the bushy tail

Start the tail at the rear of the rump and widen it into a bushy outline according to the reference, retaining the change in thickness at its root. The tail can be near the ground or raised with posture; do not force it horizontal. Put the pale tip at the end, separate from the pale belly. At small size it should read as connected to the rump rather than a floating feather or background ornament.

Arrange red and pale areas

Lay a mid-value reddish brown on the body, then distinguish lit and shaded back, cheek and belly planes. Adjust to the reference light rather than claiming one color value fits every fox. Dark ear backs and legs can use deep brown instead of absolute black, retaining enough value separation to read the limbs. Give the pale tail tip and belly distinct boundaries so they do not merge and flatten the form.

Suggest fur with limited marks

Make the large color areas work first, then add a few short strokes along the direction of fur on neck, back and tail. The silhouette can be irregular without identical spikes everywhere. Tail fullness comes from its overall width and values, not a ring of equal teeth. Hide details and compare: if the structure still reads, retain only helpful marks. Do not invent texture where the photograph is unclear.

Place background and ground contacts

Use muted grass, earth or neutral background colors to support the subject. The environment is a composition choice, not a claim it comes from the same location as the photo. Add modest foot contact shadows consistent with one light source and distinguish them in value from dark legs. If the white tail tip merges with the background, change nearby value rather than outlining the entire fox. Hide the background to check leg and tail-root overlaps.

Troubleshoot and export

For floating feet, inspect visible ground contacts and near-far leg overlaps. For a detached tail, check the tail root. If the face reads as a cat, compare muzzle length and ears with the reference instead of using exaggerated color to conceal proportion issues. Save the editable original, then export a separate PNG or JPEG using a control visible in the installed version. Reopen it to inspect ears, tail and feet for cropping, return to the original to check layers, and mark unperformed device or print steps pending.

Compare silhouette to reference

Duplicate the artwork and temporarily simplify body and tail to one mid-value against a contrasting background. Check whether ears, muzzle, legs and tail direction still read. Restore color and compare one near-far leg overlap and the tail-root connection with the permitted photo. If they differ, revise the outline before repainting. Keep both copies and the source record; this compares visual clarity rather than scientifically measuring an animal’s posture or behavior. If an ear tip merges with background grass at small size, move the grass or adjust the local value before changing the ear outline. Recheck the exported edge for cropping and keep both original and export for comparison, noting the location and reason for this edge adjustment.
