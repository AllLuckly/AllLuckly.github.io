An even numerator and denominator do not make a fraction impossible: four over two is valid. The contradiction concerns the initial lowest-terms requirement. Read the classical √2 argument through original proof cards in Book Isle, preserving reasons without claiming a new discovery or automatic proof.

State the target and its contrary assumption

P1 declares √2 the positive principal square root and asks whether it can be a ratio of integers. Temporarily assume √2=a/b with positive integers a, b, nonzero b and gcd(a, b)=1. Positivity permits this sign choice; arbitrary real denominators are not allowed. Put the parity lemma/derivation on P2 and controls on P3; export PDF and retain source work. Check the radical and lowest-terms condition and bookmark P1. Both even is not a contradiction without coprimality.

Square to obtain an integer equation first

Squaring a/b gives a²=2b² after multiplying by nonzero b², so a² is even. Keep “a² even” separate from the next conclusion “a even” until its reason is supplied. This remains conditional on the contrary assumption; squaring is not a new proof that the fraction exists. Label algebra steps and copy whole expressions externally when selection is unavailable, rather than treating displayed equations as App validation.

Prove the parity lemma using an arbitrary odd integer

If integer a is odd, a=2k+1 for integer k and a²=4k²+4k+1=2(2k²+2k)+1 is odd. Since a² was even, a must be even. Values 3²=9 and 5²=25 are checks, not the universal proof; the arbitrary-k expansion covers every odd integer. Parity here is an integer property, not a claim about arbitrary real or complex inputs.

Do not replace the lemma with “every even integer is an integer square.” Six is even but not an integer square, irrelevant to the needed implication that an even square has an even base. Record proposition, converse and unrelated claims separately. The odd-square algebra covers every integer k rather than merely testing three and five.

Substitute a=2k and force an even denominator

With a=2k, substitute 4k²=2b² and divide by two to get b²=2k². The same lemma makes b even, b=2m for integer m; k and m need not match. Both a, b have common factor two, contradicting gcd(a, b)=1. No division by zero occurred. The contradiction rejects a coprime integer representation, not arithmetic involving an undefined denominator.

assume sqrt(2)=a/b; a,b positive integers; gcd(a,b)=1
a^2=2*b^2 -> a is even -> a=2*k
4*k^2=2*b^2 -> b^2=2*k^2 -> b is even
a,b both divisible by 2 contradict gcd(a,b)=1
odd-square lemma: (2*k+1)^2=2*(2*k^2+2*k)+1
1.414=707/500; (1.414)^2=1.999396 != 2

Conclude only what the contradiction establishes

No coprime integer representation exists, so √2 is irrational. This does not deny a real square root, approximation or location on the real line. If −√2 were rational, its negative would make √2 rational too. Keep the two roots of x²=2 separate from the principal-root symbol. Do not expand the conclusion to all radicals: √4=2 is a counterexample.

Starting with 2/1 has even a and odd b but fails a²=2b². Using 2/2 gives both even but represents one and also fails that equation. Even-even appearance alone is not the contradiction; derive it continuously from the same assumption and equation. Track the same a, b rather than substitute an unrelated non-reduced example midway.

Separate decimal approximation from irrationality criteria

The approximation 1.414 equals 707/500 and squares to 1.999396, not exactly two. Finite decimals are integer ratios with a power-of-ten denominator. An infinite decimal can still be rational: 1/3=0.333… repeats. Displayed digits do not prove a nonrepeating tail. A decimal screenshot without an argument is approximation evidence, not an irrationality proof.

Diagnose lowest terms and assumption levels

If notes say two even integers make any fraction impossible, restore lowest terms. If the even-square implication lacks a reason, revisit the odd-square lemma. Copying a contradiction for √4 incorrectly infers that b must be even from a²=4b²: a=2, b=1 is a valid coprime pair. Correct one step with its reason rather than paste the conclusion over a gap. Check gcd=1, nonzero denominator and temporary-assumption labels in saved notes, using an external card if export is unavailable.

Reopen and rebuild the proof from lemma to conflict

Without the conclusion, restore the contrary assumption/coprimality, derive both even and name the violated premise. Use 4/2, √4 and 1/3 controls to reject overclaims.
