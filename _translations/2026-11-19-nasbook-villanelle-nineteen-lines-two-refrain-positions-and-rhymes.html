Hearing a repeated sentence does not establish a villanelle’s stanza structure. Read a complete original nineteen-line English poem in Book Isle and record two refrain positions, two rhyme groups and the final quatrain. Identical repeated text need not have an identical contextual effect. This exercise uses verbatim refrains while acknowledging modern variations.

Build six stanzas from authored line breaks

Arrange the poem 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 4 and label L1–L19, with separate refrain/rhyme pages. Export PDF or retain EPUB source work. In Book Isle check six stanzas and nineteen authored IDs. Display wrapping and blank separator lines add no poetic lines. Revisit source work if IDs cross pages. Bookmark the opening and highlight the two sentences where selection permits; otherwise record verbatim text by ID externally without an App villanelle detector.

Register whole refrain texts rather than end words

Set A1 to I keep one lamp beside the door. and A2 to The tide will bring no word ashore. L1/L3 introduce them; L2 is an ordinary B line, not a third refrain. Preserve case, wording and periods for later comparison. Matching door or ashore alone does not establish identical lines. The two distinct refrain texts both use rhyme A; they do not each belong to a separate rhyme.

Trace alternation and count eight occurrences

Stanzas two through five end with A1 at L6, A2 at L9, A1 at L12 and A2 at L15. The quatrain closes with A1 at L18 followed by A2 at L19. A1 occurs at 1, 6, 12, 18 and A2 at 3, 9, 15, 19: four occurrences each, eight refrain lines total. Four includes the initial occurrence, not four additional repeats. Nineteen minus eight leaves eleven other lines; eight occurrences are not eight distinct refrain texts.

L16 in the quatrain is not a third refrain; only L18/L19 reproduce the two registered texts. Each tercet has one B middle line, and the quatrain has only L17 as B, giving five plus one equals six B lines. This stanza-based check independently corroborates the end-word ledger and detects a missing ending or merged final refrains.

Separate rhyme membership from refrain identity

A endings include door, ashore, ignore, floor, before, more and shore, sharing /ɔr/ in common rhotic pronunciations or /ɔː/ in non-rhotic ones. B endings gray, away, day, day, spray, sway share /eɪ/. B occupies L2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, six lines; the other thirteen are A, including eight refrain lines and five other A lines. Read the stanza scheme shown in the ledger. Lowercase a and A1 share rhyme A; notation case does not create another sound group.

Change one verb to distinguish rhyme from verbatim refrain

In a control copy change only keep at L12 to leave. I leave one lamp beside the door. retains the door rhyme, nineteen lines and six stanzas but no longer repeats this worksheet’s A1 verbatim; the action wording changes from keeping to leaving. The strict repetition check fails even though the end word matches. Modern villanelles can vary refrains, so identify the failed worksheet criterion rather than disqualify every historical or contemporary variant.

Reread identical refrains after different preceding lines

L1 first introduces the lamp; L6 follows wind and departing gulls, L12 follows repeated steps and a hinge, and L18 follows paling water. Identical wording may suggest persistent waiting or resolve in different local contexts. Pair interpretations with specific preceding lines rather than measure sadness from repetition counts or claim a sole known intention. This exercise checks lines, stanzas, rhymes and refrains, not every metrical foot. Chinese explanation supplies meaning without claiming a newly rhymed nineteen-line translation.

Diagnose missing endings, misplaced refrains and changed source

A total of eighteen may mean merging the final adjacent refrains; twenty may mean counting display wrapping. If A1 is logged at 1, 6, 11, 18, revisit L11, an ordinary B line. Whole-sentence search can locate candidates, but inspect all four contexts manually; use line IDs for scans. Keep erroneous positions and source quotations in a log rather than move lines to fit a table. Clearly name English source, Chinese explanation and the leave control so the control cannot replace the original.

Occurrence count differs from spacing: A1 index gaps are five, six and six, so it is not uniformly periodic by line number. A2 gaps are six, six and four as the ending brings the refrains together. Four search hits alone cannot establish the required positions and alternation.
