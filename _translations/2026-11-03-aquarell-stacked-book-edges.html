A book stack resembles shallow boxes, but the important distinctions are spine versus exposed pages and which surfaces the next book covers. Paint three fictional untitled books, establishing orientation before covers and page layers. Individual pages need not be countable; clear boundaries and selected directional marks explain the construction.

Establish three differently oriented boxes

Use an approximately 1700×1500 px canvas with a broad bottom book, slightly turned middle book and smaller offset top book. Establish corners, heights and contacts with restrained guides. Keep separate layers. Check a stable monochrome stack before colours; an extreme overhang needs deliberate support, not colour used to disguise inconsistent box directions.

Assign spine and opening for every book

Mark each book’s spine side temporarily; the opposite side is the opening. Books may face differently, but one book should not have full rounded spines on both sides. Test dark spine and pale pages before titles. Correct surface width or values when unclear, then hide construction arrows so the image explains itself.

Let covers project slightly beyond the page block

Project covers modestly beyond the page block, with clearer near and narrower far edges and genuine occlusion. Avoid an equal-width coloured sandwich. Resolve cover–pages–cover in an enlarged single-book sample, then apply it to the stack, aligning corners before strengthening outlines.

Connect spine curvature to the book body

Choose a slightly rounded or flatter fictional spine. Connect it into the covers rather than attaching a tube. Match its width to book thickness and shade it under upper-left light. Reduce a rod-like bulge or add a modest turn to a flat strip, treating the binding as a selected design rather than a universal book construction.

Follow the page-block surface with texture

Use warm off-white pages with compressed, directional layer lines. Clarify only a small near patch and simplify elsewhere. Keep those marks off the spine and inside the page block. Reduce venetian-blind contrast, or add a few incomplete lines to a plastic-like block instead of drawing every page.

Use inter-book shadows for real contact

Cast each upper book’s shadow onto the cover below, varying it with offsets and contacts rather than equal black strips. Hide page texture to assess depth. Repair floating boxes through contacts and corners, or reduce deep shadow area in a black-tower result so broad cover planes remain visible.

Keep lettering minimal and fictional

Use a short invented title or simple bars aligned to the cover or spine, not front-facing text pasted onto a receding plane. Leave one book unlettered. Use available text controls or hand-letter without assuming new layout features. Quiet competing text; real titles or covers would require separate verification and usage checks.

Find misalignment through three gaps

Inspect the gap below the top book, the exposed middle-cover corner and the bottom contact. Hide details and compare flat masses; these gaps reveal perspective errors better than page texture. Repair one offending book at a time. Review hierarchy normally, weight small and overshooting lines or tangencies close up.

Compare two-book and three-book versions

Hide the top book in a copy to test the lower pair, then restore it. If only the third book confuses the stack, correct its direction or contact rather than repaint everything. A temporary common grey can test whether depth depends solely on bright colours. Restore intended colours and retain comparison previews.

Keep an unlettered master and turn one book

Keep box-structure, unlettered and finished versions with separate books. Next, turn only the middle book, rebuilding spine/opening identity, cover projection and page-line direction while holding the others and light fixed. Changing two colours alone is not a turn. Record the most consequential changed edge for future stacked shallow objects.
