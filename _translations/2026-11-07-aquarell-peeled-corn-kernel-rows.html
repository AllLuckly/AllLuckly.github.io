Identically sized, equally bright kernels resemble yellow squares on paper. Draw an invented partly husked ear of corn, establishing its long volume and turning rows over the surface, with a few curled husks exposing it. This is drawing observation, not cooking or plant identification. Layers separate cob mass, kernels and leaves; repetition, scale, overlap and omission are arranged manually.

Establish a long tapering ear first

Use an approximately 1500×1800 px portrait canvas with a mid grey-green background. Tilt the axis slightly right, making the lower ear broader and tapering toward the top rather than parallel sides with a round cap. Establish upper-left light and lower-right shade in pale yellow and mid yellow-brown without kernels yet. Separate sketch, ear mass, kernels, husks and silk. Reduce the view and correct a flat sign-like body through taper and turning values rather than dense kernels. Leave attachment room below instead of cropping the base and letting husks float from the background.

Set lengthwise rows and then individual kernels

Sketch five to seven visible lengthwise row guides, broader at the front and progressively narrower on the sides. Divide kernels within rows rather than starting with a perfect checkerboard. Cross boundaries may offset slightly and edges vary naturally while the broad row direction remains clear. Complete only two middle rows first and trace them from wide to narrow regions. If the tip retains the lower width and density, reduce upper kernels instead of squeezing the last row into a black line. Visible row count is a drawing simplification, not a universal arrangement for corn varieties.

Show only visible parts of side kernels

Compress visible kernel widths near the right silhouette rather than adding complete squares outside it. Sketch one turning kernel’s rounded whole and retain only its visible surface to anchor it on the ear. Interrupt some boundaries through slight overlap instead of outlining every kernel transparently. If sides resemble separate hanging bead chains, reconnect their row direction and values. Hide husks and inspect the cob alone for a clear turn. Do not widen side kernels merely to count every one; hidden portions may be omitted instead of sacrificing volume for countability.

Keep kernel lighting within the whole ear

Allow slight individual bulges while carrying upper-left light and lower-right shade continuously across rows. Use pale warm yellow in light and grey yellow-brown in shade without white dots on every dark-side kernel. Deepen only selected near gaps, not a uniform black mesh. If kernels resemble buttons, reduce individual cast shadows and regroup values. If a flat grid, strengthen a few central turns rather than rendering everything equally. Compare with details hidden and restored: overall volume should survive. Small colour variation must not create contradictory lighting for each row.

Unfold husks from the base, not empty space

Choose three simplified husks attached around the ear base: one turning lower left, one to the right and one behind. Give each a length direction and two boundaries, changing visible inner/outer surfaces as it curls instead of three equal green ribbons. Let husks cover some base without hiding every traceable connection. If they resemble decorative strips beside the corn, establish attachment and basal convergence rather than adding leaves. Set overlaps first and add restrained lengthwise veins inside each husk, not across background or neighbouring leaves. Three is a design choice, not a real specimen count.

Use silk for one restrained soft rhythm

Add a little yellow-brown silk near the tip or between husks, establishing one grouped gesture and then varying fine-line lengths rather than extending every strand to the canvas edge. Keep it finer and quieter than kernel rows, attached plausibly rather than emerging randomly from the sides. If metallic wire, lower contrast and crossings. If invisible against the background, retain only one or two distinguishable sections. Silk is optional; ear and husks must remain complete without it. Repair an untapered tip instead of hiding it under abundant hair, then decide whether texture helps.

Check wrapping in a simplified half-ear version

In a copy, retain a few lower/middle rows and two husks, hiding silk, and inspect compression and basal attachment. Compare a separate sketch with equally wide square side kernels to see the flattening, then recheck the outer two rows. Change width alone, not lighting or palette. If the original remains unclear, remove some nearby gap lines and recover broad values instead of increasing kernel count. This is manual surface comparison without plant recognition, automatic unwrapping or crop-variety data validating illustration proportions.

Inspect kernel gaps and husk roots after export

Save the layered original and inspect tip, side kernels and husk roots in the PNG at display size. Fade or remove some gaps if they become a black mesh rather than whitening every yellow cell. Repair local overlap if a root merges into shadow and appears detached; do not hide it with extra leaves. Keep a silk-free preview. A new tilt requires redistributing visible rows and husks instead of stretching the old grid. Record a precise location, such as the second right row failing to narrow above. Clear wrapping and attachment matter more than equal detail on every kernel.
