A miniature section displays both a top scene and underlying materials, which can easily end up facing different directions. Draw an invented grass model containing only one simplified tree, a short path and three material layers. Layer count and colour are compositional examples rather than geological or model-construction instructions. The aim is to make section and surface scene belong to the same object.

Establish the shallow block

Use an approximately 1800×1500 px canvas and establish a shallow block showing top and two sides, with a longer near edge and receding side edges. Align corners and connect top to bottom before grass or trees. Separate background, base, material layers and top scene. Test a solid-colour block small, as something that could be picked up. Add near thickness to a flat map or lower an excessively tall column to preserve the surface scene’s importance. Save the empty block; later layers must not change its original corners.

Distinguish the base from the material section

Add a narrow board-like base, slightly broader than the material block and exposing a small near lip rather than a giant separate tray. Group grass at the top, pale-brown fill centrally and dark-grey support below. These are invented model materials, not real geological ages. If base and bottom layer merge, make small adjustments to value contrast and edge thickness to distinguish them instead of thick black separators. All layers share the block. Colour aids identity without changing space.

Continue boundaries around both sides

A boundary crossing front and side must meet at one corner height rather than jumping to another layer. Wrap one boundary through the visible corner, then the next, checking thickness compression into depth. Mild irregularity is possible without losing continuity. Repair coloured-collage strips through corner alignment and less uniform width/brightness. Clarify one local value difference if layers merge instead of adding texture everywhere. A few continuous layers explain more than many disconnected ones; add the top scene afterward.

Connect the grass top to its section

Paint broad muted grey-green grass with a thin connected near edge rather than a suspended green sheet. Add grass tips to only selected near segments, simplifying the distance. Restrain an overly bright saturated top so the section stays visible. Keep optional terrain undulation modest rather than creating a hill hiding all layers. Top and side are two views of one model material; they must correspond beyond merely sharing similar green.

Keep the path on the top surface

Draw a pale curved path on the top, broader nearby and narrowing away, keeping all turns within that surface. At a section edge stop it clearly or show the cut surface of its material, not an unexplained tunnel through lower layers. Test without the tree. Reduce shadow and repair surface alignment if the path floats like a ribbon. It guides the eye rather than supplying real navigation or scale. Its width variation is perspective, and a fictional example needs no falsely precise distance.

Give the tree a definite ground contact

Use one simple tree whose trunk emerges from a definite top-surface location, slightly occluded by grass, with a few grey-green crown masses. It must not originate midway down the sidewall or on the base. Establish trunk and small top shadow before foliage. Shrink an overpowering tree rather than balance it with more props. Its silhouette and root contact matter more than individual leaves. Leave a small clear ground gap near the block edge to explain attachment.

Give side layers distinct texture scales

Use a few irregular marks in pale-brown fill, broader darks in the support layer and only one or two board-edge lines on the base. Do not stipple all layers equally. Turn texture with the side plane and stop it at boundaries. Compare layers without texture, then restore material cues. Reduce colours and grains in a patterned-cake result. Invented grains are not real soil analysis: the relevant accuracy is continuity of the parts within one illustrated object.

Unify lighting across top, sides and base

Under upper-left light, keep the top broadly lighter, one side in middle values and the other slightly deeper; tree and base follow the same direction. Different local material colours still share the plane turn. Use a grey copy when each layer seems independently illuminated instead of changing material hues again. Ground shadow belongs beneath the base, softening rightward, while tree shadow belongs on the top rather than extending into air from a side. Distinct receiving surfaces unify model and scene.

Label only what is actually shown

Optional short neutral labels such as grass, fill and base should point clearly to visible layers rather than use geological jargon to seem precise. Keep them separate from tree and path and test the longest label at normal size. Omit words if the drawing already reads. Move tangled labels rather than repeatedly thickening leaders. Check visible layer count against label count; a three-layer picture cannot be described as four. Information agreement and perspective each need their own verification.

Keep the empty block and vary the section view

Keep empty block, untextured section and PNG with separate top scenery. Next, turn the model toward another side and rebuild corners, layer joins, path compression and tree shadow while materials and layer count remain fixed. Flipping labels and path is not a new view; exposed near/far planes change. At use size preserve principal boundaries and root contact while omitting grains if needed. Record the corner most prone to a height mismatch and begin there next time. One coherent section and scene matter more than extra miniature objects.
