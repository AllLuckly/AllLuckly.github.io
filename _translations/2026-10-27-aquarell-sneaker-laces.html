Laces are complex, but they belong on an established shoe. Draw a three-quarter-view sneaker from its wedge-shaped sole, rounded toe and tongue before weaving laces in groups. Use brushes, colours and layers rather than an automatic line drawing or shoe model.

Let the sole establish direction

On an approximately 1700×1300 px landscape canvas, point the toe forward left and heel rear right. Build a shallow tapered sole with a rounded toe, longer near edge and narrower far side. Separate background, shoe, laces and details. Correct a brick-like silhouette before textures, leaving room around it and for a shadow.

Separate toe and tongue

Raise the toe into a broad curve leading to the higher instep and tongue. Light the toe and turn the near side darker. Let the side panels overlap the tongue edges instead of leaving it floating. Save a lace-free stage and establish a believable foot opening before proceeding.

Place paired eyelets along the instep

Arrange four or five eyelets on each side of the tongue, shrinking and tightening into the distance. Embed them in the upper with tiny dark centres and restrained lit edges. Check paired positions around one tongue and correct them before lacing; do not bend laces to accommodate misplaced holes.

Give every lace segment an origin and destination

Start with one crossed pair, painting narrow bands rather than single lines. Decide the upper segment and interrupt or shade the lower one. Run each band into an eyelet. Resolve two groups before simplifying the rest. The lacing need not reproduce every real knot, but its neighbouring paths must remain traceable.

Add local shadows beneath the laces

Place short narrow shadows where laces cross the tongue, following their bends. Avoid equal black outlines on both sides. Use off-white bands with only selected brighter turns. Add slight thickness if they read as painted lines; narrow them and soften darks if they resemble heavy ropes.

Distinguish materials through mark scale

Contrast a smooth toe with a small patch of directional woven texture on the side. Simplify the distance and use broader sole planes with a few grooves. Keep texture subordinate to lighting. Optional decoration can be an invented simple shape, added after structure without obscuring eyelets or laces.

Check grounding and the foot opening

Keep the opening dark but retain an inner heel plane. Ground the lowest sole with a narrow contact and a rearward cast shadow. Check opening and proportions normally and small, repairing stray eyelet lines close up. Correct contact or opening geometry locally rather than darkening the entire picture.

Resolve one lace knot clearly

If adding a knot, study two broad bands separately: one loop and one underpassing wrap. Establish upper and lower crossings before simplifying the shoe version to a loop and tail. Avoid unexplained floating white segments or a knot merged into the foot opening; this is visual structure rather than a precise tying lesson.

Trace an overlap sequence

Trace the first two lace groups segment by segment, naming what each overlaps and what hides it. Repair one impossible crossing in a saved copy. Then examine the gaps, which should compress along the instep. Negative spaces expose placement errors more clearly than repeatedly tracing fine bands.

Save the unlaced stage and vary the view

Keep shoe-only, laced and finished versions, checking the PNG for broken fine bands. Next, turn the toe farther toward the viewer and rebuild sole, eyelets and tongue before the same lace groups. Compare their compression without merely stretching the finished picture into a supposed new view.
