A poem’s I may be a created speaker, while you may address a person, reader or abstract object. Treating first person as autobiography skips evidence that needs checking. Select a short poem in Book Isle and record pronouns and speaking positions individually. People make these judgements; this assumes no pronoun recognition, generated interpretation or automatic reconstruction of the author’s life in the app.

Preserve the poem’s scope and edition

Choose a short poem you may read and record title, poet, translator, edition, stanzas and necessary page position. Read the whole poem, not one sentence from social media. Note whether an epigraph, role label or quotation frame identifies a speaker, without treating every editorial introduction as the poem’s voice. For an unselectable scan, record image page and stanza-opening words manually without claiming a text layer. Create five external fields: wording location, pronoun, speaking layer, reference candidates and evidence. This task does not alter line layout or dictate pauses from punctuation and line breaks; it concerns reference.

List pronouns before assigning names

On the first pass, record I, you, he, she, it, we and other pronouns by stanza and short phrase. Preserve singular/plural and distinctions absent from the text. If a Chinese gender distinction reflects translation, identify the translation you read rather than claiming an original-language check. Mark an omitted subject as omitted; do not automatically supply the nearest earlier name. Pronoun occurrences are not character counts: one person can recur and several people can use the same person. An accurate inventory before candidate assignment helps prevent collapsing every pronoun into one role after reading the ending.

Separate current speech from quoted speech

Use this invented example: the speaker writes I heard her say, I will return. If the typography clearly makes the latter phrase her quotation, the first I and quoted I may differ. Record quotation boundaries, speech verbs and speaking layers separately instead of merging identical pronouns. Without quotation marks in a real poem, inspect grammar and context while retaining alternatives if evidence is insufficient. This teaching sentence is not attributed to a poet. An I in a translator’s note or title also needs its region labelled; not every word on the page belongs to one speaking layer.

Require context for the addressee

Inspect nearby address terms, places, actions and objects for each you, listing possible addressees and evidence. You may address a person, the self or an apostrophized object; being read does not automatically make it address the reader. In the separate invented phrase you, quiet city, the address supplies city as a candidate. It does not make every you refer to a city. A changed address in the next stanza warrants another reference state rather than forcing one addressee throughout. Leave uncertainty instead of inserting the most familiar name from the poet’s biography.

Do not automatically expand we or they

For we, ask whether it includes the addressed you, whether it names a limited group and whether the poem specifies membership. We does not inherently mean all readers, and a crowd image does not establish one shared voice for the whole group. Separate known members, possible members and unknown scope rather than deriving a social statistic from a plural pronoun. Check they against context-supported referents, not merely the nearest noun. Deliberate ambiguity can remain a reading finding; every occurrence need not map uniquely. A literary reference note is not a population register.

Keep author information as a separate evidential layer

If an introduction or reliable author source connects the speaker with life experience, record provenance, scope and the lines involved. A connection does not establish that every I is the poet or that events occurred literally in real chronological order. Finish the internal reference record first, placing external material alongside as supporting interpretation rather than overriding a visible role change. Attribute translator, editor and reader interpretations separately so a later note does not retain one unproven identity. Biography research is optional: the text alone can support a bounded record of voices.

Return to the smallest relevant phrase when references conflict

If you assign one I to two people, check quotation, stanza or role boundaries before merging names. If you seems to shift, inspect changed address terms and actions. Mark unread pronouns rather than supplying them by intuition. Search locates matching words; it cannot establish shared reference, and missing hits require checking searchability. Revise personal notes, not the poem. Retain first candidates and reasons for changes so you can distinguish narrowing from new evidence from merely tidying an untidy table.

Explain each speaking relation after returning

Leave the reader and return. Locate three important pronouns using poem title, stanza-opening words and phrases. State the current speaker, addressee and reference candidates, then identify evidence. Three checks are a sample, not a unique interpretation of the whole poem. Audit whether the summary turned an uncertain candidate into the real poet’s name or expanded we into everyone. Save important notes through an available method and reopen them to inspect the five fields, keeping the collection original separately. The result is a traceable reference record distinguishing textual facts, interpretations and unresolved points, not a certified standard answer.
