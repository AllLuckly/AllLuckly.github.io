A word occurring three times need not end three clauses. Fix boundaries first. Read an original door-side passage in Book Isle, preserving common endings and different preceding text, then move one ending in a control. This is formal/contextual reading, not automatic rhetoric annotation or a conclusion from whole-book search counts.

Save the complete original three-clause material

Card E1 reads:“I left the note by the door; he left the coat by the door; she left the lamp by the door.” The note, coat and lamp are left by the door by three people. Semicolons delimit three coordinate clauses and the period closes the sentence; optional layout breaks do not change syntax. Import textual PDF/EPUB with E1 and punctuation intact. A narrow screen’s wrapping creates no authorial clause. These sentences are original exercises, not attributed quotations.

Extract three terminal words excluding punctuation

Remove terminal punctuation and extract each clause’s last three words: all are“by the door.” Pair full clauses with extracted endings rather than searching door alone. Declare any case/punctuation normalization in advance; different prepositions cannot silently be merged. Initial I/he/she differ without defeating repeated endings. Check a missing the against the source instead of replacing word identity with approximate meaning. The conclusion concerns these three declared clauses.

Left also occurs three times in E1 but not terminally. Counting repeated words alone conflates structures. Last-word door and last-three-word by the door both repeat here; state the unit rather than extracting one word from one clause and three from another.

Describe the formal ending before interpreting its function

A sequence of clauses or lines ending in the same words can be epistrophe. Establish that form before interpreting how one place gathers different objects. A supported candidate is that changing note/coat/lamp against an unchanged doorway returns attention there. No rule makes every repeated ending angry, ironic or sad, and the actions do not reveal whom anyone awaits. Separate formal evidence from bounded emotional interpretation.

Check beginning repetition in a separate control

For E2 use:“By the door, I waited; by the door, he listened; by the door, she wrote.” The common phrase begins clauses, while waited/listened/wrote end them. Do not keep calling a fronted phrase terminal or collapse both positions into one count. E2 is a beginning-repetition control without invalidating E1. Real passages can repeat at both ends; document each scope rather than forcing one label per passage.

Move only the second occurrence to expose search limits

Copy E1 to E3 and change only clause two to“he left the coat by the door at dawn.” The full passage still contains three“by the door” occurrences, but the second continues with at dawn and no longer shares the same final three words. Two other endings can still correspond; state the reduced scope instead of claiming all repetition vanished. The change concerns position, not door count. Do not delete at dawn to fit a desired count; return from search snippets to whole clauses.

E3’s second last-three-word group is door at dawn, not by the door. Extract it to check whether reading stopped at a familiar phrase. Translation can move the phrase inside a clause; recheck position instead of assuming semantic equivalence preserves the formal pattern.

Link highlighted endings back to actors and actions

In a supported textual format highlight endings and note E1’s three clauses/objects. If only whole-passage selection is practical, excerpt it and mark endings externally without inventing a rhetoric panel. Return by E1/E2/E3 identity rather than treating controls as revisions of one source. Punctuation and syntax still define boundaries after layout changes; highlights do not. Keep source wording distinct from your extraction table, including for this original exercise.

Diagnose synonym merging and truncated views

An ending near the door may be similar in meaning but differs under this word-identity test. Do not erase that through translation. A page break after by the door may hide at dawn on the next page; reconstruct the sentence first. Semicolons, commas and periods mark different levels, and complex clauses need syntax rather than a cut at every comma. Correct either extraction rule or text at a time, preserving the former judgment/cause instead of copying three search hits as three endings.

Reopen and deliver location evidence and interpretation separately

Reopen E1 and re-extract three endings without the old table, then compare E2’s beginnings and E3’s continuation. Explain why three occurrences need not be three endings and semantic similarity need not be word identity. Give one interpretation bounded by the wording. Restore punctuation/position and redefine units after changed translation or authorial lineation. This establishes local structure and interpretation, not automatic detection, certain author psychology or one emotional effect for all repetition.
