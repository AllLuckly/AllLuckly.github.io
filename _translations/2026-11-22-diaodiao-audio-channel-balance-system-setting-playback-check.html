Stereo music that seems louder on one side may reflect the recording, headphones, system balance or the selected output. 调调 plays local audio. This exercise separates those conditions and checks whether a permitted test recording is audible in the intended channel on your current device. The balance control discussed here belongs to the operating system, not to 调调.

Choose an identifiable stereo recording

Prepare a short recording you made or may use, ideally with a left-only cue followed by a right-only cue. Confirm its channel contents beforehand in an external audio tool or a reliable player. A normal stereo song does not necessarily identify its instrument positions precisely and cannot be treated as a rigorous channel test. Preserve the original and do not overwrite your library. Note its name, format, duration and each cue position. The public listing names MP3, M4A, AAC, WAV, AIFF, CAF and FLAC among supported formats, but import also depends on whether the system can read the audio track.

Record the device and output

In a quiet place, lower the volume and select headphones marked left and right that you can safely use. Record whether they are wired or Bluetooth and which output the system has selected. Wear them correctly and do not conduct the experiment while attention to traffic is needed. Make three columns: condition, where the left cue was heard, and where the right cue was heard. A single earbud, mono device or hearing condition may prevent a valid left/right distinction; record the limitation and choose suitable equipment or ask someone able to distinguish channels to help. Do not raise the volume excessively.

Record the original system settings

Open Settings on iPhone or iPad and find the audio and visual accessibility settings. Labels may differ by OS version. Record whether mono audio is enabled and the original position of the left/right balance slider. Mono can combine the channels, so it prevents this independent-channel comparison; an off-center balance may make one side hard to hear at a low volume. These are device-wide settings, not settings limited to 调调. Recording the starting values lets you restore them later.

Establish a reversible listening baseline

Only with the user’s permission and if the setting can be restored, temporarily center the system balance and turn mono audio off; keep the other conditions fixed. If your interface or accessibility needs make that inappropriate, leave the settings alone and mark the independent-channel baseline as unavailable. Open 调调 and find the already imported test track in the visible library. If necessary, import your own recording through the file picker, identify its name and duration and recheck the volume before playing.

Play from the start and note the ear in which each cue is heard and the selected output. An output icon is not proof of where sound is actually audible: listen. If each cue is heard on its intended side, the observation applies only to this file, these settings and this output at that time; it does not establish that every file or device is correct.

Change balance once and compare

Keep the file, headphones, volume and mono state fixed. Pause playback, move the system balance slightly left, return to 调调 and replay both cues. Record the change in each side; there is no need to move the slider to its extreme. This does not require an in-app balance control. Return the slider to the center and repeat the same segment, noting whether the right side returns. A single changed condition helps identify a contribution from system balance, but does not by itself rule out a connection issue or the recording.

If you also want to examine mono audio, enable it in a separate round, record the effect and restore the original setting. Pause and lower the volume before replaying. Do not combine its change with the balance adjustment. Results depend on the system, device and output, and accessories need not behave identically.

Troubleshoot one layer at a time

If a cue is always missing, confirm that the selected item is the prepared test recording and that you listened at the correct time. Check the actual system output, headphone orientation and connection, mono setting and balance. If needed, try another known-working pair at a safe volume and record it as a new round. If another player has the same issue with the same original, investigate file or system conditions. If the difference appears only in 调调, record the installed version, format, reproducible steps and a privacy-safe result for app support. Do not start by deleting the library, reinstalling or changing server settings.

Deliver and restore

Record the test-file identity, original system settings, output device, cue positions, changed condition and actual result in each round, and any remaining uncertainty. Restore mono and balance to their recorded starting values and confirm the normal output and volume are restored. Keep the original file regardless of the result, and omit private playlists and credentials from the record. This exercise is based on public information; no physical-device or headphone test is claimed.
