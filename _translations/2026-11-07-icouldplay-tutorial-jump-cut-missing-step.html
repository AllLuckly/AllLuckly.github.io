A hand or object may jump because an editor omitted a process. iCouldPlay can help reread the existing video, not recover absent steps. Use paper-card material or an authorised course with a clear edit to record visible states, unseen actions and questions instead of assuming the player dropped images.

Choose a harmless example

Choose cards, paper folding or stationery, avoiding medical work, live electrical repair and other specialist procedures. With an existing editor, compose self-recorded flat-card and folded-card shots into one sample file; otherwise use permitted existing material without installing software. Preserve file, duration and author notes, checking any declared omission. The task identifies a presentation gap rather than teaching the omitted action.

Write the goal and necessary states

Write a goal such as understanding how the flat card becomes folded, naming required fold line, contact and final orientation. Do not invent a standard procedure. If the course promises a finished-object showcase rather than full instruction, preserve that scope. Judge gaps against what the segment promises and what this task needs, not against a rule that every edit is faulty.

Record the last visible state before the cut

Play from several seconds before the suspected cut. Record the last clear state and approximate time, such as a flat card, a marked upper-left corner and both hands not yet touching the card. Do not project a later crease backwards. Mark obscured details unseen rather than certainly absent. Preserve UI precision; ordinary pause is not frame-level evidence.

Record the first clear state after the cut

Continue to the first clear post-cut state and independently record shape, mark orientation, hands and background. The edit may omit more than one action or change viewpoint, sample or recording order. Record changes without explaining their unseen cause. A hidden corner mark does not prove a different card; keep identity uncertain rather than assuming continuous action.

Turn omissions into specific questions

Compare the logs and ask which edge folded first, when the marked face turned or whether flattening was needed. Questions are not reconstructed instructions. Record explicit narration and time, separating described-but-unshown actions from entirely missing information. For “repeat the previous action”, locate the earlier complete demonstration before assuming every intermediate change is covered.

Check that seeking did not skip the material

Play normally from earlier through the whole gap to exclude your seek skipping it. If continuous playback reveals the action, correct the note to incomplete seeking observation. A black image with ongoing sound needs separate playback diagnosis rather than an automatic editing explanation. Keep both conditions and results; this establishes edited presentation, not completeness of original camera files.

Check available contextual explanations

Inspect existing chapter labels, closing notes or accompanying author text when present for another complete demonstration. Without chapters, use your manual times rather than an invented app-generated step list. Identify supplemental material by title, version and location; content from another edition is not present in this file. Prepare specific author questions without automatically sending messages.

Keep an unresolved learning state

When a necessary action remains unseen and unexplained, mark that step unresolved. Stop at understanding the two visible endpoint states rather than acting out an imagined missing step. Even with harmless paper, the habit prevents treating an outcome as process evidence. Reaching the end does not establish understanding. Keep open questions externally instead of deleting them to mark completion or substituting watched status for learning verification.

Finish with before, after and question records

Finish with the file’s before and after states, continuous-playback check and missing information, bounded to this version. Restore position and preserve media without claiming frame restoration, automatic cut detection or course repair. Append later author evidence with its source rather than overwriting the original observation. This is an ordinary viewing workflow, not exhaustive device or forensic editing tests.
