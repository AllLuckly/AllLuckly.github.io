Similar circles in successive shots can form a graphic match without proving one object or continuous event. Use a paper plate and drawn paper sun to observe a transition in iCouldPlay, recording visual similarity separately from identity evidence. Optional editing belongs in a familiar external tool, not assumed app match-cut creation, tracking or recovery of missing action. Without footage, keep a paper exercise explicitly separate from playback verification.

Give two original shots separate identities

Shot A is a white paper plate against blue; B is a yellow sun drawn on grey paper, with its central circle approximately matching the plate’s size and position. Preserve drawings or original shots, not merely the edited second. With a familiar editor, place A at 0–3 seconds and B at 3–6 for a direct cut. Otherwise use permitted footage or a paper plan without installing unfamiliar software. These are different objects: the drawn sun is not footage of a celestial body or a hidden interpretation of an existing film.

Name the visible properties that match

Keep separate contour, position, size, colour and background fields rather than one resembles label. This example matches central circular shape and approximate position, not white/yellow colour or background. Inspect complete shots, not a blurred cut instant. Use relative locations such as slightly left of centre without invented pixel coordinates. Record cropping/scaling conditions instead of attributing crop-created similarity to the source. Retain matching and differing properties rather than selecting evidence for a predetermined conclusion.

Watch continuously and then inspect both anchors

Open the identified self-made/permitted file and play normally from a complete A shot through stable B. Record direct cut, dissolve or another transition rather than ignoring type because circles match. Then select readable content anchors on both sides, using actual displayed time precision without frame-accurate claims. Replay from earlier if seeking skipped the boundary; do not blame the edit for a viewer-created gap. A paper-only plan leaves playback untested: planned 0/3/6-second boundaries are not device observations.

Use separate evidence for object identity

The plate’s three-dimensional rim and the sun’s drawn rays/paper distinguish them. Matching centre, size or colour alone proves no shared identity; one object can also change silhouette with viewpoint. Separate visual-match and identity fields, leaving unknowns unknown. Do not infer a real plate-to-sun transformation or generate an animation as original evidence. Record any creator account with provenance while keeping it distinct from visible shots rather than treating your association as production fact.

Separate event continuity from graphic continuity

Graphic resemblance can smooth a cut or invite comparison without establishing shared time, place or causation. Event continuity needs additional scene, action, sound or creator evidence. If A shows a held plate and B a drawing alone, retain unseen action instead of inventing a handoff. Use your own observed/possible/missing-evidence labels without automatic film-analysis claims. Graphic matching need not be perfectly seamless, and a small size difference is not automatically a production error; state evaluation scope.

Change one viewing condition per comparison

If actual fit/fill controls exist, record the original and change one setting, checking plate rim and sun rays for cropping. Better apparent alignment may be changed visible extent, not rematched source. Keep orientation, media and speed fixed. Temporarily disable selectable captions covering an edge and restore them; separately note burned-in text. Without controls, retain the original mode rather than invent pixel-alignment/tracking buttons. These checks clarify visibility, not creator intention or actual identity.

Keep a counterexample as a separate version

On paper or in your own copy, move B’s sun right with everything else fixed: circular similarity remains while positional matching weakens. Do not rewrite an altered source as original evidence or change colour, size and transition together. This identifies your comparison property, not certain authorial intent. Distinguish files by name/duration and confirm what iCouldPlay opened. If no copy is made, label a hypothetical exercise rather than a test. Preserve source, plan and observations separately; a planned still is not playback evidence.

Reopen and finish the property/identity record

Reopen the same version and play between A/B anchors, verifying contour, position, distinct identities and unresolved event relations. Restore ordinary display/captions, preserve source and first notes, and append correction reasons. Finish by explaining resemblance and its limits, not declaring one object. No device frame-level acceptance or effect-production test is claimed, nor automatic graphic-match detection. Terminology supports observation; a new film needs new anchors/evidence rather than one paper-example interpretation for every cut.
