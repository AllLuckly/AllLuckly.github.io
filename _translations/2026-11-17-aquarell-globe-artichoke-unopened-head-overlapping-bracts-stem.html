Draw an oblique unopened globe-artichoke head, establishing its full volume before wrapping overlapping bracts around it. Visible firm green bracts are not open petals. Keep one unopened state without adding purple flowers or a cutaway. Build depth through overlap and turning surfaces rather than mechanical scale duplication.

Set the unopened state and stalk axis

Use an approximately 1500×1800 px canvas, sketching a slightly oval head with a short stalk below and an axis tilted rightward. Gather the top and connect the base to the stalk rather than balance a loose leaf ball on a stick. Leave background around outer bracts, without cookware or cutting tools. Separate sketch, base colour, bracts and shadow; save native work. Pixels and shape are compositional, not food measurements. Keep an intact unopened exterior without exposed edible internals or a contradictory full purple flower.

Wrap bracts around the head rather than radiate from its tip

Begin with larger outer bracts, broad bases below and tips wrapping upward, not all sprouting from one top point. Make front bracts broader and side bracts foreshortened, with far tips partially visible. Sketch three wrapping row guides and stagger placements; these are layout aids, not a claim of exactly three botanical whorls. Bases may be hidden and tips overlap neighbours. Preserve readable occlusion rather than a flat tiled-paper pattern.

Distinguish bases and tips through staggered overlap

Temporarily number eight representative front bracts, identifying concealed base and visible tip for each; eight is not the full head count. A near bract may cover parts of two behind it. Retain the front contour and stop hidden lines instead of drawing complete isolated diamonds. Slightly lifted tips can have small shadows without making every point a spine. Check overlaps in monochrome before green-purple colour. Move guide numbers to a hidden layer rather than leave them on the finished vegetable.

Keep the apex gathered rather than flowering

Reduce visible bract size toward an apex of gathered small forms, without a complete purple flower emerging centrally. Unopened does not mean a regular screw thread; slight asymmetry can retain one full head. Repair a large dark central hole through near/far bract closure rather than call it the natural centre. Do not add a concentric cutaway merely to show the heart. Receptacle and floral interior remain concealed here; reference notes can explain them without forcing all anatomy into one exterior.

Connect one stalk continuously below

Extend one substantial short stalk below the head, matching its volume and near-side turn. Basal bracts may overlap its root, but do not give each a long petiole like a vegetable bunch. An oblique cut stalk end may have a small subdued ellipse without turning the whole head into a dissection. Place contact shadow consistently with the chosen resting pose and background. Establish support through composition rather than invent a pot or three roots to make it stand. Resolve placement before texture.

Show bract thickness through broad light and narrow seams

Use muted grey-green, lighter near and cooler dark far, with optional purple tips that identify no cultivar. Give bracts broad soft light areas and narrow overlap seams instead of an equal black network. Sparse longitudinal texture can narrow toward tips without a full leaf-vein tree. If it resembles a pine cone, inspect broad fleshy bracts, enclosure and roundness rather than merely recolour green. Keep eight representative forms distinguishable small; remove obstructive texture before altering correct overlaps.

Audit overlaps and exclude an accidental cutaway

Hide colour variation and fine texture, then audit eight labels for concealed bases and overlapping tips. If every bract is complete, select real occlusion edges; remove erroneous emergent flowers to restore the unopened state. Rejoin a divided stalk into one support. Preserve a failing copy and repair local lines rather than reverse whole layers and invert other overlaps. Three row guides and eight examples remain layout tools, not app-derived natural counts, plant age or food-quality conclusions.

Reopen and check gathered apex and layered volume

Save native work, hide labels/row guides and reopen a PNG. Read stalk support and fullness at fit-to-window size, then inspect bract tips, narrow seams and closed apex enlarged. If it becomes a patterned ball small, soften far edges and restore broad front surfaces rather than outline everything black. Reopen base-colour and bract layers separately. Document an intact unopened head, eight emphasized bracts and omitted internals. Leave unperformed actions pending; a static image proves neither cultivation, cooking or food safety nor automatic identification.
