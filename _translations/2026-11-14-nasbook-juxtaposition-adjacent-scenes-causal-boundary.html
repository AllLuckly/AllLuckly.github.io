Adjacent passages invite comparison without establishing simultaneous events or causation. Read original juxtaposed scenes in Book Isle, recording observations and interpretations separately and checking paired details. Do not turn a reader-supplied social relationship into an explicit textual fact.

Preserve both paragraphs and their scene boundaries

Create two original paragraphs: “Inside the window, a feast’s silver platters reflected the light; one plate of bread remained untouched. A server replaced the candles.” Then: “Outside the window, a quay worker broke a small piece of bread and left half on the paper. The last boat on the river reached shore.” Name the chapter “The Same Window” and import a readable copy while retaining the source. Copy both paragraphs, assigning platters/server indoors and worker/boat outdoors. Two bread fragments alone erase setting and action. Use external notes with opening anchors if selection is unavailable, without inventing hidden annotation controls.

Consult the definition without classifying all adjacency

The glossary in the official CCEA poetry guide describes purposeful proximity of contrasting things, people or ideas. Here identify indoors/outdoors and untouched plate/halved small piece before interpreting contrast. The source offers a framework, not proof that any consecutive paragraphs strongly contrast; they may simply continue events. Mark comparable details rather than applying the label solely because passages appear next to one another.

Pair details without inventing quantities

Record one untouched plate indoors versus one small piece halved outdoors. The comparable evidence is quantity wording and use, not measured mass. No plate weight, grams, guest count or daily food intake is given, so no ratio or claim that the half-piece is his only food follows. Silver platters versus paper show different serving surfaces, but prices and ownership remain unspecified. Pair original words before proposing resource-contrast effects; symbolic difference cannot become precise economic data or a full account of lives.

Keep spatial proximity separate from simultaneity

Indoors/outdoors around one window establish spatial relation but supply no clock times or meanwhile. Sequence may suggest simultaneity, yet record the time relation as unspecified rather than the same minute. “Last boat” refers to an undefined narrative scope and cannot establish midnight. Later chapter evidence may justify an updated record with its location, but do not rewrite later confirmation as something already known on first reading. Preserve the evidence boundary across reading stages.

Separate contrast effects from causal claims

Propose effects such as contrast in resource use or differing rhythms in one place. Support each with words: untouched/halved for the former, candle replacement/boat arrival for the latter. Juxtaposition alone does not establish that the feast causes hunger; no employment, exchange or distribution chain is stated. Nor is the worker’s reason for leaving half known: for himself, another person or another reason remain possibilities. Alternatives must be constrained by details, while unstated motives stay blank rather than being completed by notes.

Reverse paragraph order to compare effects

Duplicate the text and reverse only paragraph order, keeping wording and within-paragraph sequence. Read worker before feast and record where your attention goes first. Events have not changed; information arrival order has. This does not prove original authorial intent. Separate rewritten controls can add meanwhile, introducing simultaneity, or therefore, requiring causation. Label them rewrites rather than returning them to the source as evidence. Change one condition per comparison and record order, temporal linkage and causal linkage separately.

Troubleshoot merged paragraphs and overstrong labels

If import removes blank lines and merges paragraphs, consult the original boundary and record the formatting change. Juxtaposition does not depend only on whitespace, but boundaries affect attention. A note titled “The feast deprived the worker of food” needs agent, action and causal evidence; without them revise it to a contrast candidate. A Book Isle tag organises notes rather than certifying a claim. Excerpt cards should preserve both sides and unspecified time/motive instead of compressing everything into an unsupported social conclusion without recovery clues.

Reopen and list facts before interpretations

Save notes, leave and reopen using the indoor/outdoor opening anchors. Without old interpretations list facts, two paired details and unspecified time/motive. Then add resource contrast and life-rhythm candidates, tracing each to words and checking no direct causal claim slipped in. The reversed copy changes reading order while retaining event wording; connective rewrites remain labelled. Another reader should reproduce comparison and its limits, without inferring automatic author-intent discovery or causal proof. Use anchors after font changes rather than requiring fixed screen pages.
