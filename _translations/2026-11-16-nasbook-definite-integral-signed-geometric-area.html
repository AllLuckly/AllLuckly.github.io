Below-axis geometric area remains nonnegative, while a definite integral includes negative contribution. Use f(x)=x on[−1, 2], split the two triangles and check signed integral versus total area in Book Isle. Keep two external ledgers and return to symbols/diagrams by page. Seeing an integral sign is not symbolic integration or automatic graph analysis by the app.

Prepare one coordinate system and interval

On card I1 draw y=x with ticks−1, 0, 2 and points(−1,−1),(0, 0),(2, 2). Declare invented axis units and direction from−1 to 2. Shade the left below-axis and right above-axis triangles differently but retain readable labels. Import a clear PDF/EPUB and verify I1/endpoints. If zoom shows only the right portion, restore the full graph instead of declaring the whole interval positive. Compute from premises rather than screen pixels.

First calculate two nonnegative geometric areas

The triangle on[−1, 0] has base/height 1 and area 1/2; on[0, 2] base/height 2 give area 2. Use nonnegative perpendicular distances in the geometric-area column, not−1/2. Record negative/positive function signs separately for signed contribution. A negative sign must identify its ledger. Right area one suggests a missing factor 2 or a sloping side mistaken for perpendicular height. The pieces share only the origin boundary and have no overlap to subtract.

Assign integral contributions by function sign

With lower limit smaller than upper, the left contributes−1/2 and right+2, totalling 3/2=1.5. An integral may be positive, negative or zero without negative physical material. Adding both areas positively gives 2.5, not this signed integral; interval length three is not the answer either. Record left contribution, right contribution and total with graph/paragraph references rather than clipping only 1.5 and losing sign provenance.

Use three width-one bins[−1, 0],[0, 1],[1, 2]. Midpoint values−0.5, 0.5, 1.5 sum to 1.5. Exact midpoint agreement here belongs to the line, not arbitrary curves. Left-endpoint sum is 0 and right-endpoint sum 3, showing finite sums depend on sample position rather than deciding the integral arbitrarily.

Use absolute value for total area separately

For total geometric area integrate the absolute value of f or add 1/2+2=5/2=2.5. Taking the absolute value of the completed signed result 1.5 still gives 1.5 and is not equivalent. Label total ledger T and signed ledger S with their own questions. Reflecting the left segment above the axis illustrates the absolute value of f, another function; do not alter the original and claim x is positive at negative input.

Cross-check independently by antiderivative endpoints

Use antiderivative F(x)=x²/2: F(2)=2 and F(−1)=1/2, giving upper minus lower 3/2. Negative input does not make its square negative. For total area split at zero and handle the absolute value of f, rather than erasing an endpoint sign. Reversing limits from 2 to−1 gives−3/2, while geometric total remains nonnegative. Limit orientation and function sign are distinct sources of signs and need separate records.

On[−2,−1] the same function lies wholly below the axis:F(−1)−F(−2)=0.5−2=−1.5, while total area is 1.5. Although the signed magnitude matches I1, retain the different interval in the heading. This tests mistaken nonnegative-area rules applied to integrals without making geometric area negative.

Use a zero-integral control to reject zero-area inference

For the same function on[−1, 1], two areas 1/2 cancel to signed zero while total area is one. Thus zero integral does not imply no region, without claiming every zero-integral function has these triangles. Keep control Z1 separate from I1; do not copy its total one into[−1, 2]. Highlight the inference and its counterexample conditions together where supported, or record them externally without assumed OCR/formula understanding.

Diagnose reversed fractions and mixed units

Chinese二分之三 means 3/2, not 2/3; showing 1.5 exposes reversal. A signed 2.5 may have lost the below-axis minus, while total 1.5 may come from taking absolute value too late. Two length axes yield square units; other axis quantities require their own product-unit interpretation, not square metres for every integral. This exercise uses invented units and no physical measurements from the reader interface.
