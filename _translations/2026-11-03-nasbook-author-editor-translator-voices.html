A book’s body, footers and chapter-end notes may contain the author’s words, editorial additions and translator commentary. Locating a note is only part of the task: attribution matters when citing it. Verify voices within one edition, rather than comparing annotated and unannotated editions or treating a particular footnote style as proof of authorship.

Read the edition’s annotation guidance

Record author, translator, editor and edition and inspect editorial preliminaries or annotation rules. Do not infer authorship from stars, numbers or brackets when no rule is stated. Compare the current file with a reliable original where conversion changes layout. Distinguish historical-edition credits from the current translator. Choose body text, a signed note and an uncertain note as samples before attempting broad classification.

Verify the body-to-note connection

Follow a supported note link or locate it manually by page. Check chapter, number, referenced phrase and return path because numbering can restart. Correct linkage and correct attribution are separate checks. A bad jump calls for file/version inspection rather than a claim that the note changed its argument. Retain the body phrase and note opening instead of relying on an isolated superscript.

Record the contributor from explicit evidence

Use signatures, translator initials and the edition’s rules to identify a contributor and state the evidence. First-person wording alone does not prove the original author. Distinguish the person supplying a note from a scholar quoted inside it. Ordinary notes can hold role, evidence and location without metadata fields. Preserve unresolved attribution explicitly rather than inventing a name to complete the table.

Identify the note’s function before its claim

Describe whether the note explains terminology, identifies an edition, adds evidence, corrects a fact or offers another interpretation. Function limits its evidentiary use but does not itself identify authorship. A translator’s explanation is not automatically the author’s endorsement, and editorial disagreement does not mean the body was rewritten. Compare the linked passage and retain genuine tension. Keep your judgement separate rather than combining the voices into an unattributed unified claim.

State the role when quoting

Before quoting identify body text, editorial note or translator note, then record edition, chapter and role. Attribute both separately when used together; do not append supplementary wording to fabricate an author sentence. Preserve qualifications and keep personal analysis outside quotation marks. Formal citation style can be checked for its intended context. This workflow establishes source layers, not a universal citation standard; first make the same edition and passage recoverable.

Test references to both source layers

Retain usable references to both body and note and test numbering, phrases and attribution. Screen pagination or front-matter offsets make a bare number incomplete. Add an accompanying attribution record if note export omits it and reopen the output. Preserve original books separately from readable notes and sync records. Both working anchors support later reinterpretation through a verifiable textual relationship.

Retain conflicts and uncertainty

If preliminaries and a signature disagree, preserve the conflict and consult a reliable same-edition source rather than deciding on the publisher’s behalf. Possible exceptions, historical quotations or lost conversion markers remain hypotheses to check. Do not import another edition’s rules. Date corrections and their evidence. No source editing or automatic attribution model is needed to prevent an unresolved identity from becoming a false citation.

Acceptance and common confusions

Finish with verified body/note correspondence, evidence for at least one contributor, explicit unknowns and separate interpretation. Correct numbering does not prove authorship, nor does first person. Extend the tested method within the edition and recheck exceptions instead of universalising it to every book. The useful result is knowing who supplied a passage, where the evidence lies and what remains unconfirmed.
