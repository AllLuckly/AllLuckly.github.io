A bearded iris has a layered centre that easily gains extra petals when every purple shape is counted alike. Draw a simplified garden bearded iris in Aquarell, separating three outer falls and three inner standards before suggesting central style arms and beards on falls. This does not cover every iris species or unusual doubled forms.

Reserve directions for the layered groups

Use an approximately 1600×1800 px portrait canvas with the flower above and visible stalk below. Mark the base and three outward-then-downward fall paths, one frontal and two lateral, plus three upward standard paths. Count three plus three before patterns or beards. Differentiate their poses if they become six flat radial petals. Enlarge a cramped subject instead of reducing a group to two. These are view choices, not equal-angle projections or an assumption that all rear organs are visible.

Turn each fall from a narrow haft into a lower blade

Build three falls with narrow basal hafts, expanding outward and turning down into broad blades. Describe turns with tone rather than a black cut. Keep the front fall fuller and compress lateral ones through perspective and overlap. Repair basal support if they hang on strings; add broadened turning surfaces if they look like ribbons. Keep contours simple before ruffles, which cannot hide a broken base. Beards and bright patches on falls are not extra petals.

Raise standards around the centre

Raise three standards from the basal region, upright or arched, with layered central space instead of a solid purple ball. Stop occluded contours and use visible tips, not transparency, to indicate rear parts. Separate edges and tones if they form a closed cap. Restore the chosen raised pose if all droop below falls. Related colours are fine, but direction and attachment must work without labels. Observe unusual cultivars separately instead of forcing this simplified form onto them.

Keep style arms distinct from extra tepals

Suggest style arms as smaller, paler central structures above fall regions, clearly subordinate to the large tepals. Clarify the front group and abbreviate obscured ones without exposing every reproductive organ. Reduce oversized central “petals” and restore their central position. Do not call all pale structures stamens or turn style arms into long needles. This identifies major visible categories rather than a full dissection; omit uncertain hidden details and retain the simplification note.

Place each beard along a short central fall region

Place short dense strokes along a visible fall’s central basal region, following its turn, over a warm yellow base. Beards are not long stamens projecting from the centre or hairs on every standard. Abbreviate hidden side beards rather than drawing through foreground parts. Shorten and break up rope-like edges; reduce a separate yellow-petal shape and attach it to the fall surface. Beard colour, length and form are choices for this bearded example, not universal iris traits.

Support the whole flower at its base

Connect the stalk continuously below the flower base, with optional restrained green enclosure, never from a fall tip. Separate the lowest fall and stalk tonally so the stem does not become a crack through a petal. Use narrow stalk shade and softer floral shadows with open ground around the three-plus-three silhouette. Adjust composition if top-heavy instead of inventing braces or clips. Leaves may be omitted; adding an entire rhizome, leaf fan and inflorescence would obscure this single-flower task.

Hide ruffles and recount the two groups

Hide beards, texture and folds and trace the visible parts of three falls and three standards from the base. Occlusion must explain hidden parts without transparency. Inspect style arms and folded edges if seven or eight tepals appear; adjust the invented view if only five can be understood. Restore detail and verify beards on fall regions and smaller central style arms. Equal-sector symbols and numbers cannot replace form. Repair one ownership issue at a time before more texture.

Reopen and count categories, not purple shapes

Save native work, hide group axes and labels, export PNG and reopen. Identify raised standards, spreading falls and stalk when small, then style arms and surface beards enlarged. Reduce overlapping dark lines and restore size hierarchy if the centre becomes a many-petalled mass, rather than erasing a hole. Reopen editable groups and note a simplified garden bearded single flower, not all cultivars. Success concerns ownership of the two threes and associated structures, not cultivar identification, pollination, microscopic dissection or a claim of automatic organ identification.
