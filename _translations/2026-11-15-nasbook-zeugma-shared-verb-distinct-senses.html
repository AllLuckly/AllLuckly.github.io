A verb governing two objects does not always create the same witty effect. Read an original English sentence in Book Isle, map its syntax and then check how the shared word applies to each object. BYU uses zeugma broadly and treats syllepsis more specifically; retain that distinction. The reader locates evidence without automatic classification or inferred comic intentions.

Fix an original sentence and context

Use original A Mira lost her ticket and her patience., with context that her ticket cannot be found and the train remains delayed. Preserve English, context and location; a Chinese gloss does not replace the original syntax. This is no attributed quotation and does not imply permanent ticket loss. External arrows suffice if highlighting is unavailable, without hidden grammar tools. Preserve A before creating controls.

Map one lost to both objects

Find subject Mira and one lost governing coordinated objects her ticket and her patience. The verb occurs once, not once in each of two assembled excerpts. Broad zeugma concerns this shared governance without necessarily requiring semantic disparity. Record object boundaries and and; repeated her is not the governing word. Use the full sentence rather than split it into separate clauses at screen wrapping.

Check physical loss versus loss of patience

With ticket, lost means the object cannot be found; with patience, it means depleted composure, not a physical item named Patience left on a platform. Shared syntax with differing semantic applications fits the narrower discussion in BYU’s syllepsis entry. Other teaching traditions may use different labels, which should be declared. Context supports this reading; concrete-plus-abstract objects alone do not automatically establish every such case.

Split the wording to inspect shared structure

B is Mira lost her ticket, and Mira lost her patience.. Both subject-predicate units now appear, with similar events but no single occurrence directly governing both objects. The change repeats subject/verb, not objects, and does not erase events. Semantic contrast or humour may remain, so structural change does not reduce every effect to zero. Keep identifiers separate and count occurrences in the sentence alone, not explanatory prose.

Keep broad scope in a same-sense control

C is Mira lost her ticket and her glove.. One lost still governs two objects, both physical losses in context. It may fit broad governance while lacking A’s obvious sense shift, so do not label it absolutely non-zeugma. Separate shared syntax from changed meaning instead of inventing an abstract glove to fit a label. Only the second object changes; identify preserved and altered conclusions without ranking writing quality.

Separate word ambiguity from reader effects

Wordplay may activate multiple senses of a form; this task additionally traces its governed constituents. Merely noting polysemy is incomplete. Descriptions can overlap rather than require one exclusive label. A may compress a practical problem and emotional response, but humour is reader/context dependent. The background does not say lost tickets caused the delay or uniquely caused impatience; coordinated objects do not establish a full causal chain.

Check split translations and object boundaries

A Chinese gloss repeating verbs cannot establish two lost occurrences in English A. Nor may B’s second verb be deleted to manufacture A. Wrapping preserves governance, while missing punctuation calls for source checking. Patience as a character name would require reassessment. Share original sentences, context and arrows rather than a whole private work. No unperformed grammar tool receives credit; uncertainties remain candidates.

Reopen and audit syntax and sense separately

Reopen A/B/C and count lost in each sentence, identify objects and explain same/different applications without labels. Retain English, gloss identity, broad/narrow conventions, object control and effect limits rather than only “wordplay”. Reanalyse changed translations/context instead of importing old notes. Bookmarks locate sources; another reader should verify the shared word and meanings without automatic scoring or a uniquely proven authorial intention.
