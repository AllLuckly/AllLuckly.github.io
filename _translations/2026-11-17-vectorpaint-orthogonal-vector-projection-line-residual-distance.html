Project p=(3,1) onto the line through the origin with direction u=(1,2). Draw q and residual p−q separately, proving the nearest point algebraically rather than trusting a right-angle icon. Calculate externally and arrange points, arrows and labels manually in VectorPaint.

Define a full line and a nonzero direction

Let L={t(1,2): t real}, not only positive t or t between zero and one. This projects onto a full line, not a finite arrow. Direction u must be nonzero, while zero is an allowed input. Save p/u/L conditions and separate input, projection, residual and calculations. Units are dimensionless. Distinguish vector displacement from endpoint P. A drawn arrow for u illustrates direction and one length without restricting the line at its endpoint.

Use the dot product divided by squared direction length

Compute p·u=3×1+1×2=5 and u·u=1²+2²=5, hence q=(5/5)u=(1,2). The denominator is squared length, not √5. The signed scalar component along the unit direction is 5/√5=√5, which multiplied by u/√5 still gives q. Separate scalar √5 from vector (1,2), rather than call them conflicting answers. Check q is a multiple of u and belongs to L; membership plus the next orthogonality condition establishes projection, not the coefficient alone.

p=(3,1); u=(1,2); L={t*u : t real}
p dot u=5; u dot u=5
q=((p dot u)/(u dot u))*u=(1,2)
r=p-q=(2,-1); u dot r=0
|p-t*u|^2=5*(t-1)^2+5
v=2*u=(2,4): ((p dot v)/(v dot v))*v=(10/20)*v=q

Plot origin and endpoints at equal scales

Use a suggested 1800×1900 canvas, screen origin (500,1500), 200 pixels per unit equally: X=500+200x, Y=1500−200y. P maps to (1100,1300), Q to (700,1100); distinguish OP/OQ styles. Display t from −0.5 to 2.5 only as a window with dashed continuations, not the line’s domain. Place from guides before arrowheads, acknowledging approximation without numeric controls. Unequal axis scales would distort right angles, so retain equal scales here. Window endpoints (−0.5,−1),(2.5,5) map to (400,1700),(1000,500). Label a displayed segment, retaining negative continuation rather than a positive ray only. Keep the right-angle marker small enough to expose the residual tail.

Draw the residual from Q toward P

Residual r=p−q=(2,−1) is drawn from Q to P, with its tail at Q. Screen displacement (400,200) points down because mathematical y decreases by one; it is not r=(2,1). Since u·r=2−2=0 and both are nonzero, mark the right angle at Q. An optional origin-based r is a translated display, not extra residual mass. The identity p=q+r uses head-to-tail connection, not three origin arrows visually mistaken for a triangle.

Verify nearest distance for every line point

For any tu=(t,2t), squared distance to P is (3−t)²+(1−2t)²=5(t−1)²+5. Only t=1 attains minimum 5, so Q uniquely minimizes distance, √5. This uses real Euclidean distance, not visual preference. Also |p|²=10, |q|²=5 and |r|²=5; ordinary lengths do not satisfy √10=√5+√5. A displayed window cannot replace the all-real argument. The minimum follows from formulas, not pixel measurements.

Test rescaled direction and zero input separately

With v=2u=(2,4), p·v=10 and v·v=20, so (1/2)v still gives (1,2). Rescaling direction preserves the line and projection. Dividing only by |v| before multiplying raw v gives a different vector. Zero input legitimately yields zero projection/residual, though no measurable angle belongs to a zero residual. Zero direction instead makes the denominator zero and fails to specify the original line. Keep these two zero cases distinct. Input w=(2,−1) dots to zero with u, yielding zero projection and residual w. Input u yields projection u and zero residual. These distinguish input on versus perpendicular to the line without changing direction. Zero outputs are valid; the nonzero-direction condition does not forbid them.

Check direction, denominator and arrow endpoints

If Q misses L, check its multiple of u; if on L but not perpendicular, check dot product and squared-length denominator. For reversed residuals inspect p−q and screen direction from Q to P rather than delete a minus sign. A distorted right angle calls for axis scales, not arbitrary movement of correct points. Repair one copy factor and retest v=2u. Moving p requires recomputing q/r; layers do not update projection automatically. Recheck both membership and orthogonality instead of adding an icon.
