A scallop shell is more than lines radiating from a point: the width and lighting of each ridge create its form. Paint a shell on a table, establishing the fan with major ridges before adding incomplete growth lines. Texture should explain the surface instead of filling every gap.

Set up a separate study

Create an approximately 1400×1600 px canvas with the hinge below and breathing room above the fan. Separate background, shell, major ribs and growth lines. Test one brush for the width of raised surfaces and another for fine lines; a ridge is a plane, not merely a thin mark.

Check the fan and major ribs at small size for a consistent central spread and side compression. Save a texture-free version as a comparison. Keep bands separate from the ribs so overly strong transverse colour can be reduced without repainting the entire shell volume.

Open the fan from the hinge

Mark a narrow hinge below and open a broad fan above it. Tighten the sides and raise the centre slightly rather than drawing a perfect semicircle. Add small hinge wings, with one partly occluded to suggest a tilt. Check the plain silhouette before adding scalloped edge details.

Mark the fullest region with a gentle arc, offset from the geometric centre if useful. Light the upper left and darken the lower right. Add a faint table shadow to clarify the tilt. Let only a short lower segment make strong contact, rather than grounding the entire edge.

Vary the spacing of the main ribs

Draw seven to nine ribs radiating from the hinge, broader centrally and compressed at the sides. Curve them subtly across the shell. Align raised ribs with outward scallops and grooves with inward dips at the edge, so the interior pattern and silhouette belong to one structure.

Paint three main ridges first, using pale pink-cream on lit sides and muted grey-pink on shaded sides. Keep groove darks narrow. Equally wide black lines produce a paper fan; soften them and vary the width of the illuminated surfaces instead.

Wrap colour bands across the shell

Add one or two incomplete muted violet or tan bands crossing the radial ribs along the shell’s curvature. Widen them over ridges and narrow or interrupt them in grooves. Keep the original lighting intact; begin with low contrast before deciding whether the bands need more emphasis.

Let parts of each band fade away and vary its thickness. Avoid adding unrelated bright colours. Test a small strip crossing three ribs beside the study, observing how the colour changes across lit and shaded planes before applying it to the shell.

Keep fine growth lines broken

Add short curved growth lines with a fine brush, denser near selected outer areas and sparse near the hinge. Break them at strong lights and deep grooves. They should remain weaker than the main ribs. If a grid dominates at small size, remove the densest group.

Suggest a few thin outer edges with pale strokes rather than a continuous white ring. Pair a small side dark with a nearby light to indicate thickness. Concentrate the brightest marks on two or three lit ridges, keeping the rest in middle values so the shell does not resemble a metal badge.

Review fan shape, volume and texture separately

Hide fine texture and check whether the fan silhouette and major ribs already show curvature. If not, correct the values beside the ribs. Restore the bands to check surface wrapping, then the growth lines. Each layer should add information without weakening the larger structure.

If it looks papery, check edge thickness, ridge width and contact shadow. If it resembles a gear, soften overly regular scallops and lose some edges in light. A second version using only three values can test whether volume exists before colour bands and texture are added.

Review and plan the next study

Review the fan, ridge widths and then supporting growth lines. Inspect accidental outer-edge gaps close up but judge rib clarity at normal size. Use one well-lit ridge as a reference; other areas may be less complete while following the same structural rule.

Save the layered shell and PNG, then make a three-value version without colour bands. Check whether the grey study still reads as raised shell. Next, change only the hinge angle and rebuild side compression while retaining the palette to test your grasp of radial structure.
