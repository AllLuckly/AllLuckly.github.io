Grey or overly bright HDR playback can involve source data, server processing, playback path and display conditions. Public HDR support does not promise identical results for every file and screen. Observe an authorised same-source clip under recorded conditions rather than applying arbitrary brightness values universally.

Record actual source attributes

Retain a known original and inspect codec, dimensions, colour metadata and production notes where available. Do not infer everything from an HDR filename. Identify any standard-range counterpart and its production history instead of assuming a darker copy is a calibrated reference.

Choose highlights, shadows and familiar colours at three timestamps. Extreme black-and-light imagery or compressed third-party screenshots cannot establish intended overall appearance. Keep source evidence separate from environment-dependent impressions.

Fix device and viewing conditions

Compare the same device, orientation, ambient light and recorded brightness without changing several system options or screens together. This establishes repeatable observation; it is not a universal calibration procedure and does not require changing system protections.

Distinguish interface theme from video colour. A darker surround does not convert the file's colour space. Compare brightness only at the same frame and restore it; brighter does not establish complete HDR operation or faithful author intent.

Inspect the identical local original

Import it locally at normal speed with stable framing and simple captions. At each timestamp describe highlight texture, dark boundaries and specific familiar colours. Concrete areas and symptoms are more useful than attractive or grey judgements.

Record flicker, green pictures and sustained black output as playback faults separately from subjective preferences. Compare another suitable tool under documented conditions, without declaring it an absolute reference. Single-screen observation does not replace professional measurement.

Verify the remote playback version

For NAS or server access, establish whether playback still uses the original. Processing may change dimensions, encoding or colour information. Record available management evidence; an HDR catalogue badge does not validate every delivery stage.

Compare identical scenes without changing brightness. Investigate returned content and processing before blaming protocols or player colour. Mark unknown transcoding honestly instead of treating matching titles as identical bytes.

Use screenshots only as location aids

Screenshots can identify areas and timestamps but may undergo different display or saving treatment. Do not use two captured brightness values to prove complete HDR performance. Describe missing details and colour differences alongside relevant file and device conditions.

For compatible copies of your own footage, retain originals, specify output and recheck colour, duration and sound. Renaming extensions is not conversion, and arbitrary transcoding is not automatically lossless repair. Resolve network faults before converting an entire collection.

Keep a sample-specific conclusion

Separate confirmed source, local playback, remote identity and observed appearance, documenting unknowns. A working version can remain useful without declaring every HDR file supported or unsupported by the device. This test concerns its actual sample and path.

Completion means repeating the same frame, file and display conditions and identifying likely layers of difference. Restore experimental settings and check ordinary content. Distinguish repeatable observations from unknowns rather than treating a catalogue badge as final display verification.
