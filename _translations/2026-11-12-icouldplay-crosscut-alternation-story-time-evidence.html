Alternating A/B scenes may suggest simultaneous action, but alternation alone supplies no real synchronization evidence. Observe owned or permitted footage in iCouldPlay, using two paper-action lines to record file order separately from story time. Crosscutting describes organisation, not automatic reconstruction of chronology, character relations or authenticated capture times. Unproduced examples remain plans.

Identify both action lines independently

Plan red A with a round paper piece: A1 left, A2 right. Blue B uses a square: B1 front, B2 flipped. Label separate roughly2s segments A1,A2,B1,B2 and retain originals; labels identify samples, not exact recording clocks. Make independent tabletop demonstrations only with external capture/editing, not a new iCouldPlay feature. Otherwise draw four unperformed storyboard panels. Do not impose identities on unfamiliar red/blue props; establish checkable line features rather than inferring people or places from colour.

Record four segments and eight file seconds

Plan A1,B1,A2,B2,2s each: file intervals0–2,2–4,4–6,6–8s, total8. Viewers see A1 then B1; that establishes neither real A-before-B order nor A lasting until B’s shot ends. Two seconds is a target; replace intervals with actual durations if different. Record intervals/labels without reading A1’s one as first second/day. Separate paper sequence from produced file. No export means no claimed eight-second playback, and shot count is not an event count.

Keep observed order within each line

Inspect A alone: left/right are two identifiable states, without invented route or exact duration if the intervening process is absent. B’s front/flipped sequence has its own order; insertion between A shots does not establish B occurring during A’s action. Keep A-order, B-order and inter-line relation separate, leaving the third unknown without evidence. Absence of a split-screen establishes neither non-simultaneity nor a long gap. Add complete processes or credible clock records if provided; edit order cannot testify for missing original timing.

Add a counterexample with explicit dates and times

Make a paper counterexample explicitly assigning A to November10 at10:00 and B to November11 at10:00, same timezone and trusted records, retaining A1,B1,A2,B2. Alternation remains while the lines are not simultaneous. Preserve the one-day difference rather than copying only10:00. An added date caption is itself an author label requiring support in real footage. These are invented premises, not source metadata. The example shows alternation alone is insufficient for synchronization, not that every crosscut is asynchronous.

Log cuts and evidence in normal playback

For a produced copy, play beginning to end and log red/blue cards, shape and state at each switch, first verifying four-shot order only. Pause through an available control to read dates, then resume, adding anchors for coarse timing. Do not change track, source and speed together and mix viewing changes into evidence. Temporarily disable optional captions obscuring cards and restore them. Burned-in dates remain image content, not removable by subtitle-file switching. Unmade samples yield a storyboard, not playback evidence or an assumed automatic story-line column feature.

Give sound bridges and matched actions separate evidential status

Continuous music or narration can link lines without giving them one clock time. Matching gestures, looks or cuts may be post-production organisation, not proof that A hears B, people respond to each other or share a place. If dialogue, clocks or plot explicitly establish simultaneity, record that narrative evidence and location, still separately from simultaneous recording. Say the work invites a simultaneous interpretation, not verified real-event synchronization. Textbooks vary in terminology; record actual alternation and time evidence rather than only a crosscut label.

Check shot identity and missing timezone information

If replayed A1 appears to be A2, check version, edit start and cropped labels; resume position is not the source shot number. Minute-resolution10:00 labels do not establish the same second. Record missing date, timezone, trusted source and precision fully. The counterexample’s same-zone premise does not transfer to real footage. Start a version record for omissions/reordering rather than overwriting first observations. Reused shots and alternating lines are distinct: log reuse separately, not four segments converted into four independent real events.

Reopen and preserve three layers of order

Leave playback, then reopen the same version and play from A1, verifying file order, each line’s states and date evidence. Preserve sources, edited copies and first notes, appending correction reasons. Explain that8s is file duration, A1 is not a timestamp, identical10:00 lacks date/timezone, and alternation proves neither simultaneity nor its absence. Storyboard-only work leaves playback unperformed; actual viewing remains sample-specific. New footage needs new identities/time sources, not these dates or claims of historical authentication, person recognition or capture synchronization detection in the player.
