A C-clamp drawn as a letter C plus a screw often loses the pad orientation and jaw gap. Hand-draw an invented empty, slightly open clamp in Aquarell, establishing frame and facing surfaces before threads and materials. This exterior study provides no physical tightening, load or repair procedure.

Reserve room for the frame and jaw gap

Use an approximately 1600×1700 px canvas with the opening rightward and screw rising from the lower end toward the fixed upper jaw. Mark contact centres and screw axis before the C-shaped envelope, preserving ground inside. Open the right-side gap if it resembles a padlock; add no shackle. The clamp may tilt in the composition, but both working faces must still face each other along the screw axis. Pixels are compositional, not actual jaw width, and an empty clamp needs no dark invented workpiece.

Keep the C-frame continuous and open

Give the frame continuous inner and outer contours from upper end around the back to lower end. A squarer back is fine, but not two disconnected hooks. A narrow darker blue-grey side suggests thickness. Add end volumes if it looks like lettering; restore the open negative space before texture if filled. Invented thickness establishes no strength. The fixed jaw belongs to the upper frame end, never a floating disc in the gap.

Pass the screw through the lower boss

Draw a thickened lower boss with exposed screw portions above and below on one axis, omitting the hidden middle. Move a grazing screw into the boss and align segments before threads. Its top faces the fixed jaw; its bottom supports the handle. An arrow cannot repair reversed ends. The boss is an exterior simplification without invented internal nut sections; diagonal strokes establish neither measured pitch nor a machining drawing.

Face the swivel pad toward the fixed surface

Attach a broad pad to the screw top, its working face upward toward the fixed upper face, leaving a gap. A slight tilt needs a short connecting neck, not another rod through the frame. Establish facing planes before edge thickness. Flatten a cup-like pad or connect a floating coin. The referenced component can swivel, but this drawn angle proves neither actual motion nor clamping of an object.

Pass the handle through the bottom socket only

Draw one transverse handle through a bottom socket, with unequal exposed lengths allowed and small rounded end caps. Hide its middle inside the socket; it must not pass through the pad or frame back. Connect two apparently separate stubs with a checking line, then conceal that line. Make a jaw-like handle narrower and clearly transverse. This depicts a sliding handle, without transparent internals, extension-bar advice or torque calculations from exposed lengths.

Align threads and materials with the screw

Suggest a few turns on exposed screw portions, following one axis and stopping at the boss. Clarify near turns and abbreviate far ones without making detached rings. Separate frame and steel through related warm/cool greys, retaining the pad neck. Reduce zebra-like contrast and soften a shadow resembling another screw. Keep texture on a hideable layer. Establish jaw, screw, frame and handle ownership before wear; noise cannot conceal gaps.

Troubleshoot from contact faces to handle

Hide threads and highlights. Trace fixed jaw around frame to boss, then screw upward to pad and downward to handle socket. Verify an open gap, coaxial exposed segments and one handle. Repair a backwards pad rather than hiding it with a workpiece. Stop wrongly visible edges individually instead of blackening joints. Temporary path numbers stay on guides. Record precise breaks rather than a vague awkward appearance so the next revision has a clear target.

Reopen and check the empty gap and connected parts

Save native work, hide axes/numbers, export PNG and reopen. At small size inspect C-shaped negative space and facing jaws, then enlarge for screw segments, pad neck and handle occlusion. Restore a lost handle through contrast or subject size, not two thick rods. Remove exported guides in the original rather than cropping jaws. Reopen editable structure/texture layers and note the empty, no-workpiece condition. Success is readable attachment, not tested force, reliability or new mechanical simulation.
