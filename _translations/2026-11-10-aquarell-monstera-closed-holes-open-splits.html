Monstera holes and splits are different negative spaces: a hole is enclosed by blade, while a split reaches exterior background. Draw an invented mature-type leaf with four enclosed holes and three edge splits to practise connectivity. Counts are compositional, not universal, and a hole-free juvenile leaf is not an error. Construct blade and background manually in Aquarell without automatic plant recognition or hole generation.

Establish the midrib and broad heart-shaped envelope

Use an approximately 1600×1800 px portrait canvas with warm grey-cream background, tip upper right and petiole at the lower-left base. Establish broad heart-like envelope and continuous midrib, with modest side asymmetry belonging to one blade. Separate blade, openings, veins and shadow; use muted deep green. Initially leave it unperforated and check petiole at base, not tip or a hole. Repair two-leaf-like division or hand-like early cuts. Pixels/tilt are compositional, not a universal mature-leaf or identification standard.

Enclose four holes with blade bridges

Place four varied oval/elongated holes beside the midrib and number them temporarily. Retain continuous blade bridges around each, avoiding accidental contact with the outer contour or a severed midrib. Do not arrange equal button-like holes. Inspect silhouette/openings alone and trace closed perimeters. Restore background identity if black spots appear, or reduce excessive rims/inconsistent background if sticker-like. Four is an illustration condition, not a botanical definition, and shapes/angles need not match exactly.

Connect three splits to exterior background

Add three inward edge splits, stopping short of the midrib and retaining continuous central blade. Label them separately from four holes. Each mouth must reach background; an accidental green bridge would turn one into an extra enclosed hole. Reduce depth and reconnect tissue if fragmented, or reopen a painted dark-green line. Similar shapes can differ topologically; connectivity, not darkness, defines the distinction. Preserve petiole/midrib continuity and add veins later without sealing openings.

Vary opening edges with the leaf plane

Keep background through openings continuous with one environment. Use tiny value edges for thin leaf thickness, not thick white rings. Curvature may compress or partially occlude a hole; record hidden extent in the sketch. Reduce rim thickness/equal dark shadows if wooden-board-like, or restore turning/background continuity if printed-pattern-like. Treat splits as the same thin surface, not tall walls. A simple background band may continue through holes diagnostically, but incompatible interior/exterior environments need a spatial explanation.

Keep veins on tissue rather than spanning voids

Retain a base-to-tip midrib and selected outward side veins, ending or occluding them on visible tissue rather than drawing green bridges across holes. Hide texture to test four holes/three splits, then reject false joins. Reduce equal dark veins if engineering-skeleton-like or black wrinkles if cloth-like, restoring broad blade. Model gentle undulation without darkening every rim. These are vein cues, not complete anatomy or evidence of age, care quality or health.

Separate blade and void under one light

Use warmer upper-left green and cooler turning planes without brightening background through holes as though it were leaf highlight. Restore background rather than calling uniformly black holes shadow. Connect a mid-green petiole and cast lower-right shadow; optional lighter openings within shadow are simplified cues, not exact projection calculation. Soften a second-leaf-like shadow. Count blade openings with shadow hidden, then ensure projected marks do not invent another hole/split. Real negative space and light within shadow are separate evidence.

Audit connectivity rather than resemblance alone

Keep monochrome tissue. Trace each of four closed holes, then enter three splits from exterior background. Correct totals do not excuse a hole accidentally opening at the edge. Change one bridge or mouth with other locations/light fixed. A juvenile reference may lack these openings; save separately with revised conditions rather than forcing holes to match exercise numbers. Distinguish natural variation from this composition. Remove diagnostic labels so tissue/background relationships explain connectivity.

Retain thin tissue bridges in export

Save the layered original and inspect four holes, three splits and midrib in a PNG at use size. Widen a disappearing bridge locally and quiet veins if a hole becomes a split rather than outlining everything. Reopen a green-sealed split. Record a fault such as the right bridge at hole two merging with background for numbered repair. Judge connectivity, continuous petiole/midrib and thin volume, not maximum holes. Mature-leaf sources do not make this a care, health or species diagnosis. No plant experiment occurred; new views need revised rims/hidden tissue, not shifted old holes.
