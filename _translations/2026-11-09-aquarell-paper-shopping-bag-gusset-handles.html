Shopping-bag decoration can conceal a blocked mouth, broken gusset or floating handles. Draw an empty upright paper bag, establishing front/back walls and a side gusset, then connecting two handles to their respective walls. Check folds and anchors manually in Aquarell without automatic paper folding. Show only externally visible base edges instead of combining an interior bottom-fold diagram with exterior perspective.

Give the opening front and back walls

Use an approximately 1600×1900 px portrait canvas with a pale grey-green background. Turn the bag slightly right to expose front and right side. Use warm kraft-paper colour, locating front and farther rear upper edges and joining them with side edges. Separate sketch, back wall, gusset, front wall, handles and shadow. The mouth is not a black stripe pasted above the front; check interior space without handles. Open it modestly if box-like or reconnect rear rim and sides if stage-flat-like. Leave margins above handles and below the base.

Construct the gusset from connected planes

Place an inward vertical fold within the right side, connecting both adjacent side planes to opening and base. Keep it on the side rather than jumping onto the front. Use light diagnostic lines and omit tears. If it looks cut open, lighten the gap and reconnect planes. If patch-like, repair upper/lower junctions. A slightly asymmetric empty bag still needs coherent folds rather than random curves. Establish planes prior to kraft texture; dense grain cannot conceal a gusset disconnected from the mouth.

Give the base width rather than a pointed seam

Place the base on the table, joining front and visible side lower edges into a supporting outline with a modest right-side return. Do not taper both walls to one point like a cone. Hide ground shadow and check a complete base, then restore tight contact. Add necessary width and side depth if envelope-like; reduce paper-edge thickness if wooden-box-like. Real bottom folds and adhesive constructions vary. Do not expose an invented hidden assembly or claim manufacturability merely from a rectangle.

Assign four handle anchors to two walls

Mark two anchors on the upper front wall and two on the back. Pair the front two into one handle and rear two into the other, using dots and arc centrelines. Do not connect front-left to rear-right across the mouth. Keep anchors on paper walls, not suspended inside the opening. Record concealed rear anchors in the sketch without exposing them all in the finish. Aerial-like handles need two endpoints each; a long strap across the mouth suggests confused pairing. Four anchors belong to this two-handle example, not every bag design.

Occlude handle arcs correctly at the walls

Choose flat paper-strip handles, broader across arc tops and narrower when turning sideways. Let walls conceal rearward endpoint portions rather than pasting all four ends flat. Offset the two arcs slightly for readability without moving anchors off their walls. Trace each strip between endpoints with wall layers visible. Reduce cylindrical edge highlights if rope-like; repair occlusion if cut-looking instead of blackening gaps. Arcs describe a static shape, not load capacity. Hanging weights are unnecessary and would not prove fastening strength.

Distinguish fold shading from tears

Light upper left, retaining broad warm-brown front light and quieter grey side/interior values without a solid-black gusset. Vary the opening interior so the rear rim stays identifiable. If torn-looking, widen and lighten excessively narrow black fold shadows. If icon-flat, expose modest side and opening depth. Suggest sparse low-contrast fibres rather than black wrinkles everywhere. Tighten handle shadows near paper walls only, not equal black borders around airborne arc tops. Every dark line needs a surface-turn or occlusion explanation.

Approve structure without printed decoration

Keep a blank structural version and inspect opening-to-interior, gusset top/bottom connection, grounded base and two handles/four anchors. It must read as a bag without print; repair weak relations prior to decoration. Change one anchor or fold without twisting the whole viewpoint. A flattened variant needs new wall separation, handle occlusion and base outline, not horizontal compression retaining old shadows. Remove diagnostic front/back labels from the PNG. Labels locate faults but cannot substitute for readable structure.

Keep handles separate from the mouth at output size

Save the layered original and inspect handle arcs, rear rim and right gusset in a PNG at use size. Lighten the opening locally and retain the rear edge if it becomes a black stripe, rather than brightening the bag. Separate arc tops or values if handles merge into one loop. Keep base shadow from swallowing side width. Record a specific anchor error, such as the rear handle’s right endpoint attached to the front wall. This static spatial illustration is not a load or manufacturing test. Later print must follow paper planes rather than float across opening and gusset.
