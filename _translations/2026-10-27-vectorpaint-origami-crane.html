An origami-crane illustration can suggest lightness with a few straight-edged planes. Build body, wings, neck and tail from a silhouette, practising direction and stacking. It depicts a finished visual form rather than a verified paper-folding sequence.

Establish head, tail and wing directions

Use a 1000 by 700 pixel canvas and establish body, neck, tail and wing directions as a silhouette. Leave room for long tips and distinguish neck from tail through proportion and a clear head direction. Keep sketches separate and establish the whole pose before designing individual planes.

Build the body from closed polygons

Draw a closed triangular or quadrilateral body with a midtone fill and no initial stroke. Leave enough joining area for wings, neck and tail. Preserve a baseline copy and use clear corner nodes rather than many short-edge points that complicate seams and add noise.

Differentiate the wings by area and stacking

Use a larger near wing and smaller rear wing with deliberate overlap. Inspect joins in greyscale. Fix shared geometry or underlap instead of hiding gaps with heavy strokes. Recheck the wing/body angle after moving a tip so each wing remains part of the same pose.

Give neck and tail clear folded directions

Extend a narrow polygon into the neck and a short folded head, tapering the tail in the opposite direction. Test both in the body colour to judge silhouette. Distinguish them through length, bend and width before adding eye detail, and maintain the straight-edged paper language.

Explain folds with a restrained value range

Use three related values with one chosen light direction. Suggest folds with restrained lines or narrow planes rather than outlining everything heavily. Check the structure with crease marks hidden. If many lines are necessary for recognition, revisit silhouettes. Values are illustrative choices, not calculated 3D folding or lighting.

Maintain shared joins during pose changes

Raise only the near wing in a copy and recheck its body seam and relation to the far wing. Separately compare a shorter tail rather than stretching the entire crane. Keep a baseline and change one relationship at a time. Distinguish observed reference features from illustrative simplifications; polygons are not reverse-engineered folding instructions.

Check tips and the reduced silhouette

Save and inspect wing, head and tail tips in exported files. Keep small-size neck or crease adaptations in a separate simplified source. Check light planes against intended backgrounds. Label the work as an illustration and retain separate verified folding instructions if teaching. Reopen outputs and judge silhouette before internal shading.

Restore coherence when planes look disconnected

Inspect shared body direction and gaps if planes look detached. Distinguish head and tail geometrically before recolouring. Diagnose spikes through overlapping paths and extra nodes. Correct wing stacking rather than disguising it with colour. Reconfirm a coherent monochrome silhouette before restoring plane values.
