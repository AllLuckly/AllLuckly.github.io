Use public wildlife information and a permitted reference to build a flying silhouette with raised wings, contrasting undersides and a small head.

Choose a clear underside reference

The US National Park Service describes an adult turkey vulture’s bare red head, slightly raised wings forming a shallow V while soaring, and lighter flight feathers visible below. Choose a permitted flight photograph showing the underside and record source, viewpoint and usage rights. If the head is distant or obscured, do not force a conspicuous red detail. This exercise does not require approaching birds or disturbing habitat.

Plan canvas and wingspan

Create an approximately 2000 by 1400 pixel landscape canvas in the current Aquarell interface and save an editable original; these dimensions are suggestions. Leave room around both wing tips, head and tail, and lightly mark the body center and approximate span. Separate sketch, sky, subject and details with available functions. Button labels can vary by device; use identifiable entries and do not assume an automatic bird-generation feature.

Establish body direction first

Draw a relatively small body shape and use a direction line from head to tail. Decide whether the viewer sees the bird from below, obliquely below or head-on. Projected wing shape changes with viewpoint; even though sources describe a shallow V in soaring, not every image should be forced into a symmetric letter. Check the head-tail direction and body connection; if the bird reads as a dot floating on wings, revise the joins instead of adding feather marks.

Extend wings from shoulders

Mark both shoulders at the sides of the body, then follow the reference’s leading edges toward the wing tips. First use large continuous contours to establish span and a slight rise. Connect the trailing edges to the body rather than drawing two unrelated triangles. Perspective may shorten the far wing; use the photograph instead of forcing a mirror image. At reduced size, check the shallow V direction and whether either wing tip touches the canvas edge.

Separate leading edges and lighter flight feathers

Block body and leading wing areas in dark brown or gray, then place lighter gray flight-feather bands along the visible rear wing areas. The value boundary follows the wing but need not be perfectly straight. Ensure the light shape remains inside one wing before working on the other. Distance and light change contrast, so do not force pure white in a dark photograph. If the pale band floats outside the wing, revise the wing edge and overlap first.

Handle wing tips and tail

Suggest wing-tip feathers with a few separated shapes, following only what the reference resolves; keep a continuous contour when details are unclear. Extend the tail from the rear of the body, first blocking its visible fan or other actual shape, then deciding whether a few dark marks are needed. Do not attach tail feathers to the trailing wing edge or cover each wing with equal symmetric lines. At small size inspect overall wing shape before detail.

Selectively draw head and bill

Place a small head proportional to the flying view at the front of the torso. Where appropriate suggest the adult’s bare head with muted red and a visible bill with a small light shape. At a distance the head may read only as a dark mark; do not enlarge it to hold details. Reduce the picture to confirm direction, and do not add a large bright eye when the reference does not show one. Public information describes the species but does not make every feature visible in every photograph.

Compare another flight silhouette

Place the draft beside the existing osprey exercise and examine body, wing joins and light areas separately. The turkey-vulture study emphasizes the shallow V and lighter rear underwings; use the osprey’s own reference for that study. Make two small silhouettes on paper and label visible and hidden regions rather than copying wing markings between species. The illustration is an observation exercise and a single colored patch cannot conclusively identify a real bird.

Keep the silhouette readable against sky

Use pale blue-gray or muted warm gray for sky and avoid clouds with exactly the same value as wing tips. Hide feathers and bands temporarily, keeping only body and wing silhouettes. Check direction of head, tail, roots and tips. If the pale underside vanishes, increase its value contrast with the leading edge before adding a thick outline. Restore restrained details and ensure both wings still connect to one body.

Record one revision

Keep before-and-after thumbnails and note the clearest change, such as wing-tip position, width of the light flight-feather band or head direction. Compare at the same scale with similar wingspans; simple enlargement can make feathers look clearer while masking an unchanged contour.

Save and inspect the output

Save the editable original, use a share or export entry actually visible in the current version to save a separate image, and reopen it to inspect wing tips, head, tail, corners and underside values. Return to the original and identify sky, subject and sketch layers. If something is missing, check visibility and crop without overwriting the only original. Keep public and photographic sources; mark device, export or reopening steps pending if not performed.

Make a three-value swatch

Draw three small swatches beside the sketch for sky, leading wing and pale flight feathers. Make their values distinguishable, then fill a tiny bird study with them. If the pale flight feathers merge into the sky, adjust only the small sky area while retaining the wing values and compare against the original. The swatches are painting choices for this reference, not fixed color measurements for the species.
