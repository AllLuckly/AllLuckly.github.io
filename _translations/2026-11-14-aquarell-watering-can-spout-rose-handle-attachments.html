A watering can combines a container and projecting spout; loose ellipses can confuse filling and pouring openings. Draw an invented empty long-spouted can in Aquarell, with one side handle and a perforated rose, without flowing water. Establish exterior connections before restrained watercolour treatment of metal or coating.

Reserve room for the complete long spout

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px canvas, body left and spout extending upper right with room beyond the rose. Establish body axis and top/bottom ellipses sharing one orientation; the diagonal spout must not twist the container. Keep the sketch pale without lettering. If it resembles a teacup, clarify long-spout and container proportions. Reduce the whole subject if the rose is cramped instead of cutting off its connection. Pixels are exercise choices, not real capacity or brand proportions.

Separate the filling opening from the top surface

Leave a clear filling opening within the top surface; its near rim occludes a darker interior. Do not turn the entire top into a black ellipse with a handle emerging from it or connect this inlet to the rose. Distinguish warmer inside from cooler shell, with a finer far rim and thicker near rim. Remove an invented lid knob if it reads as a covered kettle. Hide bottom guides visible through the mouth instead of blackening them. The can is empty, without water-level or pressure readings.

Extend the spout continuously from the lower body

Place the spout root low on the right body and draw two gradually converging edges rising upper right, with a clear junction width. Both edges must belong to one connection, not separate sites or an independent rod stuck outside. Let near junction edges occlude the shell and stop hidden far lines. Lower the root if it resembles a straw hanging over the mouth. Restore thickness where the middle collapses to a line. This shows an exterior route, not transparent plumbing, measured flow or pressure capacity.

Fit the rose around the spout end

Draw a short fitting neck, rounded rose back and tilted elliptical perforated face aligned with the last spout segment, not the canvas. Both volumes must support the neck; a hairline cannot suspend a large disc. Remove wavy edges and add side thickness if it resembles a flower. Repair ellipse and axis before details if the face and back disagree. This orientation is an illustration choice, not a claim that every model rotates freely or shares a manufacturer’s fitting dimensions.

Place holes on one tilted face

Use a few readable dark holes, with curved checking rows guiding spacing on the tilted face. Far-side dots may compress but must not leave the disc like drops. Omit white rings that make them rivets. Reduce hole count and strengthen rim thickness if it looks like a flat texture. Remove detached dots implying active spray. No water flows in this scene, so the arrangement measures no distribution; the simplified count does not match a real rose hole for hole.

Attach both ends of the handle to the body

Draw one curved left-side handle, attached near the shoulder and lower body, with background visible inside. Give it width and thickness; small attachment plates must not read as another handle. Separate handle opening from filling mouth with a body boundary. Repair a floating lower end before decorative screws. The invented handle has no claimed load or comfort rating. Check one-handle silhouette in monochrome before restrained colour differences from the shell.

Trace the body-to-spout-to-rose route

Reduce the image and ignore holes while tracing body, root, spout, fitting neck and rose back. Check contact at every junction, then both handle roots and separate filling mouth. Stop hidden edges if the spout appears transparent through the shell. Repair fitting length and overlap if the rose floats; water lines cannot tie it on. Consistent shadows distinguish grounded body from raised spout without creating a second spout. Keep checking axes on one layer so they do not become exported braces.

Reopen and distinguish inlet, outlet and handle

Keep native work, hide rows and axes, export PNG and reopen. Identify filling mouth, spout root, rose and single handle at small size, then inspect dots staying on the face. Adjust local tones if a dark mouth swallows a handle root. Recompose a cropped rose rather than adding an isolated circle. Reopen native layers and note the no-flow still-life condition. Readable parts establish no working water supply, fitting compatibility or automatic fluid simulation.
