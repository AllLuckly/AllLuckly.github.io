Draw broadleaf cattail with its male portion still present: a narrower male portion above a brown female portion, following the selected reference. Brown below does not itself prove that every seed is mature. The male flowers may later fall away, so do not claim that both portions always coexist. Specify this state before drawing the axis, cylinders and flat leaves.

Establish one inflorescence axis on a tall canvas

Use a portrait canvas of about 1600 × 2200 pixels. Place the main stalk near centre and the head upper-middle, leaving basal space for leaves rather than filling the page with identical heads. Sketch one upright, gently natural axis joining male portion, female portion and stalk instead of pointing each differently. Separate stalk, flower portions, leaves and detail from the sketch on pale watery green without a detailed wetland. Save and inspect the whole plant small. Pixels are compositional, not plant height or growth-rate measurements.

Align the upper male and lower female portions

Block a slender upper male portion above a thicker female cylinder, with a natural junction. Follow the adjacent portions in the NC State broadleaf cattail reference without an exaggerated bare gap; this does not assert that every individual has absolutely no small separation. The axis may project slightly at the top, but do not branch two male heads sideways like antlers. Temporarily label upper male and lower female, then hide labels and check widths. These are portions of one inflorescence, not two giant flowers threaded onto a skewer.

Distinguish the selected head from a mature head lacking male flowers

Use pale yellow-brown marks for the male portion and a brown midtone for the female portion, labelled as the reference state retaining its male flowers, not a colour-based maturity test. On a checking layer, a separate framed brown female head may be labelled mature fruiting head, with male flowers potentially shed. Similar brown colours do not justify removing state notes and treating both drawings as the same instant. A lone brown cylinder is not automatically wrong: first ask which stage is shown. This state was selected for comparison, not detected automatically by the app.

Model cylinders rather than flat cotton buds

Round the female cylinder’s sides and ends along its axis, with a lighter lit side and broad soft far-side dark rather than a cut rectangular block. Make the male portion finer and looser, not a duplicate solid cylinder. Hide surface dots and check widths and curvature through large values alone. For a cotton bud, restore the distinction between portions and their stalk connection; for two trunk segments, soften cut-like edges. Share upper-left light instead of placing incompatible frontal white stripes on both portions.

Grow strap-like leaves from the base with visible flat surfaces

Arrange five focal leaves from the base at the lower edge, explicitly a composition count rather than a whole plant total. Make long straps with broad near surfaces, narrowing on edge and tapering at the tips instead of round tubes. Some may exceed the head, without all being cut level. Trace each from base to tip and stop hidden lines at crossings; none should sprout from the female cylinder. Green-strip counts alone do not distinguish cattail from an iris fan. Depict the reference’s readable basal strap-leaf structure only.

Show leaf turns through value rather than parallel black lines

Use two or three related greens to separate near and far leaves. Extend light along the broad surface, darken the edge and reveal a narrow reverse at turns. Keep longitudinal lines sparse rather than striping every leaf. Flower dots must remain subordinate to large values; keep the stalk narrower than leaves but continuous, without equally strong vertical background stripes. For wire-like leaves, broaden the surfaces; for scissor shapes, restore smooth tapering curves. Colour serves light and layering, not nutrient status, chlorophyll estimates or water quality.

Check the stage, axis and five leaf roots

Hide dots and trace from the male tip through female portion, stalk and base, avoiding two intersecting heads. Trace each of five focal leaves from root to tip, checking overlap and flat surfaces. For a huge dark gap between portions, revisit the selected adjacent reference. If the main and mature comparison colours mingle, restore framing and stage notes. Keep the faulty draft and fix one stage or connection issue at a time rather than renaming the plant to evade it. This teaching sketch does not establish actual head counts, leaf totals or individual variation.

Reopen with the stage note retained

Save native work, hide temporary root numbers, retain the necessary state note and reopen a PNG. At fit-to-window size inspect the narrow upper and broad lower portions with strap leaves. Enlarge for stalk alignment and correctly assigned sex labels. Reopen editable work and recolour one near leaf independently of the female head. If the mature comparison dominates, reduce and separate it without removing stage information. State broadleaf cattail, the state retaining its male portion and five focal leaves.
