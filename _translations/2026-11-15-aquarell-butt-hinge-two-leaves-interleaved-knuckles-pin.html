A hinge is not two plates glued along a black seam: interleaved knuckles share one pin axis. Draw an invented partly open butt hinge in Aquarell with five illustrative visible knuckles, separate leaves and a pin cap, without a door installation. The count belongs to this exercise, not every hinge model.

Open two leaves around one axis

Use an approximately 1700×1500 px canvas with a near-vertical common axis, left leaf forward-left and right leaf back-right, both readable. Begin with axis and quadrilaterals before holes. Gather disconnected inner edges to one axis region; later thickness and knuckles distinguish folded-paper geometry. Choose a clear view without measuring actual opening from screen angles. Leave room around the cap and all outer corners.

Allocate five knuckle intervals first

Divide the shared axis into five roughly equal intervals, assigned left/right/left/right/left with narrow seams. Check colours before short cylindrical volumes. All centres share one line, not five axes. Do not count the pin cap as a sixth knuckle; repair consecutive same-side assignments. Equal heights are schematic, not universal. Keep numbering on guides and gaps narrow enough to avoid floating segments.

Connect the left leaf to its three knuckles

Join the left leaf to knuckles one, three and five, leaving intervals two and four for the right leaf instead of a solid axial wall. Give each connection width and a coherent cylinder-to-plate transition. Stop hidden left edges through right knuckles. Connect button-like segments back to the plate. Show exterior wrapping without a complete metal section around the pin. The outer leaf stays one continuous plate, not three detached leaves.

Fit the right leaf into the remaining gaps

Attach the right leaf at intervals two and four without extra transparent cylinders elsewhere. Check the three-plus-two alternation in colour, then settle occlusion. Leaf thickness varies with viewpoint while the axis remains common. Reduce zipper-like teeth into cylinders and unify duplicated axes. This is assembled, not exploded; do not pull the right leaf away from the pin while claiming connected opening. A disassembly view would be a separate task.

Show only the necessary exposed pin parts

Add a small top pin cap and optionally a very short lower end on the shared axis. The middle pin is enclosed and need not be a visible black line, especially not through fixing holes. Shorten a sixth-knuckle-like cap or a long hanging lower needle. Cap shape is invented; no anti-rise mechanism details are adopted. A drawn pin establishes neither retention nor smooth motion, and no internal friction simulation is needed.

Arrange fixing holes on their own leaf planes

Place two invented holes on each leaf, elliptic as perspective requires rather than all facing the viewer. Show ground or restrained inner walls, not raised rivets. Preserve plate material around each hole. Perspective may change screen spacing; do not force horizontal symmetry. Four schematic holes are no installation template, with no specified screws, wall or door. Restrained highlights should leave holes and knuckle ownership clearer than polish texture.

Trace each knuckle back to a leaf

Hide materials and assign each of five knuckles left or right, tracing its connection to a full leaf. Counting five alone is insufficient if all attach to one side. Check coaxial centres, seams and hidden-line stops. Restore material around broken holes and realign an off-axis cap without bending the whole axis. Record interval-specific faults, revise and restore restrained material. Checking colours are manual ownership aids, not automatic leaf recognition.

Reopen and verify three-plus-two alternation and holes

Save native work, hide axis/colours, export PNG and reopen. Check connected leaves and readable opening small, then three-left/two-right knuckles and four illustrative holes enlarged. Clarify merged seams through tone instead of detaching cylinders. Remove exported numbering in the original. Reopen editable leaves/materials and note schematic five-knuckle/four-hole counts. Success is interleaving and ownership, not installation, durability, load certification or automatic assembly validation.
