A paper pinwheel needs more than four triangles arranged in a circle: its outward tips and inward-folded surfaces must belong to continuous folded paper. Draw one stationary four-bladed decorative pinwheel, using a small palette to explain paper sides, the central pin and a support behind it. Construct it manually in Aquarell without rotational animation. The image is not a cutting-and-assembly template; the goal is readable connections.

Set the centre and four outward directions

Use an approximately 1600×1800 px portrait canvas with a pale warm-grey background. Put the centre slightly above the image midpoint, leaving room for the lower support. Sketch horizontal and vertical guides, placing tips upper right, lower right, lower left and upper left rather than crowding one beneath the centre. Join centre to tips with thin lines and zoom out to distinguish all four directions. Correct a short-looking direction locally rather than lengthening the other three to match. Guides are compositional aids, not final marks or real pinwheel measurements.

Give each blade an outward and folded surface

Choose coral red for the front and pale cream yellow for the back. Begin with the upper-right blade: make its outward surface broad and its inward-folded portion turn and narrow toward the centre. Give the other three the same rotational sense; do not alternate clockwise and counterclockwise folds. Trace each blade’s boundaries and use a light line at its surface change. If four separate sweets appear, reconnect each folded face to its own outward face instead of letting it float nearby. Leave texture and highlights out, point to each red/yellow pairing and confirm four blades.

Tuck folded tips beneath the central pin

Lead all four folded tips into one small area and cover their ends with a round pin. Its diameter should hide the junction without swallowing every turn. Outward tips must not also end at the pin rim as if four rigid boards were inserted into a disc. Hide the pin layer to inspect folded-face paths, then restore it for clean occlusion. Trace an unexplained fifth central triangle to a sketch remnant or unerased back-face edge instead of colouring it as a new blade. Suggest gentle central pressure without creating five separate centres.

Explain local overlaps with restrained shadows

Light the image from upper left. Add a narrow grey-red shadow near the lower central edge of a fold, using grey-brown on cream-yellow paper. Place shadows at occlusions, not equally around every triangle. Solve the upper-right blade first, then carry that logic to the other three. If thick plastic scoops appear, reduce edge thickness and shadow width. If flat patches merge, locate the fold and add a short occlusion shadow there. Paper may bend gently, but neither complete cylinders nor black cracks are needed to explain every connection.

Connect the support behind the pinwheel

Use muted wood brown for a support extending slightly diagonally downward from behind the centre. Draw its continuous path, then conceal the frontward portions beneath paper and pin. It must neither cross the foremost red paper nor grow from a blade tip. Hide the paper to check that its axis passes near the centre; restore it and remove stray dark ends at the junction. Keep the bottom inside the frame rather than tangent to the edge. If it resembles a spear, narrow the shaft and soften its pointed top. Readable support does not require invented bearings or washers.

Check rotational colour order separately from light

Hide shadows to inspect a consistent red/yellow rotational order, then restore them and check that upper-left lighting does not reverse on the lower-right blade. Similar colour order does not require identical brightness; facing directions may vary gently. Replace duplicated highlights that contradict lighting. Save a greyscale preview and check a readable centre and separated tips. If adjacent faces merge, separate their values rather than outlining the whole pinwheel. These two checks answer different questions: how the paper folds and where the light comes from.

Use a silhouette version to diagnose crossings

Save a single-colour silhouette with the small central circle. Count four outward ends, then follow four folded paths in the colour version. Correct silhouette count with incorrect paths usually calls for repaired internal boundaries, not a full restart. Correct paths with unreadable directions call for clearer negative spaces between tips. Leave breathing room near the support so the lower blade does not merge into an equally wide strip. Compare silhouette and colour at the same composition; changing tilt, palette and blade count together would hide which correction worked.

Inspect the pin and thin edges after export

Save the layered original and inspect the pin, four blade separations and support junction in a PNG at use size. If the centre becomes a black hole, lighten and slightly reduce its shadow rather than brightening every paper surface. Recover a lost fold with a local value difference, not heavier lines everywhere. Keep a numbered four-blade sketch for review and remove guides from the finish. A new oblique view requires reassessing occlusion, not simply compressing the frontal image horizontally. Judge static folds and connections, not real rotation or assembly safety.
