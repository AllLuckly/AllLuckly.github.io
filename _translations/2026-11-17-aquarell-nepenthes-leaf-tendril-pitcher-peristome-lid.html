Draw a Nepenthes pitcher at the end of a leaf-tip tendril, making its relationship to the leaf traceable. Choose a reference showing front wings and simplify silhouette and markings originally, without a species-identification claim. A pitcher is not a branch-borne flower or simply a lidded jug.

Reserve a complete leaf-to-pitcher path

Use an approximately 1600×2000 px portrait canvas, leaf above and hanging pitcher below, joined by a curved tendril. Sketch leaf midrib and pitcher axis first; orientations may differ but the connecting path must stay within the frame. Separate sketch, leaf/tendril, pitcher wall and mouth/lid, then save native work. Dimensions are compositional, not centimetre scaling. Omit soil, prey and busy background initially. This one-leaf/one-pitcher excerpt does not state an entire plant’s leaf or pitcher count.

Extend the midrib into a tendril, not a flower stalk

Run the midrib through the leaf and extend it beyond the tip into a thinner tendril curving to the pitcher attachment. Do not perforate the leaf centre or hang the pitcher from its lid. A modest bend suffices; if the reference coils, keep coherent occlusion rather than separate rings. Temporarily trace leaf–tip–tendril–pitcher with light arrows, checking continuity. Remove those arrows later so they cannot become extra physical twigs on the plant.

Give the pitcher walls without inventing a spout

Draw a narrower upper pitcher and slightly swollen lower body with a closed base, attaching the tendril near the base rather than as a straw through the mouth. Relate both sides to one volume, firmer near and softer far, without a basal cutaway or transparent stomach. Turn back edges and front markings around the selected pose. If it reads as a kettle, remove invented spout and handle and restore plant-wall contours instead of relying on a name label to conceal faulty structure.

Surround the opening with a rim and attach the lid behind

Draw an oblique opening with a continuous thickened peristome, separating inner darkness from outer wall. Attach the lid behind the opening and hold it above, retaining a visible window. Add neither metal hinge nor tightly plugged stopper. Lid and rim are not two petals; subtle values distinguish lid surfaces. If the lid hides the entire opening, adjust view or pose rather than move the mouth onto the front wall. No invented internal liquid level is needed, and a dark opening does not mean a pitcher full of black fluid.

Keep two front wings on the pitcher surface

Following the selected winged form, place two thin longitudinal front wings turning with the pitcher wall and merging toward upper and lower regions. They are neither separate large leaves nor birdlike lateral wings. Use selected fine fringe marks rather than heavy spines everywhere. Two belongs to this selected form, not every species or upper/lower pitcher. Check wall attachment in pale lines before colour. If wings resemble suspension ropes, soften dark lines and restore thin surfaces; the real tendril remains the leaf-tip-to-base connection.

Turn markings around volume without making an identification key

Use grey-green leaf, warmer green walls, selected dark-red markings and a deeper rim, keeping lid botanically related. Widen near markings and narrow them around edges instead of uniform dot stickers. Distinguish wing and tendril shadows. Keep a modest dark opening without glowing digestion effects. Read wall, rim and lid as three levels at fit-to-window size; remove excess spots before deleting connections. Markings identify neither species, cultivar nor toxicity, and no feeding, filling, touching or collecting is needed for the drawing.

Retrace every connection from leaf to pitcher

Hide spots and trace leaf tip through tendril to pitcher base, then wall to rim and rear mouth to lid. Reconnect a lid-attached tendril at the base through the sketch instead of shifting one shadow line. Give a floating lid its short botanical connection, not a metal hinge. Restore wing surfaces if they resemble another tendril. Preserve a failing copy and repair one relation before recounting one leaf, one pitcher, one main tendril and two selected wings. Document hidden detail without inferring absent organs or automatic counts.

Reopen with excerpt and reference scope

Save native work, hide check arrows and reopen a PNG. Read leaf–tendril–pitcher continuity at fit-to-window size, then rim thickness, lid attachment and wing contact enlarged. Increase tendril/background contrast if it disappears rather than add an invented supporting branch. Reopen leaf, tendril, wall and lid layers separately. Document a genus-level winged excerpt and omitted internals. Leave unperformed actions pending; this is neither automatic identification, observed prey capture nor cultivation guidance. Review asks whether attachments remain readable.
