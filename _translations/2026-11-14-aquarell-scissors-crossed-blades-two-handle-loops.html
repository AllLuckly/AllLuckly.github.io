Scissors drawn as two pointed leaves often lose the connection to the handle loops behind the pivot. Hand-draw an invented pair lying open in Aquarell, without paper or fingers. Trace two crossing components before metal and plastic. References identify blades, loops and pivot; dimensions and overlap are choices for this illustration.

Start with two paths through one pivot

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px landscape canvas. Place a pivot slightly right of centre, two right-hand tips and two left-hand loop centres. Draw two pale continuous paths from handles to tips, crossing at one pivot without fusing. Leave a paper-coloured wedge between the tips; a modest opening suffices. If it resembles tweezers, check for a mistaken tail-only connection. If it resembles pliers, lengthen blade shapes beyond short jaws. This oblique still life uses composition points, not measured cutting travel or opening angles.

Trace each component to its opposite-side tip

Build the upper handle, neck and blade extending downward beyond the pivot along the first path, then the lower handle and upward blade along the other. Keep temporary colours consistent across the pivot; swapping colours can give one handle both blades. Show visible overlap without transparency. Label components A and B on guides if useful, then trace both before removing labels. Slight curvature belongs to this invented silhouette; forcing every handle centre and tip onto a perfectly straight line is unnecessary.

Leave genuine openings inside both loops

Draw closed outer loops and paper-coloured inner holes with readable wall thickness. One hole may be larger, but neither should be cut by the neck or open into a notch. Keep any interior shadow distinguishable from plastic rather than filling the opening solid. Repair the connection band to the blade neck if loops look glued on like doughnuts. Remove accidental guide rods inside holes. Hole shapes serve this still life and establish no fit for a hand or measured ergonomic dimensions.

Separate broad blades from narrow edges

Give each blade a broad face and narrower inner cutting-edge suggestion, tapering to its tip. Do not put both cutting edges outside and explain the gap with shadows; establish overlap near the pivot. Strengthen the near contour slightly while retaining a separate far tip. Widen needle-like blades instead of thickening outlines. Reduce highlights that make metal resemble transparent glass. Depict exterior orientation only, without edge-angle sections or treating a bright line as proof of sharpness or material capability.

Keep the pivot cap from becoming another blade

Place a small circular or elliptical cap on the established crossing, oriented with the view. A narrow dark rim is enough; avoid a black disc swallowing half the scissors. Move an off-centre cap back to the intersection. Inspect a neck edge if a third metal tip appears. Screw slots are optional and cannot replace component tracing. The blades stack rather than penetrate each other; the near occlusion edge must stop the far outline cleanly, revealing only visible portions.

Use contact shadows to establish the resting pose

Use narrow contacts under loops and low blade sections, with shadows from upper-left light. Not every edge must touch paper; small raised areas may use gaps and soft shadows. Do not let a metal plane pass through paper beside thick plastic. Break and soften a shadow resembling a third loop. Use one main plastic colour plus restrained shade and metal highlights narrower than contours. At reduced size, two handles and two blades should precede shine. Omit wear specks that weaken structural contrast.

Check ownership before elegant curves

Hide material layers and trace the upper loop through the pivot to the lower tip, then the other component. If both end at one tip, repair continuity instead of hiding it with a larger cap. Keep two closed handle holes, two separate tips and no mistaken connection from the blade wedge to a handle hole. Restore materials and remove hidden lines at the pivot. Adjust placement and loop size if excessive symmetry looks like an emblem, without randomly swapping overlap. Record the exact faulty edge rather than merely “looks unnatural”.

Reopen and verify two handles, two blades and one pivot

Check two loops, two blades and one pivot, then enlarge for leaking hole colour, pivot drift and far edges piercing the near blade.
