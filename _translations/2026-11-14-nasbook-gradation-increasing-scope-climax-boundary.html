Three listed items do not automatically form gradation, and a final item is not automatically a plot climax. Read an original passage in Book Isle, using coverage scope as one comparison dimension, and retain differences in how teaching sources use the terms.

Preserve the full three-step sentence

Create chapter “The Night School’s Lights”: “She first brought light to one desk, then to one classroom, and finally to the entire night school. The donation record did not list the amount for each occasion.” Open a reading copy through available import controls, retaining the source and opening anchor. Copy all three clauses plus the limiting second sentence, not just desk/classroom/school nouns. Use external notes if highlighting is unavailable. Preserve wording, actor and location without assuming automatic rhetorical clause extraction.

Check broad and narrow uses of the term

The BYU rhetoric entry for climax distinguishes a general increasing arrangement from a narrower chained-word usage. Here gradation means progression along one dimension and does not require a clause-ending word to recur at the next beginning. Declare that usage rather than forcing different teaching traditions into one English label. Also distinguish plot climax, where narrative conflict peaks. This passage arranges action scope; a full plot arc would require further evidence and cannot be established by the word finally.

Declare coverage scope as the comparison dimension

List desk, classroom and entire night school. The dimension is illuminated spatial/collective scope, increasing under the ordinary containment relation desk within classroom within school. Do not replace it with ascending expenditure: sentence two explicitly withholds amounts. Equipment, existing facilities or donation arrangements could affect costs, so noun scope does not determine monetary order. Record increasing coverage as the supported conclusion and amounts as unknown, keeping textual evidence distinct from assumed quantities.

Use transitions to check order rather than item count

First, then and finally organise progression, with the same actor and similar predicates stabilising comparison. Number clauses 1,2,3 and connect their scope changes. Use punctuation and syntax rather than display lines to define units. Order and relative scale jointly establish progression; three numbers or nouns alone are insufficient. Similar clause structure does not itself establish stronger emotion. An encouraging effect is a possible interpretation, not an identical response guaranteed in every reader.

Reverse the order and add an unordered control

Passage B reverses scope: “She first brought light to the entire night school, then to one classroom, and finally to one desk.” The same dimension now decreases and is not ascending gradation. Passage C lists “She brought a lamp, a map and a cup,” without a clear common size/intensity scale. Retain all versions: B changes order, C changes objects. Do not change several conditions and attribute the result to one word. Reversal may have expressive value; the task checks direction rather than declaring one version universally better.

Check word chaining separately from increasing scope

Add “Light brought readers; readers brought questions; questions moved discussion forward.” Clause-final terms recur at subsequent beginnings, establishing word chaining. Light/readers/questions/discussion do not share the original’s clear spatial-containment order, so chaining alone does not prove expanding scope. Separate comparative sequence from lexical relay. If your textbook reserves climax for chaining, declare that definition and call the original a scope progression, instead of presenting differing terminology as evidence of misreading the text.

Troubleshoot unsupported importance claims

Greatest coverage does not establish moral importance, cost or elapsed time. Check amounts, durations and evaluative words separately; absent evidence remains unspecified. A sequence may increase on one dimension with no comparable data on another. If an import drops entire, consult the original rather than filling the text from old notes. Preserve the donation-limit sentence in excerpt cards to prevent overinterpretation. Shelf sorting or ratings do not supply the passage’s comparison scale, and book stars are not clause-intensity scores.

Reopen and reconstruct order before terminology

Save notes, leave and reopen at the opening anchor. Hide labels and write coverage scope, desk→classroom→school, and unspecified amounts before applying this lesson’s ascending usage. Check the reversed passage decreases, the list lacks a shared scale and the chain establishes lexical relay only. If font changes alter pages, use opening words instead of old display line numbers. Explain dimension, order, limits and terminology, without implying automatic gradation classification, inferred donation amounts or proof of a whole-story plot climax.
