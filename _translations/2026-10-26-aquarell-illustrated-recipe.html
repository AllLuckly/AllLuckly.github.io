An illustrated recipe must be understandable before it is beautiful. Use a fictional fruit-yoghurt bowl to practise ingredient quantities, three actions and a finished-dish image. Example amounts are layout content rather than nutritional advice; verify all text against your own recipe before actual use.

Set up a separate study

Use an approximately 1600×2200 px portrait canvas with areas for title, ingredients, three actions and final bowl. Separate drawings, labels and step numbers. Test the smallest quantities for legibility and map the content list to the regions before adding food colours.

Read simple boxes, circles and arrows from top to bottom to catch missing actions or unexplained ingredients. Save the wireframe. When changing example amounts, update text before checking the corresponding drawings; an attractive recipe still fails if words and pictures disagree.

Write a complete content list first

List the title, ingredients and quantities, three actions and a finished-dish note. Use yoghurt, fruit and oats with explicit units or portions. Keep quantities consistent between words and pictures. Give each step one short primary verb rather than combining several operations.

Choose a reading direction before format. Here, use title above, ingredients and actions in the middle and the finished bowl below. Reserve room for the longest ingredient name before drawing icons. Keep quantities legible at normal size instead of squeezing them around completed pictures.

Balance ingredient drawings with their labels

Use a yoghurt cup, fruit pieces and a small oat pile with comparable visual weight. Illustrations do not measure volume; the adjacent labels give quantities. Keep each label unambiguously close to its ingredient rather than placing a number between two items. Establish correspondence before texture.

Repeat ingredient colours in the steps and final bowl, even when the fruit changes shape after cutting. Wrap a long label naturally while preserving its font size. Avoid inconsistent recolouring or shrinking only the longest label until it becomes unreadable.

Show a clear change through the three actions

Show fruit becoming pieces, yoghurt entering the bowl, then fruit and oats added on top. Repeat identifiable bowl or tool features for continuity. Use arrows only where movement needs clarification. Keep step numbers separate from quantity numbers.

A spoon handle or tilted container may describe an action without a full person. Correct any arrow that leads to the wrong destination. Review once with the words covered and once with the pictures covered: each should contribute useful information without contradicting the other.

Keep the finished dish consistent with the ingredients

Make the final bowl larger as the visual focus. Establish its ellipse and thickness before filling it. Do not suddenly add mint, honey or flowers absent from the list; update ingredients and steps first if they belong. Accuracy matters more than decorative abundance.

Concentrate detail where fruit meets yoghurt and simplify elsewhere. The oats need recognisable texture, not a counted drawing of every flake. Reduce excessive finish detail if it overwhelms the actions. Establish hierarchy among title, sequence, quantities and final dish rather than making everything equally large.

Proof the content independently of appearance

Ignore the colours and read ingredients, units, actions and final components in order. Check consistent units, copied numbers and clearly labelled examples. Mentally follow the process and identify objects without an origin or actions without a result. Do this before painting to catch missing steps early.

Check the smallest labels on a normally displayed phone export. Remove nonessential notes and decoration before enlarging key text. Replace fictional content and verify the real recipe before publishing. For another combination, reuse the layout but validate the content structure before drawing the ingredients.

Review and plan the next study

Read the smallest labels at normal size, compare each action, then check the final image’s area small. Ignore colour for a separate units-and-numbering pass. Neat appearance is not evidence of accuracy; the test is correspondence among ingredients, actions and result.

Replace fictional content before actual use and name example and real versions separately. Reopen the PNG and read the process in order. Keep labels editable. Next, change one fruit and track it through list, steps and final dish to practise a consistent content update.
