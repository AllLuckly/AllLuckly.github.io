A pale tennis-ball seam drawn as a flat S can turn the sphere into a patterned disc. Invent an oblique view in which one closed seam turns over the surface, disappears and reappears. The focus is surface attachment, not competition specifications. Establish the route and broad shading before restrained fuzz; Aquarell does not generate a spherical texture map or determine how hidden sections connect.

Finish a seam-free sphere first

Use an approximately 1600×1600 px canvas with a mid blue-grey background and a roughly circular hand-drawn silhouette. Choose a muted yellow-green base, lighting the upper left and shading the lower right, with a short dark tabletop contact. Separate sketch, sphere, seam and fuzz. Hide the sketch and inspect volume rather than keeping a uniform centre inside a black ring. If it resembles a disc, broaden the mid-green transition and weaken the continuous outline. If it glows like a lamp, reduce the brightest area and restore a stable middle colour. The sphere must work without its seam before the route has a surface to follow.

Connect the closed route in the sketch

Sketch a curving double-arc route over the sphere, broader on the near portion and compressed toward the side, imagining its rear continuation joining back. Use different sketch colours for visible and hidden sections, omitting hidden lines from the final. Trace one full route without jumping to an unrelated decorative stroke. This is one simplified view, not an exact reproduction of a brand’s seam. If the marks become two disconnected smiling arcs, determine their connection behind the ball before retaining only visible sections. Adding more curves would create complexity without resolving continuity.

Change apparent width at the side turn

Paint the seam in warm ivory, slightly wider near the viewer, narrowing toward the silhouette and disappearing plausibly rather than cutting beyond it at constant width. Draw both boundaries before filling, checking that both curve with the sphere. The nearest arc need not pass through the geometric centre, provided the viewpoint stays consistent. If the seam resembles a soft tube attached outside, narrow it and remove its continuous highlight. If it resembles a deep split in the shell, weaken the black shadows on both sides and restore its identity as a pale material band. Slight recession is acceptable; a hole dividing the sphere is not.

Carry lighting continuously across the pale band

With upper-left light, the lit seam is paler while its lower-right continuation darkens slightly; it should not glow pure white throughout. Surface shading continues from green areas across the seam despite the material-colour change. Compare with the seam hidden and restored: broad volume should remain similar. If restoring it flattens the sphere, correct an overly bright band or faulty wrapping direction rather than adding more black to the ball as a remedy. Use a narrow dark edge only in selected places to suggest slight recession. Equal cast shadows in every direction would imply a separate raised cord.

Group a little fuzz near the contour

Use short marks a little lighter or darker than the ball, starting with two or three small fuzzy groups on the lit contour. Vary their directions rather than making equal outward spikes. Interior texture should be lower contrast and grouped within the broad shading, not dense salt-like dots everywhere. Inspect at normal size before refining a few broken marks at zoom; fuzz must not conceal the pale seam. If it looks like a plush toy, shorten strokes and reduce contour fibres. If too plastic-smooth, add tiny variation in one lit midtone patch rather than noise over the entire image. Mark density should describe material, not fill every empty space.

Check occlusion and contact edges separately

A seam’s disappearance at the side is different from tabletop contact. Hide shadow first and ensure the seam ends inside the silhouette without accidentally exposing rear sketch lines. Restore shadow and check that the bottom meets the table and the shadow extends lower right without entering the lit sphere. If the ball floats, adjust bottom edge and contact shadow rather than dragging the seam downward like a support. If seam and shadow merge into one dark line, weaken one segment so their information separates. Identify whether each mark belongs to surface or tabletop instead of allowing every arc to become one decorative pattern.

Compare against a flat-S counterexample

In a copy, replace the seam with a constant-width S facing the canvas while retaining colour and lighting. Compare which version better shows the surface turning. Do not change background and fuzz simultaneously, which would obscure the cause. If the original also looks flat, correct side compression and disappearance positions rather than adding another horizontal line as a volume symbol. Hide all texture and compare one green mass plus one pale route: volume should survive this reduction. The counterexample is a personal diagnostic, not an automatic application assessment or a claim of measurements against real sporting equipment.

Check the pale seam after export

Save the native layered artwork and inspect the narrowest seam, brightest seam and bottom in the PNG at use size. If the pale band merges with the background into a side notch, locally darken it or retain a green edge rather than outlining the entire ball in black. If reduced fuzz becomes a dirty contour, remove some marks before exporting again instead of covering blur with high-contrast dots. Keep a texture-free structural preview. In a future invented view, turn the seam and reconsider hidden portions. Record a precise flat-looking segment, such as an upper-right arc that does not narrow, rather than blaming every problem on an unrealistic brush.
