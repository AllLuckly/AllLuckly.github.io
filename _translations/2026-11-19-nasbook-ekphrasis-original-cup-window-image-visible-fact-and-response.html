A poem may respond to an artwork rather than merely inventory its objects. Externally create the original geometric image Cup and Window and place it beside four original English lines, then trace their relation in Book Isle. The white cup and blue circle are visible anchors; other statements add imagination. Keep these kinds of evidence distinct without requiring App image interpretation.

Create one artwork version externally

The SVG defines a 200×120 canvas, beige background, upper-right blue circle, lower-left white cup and rightward gray shadow, all original for this exercise. Read four lines and locate their visible anchors

The poem has four authored lines regardless of PDF wrapping. White cup, circle of blue and below in L1 match the image. Waits assigns a stance rather than measures a cup’s mind. L2’s longer reach can be compared with the authored horizontal widths 80 and 35; this designed planar extension does not prove a real three-dimensional cup would cast that shadow in natural light. Pair anchors first, then mark linguistic additions.

L1 A white cup waits below a circle of blue.
L2 Its shadow reaches farther than its rim.
L3 I lend the painted room a sailor's name.
L4 The painted wall returns no spoken word.

L4’s non-answer may respond to the speaker’s imagination; a static drawing has no recording, so this is not measured acoustic silence. Ground ekphrasis in the artwork relation

Use the scope of poetic description, interpretation or response to visual art. The explicitly supplied original drawing counts as the artwork; a famous painting is unnecessary. Response may imagine, question or alter perspective instead of listing objects, but any poem mentioning a cup and window is not automatically ekphrastic. Here the paired version, title, painted wording and two matching anchors support the relation. The term has a broader descriptive history not exhausted by this worksheet.

Use two controls against keyword-only decisions

First add “a boat appears in the image” to the interpretation ledger. Comparison with image A should restore “the poem may suggest seafaring, but no boat is drawn,” rather than alter the image to fit a story. Second remove both painted tokens from the poem and reassess the relation using its paired title and creation note; loss of a keyword alone does not destroy an artwork response. An unrelated observation of a real cup without an artwork relation is not proved ekphrastic merely by similar objects. Test evidence, not a permanent keyword switch.
