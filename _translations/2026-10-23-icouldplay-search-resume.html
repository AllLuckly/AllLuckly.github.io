As your video collection grows, two common questions are “I imported it, so why can't I find it?” and “Why did yesterday's half-watched video start over today?” In iCouldPlay, search scope, media sources, and playback progress serve different purposes. This guide first locates the same item, then checks its resume behavior.

First identify the source holding the video

Recall whether the target file came from a local import, a NAS folder, or a media server such as Emby. Open that source and confirm that it is currently accessible. If the server is offline or the account has expired, empty search results do not prove that the video was deleted, and trying more keywords cannot repair the connection.

The same work may have copies in multiple sources. Select the source you watched last before entering keywords, so you do not start from a different card with the same poster. If you have forgotten the source, check recent viewing records or your own filing system first, then narrow the search.

A remote file that has not entered the library may not appear in library search. First check whether it has been browsed or scanned. “It exists in the server folder” does not automatically mean “the current search scope includes it.” To organize a file source, see Folder scanning.

Start searching with a short, distinctive term

Open library search, enter two or three distinctive characters or a word from the title, and submit the search. Start without the extension, resolution, year, or multiple decorative symbols; find the most recognizable part first. A long full filename may differ from the title displayed in the library.

If there are no results, try a common translated title, the original title, or words you used when naming the file. Change only one kind of clue at a time and wait for the search to finish. When a remote source is being queried, a search still in progress after typing is different from a completed search with no results.

If the current version offers scopes such as all sources and current source, check which is selected. If a source failure is shown, open that source separately to check its connection. Do not mistake completed results from some sources for confirmation that every other source was also searched.

Open the details to confirm the item, then play it

After finding cards with the same title, check their source, year, and episode information. A movie and a television series with the same name may both appear, and a particular episode may be grouped under a series detail page. Confirm that it is the item you watched before trying to resume; this reduces the chance of mistaking a new copy for lost progress.

Your own videos may have no poster but still have accurate names. Do not identify content from images alone; matched posters can also be wrong. If a card's metadata conflicts with the video, first use Matching-correction tutorial Identify the source so you do not repeatedly save progress on the wrong item.

For a series, open the relevant series and check the season and episode. A search result for the whole series does not mean that entering it will play the episode you want. Check the currently selected item before deciding whether to resume the previous scene.

Use a midpoint to test resume playback

Choose an ordinary video, play to an easily remembered scene partway through, and note the approximate time. Leave through the player's normal Back control, return to the library, and reopen the same item. Check whether playback resumes at a reasonable position rather than judging only by a progress bar on the card.

For the first test, choose neither the opening few seconds nor a point near the end. The app or server may handle starting, completion, and continued viewing differently, so a midpoint is better for checking basic resume behavior. Do not assume progress is saved to an exact second; check whether it returns reasonably close to the scene you noted.

Keep the same source and file throughout the test. Watching half of a local copy and then opening another copy on a server does not necessarily share progress. Even if the title and poster match, verify the two sources separately.

Check the effect of “Always Play from Beginning”

In player settings, check “Always Play from Beginning.” If it is enabled, reopening a video at the start may be exactly what the current setting requests. Note the original value, adjust it to your viewing habits, then reopen the same item to test.

For courses or movies, you usually want to resume where you stopped; for short practice clips played repeatedly, you may prefer to start from the beginning each time. Choose settings around your own use rather than treating one behavior as best for all content.

If playback still does not resume after disabling start-from-beginning, continue checking the item's identity and source records rather than repeatedly toggling the switch. Server media, local files, and different playback paths may save progress differently; testing them separately makes the problem easier to describe.

When the item does not appear in Continue Watching

First confirm that the video played for a while and that you exited normally. Then check whether the current list is restricted by source, type, or recent items. Content played to the end may no longer appear as “in progress,” so a disappearing card alone does not prove that its record is damaged.

Clearing playback history affects viewing records. If you previously cleared caches or history, recall which kind of data you selected. A media file still being present does not guarantee that its playback progress remains. Before future cleanup, read the operation's description and treat files, caches, and history separately.

If you have just renamed, moved, or reimported the file, the app may be dealing with a new path. Check the original item, then compare the new one; do not immediately delete all history to “resync.” If the old position is still available, note any important timestamps before continuing to organize the files.

Build a daily routine that makes videos easy to find again

When you finish watching a long video, exit normally. Next time, start from Continue Watching under the same source. For a series, check the season and episode, and note the current scene before switching to another version of the file. This small habit is more reliable than relying entirely on recognizing a poster.
