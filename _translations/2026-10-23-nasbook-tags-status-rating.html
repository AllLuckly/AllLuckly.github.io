Shelf organization need not begin with dozens of folders. In a growing personal library, tags describe what a book concerns, reading status describes your stage, and ratings describe your response afterward. Giving them separate jobs makes Book Isle's filters easier to use.

Define the roles of the three information types first

Practice with six books, ideally including fiction, reference material, and current reading. Choose three subject tags, such as Literature, Photography, and Work Reference. Confirm each book's actual reading state, and rate only books on which you have formed an opinion. Do not create three near-synonymous tags for Pending, Want to Read, and Unread; they make filters increasingly vague.

A book can have several tags, while status represents its current stage. Photography essays may belong to both Photography and Literature without needing duplicate book records. Establish rules with six books and confirm you still understand them a week later before applying them library-wide. This is usually steadier than organizing hundreds at once.

Give one book clear tags

Long-press a book on the shelf, open Book Details, and find Manage Tags. Select an existing tag or create a short, clear one through the actual interface. Add Photography to the practice book, confirm it in details, then filter the shelf by that tag. Completion means finding the book again through its tag, not merely seeing the editor close.

Keep tag names stable. Use Work Reference consistently rather than Work Materials, For Work, and Office Reference together. Broad tags can be split later; overly fine ones create hesitation during import. Identify at least two or three books for each tag first. For one incidental document, searching its title may be easier.

Use reading status to plan the current list

Open Edit Reading Status in details and select the stage matching reality. Mark completed works Read, newly started ones In Progress, and untouched ones Unread. Do not mark completion early to improve statistics. Status primarily helps choose your next reading.

One practical approach is to keep two books In Progress: one long work and one short reference item. Leave others Unread for now. Review status weekly and move abandoned books to an appropriate state. Software cannot distinguish buying from reading on your behalf. Manual status must reflect real habits.

Keep ratings personal

Open Edit Rating and choose a simple scale first. The highest level might mean you would reread it, the middle that it was useful but not urgent to revisit, and a lower level that it did not meet current needs. Ratings need not pretend to be objective or agree with public websites. Their greatest value is helping your future self decide what to reopen.

Unread works can remain unrated; Unrated is not the lowest score. Reference books may also need different criteria from novels. A manual that need not be read cover to cover can be extremely useful. A note such as 'Chapter three is clear; consult the appendix later' explains a rating better than stars alone.

Verify a small sample before batch editing

After confirming the single-book process, select two or three related books in Edit Shelf and apply the relevant tag, status, or rating action. Check the selection count and covers before applying so one intended volume does not become an entire set. For ratings and reading states especially, a shared series does not mean every volume is finished or deserves the same judgment.

After batch editing, return to the normal shelf and combine tag and status filters, such as Photography and Unread. Only genuinely matching records should remain. If empty, remove one condition to determine whether the tag failed to save or the status is too restrictive. Filtering changes the visible scope, not which other books are stored on the device.

Test the rules with a week of actual reading

After a week, ask whether you actually found books through tags and still remember each tag's meaning. Do not keep adding unused subdivisions. Consolidate duplicates gradually, checking existing associations before deleting or renaming. The goal is easier choice, not forcing every book into a complex structure.

At the weekend, continue one book from In Progress and update finished books with a status and one-sentence assessment. Tags then handle long-term subjects, status handles near-term plans, and ratings support reflection. If a book has been absent because of filtering for a long time, clear the filters to find it and check for mistakenly assigned tags or status.

Questions and limitations

Can tags replace file backups? No. Tags and status are management information. Original book files still need separate preservation. Adding a book to a tag does not copy its file. Read deletion prompts carefully rather than assuming another tag contains an independent copy.

Will ratings automatically be published online? These ratings organize a private shelf. Selecting stars does not by itself submit a review to a public reading website. If an external synchronization feature is used later, check its scope and confirmation process separately. Local stars do not prove an external account was updated.

Why does the Read list contain books with incomplete progress? Manual status and reading position express different things. You may skim, consult selected passages, or mark completion manually. Decide how you use Read before standardizing it. Do not alter actual progress arbitrarily just to make numbers agree.

Continue organizing

If titles and series remain disorganized, start with Book metadata organization; to retain your reflections, continue with Highlights and notes. Clear classification rules make later search and synchronization checks easier.
