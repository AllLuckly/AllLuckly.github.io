Use a permitted dorsal reference to draw a two-tailed swallowtail’s wing patterns and hindwing tails.

Choose the subject

Select a permitted photograph showing a two-tailed swallowtail’s dorsal wings spread, and record its source, view and any obscured parts. The National Park Service describes roughly parallel black markings on yellow wings and the tail-like extensions of some swallowtails’ hindwings. Individual posture and wear affect the visible outline; depict the chosen photograph without inventing a standard shape for hidden areas.

Set up and save

Create an approximately 1600 by 1400 landscape canvas in Aquarell; dimensions are adjustable practice choices. Use the installed version’s visible layer controls to separate sketch, wing base colors, markings, body and background, then save an editable original. Leave margins around all four wings and hindwing tails. If the photo crops a wingtip, select a more complete reference instead of guessing its extension.

Establish the body axis

Mark the head, thorax and abdomen positions with light lines, then note the two antenna directions. Compare thorax placement between the four wings and the abdomen length before adding black marks. Use the axis to check that wing roots join the thorax, without forcing a natural reference into perfect bilateral mirroring: angle, perspective or wear may create differences.

Block forewings and hindwings

From the thorax, block the visible outer edges of left and right forewings and hindwings. Compare the length, width and boundary of the four large shapes. Fore- and hindwings may partly overlap in the reference: stop visible boundary lines at the overlap rather than drawing every hidden edge through it. Zoom out to check that all four wings attach to one body and that tips have margins. Temporarily sketch the four wings in different light colors and trace each outline back to the thorax; restore a unified sketch color afterward rather than retaining these guides in the final image.

Locate the tails

Find the slender tails on the hindwing edges in the reference and draw their roots and directions as extensions of those edges. Do not attach them to the abdomen or mistake forewing tips for tails. If one is obscured or worn in the photograph, draw only what is visible. Zoom in to check continuity with the hindwing and out to ensure a fine tip does not vanish into the background.

Lay in yellow wings

Apply a soft yellow on the wing base layer, keeping fore- and hindwing edges clean and leaving fine markings and dark borders for later. Let the reference lighting determine values rather than copying identical highlights to both sides. If one side is shaded, change its value modestly while preserving the broad shape. If bright yellow loses its edge, first try a neutral-gray background to distinguish insufficient background contrast from an overly light wing. Hide markings temporarily and verify yellow is distinct from body and background.

Place the roughly parallel marks

Follow the reference to place the direction and spacing of the major dark marks on the yellow forewings. Start with the most visible few and compare their distance from wing roots and margins. Roughly parallel does not mean mechanically identical length and width. Where a stripe thickens or joins a dark margin in the photo, follow that visible form rather than leaving marks floating outside the wing.

Handle margins and small color patches

Observe dark hindwing margins and any small blue or orange markings visible in the reference. Compare large color regions before adding tiny patches. Not every photograph reveals all colors, so do not fill gaps from another view. Let the dark border follow wing turns while keeping tail roots connected; if it consumes too much yellow, correct the boundary before adding detail.

Compare both sides and body

Hide the background and shrink the view to check the head-thorax-abdomen relationship, four wing roots, spread angles, hindwing tails and major stripes. Restore the background and check overall legibility. If wings appear pasted onto the abdomen, return to the sketch to relocate their roots. If one side appears larger, compare the original perspective before adjusting rather than automatically mirroring it.

Troubleshoot and export

If stripes and veins become a dark mass, remove small lines and retain yellow gaps between the main marks. If tails disappear, adjust the background value before thickening a whole wing. Save the editable original and export a separate PNG or JPEG using a control visible in the installed version. Reopen it to inspect tips, tails, colors and cropping, and record the reference; this tutorial claims no device or print test. If tails remain indistinct in the export, inspect linework and background at the same scale in the original and export again instead of substituting an enlarged screenshot for the canvas inspection.

Compare backgrounds

Duplicate the editable original and try a light gray and a medium neutral background without changing the butterfly silhouette or pattern. Compare the readability of yellow wings and fine tails on both. View exports at the same size and check whether dark margins obscure the body or erase yellow gaps. Keep the original and comparison copies so a later session can revisit wing roots, overlaps and values.
