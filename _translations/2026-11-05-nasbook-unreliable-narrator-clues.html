A character remembering an event does not mean the text independently establishes it. Select three questionable narrative passages in Book Isle, recording who speaks, when and how other passages present the event, while retaining a provisional personal interpretation. This is manual close reading, not automatic unreliable-narrator detection or a whole-book verdict from a few clues.

Choose one edition and one specific question

Choose one edition of a novel you are entitled to read and record title, author, translator and source. Limit the question—for example, whether a character actually witnessed a meeting. Record the chapter reached and avoid searching later revelations for premature evidence. Contents, search and notes are locators whose scope depends on the current format. For unselectable scans, record chapter/image page externally instead of claiming exact text anchors. A narrow question preserves your reading position and prevents all apparent contradictions becoming one vague untrustworthy-character label.

Record the speaker and narrative layer first

Read surrounding text to distinguish first-person narration, dialogue, reported speech and dream or memory. Quote only the short fragment needed and label paraphrase separately; reporting another person is not direct witnessing. Record addressee and timeline position, distinguishing a later account from an event presented as seen at the time. Narrator and author differ too: a fictional judgement is not automatically the author’s real-world position. Retain the first account even without a conflict. Its purpose is an attributable statement, not mandatory proof of a lie.

Find two relevant passages rather than identical words alone

Within chapters already read, locate two more passages about the same meeting, using distinctive phrases only where search is supported and reading full contexts. One may be another character’s account, another an action or setting presented in narration; identify each rather than calling both independent facts. Matching words may refer to another meeting, so verify people, time and place. If nothing is found, record that limitation instead of inventing a missing scene or reading ahead merely to reach three entries. Three is a manageable comparison scope, not a reason to manufacture evidence.

Separate accounts, textual presentation and inference

Use separate personal categories for a character’s account, what the text presents and your inference, each with locator and necessary short wording. Presentation may itself be viewpoint-limited, so do not call that column absolute truth. An account of being present throughout and a passage saying someone arrived later can be an unexplained difference; an unspecified arrival time remains unspecified. Retain clues both supporting and weakening the hypothesis. A wide table may live externally while app notes retain sources; no specialised narrative-analysis database is assumed.

Check scope and memory before calling a contradiction

For apparently opposite passages, verify day, person and time span, then omitted negation, exceptions or reporting layers. Uncertain memory, limited knowledge, concealment and deliberate lying are different possibilities; the record does not automatically choose among them. Narrative omission does not prove an event absent, and later information may clarify it without authorising a confident guess now. Phrase an investigable question, such as what occurred before arrival, instead of a liar label. This constrains personal interpretation without prescribing one mandatory literary reading.

Date earlier interpretations when later reading changes them

For a later clue, add its locator and source before revisiting the old explanation rather than erasing it. Distinguish former provisional view, revised view and reason so the evolution remains visible. Resolving this misunderstanding does not establish the character’s universal reliability. Mark ending-related records for spoilers and keep them separate from early-reading cards when sharing. Use available notes for essential text and an external log for complex revision history without assuming automatic reasoning-history tracking or contradiction merging.

Retrieve the passages from your record after leaving them

Leave the reading page and retrieve the three located passages from the record alone, rereading context for speaker and narrative layer. Layout changes can alter screen pages, so chapters and short phrases are stronger locators than percentages. If a direct jump fails, use retained clues before deleting notes. Export important records through an available method and inspect distinctions between quotations, interpretations and locations externally. Keep the book separately; notes are not a complete novel backup. List verified and unresolved items separately rather than equating a full table with a solved question.

Preserve uncertainty when clues still conflict

For unresolved conflict, check editions and misattributed speakers before arguing from memory. Search failures need word-form and text-layer checks, not a claim the book never mentions it. Narrow an expanding log back to one question instead of deleting inconvenient qualifications. Deliberate ambiguity may remain open without a final verdict. A useful rereading record explains who said what, what else the text presents, why your interpretation remains provisional and which evidence is missing; it does not need an app-assigned character label.
