Image favorites keep a comic page worth revisiting as a personal reading reference, such as a composition, a character's appearance, or a visual detail. This differs from favoriting a whole comic, saving to Photos, or creating a page bookmark. This guide favorites two pages from your reading, then finds them by work and verifies their sources.

Choose two clear pages first

Open an online work you are authorized to read whose current source supports image favorites. Choose an ordinary single page and a page in another chapter. Local-file pages may report that image favorites are not currently supported; use a page bookmark or an explicit save-image entry instead. These save different things, so check their results separately. Note the title, chapter, and page number. Start in single-page view to avoid uncertainty about which image a spread's button targets. If an image has not fully loaded, wait until it is clear before favoriting it.

Personal favorites do not change a work's copyright or grant permission to distribute the full set of images publicly. For personal review, retain the work, author, and chapter details so you can find the context again. For public discussion of composition, select material within the actual authorization and an appropriate scope, retaining the author and work information rather than treating an in-app favorite as permission to share.

Use image favorites in the reader

Reveal the reader controls and find the heart or “Favorite” action for images. This acts on the current reading page, unlike the whole-work favorite in its detail page. Tap and observe the icon or feedback, then choose a page in another chapter and repeat. Do not rapidly tap the same heart repeatedly, as tapping it again may remove the favorite.

If the current version supports a quick favorite gesture, verify the result with the button first before enabling the gesture. Gesture direction may relate to reading direction; trying it while turning pages on the first attempt invites mistakes. Knowing which page was favorited matters more than speed, especially with continuous long images or two-page mode.

Review pages through Image Favorites

Return to “Image Favorites” on the home screen or wherever your current configuration provides it, and find the work. The list groups favorited pages by work. Check title and source before viewing the images. Arbitrary custom favorite folders may not be offered, so start with work grouping, search, and sorting rather than looking for a folder-management button that is not present.

Open one image and compare it with the intended page, checking chapter and position clues. Some entries open work details when tapped; image viewing and returning to the reader may be separate menu actions. Choose “View Image” or the corresponding action in the current interface. Reaching work details does not mean favoriting failed; continue to the correct viewing entry.

Narrow the list with search and time filters

Once the collection grows, use search on the Image Favorites page, starting with a distinctive part of a work's title. If its sorting panel offers time ranges or count conditions, set one condition first and observe the result. For example, view works favorited in the past week rather than combining date, title, and count filters and confronting an empty list.

If the result is empty, clear the restrictions first and confirm that the original record remains. A filter only changes the current display; it does not delete images from favorites data. Same-named works from different sources may appear separately, so checking source and chapter helps distinguish them. Favorite counts can identify works you revisit often, but cannot directly measure quality or reading completion.

Remove one favorite you no longer need

Return to that page in the reader and use its favorite button again, or remove the target through the currently available image-favorites management action. Then return to the list and confirm that only this image was removed while the favorite from the other chapter remains. Before multi-select deletion, check whether you selected individual images or a group under a whole work to avoid deleting the wrong scope.

Keep a few meaningful pages per work rather than favoriting every page. If your goal is simply to remember where to resume, reading history or page bookmarks are more direct. Image favorites suit reviewing visual content. Choosing the appropriate entry point reduces duplicate records and later cleanup.

An image favorite is not a separate offline file

A favorite record may contain source, chapter, and image information, but that does not guarantee the original image can be retrieved in every offline situation. To retain a page you are authorized to save over the long term, use an explicit “Save Image” function and reopen the file in Photos or its destination to check it. Saving externally and favoriting within the app are separate workflows.

Changes to a remote source may invalidate an old image address or chapter. A favorite can remain a useful reference, but does not guarantee permanent access. Keep original files for important pages of your own creative work instead of retaining only reader favorites. Before clearing caches or moving devices, check image data against the actual sync and backup scope; migrating list names alone is not enough.

Frequently asked questions

Why did favoriting a page not put it in Photos? An in-app image favorite is different from saving an image through the system. To put it in Photos, use an explicit save action, grant the necessary permission, and check the actual file in system Photos. A changed heart icon does not mean the image has been exported.

In two-page mode, is the left or right page favorited? That depends on how the current reader handles the target. For a first test, switch to single-page mode or use an entry point that explicitly identifies an image, then verify it in Image Favorites. Do not save spreads in bulk without confirming the target.

Why does an image favorite have a record but fail to load? The source may be offline, its address may have changed, or the local image cache may be incomplete. First check whether the original page of the same work opens, then assess the favorite record itself. A record's presence cannot replace access to the original content.
