Paint an imaginary columnar basalt face viewed slightly above some exposed ends, showing irregular polygonal tops and long descending sides. The U.S. National Park Service explains that cooling, contracting lava can form columnar joints and that not all columns are perfect hexagons. This exercise does not reproduce a particular site or establish real rock age, strength or climbing safety.

Establish rock direction and visible height

Create an approximately 1900 × 1500 landscape canvas, placing the rock face just left of center with background above and room for fragments below. Block in the whole mass lightly, then sketch several generally downward but not perfectly parallel guides. Separate sketch, background, tops, near columns, far columns, fragments and texture and save an editable draft. Column count and curvature are artistic choices, not measurable site dimensions. If the reduced image looks like striped wallpaper, first check column direction and top-to-side correspondence.

Arrange irregular polygons on top

Draw a small group of neighboring tops with differing side counts, including five and six, and allow side lengths to vary slightly. Adjacent faces share one edge rather than two misaligned lines. Mark six corners on a central top and place a five-sided neighbor as a check. Do not claim every natural column is strictly hexagonal or that a pentagon identifies a particular site. Light temporary numbering only checks side counts and can be hidden afterward. If a shared edge has two different endpoints, establish the junction again before coloring the adjacent faces, checking for accidental white gaps at the junction.

Extend sides from visible top corners

Carry visible near-side corners downward to form elongated polygonal faces sharing edges with the tops. Columns at the frame edge may be partly obscured; do not pull all corners toward one point like a spire. Pick two neighbors and identify their tops, shared edge and downward sides before extending the group. If a side emerges from the center of a top, repair its corner instead of covering the error with cracks.

Use three gray-brown values for light and turns

Assume upper-left light. Use lighter gray-brown on lit tops and middle or darker gray on sides according to direction; neighboring lit columns may differ slightly to reveal boundaries. Do not outline every joint with the same thick black line: natural rock and illustrative dark seams are not identical. If near and far columns merge, reduce distant contrast or adjust adjacent color planes rather than turning every edge white.

Let cracks follow columns instead of crossing everything

Add short cracks to a few sides, turning marks with the face or local break and reconsidering depth at shared edges. Avoid one dark line crossing every column indiscriminately or the same pattern on each face. Add irregular fragments at the base, touching ground and partly hiding column feet. These are pictorial elements, not a diagnosis of actual rockfall risk.

In a copy hide all surface cracks and trace the central six-sided top, a shared neighboring edge and one descending side using only the color planes. Restore texture and check that structure remains readable. If cracks dominate the outline, lower their contrast. Keep the prior draft and fix junctions individually, so changing both texture and perspective at once does not obscure what helped.

Separate top tiling from vertical side rhythm

On top, adjacent polygons should form a connected surface rather than overlap like separate stickers. On the side, long edges lead toward the ground, with apparent width varying by depth. A six-sided top does not require all six side edges to be fully visible from the chosen viewpoint. If top and side look unrelated, mark one shared edge, check neighboring colors and reduce other interfering lines.

Hide layers to inspect connections

Hide fragments, cracks and highlights in turn. Check that each visible top sits at the upper end of its side and that column feet meet ground. If blue background appears through columns intended to meet, adjust local boundaries or layer order; consult sketch lines if unsure whether overlap is intended. Restore fragments and check that they hide only small near portions of the feet rather than making the whole group float. Color and lighting establish pictorial relationships, not geological identification.

Save and inspect the output

Save the native draft, export through an actually available control and open the image elsewhere. Reduce to inspect column rhythm; enlarge shared polygon edges and their connections to sides. Reopen the source and hide only cracks, confirming the main planes remain. For white gaps, check selected file and hidden layers first. Identify the scene as imaginary and cite the reference; leave unperformed export or reopening pending. Do not use the illustration for routes, measurements or safety decisions.
