An excerpt needs a verified edition and context even when it looks like source text. Trace three saved Book Isle notes back to their passages and check author, translator and qualifications. Attractive cards or search matches do not establish quotation accuracy.

Define the intended use of three excerpts

Choose notes for personal review, paraphrase and possible direct quotation. State each purpose and record title, author, translator and edition. Work on a small sample, retaining originals. Verify matching text for older or cross-device notes; titles alone do not establish the same translation or revision.

Return from the note to the actual passage

Use available note/highlight navigation, search or contents to locate the passage. Image-only material may not be searchable. Compare context and edition for mismatches and do not accept a merely similar sentence as a match. Record the verified location before comparing wording.

Check quoted wording character by character

Compare direct-quote candidates exactly, especially negatives, values, units and omissions. Copying or recognition can introduce errors. Label edits and ellipses rather than presenting revised wording as verbatim. Keep paraphrases distinct, with their source, so later readers can identify original text versus personal expression.

Restore qualifications and subject context

Read a complete surrounding discussion to identify examples, objections, quoted speakers and qualifications. Add a brief context note. Distinguish the book in hand from an original source it cites; do not imply direct verification of material not actually checked. Decide suitability only after defining the evidence scope.

Verify author, translator and edition fields

Check title/copyright pages rather than shelf metadata alone. Keep author, translator, editor and compiler roles distinct. Prepare fields for the actual writing requirements without treating this workflow as a formal citation standard. Mark unknowns explicitly instead of filling them from similar covers or titles, and retain source-page references.

Keep an audit note without losing the original

Retain important original excerpts and add verification date, location, differences and intended use. Scope checked status to the actual note. Recheck card/image attribution rather than correcting only accompanying copy. Keep source book and note for review, and follow actual permission requirements for public use.

Verify provenance survives export

Export important notes and reopen them externally to inspect provenance, source text and personal comments. Keep companion records if a format omits them. Note exports are not whole-book backups or guaranteed full-state restoration. Use the verified revision in writing and recheck wording after changing translations.

Preserve uncertainty instead of guessing repairs

Investigate paraphrase, edition and secondary citation before blaming storage. Withhold uncertain wording from direct quotation. Resolve attribution against explicit source evidence. If context contradicts the intended claim, revise the claim rather than remove qualifications. Audit wording, identity, context and use, marking remaining uncertainty honestly.
