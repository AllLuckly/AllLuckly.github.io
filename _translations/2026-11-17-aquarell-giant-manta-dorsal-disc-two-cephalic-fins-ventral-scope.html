Draw a giant manta from slightly above and ahead, with broad joined pectoral wings, two cephalic fins before the mouth and a thin trailing tail. Keep a dorsal main view; any ventral information belongs in a separate panel, not gill slits showing through the back. Pose, lighting and markings are invented, not an individual photograph or identification record.

Establish a broad disc and dorsal axis

Use an approximately 2200×1600 px landscape canvas. Locate head, central body and trailing axis, reserving broad pectoral space. Draw one continuous disc silhouette; perspective and gentle bending allow asymmetry but both sides attach to the same body. Make near slightly larger and far narrower without treating one as wing and one as tail. Separate sketch, dorsal colour, cephalic fins and tones. Pixels do not measure metres. Keep one spread dorsal pose without leaping/rolling cues or flipping half the body to expose its underside.

Join broad pectoral bases continuously to the body

Extend broad triangular pectorals from both sides, fuller leading edges, inward-sweeping trailing edges and tapered tips without feathers. Their roots are not little hinges or background cracks. Check a continuous monochrome disc, using gently bent edges for flexible surfaces without counting fine rays. Add central volume and distinct edges if it resembles a kite; restore width if it becomes a fish with narrow side fins. Symmetry in this simplified exterior cannot prove identical real fin lengths.

Keep the mouth at the front, not belly centre

Locate the broad mouth at the leading head edge. In this dorsal view, a visible line and edge volume suffice without a fully open black cavity. Eyes sit laterally; conceal the far one if necessary rather than move both to the dorsal centre. Join head and disc edges continuously without a human face on a triangle. Omit teeth, filtering plates and digestion, and avoid a facial-expression mouth. Any frontal detail needs a labelled separate panel so it cannot become a second mouth in the main view.

Curl two cephalic fins from either side of the mouth

Place one soft cephalic fin on each side before the mouth, choosing a slightly rolled pose with continuous roots and inward-curving tips. They are neither hard horns nor dorsal ears. One may be partly concealed, but retain two traceable roots. Use thin curl shadows without eye-like black holes. Temporarily distinguish cephalic and pectoral fins with check colours, then restore the palette. Do not count pectoral tips as cephalic fins. This roll specifies exterior pose, not observed feeding.

Attach the thin tail at the rear centre

Attach a tapering, gently curved tail at the rear centre, never from a pectoral tip. Show broad visible relationships at its root, omitting small projections and internal anatomy without inferring absence. Keep contrast readable but avoid a third broad fin. Make no spine-identification or contact-safety claim. Hide tones to trace body axis to tail tip, then restore them and inspect the root. If a small dorsal fin is visible in the reference, retain it without counting it as a cephalic fin.

Separate ventral-gill scope from invented dorsal markings

Use deep blue-grey with selected pale shoulder patches and soft modelling, not an identifiable real animal’s markings. Gills are ventral: add no two dorsal rows of slits. If needed, mark only the gill-region position in a separate labelled ventral panel, without a complete slit count or detail. Do not share its fin contour with the main view and imply transparency. A hidden pale underside must not move onto the back; palette does not cover every colour type. Read disc, two cephalic fins and tail before texture.

Inspect pectoral, cephalic and tail roots

Hide markings/highlights and trace left pectoral root through body to right, then two cephalic roots before the mouth and tail from the rear axis. Soften rigid horn-like lines into curled thin surfaces. Reattach a wingtip tail centrally. Move dorsal gill slits to a separate ventral panel or remove them instead of recolouring a viewpoint error. Preserve a failing copy and recheck all three attachment groups after each local repair. Symmetry, labels and spots are manual choices, not automatic identification or real individual tracking.

Reopen and retain dorsal/ventral boundaries

Save native work, hide check colours/labels and reopen a PNG. Read disc and tail at fit-to-window size, then inspect two anterior cephalic fins, eyes and tail root enlarged. Retain any ventral-panel title. If a near pectoral conceals the far cephalic fin, adjust locally or document omission rather than add it behind the head to meet a count. Reopen editable outline, cephalic, colour and caption layers. Document dorsal view, omitted internals and invented markings. Leave unperformed actions pending; review substitutes for neither field observation, identification nor automatic organ segmentation.
