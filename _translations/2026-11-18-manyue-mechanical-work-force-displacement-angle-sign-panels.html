A person visibly straining to pull a cart does not directly establish mechanical work. Read four panels of the same horizontal move in ManYue, returning to force, displacement and angle before delivering a signed energy ledger. Values are original ideal assumptions; the reader displays pages, without measuring force or performing the external calculation.

Give all four panels the same horizontal displacement

In an external editor draw A to B two metres to the right. Copy its endpoints into four panels with rightward, upward-slanting, vertical and leftward constant-force arrows, all 20N. State that each arrow is one specified force, not the resultant of every force. Export three PDF pages for assumptions, calculations and controls, marked P1–P3, retaining editable work. After import, confirm three pages and bookmark the panels. If displacement or force is missing, record it as unknown externally rather than infer a number from facial strain.

Measure the angle between force and displacement

Zoom the slanted panel. The stipulated 60° lies between force and rightward displacement, not the vertical. Externally use W=Fs cosθ: 20×2×1/2=20J. Alternatively resolve the force into 10N along displacement and about 17.321N perpendicular to it; only the former multiplies two metres. Components represent the same force, so do not add the original 20N again. Mark 60° between the two arrows. Read the stipulated angle even when enlarged rather than claim precision from a screen protractor.

Add a separate 120° constant-force control: the along-displacement component is −10N and work −20J. It shares force magnitude and distance with the 60° panel but reverses work sign, demonstrating the missing angle condition. Mark the new angle against the same displacement direction, not as an interchangeable supplement. Keep the original four answers unchanged.

F=20 N; displacement=2 m to the right
angle 0 deg: W=40 J
angle 60 deg: W=20 J
angle 90 deg: W=0 J
angle 180 deg: W=-40 J

Check parallel and perpendicular panels separately

A rightward force parallel to displacement gives θ=0 and 40J. A vertical upward force perpendicular to the horizontal move gives θ=90° and zero. Zero work does not mean no force, nor that every form of the object’s energy remains unchanged; it describes this specified force over this displacement. Retain the 20N upward arrow instead of deleting it to fit the answer. A real path with upward displacement would change the assumptions and require recalculation rather than this zero.

Keep the opposing panel’s minus sign in the ledger

A leftward 20N force opposed to two metres rightward gives θ=180° and −40J. This force removes energy through the motion; it does not make the travelled distance negative two metres. With right positive, multiply F_x=−20N by Δx=2m for the same result. Use either signed components or magnitudes and their angle, not a negative force followed by another negative cosine. Give force direction, displacement direction, angle and work separate columns so the minus sign retains its reason.

Keep waiting and detours within their stated conditions

Add a panel holding position for ten seconds. In the stipulated ground frame there is no displacement, so this force’s mechanical work is zero; ten seconds cannot replace two metres in Fs. Human energy expenditure concerns another system, so zero external work does not imply no metabolic energy use. A curved route under the same constant force still permits endpoint-displacement dot product. If force direction changes along the route, use segments or integration, not total path length with an arbitrary single angle. Record changed assumptions separately, retaining the original four results.

Do not substitute one force’s work for net work

Add a constant leftward 5N resistance to a copy of the rightward panel over the same two metres. Its work is −10J while the original specified force still does 40J. Only if all other forces do no work horizontally is net work 30J. Keep the original 40J row, listing individual forces before summing. Net work corresponds to kinetic-energy change; one force’s work generally does not. Mark this as a separate copy. With no mass or initial speed supplied, do not invent a final speed at B.

If this resistance copy additionally starts with 10J kinetic energy, 30J net work gives 40J final energy, not 50J from adding the original force work alone. The new initial value supports an energy conclusion, but without mass still gives no final speed. Separate givens, deductions and missing conditions rather than invent further motion after one correct calculation.

Inspect reference arrows before changing arithmetic

For a wrong sign, check that the angle is force versus displacement rather than the page edge, then check for double use of signed components and cosine. A vertical-panel answer of 40J suggests missing cos90°; 400J may mix the ten-second hold into displacement. Change one condition only in an owned fault copy, then return to the original bookmark. If labels overlap arrows, zoom or revise the external source rather than assume direction changed. Leave unreadable panels unconfirmed.

Reopen and deliver four panels plus one net-work control

Close and reopen the PDF, return to P1 by bookmark and predict 40,20,0,−40J before consulting P2. Record whether you can explain zero and negative work and why the resistance copy has 30J net work but 40J from the original force. Deliver direction diagrams, a force-by-force ledger and one correction reason. Mark unperformed reopen/bookmark actions pending. This checks reading of an ideal constant-force model, not ManYue force measurement, automatic arrow recognition or a completed physical cart experiment.
