Dense thimble dimples can look like through-holes while its actual bottom opening is overlooked. Choose a metal closed-top, open-bottom thimble tilted on a table so that opening is visible. In Aquarell, check closed tip, tapered cup body and exterior impressions separately. This is neither a universal thimble design nor instruction in needle pressure or finger sizing.

Leave room for the opening in the tilted pose

Use an approximately 1500×1300 px landscape canvas with pale grey-brown background. Aim the axis from lower left toward upper right, opening lower left and closed tip upper right. Block a slightly tapering cup, not a straight cylinder. Rest the near bottom rim on the table with the far side raised a little, using shadow to establish tilt. Separate sketch, metal body, opening, dimples and shadow; start mid-grey. Remove any stem and shorten the body if wineglass-like; broaden the rounded tip if conical-hat-like. Pixels set composition, not object size or finger fit.

Give the bottom mouth rim, wall and cavity

Make a tilted elliptical mouth, crisp near rim and far portions occluded by the body. Dark grey inside must read as cavity rather than a black cap. Continue the inner wall toward the closed tip, darkening inward and retaining narrow metal thickness near the lip. Add receding cavity if ring-like, or reopen a bright filled centre if plug-like. Align mouth centre with body axis rather than contradictory viewing directions. Locate the full ellipse in a checking layer; finish only visible arcs without forcing hidden far rim through the body.

Keep the tip closed rather than making a tube

Close the upper-right tip with a gently rounded surface, tapering the outline and joining side wall through a weak turning arc. Do not duplicate the dark mouth ellipse there and create a through-tube. Shade under one light source without a deep seam separating an independent lid. Soften a flat cap and hard join if battery-like; adjust mouth width and tip position if bucket-like. Hide dimples to check closure. A few top impressions may remain, but must neither break closed volume nor become light-leaking holes.

Start with sparse rows wrapping the body

Choose three or four illustrative dimple rows around the middle. Curve rows with the body, keeping clear near spacing and compressing them toward turning sides. Marks are not equal screen circles: broader centrally, narrower or partial arcs near edges. Test one small patch at reduced size rather than covering everything at once. Soften straight rows and stagger locally if graph-paper-like. Reduce overlap edges and pointed ends if scale-like. Row count simplifies this illustration; it is not a claim about actual dimple totals or all product designs.

Model each impression with tiny value changes

Use a tiny dark side and narrow light side consistent with overall illumination to suggest a shallow impression. Neither black centres nor white highlights are required; far marks can remain faint grey. Compare mouth and dimples: the mouth has wall and deep cavity, while impressions are small surface relief. Reduce black centres and shorten highlights if perforated. Recheck local values and edges if rivet-like rather than adding cast shadows to every mark. Lower texture-layer contrast independently instead of repeatedly darkening the entire metal body to suppress scattered dots.

Separate smooth rim band from textured body

Leave a relatively smooth exterior rim band where dimples stop rather than crossing into the cavity. Turn it around the ellipse, clearer and wider near, weaker, narrower and partly hidden far. Reduce a separating seam and reconnect to the cup wall if it resembles an added ring. Remove dots intruding inside. Keep ground shadow close to the contacting rim and softer beneath the raised tip; do not duplicate one black shape inside and outside. Check without highlights that the band still encloses the mouth rather than relying on a lone white thickness line.

Prioritise broad reflection over small texture

Retain a broad mid-grey face, soft lengthwise highlight and darker unlit side. Adapt dimple contrast within light and dark regions instead of copying identical black-and-white marks everywhere. Strengthen limited reflection edges and value range if plastic-like; merge fragmented glints if glitter-like. The cavity should generally be deeper than surface impressions while preserving near-wall thickness. At small size, read cup and opening ahead of texture. If texture dominates, lower its density and sharpness rather than adding competing highlights.

Verify the one actual opening

Keep the native layers and inspect an exported PNG separately: one visible bottom opening, closed tip, non-perforated dimples and continuous smooth rim. Reduce dark dimple centres if colander-like rather than sealing the genuine opening. Restore inner wall and occlusion if solid-looking instead of deepening every impression. Exclude guide ellipses and row lines from sharing. Record the selected closed-top style; an open ring-style thimble needs a new tip check. Conclusions concern this illustration’s legibility, not product workmanship, durability or use.
