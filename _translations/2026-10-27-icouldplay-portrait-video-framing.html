A portrait recording on a landscape screen can leave side space; filling the screen may remove captions or hands. Use your own marked clip to distinguish orientation, fitting, cropping and stretching before choosing a view. These are separate display conditions, without one ratio setting solving them all.

Mark the observable frame boundaries

Record a vertical clip with a centred subject and identifiable objects near all four edges, leaving some margin. Note orientation, duration and original playback. Confirm the markers are already in the source rather than expecting import to add them.

Four numbered cards and slow camera movement make useful references. Retain the original and save any rotation or conversion separately. Screen rotation and changing a file's orientation metadata are different operations; leave the file unchanged initially.

Import and establish normal playback

Open the imported clip with its initial display mode. Check upright subjects, edge markers, sound and duration. A rotated thumbnail with correct playback is a preview issue distinct from actual video orientation, without requiring immediate whole-file conversion.

For real quarter-turn rotation, compare the source tool and device orientation lock. Changing width ratio does not rotate a picture. Record the file and playback path; use controls actually available without assuming a special rotation button exists.

Inspect the whole picture in portrait

Keep the device vertical and choose available fitting or aspect controls that preserve the frame. Inspect all markers and natural proportions, using round objects as references. Empty screen space is not lost content and differs from black borders already recorded into the video.

Keep this as the full-frame baseline. A correct central face does not establish visible hands or edge text. Lessons, dance and demonstrations may require bodies and props together; choose for the task instead of treating a filled screen as universal success.

Compare cropping in landscape

Rotate the device, inspect the initial full frame and try other actual ratio choices one at a time at the same timestamp. A larger subject with missing edges reflects a display tradeoff, without deleting source pixels or automatically creating an exported crop.

Some options stretch rather than crop. Compare round markers and body proportions. Return to the known complete view if unsuitable. Record the original setting so repeated changes do not obscure how to restore ordinary playback.

Check captions and recorded text

At text-bearing moments, inspect clipping, edge placement and control-overlay obstruction. Hide controls before judging permanent cropping. External text captions can follow separate display rules, while text baked into the video is cropped with the picture.

For essential small text, preserve the whole frame and inspect closely rather than removing other important areas. Create a separate edited landscape composition when needed for delivery, retaining the portrait original. Viewing controls do not generate a new deliverable or reconstruct missing backgrounds.

Choose a default across several scenes

Check opening, edgeward movement and ending so a static first frame does not dictate an unsuitable crop. Write down what must remain visible and choose stable framing across action rather than initial visual appeal.

Reopen and observe whether the version retains settings and affects a landscape clip too. Do not promise per-item scope. Check proportions and edges in that second sample and restore values as needed to expose unintended global changes.

Distinguish source framing from viewing choices

Record original orientation, device, selected view and sacrificed edges. Retain the full-frame reference for later devices or versions. File-specific orientation errors need file and source details, without condemning all portrait recordings or reimporting an entire album.

Completion means explaining screen orientation, recorded orientation and current crop or stretch separately. Preserve important content and natural proportions. Make and verify an independent edited copy for a new delivery ratio; keep everyday viewing settings easy to restore.
