A foxglove raceme suggests hanging bells, but each flower is not a separate ring on the stem. Reference common foxglove, Digitalis purpurea, and arrange six illustrative pendent tubular flowers. Connect short stalks, swollen tubes and downward-facing mouths in Aquarell. Six is a composition count, not a species total. Draw from lawful references without turning morphology practice into cultivation, food or medicinal instructions.

Establish the main axis and one-sided arrangement

Use an approximately 1300×1900 px portrait canvas with pale grey-green background. Bend the axis slightly right and direct six illustrative flowers mainly lower left. Block one continuous axis and six flower envelopes, smaller above and larger below, leaving two clear group gaps. Separate axis, tubes, throats, spots and leaves; use soft purple-pink. Revise side direction and heights if Christmas-tree-like rather than adding a mirrored row. The source notes hybrid variation; this selected one-sided composition is not universal, nor are size differences measurements of flower age.

Find each flower’s short stalk connection

Attach each narrow upper base to the main axis through a gently curved short stalk, not a line from the mouth’s centre. Number all six in the checking sketch and trace narrow ends to attachments, then remove numbers. Shorten dangling strings and clarify bases if string-light-like. Add stalk and local occlusion if pasted beside the stem. Neighbours may hide connections, but the sketch should reach the main axis rather than another flower. Suggest the green calyx sparingly without a star larger than the purple tube.

Expand each tube from narrow base to mouth

Expand from the narrow base down-left into a swollen bell-like tube, with a wider mouth and both sides bending along one axis. Establish one central flower first, not two unrelated ellipses, then repeat the method without identical silhouettes. Give near and far walls, the near wall hiding part of the interior rather than showing all through. Soften mechanical lines and uniform rims if funnel-tool-like. Restore enclosing sides if petal-flat. Keep gaps or explicit occlusion between neighbours instead of merging all six into a long purple bag.

Turn the downward mouth between inner and outer surfaces

Make an irregular opening tilted with each tube axis, near lower lip slightly flared and far upper edge quieter and shorter. Do not turn every flower directly frontward. Give the main throat a lighter interior and somewhat darker upper recess, connecting near wall to outer tube with soft purple. Add inward turning rim and thickness if mouth-sticker-like; simplify excess corners if torn-cloth-like. All six need not show complete interior bottoms: side-facing mouths can reveal only portions. Check axis and mouth together for a pendent pose, then add spots, which cannot replace enclosure.

Arrange throat spots on the inner wall

Add sparse deep-purple marks to visible pale throats, narrowing them around the inner wall and quieting distant ones rather than scattering equal dots outside. Test a main-flower patch for interior depth, then suggest marks in other visible mouths. Reduce raised glints and regular spacing if strawberry-seed-like. Separate selected spots and soften darkest clusters if stained. Count is an artistic omission, not cultivar identification. Keep rim and spots separate; reposition marks when reshaping a mouth instead of leaving them floating outside or passing through the near wall.

Overlap flowers while preserving individual boundaries

Let the main flower hide part of a rear side while the rear base still traces to its own stalk; only a rim segment may show. Separate flowers with shared-light value differences and small contact shadows rather than heavy black seams everywhere. Add one or two quiet green leaves low down without covering every stalk base. Vary angles and soften rigid rims if stacked-cup-like. Restore two larger gaps if a purple mass, then refine occlusion. Depth depends on arranged overlap, not complete visibility of every flower or imagined extras behind it.

Read the raceme first and individual flowers second

At roughly quarter size, check continuous axis, one-sided pendent grouping and clearest main mouth, then enlarge all six connections. Reduce upper flower size or spot contrast if top-heavy. Reposition two lower flowers and gaps if crowded rather than deleting stalks and leaving floating mouths. Keep the background quiet without white halos around every bloom. Suggest limited leaf-edge hairs while retaining broad tube surfaces. Record reference cultivar and view; do not attribute your six-flower count and uniform purple scheme to the source as fixed facts.

Save axis, stalk and mouth checks

Keep the layered native work and inspect an exported PNG separately for continuous axis, six stalked flowers, compatible mouth directions and spots confined to chosen inner walls. Repair tube sides and rim occlusion if flat discs rather than increasing spot density. Lighten a lost pale throat locally instead of whitening the whole raceme. Label the original arrangement as based on common-foxglove morphology, not evidence of practical plant use. A white-flowered or differently angled study needs renewed values and connections, not a colour change presumed to fix structure automatically.
