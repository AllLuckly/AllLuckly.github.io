A fine strainer needs a readable form without becoming an opaque metal bowl. Hand-draw an empty conical strainer, turning mesh along its gathering surface while allowing ground through its openings. Mesh and joints are legible simplifications, not a pore-size reproduction or a filtration or food-use test.

Reserve the whole rim and handle

Use an approximately 1800×1300 px landscape canvas, rim left, handle extending upper-right and cone tip lower-left. Begin with a tilted ellipse and tip, joining both rim sides into a readable elevated view. Mesh cannot rescue an excessively flat opening, so postpone it. Leave room for the entire handle end rather than a rod entering from outside the frame. Canvas pixels do not represent actual length. With no cup or liquid in this study, establish direction through the strainer itself.

Give the rim thickness and an open centre

Add close inner and outer ellipses for a narrow metal rim; its near edge hides mesh attachment behind it. Width may vary with turning, without swelling into a lid. Keep the interior related to the ground and the far rim subdued. Remove an accidental solid interior or join disconnected rings with a short thickness face. Both contours describe one opening in perspective, not two stacked strainers, and the far edge needs no additional handle.

Establish the cone with sparse guides

Draw five or six pale guides from below the rim toward the bottom and a few wrapping arcs. Spacing gathers where the cone narrows instead of overlaying a flat square grid on a triangle. The tip may be rounded rather than needle-like. Hide the rim temporarily to check that both sides join one bottom region. Guide count is illustrative, not a wire count; heavy structural ribs on every guide would change the fine-mesh object into something else.

Cross two mesh directions without filling holes

On a detail layer cross two fine line families, darkening a few intersections while retaining small ground openings. Keep near mesh clearer and far mesh lighter; dense overlaps should not become solid black. Resolve several rows in one near patch and omit elsewhere as a scale choice, not actual missing mesh. For harsh visual interference, reduce contrast or density before shrinking the brush further. The illustration need not expose every real pore; legibility matters more than a mechanical repetition count.

Attach one handle to the rim

Extend one flat handle from a definite solid joint at the right rim, optionally ending in an invented hanging hole. Align both handle edges and add a short dark thickness face. A hole reveals ground rather than a white sticker. Do not grow the handle from the middle of the mesh or duplicate it on the far rim. Limit the joint to the reference and invented simplification, without an internal weld section. Concentrate attachment shadow near the rim instead of turning the whole handle into a heavy mesh outline.

Separate transmitted ground from highlights

Place a low-contrast warm ground behind the object. Ground seen through mesh stays related to it, while rim highlights can be brighter and more continuous. A uniform milky centre sprinkled with dark dots resembles a perforated solid bowl. Test another background in a separate copy, changing only that colour and rechecking the form. Mesh tones suggest depth without calculating spectral transmission. Keep the cast shadow soft and avoid drawing it as another complete crossed mesh.

Audit openness rather than tidy grids alone

Hide fine texture and check rim, bottom and handle attachment, then restore mesh to inspect gathering and curvature. A tidy grid that ignores the cone needs revised guides and local repainting. Repair a wrongly closed opening through occlusion, not a larger ellipse. Hanging holes, mesh gaps and reflections are different boundaries, not one universal white mark. Name volume, attachment or texture faults separately. Where the reference hides a joint, retain an exterior simplification rather than invent unsupported mechanism parts.

Reopen to check near and far mesh

Save layered native work, hide guides and export PNG. Inspect rim and tip at fitted and enlarged sizes. Fine lines may merge into grey small, but the cone should not become a solid black triangle; lighten far mesh and retain a few clear near lines if necessary. Check the complete handle and ground-filled hanging hole. Reopen editable ground and mesh separately. Label the texture a visual simplification without measured pores or filtration results. Completion concerns openness, surface continuity and attachment, not manufacture or food-use instructions.
