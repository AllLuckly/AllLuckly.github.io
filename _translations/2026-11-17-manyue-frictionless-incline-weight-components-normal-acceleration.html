Many arrows on an incline diagram do not necessarily mean many physical forces. Separate actual external forces, weight resolution and motion into three panels and inspect their directions and quantities in ManYue. Data and calculations are externally prepared; the reader displays them and preserves reading location, without automatic free-body drawing or motion simulation.

Exclude hidden rope and friction forces first

Draw a 2kg block on a fixed straight incline 30° above horizontal with stipulated g=10m/s². Neglect friction and air resistance, include no rope or push, and maintain contact with zero normal acceleration. Speed may be zero at release without static equilibrium; the released block accelerates downslope. Put conditions on P1,components on P2 and results on P3. A hand-held preparation state needs a separate frame with its extra force, not arrows mixed into the released free-body diagram.

Draw only the two external forces on the block

Weight w=mg=20N points vertically down. Normal force N is perpendicular to the slope and away from it, not vertical or downslope merely because the block moves. The block’s force on the plane belongs to the plane’s diagram; it is the interaction partner of N, not another balancing force on the block. Label sources Earth and contact surface on the two solid arrows. Leave N’s magnitude unknown before resolving weight instead of extracting exact values from hand-drawn length.

Rotate axes to parallel and normal directions

On P2 retain the same weight and choose downslope positive and outward normal positive. Weight makes 30° with the inward normal axis. Its downslope component mg sin30° is 10N; inward-normal magnitude mg cos30° is 10√3N≈17.321N. Draw components dashed and label them a resolution of w, not two additional interactions. If weight and components coexist, identify auxiliary arrows so a sum does not count gravity three times. Treat 30 as degrees in calculations.

m=2 kg; g=10 m/s^2; theta=30 deg
w=20 N
w_parallel=mg*sin(theta)=10 N (downslope)
w_perp=-mg*cos(theta)=-10*sqrt(3) N (outward axis)
N=10*sqrt(3) N
F_net_parallel=10 N; a_parallel=5 m/s^2

Balance only the normal component

Contact imposes zero normal acceleration, so N−10√3=0 and N=10√3N, below 20N. The plane has not removed weight; only the perpendicular component needs cancellation in that direction. A 10N downslope component remains, so zero normal net force does not mean zero total net force. Note that N≠mg here, with equality recovered on a horizontal plane under matching assumptions. Oblique ropes or other forces require a new normal equation rather than unconditional N=mg cosθ.

Label each arrow’s target: the normal acts on the block and the block’s contact force acts on the plane. Equal and opposite magnitudes cannot cancel in one object’s equation when targets differ. Check objects before numbers;a neat zero sum cannot validate a mixed-object diagram.

Use Newton’s second law along the slope

With no friction or other tangential force,10N divided by 2kg gives acceleration 5m/s² downslope. Record velocity separately: it can be zero at release while acceleration is nonzero. An initially upward-moving block can still accelerate downward, so velocity arrows do not determine forces. If a later panel shows displacement, label its time interval rather than call arbitrary panel spacing measured speed. This ideal trend does not promise exactly 5m/s² on a real rough board.

Holding the block stationary on this frictionless incline needs an additional 10N upslope force with a stated source,creating another exercise. A desired rest state does not invent an upslope normal component. The new diagram can retain the same normal force but needs another tangential term and an explicit change ledger.

Cross-check angle limits and vector magnitude

At zero incline the downslope component is zero and N=20N; at 60° they become 10√3N and 10N, checking sine/cosine assignments. At 30°, squared component magnitudes sum 100+300=400, recovering 20N, not 10+17.321. Orthogonal vector components do not add by scalar magnitudes. An external drawing scale 8pixels/N gives 160pixels for weight,80 for the tangential component and≈138.564 for N. This is illustration scale, not a force measurement, and zoom requires reading labels.

Find erroneous arrows by their target object in ManYue

Import the PDF, inspect frictionless/rope-free P1, solid/dashed P2 and 5m/s² on P3. Bookmark components and specify whether each arrow acts on block or plane. A vertical normal needs direction correction; zero net force may wrongly cancel the tangential component; four real forces may duplicate weight components. Change one item, reread conditions and keep the error copy. Leave unperformed import,bookmark or orientation observations blank. A static image is not a physical motion experiment.

Moving an arrow without correcting its legend leaves contradictory explanations. Recheck arrow, name, target in the same correction,then return to the full page and ensure the selected system did not accidentally include the plane too.

Reopen and deliver two forces, two components and one motion result

Reopen the bookmark, count two real interactions, identify two auxiliary weight components, then state N=10√3N and downslope acceleration 5m/s². Explain that normal and weight are not an action-reaction pair and rest at release does not imply equilibrium. Save conditions, angle controls, units and actual operations; without reopening record paper-only success. Friction, springs, ropes or lost contact require a new force diagram rather than this two-force model in every slope scene.
