One arrow from dense to sparse can suggest every particle travels one way. Use original two-compartment panels and a stipulated crossing record to distinguish paths, net transfer and concentration denominators in ManYue. These are model ledgers, not completed microscopy or automatic particle tracking by the app.

Specify a closed system and passable boundary

Externally draw equal 1L compartments with 100 identical particle markers,80 left and 20 right. Provide a passable opening, sealed outer boundaries and no gain/loss, bulk flow, reaction or active pump. State continuous random motion; dot diameters do not measure molecules. Save conditions, crossings, equilibrium controls as P1–P3 PDF pages with separate particle and page labels. In a selective-membrane diagram first check whether this substance can pass; a concentration difference alone does not ensure passage.

Calculate concentration before drawing a net arrow

Initial model concentrations are 80 and 20 particles/L, a fourfold ratio. Equal volumes allow particle counts to compare concentrations; retain that condition rather than generalize that more particles always means higher concentration. Label a broad arrow net-transfer tendency, not mandatory rightward motion for every particle. Give one particle a short zigzag path including both directions, showing one trajectory is not a whole-population concentration change. Without time/distance scales do not derive diffusion coefficients or speeds from illustrative steps.

Count both directions before taking their difference

Stipulate 20 left-to-right crossings and 5 right-to-left during one window. This illustrative record is not uniquely implied by 80/20. Net rightward transfer is 15: left 80−20+5=65 and right 20+20−5=35, preserving 100. If one particle returns, count each directional crossing event without calling event totals distinct-particle counts. Gross crossings are 25 and net transfer 15, different quantities. Show both 20 and 5 arrows; the smaller reverse arrow cannot be erased.

initial: L=80, R=20; volumes=1 L each
crossings: L->R=20, R->L=5
net L->R=20-5=15; gross crossings=25
final: L=80-20+5=65; R=20+20-5=35
L+R=100

An evenly filled reference does not mean motion stops

On P3 use an equal-volume equilibrium reference with mean 50 particles on each side, mean concentration 50/L. Dynamic equilibrium has no sustained net concentration-driven transfer; particles do not stop or remain exactly 50/50 at every instant. Finite systems fluctuate, so a49/51 snapshot does not prove pumping or broken counting. An illustrative window with 8 crossings each way has zero net and 16 gross events, demonstrating continued motion. These 8/8 events are not rates calculated from temperature or count.

For the zero-net window,start 50/50 and update left−8+8,right+8−8,ending 50/50 with nonempty paths. Equal endpoint counts do not imply paired simultaneous crossings or identical numbers in every future window.

Use25 and 75 to check a volume change

Create a separate control with left 1L,right 3L,total 100 particles under matching ideal conditions. Equal mean concentration is 25/L, giving mean counts 25 and 75, not 50 each. Equal counts would yield 50/L versus≈16.667/L, still unequal. Keep this control separate rather than silently enlarge the main example’s right chamber. Under the stipulated conditions equilibrium equalizes concentration, not total counts. Different media,energetics or accessible space require another model; visible volume alone does not solve every membrane phenomenon.

Separate passive transport from having no energy

Note that diffusion needs no active ATP-powered pumping of this substance, while particles retain thermal motion and collisions. Added pumps,reactions or bulk flow need separate flux terms; not every rightward change is diffusion. Each substance has its own gradient, so another color’s opposite distribution does not refute this substance’s tendency. Check the legend before counting water,solute and channel icons into 100. This passable single-substance model does not collapse selectivity,osmotic pressure and electrochemical gradients into one arrow.

Troubleshoot totals and direction ledgers in ManYue

Import the PDF, verify two 1L labels,read 20/5 and separate equal/unequal-volume references. Bookmark P2 and check both final counts. A 60/40 result may omit reverse 5;55/45 may subtract both directions from the left. If the total differs from 100, inspect paired gains/losses. Net zero confused with no crossings calls for the 8/8 example. Correct one entry and retain the prior draft. Without import,reopening or sampling record paper-only checks, not device-observed molecules.

Record both endpoint frames for the crossing window. Read the boundary-arrow directions before adding or subtracting counts, and do not combine directions from different windows. Endpoint counts alone determine net change, not unique gross crossings; 65/35 could result from other, larger two-way activity as well.

Reopen and deliver paths, net transfer,equilibrium conditions

Reopen and recalculate 65/35,25 gross crossings and 15 net, then explain continued two-way motion at equilibrium. Acceptance identifies 50/50 as an equal-volume mean reference and 25/75 for volume ratio 1:3, without assigning diffusion direction from one fluctuation. Deliver conditions,event ledger,volume denominators and observations; unperformed operations stay pending. A reacting system or impermeable membrane needs new boundaries rather than this uniform reference or counting arrows as particle statistics.
