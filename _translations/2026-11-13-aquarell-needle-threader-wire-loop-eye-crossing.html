A traditional threader’s wire loop may look diamond-shaped, but it is not a label glued beside a needle eye. Draw a plate handle, flexible loop and needle, making their passage relationship clear. Aquarell supplies manual illustration, not elasticity simulation or required physical threading. Enlarged wire and eye details aid readability rather than measuring real dimensions.

Choose the loop-through-eye state without yarn

Use an approximately 1700×1300 px landscape canvas, pale-grey background, handle left and nearly vertical needle centre-right. Let the loop pass through the eye and extend right. Show only the stage after wire insertion and before adding yarn, not multiple action states together. Separate sketch, handle, loop, needle and guides in mid-grey. Remove extra handles if scissors-like and repair the gap if needle and loop are too far apart. An enlarged eye is illustrative, not a flexible needle or a needle/thread size guide.

Attach both loop roots to the plate

Draw an invented rounded metal plate tapering at the right. Return both sides of the wire loop to that end, not one broken strand from the handle and another growing from the needle. Trace out along one side, turn at the far end and return along the other. Omit embossed portraits and decoration rather than copying product ornament. Shorten unexplained pendant-like connectors and restore a shared return if it resembles two needles. Depict visible attachment only, not imagined internal welds, rivets or manufacturing.

Make the eye an opening through the needle

Draw a slender metal needle with an elongated eye near the upper end and thin surrounding material, not a painted dark stripe. Align the passing loop with the eye rather than solid needle metal. Start with an enlarged eye, then reduce it while preserving a readable gap. Connect a floating ring-like eye to the needle body, and replace a tube-mouth appearance with a side opening. Taper the lower tip without adding an eye at both ends. Glints and decoration must not create extra openings in this depiction.

Pass both wire sides through the same eye

In this chosen view, pass both wire sides through the same eye and let them widen into the loop on the other side. The eye rim occludes a short far segment while near wire remains readable; the needle need not become transparent. Recover visible in-eye and emerging segments if both appear severed, and repair local occlusion if a diamond merely lies on the needle face. Complete paths belong on checking layers. Illustrated passage guarantees neither real fit with every needle nor tested stretch or spring-back performance.

Keep continuity through the loop’s deformation

Narrow the loop near the eye and expand it into a softly pointed diamond beyond it, not separate closed little loops. Establish fine contours before modest cool-grey shading, with consistent wire thickness and no bead at the return. Soften corners if rigid-frame-like and reduce excessive waves if cotton-thread-like. Deformation is illustrative, not a calculation of elasticity or allowable bend. Keep needle and plate coherent rather than distorting every part until passage ownership disappears.

Retain negative space within the eye

Leave visible background gaps around the eye and loop so the hole, two wire sides and needle rim separate. Hide glints and repair a black knot by enlarging the eye locally or reducing line width, not whitening away a strand. Use broad soft plate highlights, short wire glints and longitudinal needle shading under one light. Random white specks must not create extra holes. No yarn is present in this selected state, so hide coloured route guides rather than accidentally adding the next operation stage.

Trace out and back to the handle

At reduced size, trace from one handle root through the eye, around the distant return and back through the same eye to the other root. This is one loop. If the route must jump onto the needle outline, separate fused metals through tone and occlusion. Trace the needle from eye to tip as another continuous object. No physical threading is required, and arrows cannot explain an incorrect path into correctness; hide guides afterward. Success depends on a traceable loop and actual opening, not branding or embossed portraits.

Save a clear preview of the no-yarn state

Save the layered native work, hide guides, export PNG and reopen it. Check two roots, one return, one shared eye and a continuous needle. Recover a lost loop through local contrast or larger composition, not a broad ribbon. Restore an eye filled by highlights in the original rather than adding labels. Record an enlarged static no-yarn diagram, not the full threading procedure, and inspect different needle types afresh. Both directions should trace clearly without mistaking the loop for a sticker, eye for engraving or drawn thickness for measured wire diameter.
