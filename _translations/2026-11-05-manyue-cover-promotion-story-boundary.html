Characters together on a cover need not have met in the story, and a prominent introduction line may be promotional summary rather than dialogue. Compare an edition’s cover with completed content in ManYue, recording expectations and their support. This neither rates the work nor dismisses all promotion, and does not use cover hints to spoil unreached events.

Confirm the cover belongs to this edition

Record work, volume, language and file location, identifying an official cover, special-edition alternative, promotion or source-added image. A bookshelf thumbnail can come from metadata and differ from the actual cover, so inspect the page. Mark unclear provenance unresolved rather than certifying a same-name search image. Start with one cover from the same volume, excluding later-volume art. Edition identity is needed to separate revision, promotion and uploader metadata differences.

Separate wording provenance from image composition

Record necessary title and promotional wording with attribution, separately describing figures, clothes, props and setting. Quotation styling alone does not establish verbatim story dialogue; locate any counterpart. Subtitles, award claims and speech have different identities, and unverified commercial status should not become a current fact in your notes. Describe arrangement before inferring companionship or a battle. Classification identifies what produced the expectation.

Record expectations as reader responses

Write two or three specific expectations—ensemble adventure, opposition or lighter tone—and state your completed reading boundary. Retrospective analysis is influenced by memory, not an authentic first prediction. Expectations are not an authorial promise checklist, nor must every cover detail appear literally. Avoid vague excitement labels. Keep hidden-identity or ending expectations in private spoiler notes rather than explanations for a first-time reader.

Find bounded correspondences in the story

Within completed material, classify direct occurrence, thematic echo, not yet found or unresolved. Separate appearances establish that both characters belong to the work, not that they met. Unmatched clothing may be design, special art or later story evidence; leave it unknown without jumping to the ending. Attach chapter and image locations so another work’s remembered plot is not imported. Keep every conclusion within checked content.

Recognise nonliteral composition without forcing chronology

Covers may juxtapose places, periods and scales to express relationships or themes. Inspect spatial continuity, contact and labels such as commemorative or concept art. A shared image alone does not place it in story chronology. Oversized figures may express hierarchy rather than literal giants. Keep compositional interpretation apart from factual conclusions, supported but not presented as the author’s uniquely confirmed meaning.

Compare slogans with the story’s tonal scope

For terms such as light, mysterious or energetic, compare supported sample characteristics without demanding one label fit every chapter. Restore scope in your recommendation, such as lively dialogue in the first two chapters. Verify source and paraphrase before alleging deception from an event-like slogan. Separately check factual numbers, edition or awards through reliable public sources, omitting unsupported ones. Experiential promotion and factual claims require different checks.

Keep cover-to-story references

Keep supported references or an external log for cover, wording and two story examples, mapping each expectation to its trigger. Leave the reader and return, checking that you have reopened this edition’s cover rather than a different bookshelf thumbnail. Add edition and chapter to collected images without distributing a volume for one comparison. Check changed covers and slogans after edition changes before reusing notes. Share commentary and necessary brief quotations within the agreed spoiler boundary.

Finish the expectation–fact boundary record

Finish with cover identity, promotional wording, expectations, story evidence and unknowns separated. Grouping may be nonliteral; absence within completed material is not permanent absence; retrospective fit does not prove an accurate first prediction. Explain how the cover introduces the work without requiring a literal story screenshot. Preserve scope and references so appreciation does not turn promotional associations into plot facts.
