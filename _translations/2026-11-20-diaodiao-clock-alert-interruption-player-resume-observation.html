Use one reversible ordinary Clock alert to observe system notification, 调调 playback state and audible sound separately before, during and after the alert.

Prepare a long owned recording

Choose an authorized recording long enough for the planned countdown, noting its title, duration and recognizable cue. Use your own iPhone at a suitable volume. Ensure no important timer or media must continue. This exercise creates one test system alert; it does not require a phone call or another person’s message. 调调 publicly describes playback and background controls, and Apple Clock offers audible timer alerts, but neither proves that this app will pause, duck or resume for an alert.

Record existing timer and player conditions

Inspect existing Clock timers and the actual When Timer Ends sound, recording the original setting without deleting, resetting or changing timers used for other tasks. Verify the track plays in 调调 and record playhead, track, output, volume and whether an app sleep timer is active. If the existing timer cannot be identified or canceled, mark the comparison pending; an old countdown could otherwise confuse the observation. Preserve the source and settings.

Establish an identifiable playback baseline

Start well before the end of the recording, play briefly and verify recognizable sound from the selected speaker or headphones. Record current time, track and approximate position, and whether you will observe in foreground or on the Lock Screen. Resolve a preexisting playback or output issue before the alert trial. A playing indication, moving playhead and audible sound are distinct evidence; manual observation is not a precise audio measurement.

Create an ordinary audible system alert

In Apple Clock, create a short test timer using a regular audible end sound, not the system Stop Playing option. Record sound, duration and start time, then return to the same track in 调调. Apple documents timer end sounds; interface and choices vary by OS. Ensure only this test timer was started and the track has sufficient time remaining. Stop and record the reason if it cannot be distinguished from an existing task.

Observe the alert without assuming an outcome

At the end, observe whether the system shows an alert, whether it is audible, and whether music continues, pauses, becomes quieter or behaves otherwise. Record state and time before dismissing the alert using the actual system control. If music only seems quieter, label that as a subjective observation and identify the output. Without measurement tools and repeatable conditions, do not invent decibel or latency values. Headphone and speaker environments can differ; do not transfer one observation to the other. If overlapping sounds cannot be distinguished, mark auditory evidence insufficient rather than assuming ducking. If the track naturally ended, output changed or another app started playing, document that condition instead of attributing every change to 调调.

Check player and sound after the alert

After dismissing the alert, return to 调调 and inspect track, playhead and play or pause state, then listen for the original recording. Record any manual resume action; do not call a manual action automatic recovery. Absence of automatic continuation is not automatically a defect. If practical, repeat once with the same track and conditions, preserving both results. A single trial does not establish stable compatibility with all phone calls, notifications or headphones.

Troubleshoot sound, alert and app separately

If the alert is inaudible, inspect its Clock end option, volume and actual output. If music is absent, inspect player state, natural track end and current media destination. If no alert appears, verify the test timer actually started and ended without disabling all system notifications or changing unrelated apps. Change one condition per retry and preserve the earlier state. An audible alert does not prove the player paused, and a playing icon does not prove the listener heard music.

Restore alert and playback settings

Stop or remove only the test timer created for this exercise and restore the earlier Clock end sound; preserve other tasks. Restore your usual player, output and volume. Deliver three observations—before, during and after—with foreground or Lock Screen condition, observation times, manual actions and pending checks. An ordinary alert and Stop Playing serve different purposes. Public documentation informs this exercise; no physical alert or background compatibility test is claimed.
