A hierarchy chart should explain parent and peer relationships, not merely arrange names as a tree. Use a fictional volunteer group to distinguish hierarchy from collaboration. The static graphic does not synchronise personnel or establish real authority.

Write relationships before drawing nodes

List one coordinator, three teams and two members per team with explicit membership and roles. Explain cross-team membership rather than silently duplicating names. Test the hierarchy as indented text before drawing on a 1200 by 900 pixel canvas. Use fictional identities and omit private contact details.

Establish a consistent style for each level

Use consistent node treatment within each level, testing long names before duplication. Preserve insets and readable labels; colour is supplementary. Group each node’s frame and text for movement while retaining editability. Establish room for every bottom-level member before adding connectors.

Align peer nodes on a common level

Align teams on one level and their members beneath them, distributing only peers. Leave connector space between levels. Different member counts may require different group widths without invented filler people. Inspect node levels without connectors first, then restore lines to verify actual relationships.

Use a clear rule for hierarchical connectors

Connect parent bottoms to child tops with consistent paths and few crossings. Manual connectors need checking after node moves. Hierarchy may not require arrows; define the convention and apply it consistently. Do not mix responsibility and sequence meanings without explanation.

Represent collaboration separately

Describe cross-team collaboration separately or use a clearly defined, visibly distinct connector style. Avoid colour-only distinctions. If many links overwhelm the tree, create a separate collaboration note rather than filling the hierarchy with crossings. Establish accurate hierarchy before adding secondary relationships.

Update every relationship after changes

Move one member between teams in a copy after updating the list. Reconnect paths, remove stale labels and inspect changed roles. Version the chart by its effective scope. Read all members under their parents after the move to catch accidental neighbouring changes. Keep the experiment separate from the verified final hierarchy.

Keep scope and update date with the output

Save and inspect long names, insets and legend at delivery width. Keep fictional/actual scope explicit and confirm appropriate publication scope for real identities. Reopen the exact delivery file and read it top to bottom. A static chart represents a point in time and needs an identified update source.

Simplify unclear relationships

Resolve unclear lines through shorter routes and spacing rather than heavier strokes. Audit names and membership against the list. Investigate font/width changes for wrapping. If splitting by team, retain parent scope and page relationships. Sample a complete path from member to team to coordinator before delivery.
