Draw an external-hammer bell on a short handlebar section, viewed slightly from above to reveal dome and sidewall. Dome, hammer and mounting band have distinct connections. Show no invented internal gears, glowing lines or sound-wave circles in place of structure, and make no claim to reproduce its sound.

Locate dome centre separately from the handlebar axis

Create an approximately 1600×1400 px canvas and sketch a handlebar tube running lower-left to upper-right, with the bell base above it. Put the dome centre near (800,520); the faint bar axis is not another bell axle through that centre. Separate sketch, mounting hardware, dome and hammer on a pale background. Choose an external-hammer reference rather than combine it with a rotating-cap geared bell. Save native work and reduce the view to check margins around dome and tube. Pixels and centre positions are original composition, not manufacturer mounting dimensions.

Give the dome a crown, sidewall and lower rim

Start with a slightly flattened domed contour and a short sidewall below it, firmer at the near lower rim and concealed farther back. It is neither a coin pasted to the bar nor a transparent sphere. Block mid-grey or warm brass tones and expose a little base to establish layering. Vary the lower rim shadow around the surface instead of drawing a black diameter through the dome. If reflections turn it into an open bowl, restore a convex crown rather than place a cavity shadow at its centre.

Support the dome without turning it into a sealed can

Expose a small support area beneath the dome, joining it to the mounting base according to the chosen reference rather than inventing a row of posts. A small base still needs a clear contact, not a floating dome. Preserve the rim/base distinction instead of sealing everything with a thick black band. Visible support spacing gives no resonance calculation. Record fully concealed connections externally as hidden; dashed checking lines do not become visible metal through the dome.

Keep the external hammer beside the dome rim

Place a slender external hammer assembly beside the rim, with its root connected to the mounting area and its small head near the dome wall. Choose a stationary noncontact pose and retain a narrow gap. The hammer is not decoration on the crown or a second bell. Simplify its plucking portion and hammer as a readable connected part without unsupported pivots or gears. Check the clearance with background colour: the root connects, while the tip does not penetrate through the dome. The pose measures no striking travel.

Wrap the mounting band around the handlebar

Turn the narrow mounting band around the bar’s cross-section, exposing a near arc and concealing its rear portion behind the tube. Return both ends to the mounting region rather than hang a collar from the bell rim. A short handlebar segment suffices; its end ellipses share one direction. Indicate band contact with short dark edges, not a wide background gap that suspends it. Draw only clearly supported representative fasteners. Close contact in an illustration does not establish actual installation torque.

Differentiate dome and handlebar reflections

Light from upper left, using a broad soft crown highlight and narrower side shadow. Give the hammer only a small local edge, never a white bridge into the dome. Handlebar reflections run lengthwise rather than follow the dome’s circular turn. The band may be darker without becoming solid black. Warm dome against cool-grey tube is a colour choice, not an alloy or sustain measurement. Hide hardware to inspect convex volume, then restore it and keep every reflection within its component’s boundary; reflected continuity does not permit interpenetration.

Troubleshoot three connections and two clearances

Trace dome to support, hammer root to mounting area and band to bar, then inspect the hammer-head clearance and lower-rim separation. Restore justified support contact if the dome floats, a background sliver if the hammer vanishes, or band curvature if it resembles a ruler pasted to a tube. Keep the earlier copy and change one component, comparing fit-to-window and enlarged views. Supply no loudness data or riding-installation verdict from a sturdy-looking image; this checks hand-drawn structural readability.

Reopen to check that export preserves clearances

Save native work, hide axes and checking colours, then reopen a PNG. At reduced size distinguish one dome, one external hammer assembly and a mounting band, then enlarge gaps, rim and tube ends. If the hammer merges with the dome, slightly widen its explanatory clearance and recheck the silhouette instead of deleting it. Reopen separately editable dome and hardware colours and record version and simplifications. Leave unperformed export, reopening or listening pending; this is no completed bell-acoustics or device-drawing test.
