Large waves or colours absent from a fine grid may involve capture, resampling and display conditions; one view does not diagnose failed graphics hardware. Observe a recording of your own black-white grid in iCouldPlay, keeping original image, recorded file and current display evidence separate. No moiré removal, sensor modification or lost-detail recovery is assumed. Without an original or trusted copy, record appearance and missing evidence rather than a confirmed cause.

Establish an identified black-white source grid

Use a familiar drawing tool for an original regular black-white grid, optionally saving a coarser control. Record dimensions, spacing and filenames without private documents or unknown test software. Include only grid and simple version label, not prepainted rainbow/waves later misreported as artefacts. Preserve recordings/photographs and identify capture method; otherwise retain a paper plan. Spacing is a sample condition, not a guarantee of moiré. Trace every later observation to its source rather than calling different-density images one original.

Record three evidence layers separately

Use separate rows for original image, recorded video and current iCouldPlay display/orientation. Record file identity, duration, export method and available dimensions, not window width as recording resolution. Camera filming and software screen capture differ; sensor, display grid and scaling enter at different stages. Do not explain all paths with one label. A display screenshot alone cannot reconstruct unknown first/second layers. This is manual record organisation, not automatic sampling-chain or artefact analysis by the app.

Inspect a stationary grid under fixed conditions

Open the identified recording and play to a stable grid, recording approximate locations of added broad bands, colours or waves. Keep speed, orientation and fit fixed rather than zooming while claiming temporal change. Record real grid motion separately from a static comparison. Mark resolution limits instead of imagining colours in blur. Describe visible patterns without promising recovery of every line or generalising one quiet view to no aliasing on every device.

Change one visible display scale at a time

If fit/fill controls exist, record the original and switch once, checking pattern change and cropping. A display-sampling change does not repair the recording or guarantee removal when restored. Without controls retain one mode rather than seeking an unverified anti-moiré button. Test physical rotation separately, not with a simultaneous fit change. Record initial state, change and result as stronger/weaker/moved/no clear change, not unsupported improvement percentages.

Check where the pattern is already present

Check the original grid in a trusted image/production tool, then the recording at a fixed anchor, retaining that tool’s scaling conditions too. If absent in the original but present in the copy, locate visible addition between those stages without identifying one sensor/codec as certain cause. Scale-specific visibility supports a display-related clue, not GPU failure. Another player has its own scaling and is not absolute original truth. Keep each layer’s limits explicit; missing native-size evidence stays unknown rather than replaced by a new screenshot.

Do not treat screenshots as native-file evidence

Screenshots record the displayed result; later resizing or sharing may resample or compress them, so changed patterns do not automatically mean changed video. Retain device, orientation, mode and anchor, preserving video separately. Share only your nonsensitive grid and necessary identity, not private media or credentials. Blurring a still does not repair the source, and cropping prominent bands is not removal. Stills document appearance, not the complete sampling chain, true colour or hardware health. Label processed copies explicitly.

Change spacing or capture method, not both

Optionally make a coarser-grid recording with capture/playback fixed, or retain the grid and change capture method as a separate next test. Do not change distance, pattern, resolution and display scaling together and assign one cause. Unmade comparisons remain proposed checks, not improvements. Name and preserve all copies, including unexpected results. Comparisons narrow conditions without guaranteeing removal. Camera-manufacturer explanations establish no matching iCouldPlay filter or hardware feature.

Reopen and report only observed scope

Reopen the same file, replay the same grid anchor and check the original mode, then restore ordinary orientation/position. Retain source image, recording, display record and unresolved cause, distinguishing evidence from candidates. Finish with traceable conditions, not repaired hardware or guaranteed moiré-free output. No GPU diagnosis, sensor test or all-device acceptance occurred, and writing a web tutorial is not a device experiment. Rebuild records for new images, exports or display sizes; old stills do not establish new conditions.
