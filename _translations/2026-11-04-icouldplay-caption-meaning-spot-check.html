Timely readable captions can still convey the wrong meaning: a missing “do not” or changed unit can reverse an instruction. Use a permitted explanation clip to inspect a few negatives, numbers, units and names. iCouldPlay provides viewing and navigation, not assumed automatic translation or semantic validation. This exercise is not a basis for high-stakes instructions. Keep unfamiliar languages and concepts unresolved rather than judging accuracy from fluent wording.

Choose phrases whose meaning can be checked

Use an explanation you made or whose provenance and meaning you know. Select a negative sentence, a number with a unit and a proper name. Record video, language, caption version, approximate locations and independent spoken or visual evidence. Do not use an entirely unfamiliar language to claim translation competence or judge a whole work from one cue. Specific samples make errors explainable. Mark missing evidence as unconfirmed instead of inventing the author’s intention to complete the set.

Read negatives with their scope

Listen from before the negative and establish which action “do not,” “cannot,” “not yet” or “not necessarily” qualifies. An omitted word can reverse meaning, while an out-of-context half sentence can unfairly suggest an error. Summarize premise, action and exception and compare caption scope. For a self-made example such as not closing a page before saving a sample, inspect the prerequisite-to-action relationship rather than requiring identical surface wording.

Bind each number to its subject

Check which object a number describes and whether it refers to change or total. “The second copy” and “two copies” can sound fluent while implying different actions. Verify that visible values belong to the same time and object. Preserve decimal, range and approximation meanings rather than turning about ten seconds into exact ten seconds. Keep original phrase, caption and question in separate columns so later review has a specific target.

Check unit meaning rather than numeral equality

Different numerals may be correct when units change, but conversion must preserve the measured quantity. One hour thirty minutes can become ninety minutes for the same duration, whereas megabytes and megabits are not interchangeable without conversion. Similar names do not establish equal units. Record any change instead of silently replacing unfamiliar units. Retain original units and questions when domain knowledge is lacking and check reliable references; this is ordinary wording comparison, not certified numerical interpretation.

Map names to the same entity

Names may use transliteration, translation, abbreviations or edition variants. Establish entity identity before correction: nonliteral wording can be conventional, while similar spelling may name different people or products. Prefer visible titles, official descriptions or permitted reference material and record where the evidence appears. Map a first full name to later abbreviations without merging genuinely different entities merely for visual consistency.

Separate caption errors from source errors

If speech conflicts with the picture and the caption faithfully transcribes it, the issue is not solely captioning. Record all three versions and inspect context or an author’s correction. Do not silently replace a caption with what you believe factual while labelling it original speech; keep a separate sourced personal correction. Preserve unresolved source conflicts rather than arbitrarily choosing a version. Separating transcription, translation and source content improves reports and preserves evidence.

Rewatch the full passage after editing a copy

With sufficient evidence and permission, edit only the relevant cue in a labelled personal copy. Reload it and watch surrounding sentences, checking timing, readability and naming consistency. Even a one-word negative change can affect cue length. Review another language independently rather than mechanically copying the correction. Disclose personal revisions and retain an unmodified comparison so original captions remain distinguishable from a reader’s interpretation.

Keep a compact key-phrase record

Keep media and caption identities, the three phrases, meaning evidence, issue type, revisions and open questions. Claim those checks rather than complete translation accuracy. Prioritize phrases that change action or understanding without cataloguing every normal line. For unfamiliar specialist content, preserve the location and seek reliable explanation rather than manufacturing confidence through repetition. The goal is evidence for important meaning, not rewriting every differing expression into personal preference.
