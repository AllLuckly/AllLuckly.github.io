During Chinese input, pinyin composition, candidates and characters committed to the document can be different states. How an unfinished composition behaves when switching keyboards cannot be inferred from appearance or another input method. Use two non-sensitive practice lines to compare committing first with leaving composition unfinished, and observe Jianlai and the host.

Prepare a safe copy and backup keyboard

Create an ordinary note titled “Switch observations”; do not use password, payment or real-message fields. Confirm Jianlai is installed and its Chinese pinyin layout is available, and that another system keyboard can be selected and you can return. If identity, a backup or an appropriate layout is uncertain, mark the test pending and stop. Do not change permissions, remove a keyboard or uninstall the app.

Record device, system and Jianlai versions, host and both keyboard names. Prepare columns for step, composition before switching, body before switching, body afterwards and notes. Enter labels A: and B:, save and reopen to establish that they are editable body text. Do not share personal dictionaries, private screenshots or complete keyboard logs.

Identify three observation locations

After pinyin entry, the interface may show an unfinished composition and selectable candidates; selecting one may put characters into the host body. Visual markers and positions depend on keyboard and host. Color or underlining alone cannot establish the state. Observe the actual device and record uncertainty rather than treating an uncertain item as a completed comparison.

Use “天气” as a practice target, not a candidate-ranking test. Use the system English keyboard as the switch target, or another ordinary keyboard you can clearly identify, recording its name. Keep the same host and device throughout and do not change themes, orientation or the system keyboard list at the same time.

Line A: commit before switching

Place the insertion point after A:. In Jianlai use a familiar, actually available method to enter the target’s pinyin. Select “天气” and verify it entered the body; move the cursor away to inspect if needed, but do not copy or switch before checking. Record keyboard identity, candidate selected and body. If the target cannot be found, stop and mark this round incomplete.

Once A is committed, use the system’s visible keyboard switch to select the backup keyboard. Check whether the line remains A:天气 and enter a non-sensitive letter as supporting evidence that the switch occurred. Record what happens, without assuming all hosts behave identically. If you accidentally delete or move the cursor, record the deviation and retry on a fresh line instead of overwriting the observation.

Line B: switch with composition unfinished

Reselect Jianlai and place the insertion point after B:. Enter the same pinyin as A but do not select a candidate; observe whether the unfinished composition is clearly identifiable. Record visible pinyin and candidates. If the interface already committed it automatically or the state is unclear, the condition was not established. Stop and record why rather than claiming an uncommitted state.

With an identifiable composition still present, use the current system switch to select the same backup keyboard without choosing a candidate first. Inspect the body after B:, insertion point and any visible composition. Your device determines the result: do not prefill “automatically committed”, “lost” or “retained”. Cancel and record an extra system confirmation if its meaning is unclear; do not change other keyboard settings.

Compare and check once for restoration

Place the before-switch state and after-switch body for A and B side by side. If A remains and B lacks the target text, that is only the difference observed under these conditions. What happened to B’s composition must be based on visible evidence, not a guess at internal implementation. Switch back to Jianlai and observe whether composition remains. If absent, do not assume it was saved, and do not call manually retyping it automatic restoration.

Save and reopen the practice note to check committed body text persists. An unfinished composition may not belong to the note’s body; its absence after reopening does not by itself prove saved text was lost. For important writing, checking that a candidate entered the body before switching is a practical precaution, not a universal host guarantee. Do not delete important sentences to test it.

Troubleshoot and deliver

If A changed, check whether it really was committed, the insertion point and any accidental deletion. If B’s condition cannot be established, check the active keyboard and candidate interface. Correct one condition at a time, preserving the original observation and retry. If the system uses its own keyboard in a field, record that fact; do not reset the device, clear dictionaries or alter Full Access for a matching result.

Save each line’s condition, body, switch action and unfinished steps. Restore your usual keyboard and verify the practice note opens. This exercise uses public sources and does not claim a device test. A single result applies only to the device, layout and host used; it is not a universal rule for input methods.
