Cache clearing, entry removal and video deletion can affect different things. Artwork, records and local copies serve separate roles, and some deletion actions touch real files. Rehearse with a clearly disposable personal sample before managing important content, keeping storage recovery understandable.

Prepare a sample with an independent original

Retain and open the complete original outside the app at three positions, then import a separately named disposable copy. Verify both locations instead of assuming an app-only recording is backed up. Avoid unique family or work material for experiments.

Leave the sample midway and record scene and time, then verify reopening. Note artwork too, without treating its presence as file existence. Inspect originals, playback and records as separate evidence beyond attractive cards.

Read each operation's actual scope

Inspect available cache, storage and item controls before execution. Record whether prompts describe artwork, other caches, history, sources or local media. Similarly named remove actions can differ by page and version; local and server-side deletion are distinct.

For unclear scope, retain state and inspect known storage locations instead of testing several guesses. App statistics can cover different categories from imported copies or file-provider caches. Establish the counted scope before interpreting delayed size changes as failure.

Test one cache action independently

Choose one clearly explained cache operation for the test and record its before-state. Afterwards reopen artwork and video with sound, observing reloads and retained content. Do not simultaneously clear history or remove entries, which obscures attribution.

Returning artwork may reflect metadata retrieval rather than video restoration. Playability does not prove unchanged records, so recheck the middle position. Allow actual statistics refresh without repeatedly clearing everything to force immediate zero usage.

Verify file scope before deleting a test entry

Only proceed with a disposable copy after confirming the external original and reading the prompt. Local removal may delete imported media; do not assume metadata-only effects. Preserve the reference viewing position first.

Check the resulting entry and any genuinely observable file location, without promising app-private files are always browsable in Files. Mark unknowns. Keep important material outside the selected test set, particularly similarly named recordings.

Recreate from the independent original

If removed, import the retained original and check beginning, middle and end. Reimport may create new identity or records, without guaranteeing returned progress, favourites and metadata. This verifies recovery of playable content; it does not prove that every deletion can be undone or the full database restored.

For no new item, inspect name collisions, existing entries and provider state before deleting anything else. Distinguish same-name editions. Plan media, captions, connections and viewing references separately rather than interpreting one successful reimport as complete application recovery.

Plan real cleanup by importance

Separate unique originals, retrievable copies and temporary material, beginning with verified small-scope actions. Remote items may retain metadata without large local media, so use source-specific evidence. Record preservation, removal and recheck steps to avoid accidental scope expansion.

Completion explains actual effects, retains readable originals and useful location references and recognises what reimport cannot promise. Keep uncertain items instead of resetting every source for space management. Apply tested rules from recoverable samples to formal material gradually.
