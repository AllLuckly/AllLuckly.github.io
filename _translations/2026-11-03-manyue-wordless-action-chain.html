A wordless comic offers no dialogue to explain an action: a reaching hand or a changed gaze can affect its meaning. Choose a short sequence in ManYue, establish what is visible, and only then discuss why events happen. The output is an action chain with return points to the original pages, rather than a page-by-page retelling or an automated image-recognition result.

Choose a short sequence with a beginning and an outcome

Choose three to five panels from a work you are authorised to read, ideally showing the situation before, during and after an action. Establish the intended direction and any spread relationship first. Record both page identities for a two-page sequence, or a clear landmark above the target on a tall image. Keep complete compositions rather than cropping away a character and then interpreting the absence. Start with few characters and distinctive objects. Complex chases and intercut narratives can wait until the method is familiar, so the initial exercise does not combine too many unknowns.

Write one observable action for each panel

For each panel record position, bodily action and visible objects: for example, A stands outside the door or B moves a cup toward the table. Do not substitute “A is angry” for observation. Describe facial features or posture, placing the emotional interpretation separately. Identify each panel by its sequence and, where useful, row, column or nearby object; no app panel-numbering feature is assumed. Keep character labels consistent across viewpoints. Leave omitted between-panel actions unresolved instead of inventing a complete story. A short verifiable record is stronger than an unsupported reconstruction.

Track one object through the sequence

Choose an identifiable cup, key or hat and track its position, orientation, contact and occlusion. Leaving the frame does not prove removal; look for later evidence. Similar colour alone does not establish the same object, so compare shape and setting. A four-column personal text record is sufficient without marking the source image. Retain a question mark when uncertain and revisit complete neighbouring pages. This continuous object trail is often easier to test than one exaggerated expression and can clarify who initiates an action and who experiences its outcome.

Treat time between panels as a question

Inspect light, travel distance, background changes and motion traces before deciding whether events are immediate, later or unresolved. Adjacent panels need not share a place or minute; blank space and landscape inserts can change the rhythm. State the clue before the timing interpretation instead of filling every gap from everyday experience. If later evidence shows a scene change, revise the chain openly. Rapid page flipping that resembles animation is not a substitute for inspecting the individual static panels and explaining their relationship.

Compare two explanations against evidence

Write two possibilities for the most uncertain action, such as offering an object versus letting it slip. Revisit finger contact, object direction and the following outcome for evidence distinguishing them. Keep both if the image does not resolve the difference. Do not quote a personal interpretation as though the author stated it. External plot explanations are unnecessary for this step. If consulted later, identify them as another reader’s interpretation rather than an extra explanatory panel supplied by the comic itself.

Save return points for the decisive pages

Save a supported page bookmark and test returning after leaving the reader. For multiple panels, add a personal locator such as upper-right panel or the figure beside the window; the bookmark is not assumed to target a panel. Tall images need a nearby visual landmark, not only a percentage that may differ on another device. If bookmark remarks are unavailable, keep the mapping externally. Preserve the source file and recheck the artwork before carrying observations into a replacement edition.

Retell the chain and check omissions

Retell the visible action in three to five sentences without looking at your interpretations, then check characters, objects and order against the pages. A skipped panel may contain the turning point; restore its evidence rather than adding adjectives. Keep explicit uncertainty wherever the retelling needs “perhaps.” Limit the conclusion to what this sequence supports instead of generalising a character’s personality or the work’s entire theme. Separating observation from inference makes later checking possible; it does not require one definitive plot interpretation on the first attempt.

Acceptance and common misreadings

Completion means every action has panel evidence, decisive pages are recoverable, and at least one inference states both support and limitations. A missing figure may be outside the frame or occluded. Adjacent panels require scene and time checks before a causal claim. Resolve uncertain reading direction from the original layout rather than reversing tap controls. Keep a concise action chain instead of collecting every attractive image. For more complex material, use the same checks with wider context; success on this sample does not imply automatic understanding of all wordless narratives.
