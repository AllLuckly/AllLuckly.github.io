Pines do not all have the same number of needles per bundle. This study explicitly references the three-needle fascicles of loblolly pine, Pinus taeda. Enlarge one main bundle and add two quieter companions to show a shared base. The Aquarell task concerns needle attachment and slender volume, not cone scales or species identification from three lines. Bundle count and spread angles are original composition choices.

Fix the reference scope by species

Read the loblolly needle description and note three needles per fascicle plus its basal sheath, retaining the species name in your reference record. Use an approximately 1600×1600 px canvas, pale grey-beige background and a twig rising lower left to upper right, main fascicle spreading right. Separate twig, sheath, needles and shadow, using grey-brown and deep yellow-green. Establish one bundle first; do not generalise three needles to all pines. A different species needs fresh checking. Pixel lengths enlarge the study, not convert the source’s physical needle measurements.

Use one sheath as the shared starting point

Place a narrow grey-brown sheath at one short twig attachment, oriented toward the needle fan, like a small sleeve around bases rather than a large bud. Connect all three needle guides there, then cover short basal segments with its rim. Gather separately stalked needles back into the shared sheath. Shorten a fourth-needle-like sheath and clarify enclosure. Keep the twig continuous through the attachment rather than breaking it to hang foliage. Brown values are drawing choices, not diagnoses of age or health.

Trace each of the three needles separately

Label three pale, temporarily different-coloured guides A, B and C, all emerging from one sheath mouth with slightly different curves. Trace exactly three tips in the main bundle; crossings must not generate a fourth. Keep one somewhat straighter and two gently curved, neither equally spaced rigid spokes nor broad petals. Trace backwards from each tip to base, resolving crossings first. Remove labels and restore related natural greens. Widen gaps for clarity without separating their bases into independent twig attachments.

Model slender volume without outlining both edges everywhere

Give near needle portions extremely narrow colour bands, tapering toward single-line tips. Quiet distant parts without abrupt cuts. Widths and lengths may vary while each path stays continuous, not flat-ended. Use cooler green on dark sides and sparse near highlights rather than two white borders everywhere. Narrow broad grass-like blades and extend tips; add limited near width if hair-like. The source supports slender form, while these bends are invented poses, not wind-speed evidence or measurements of a particular twig.

Occlude only short segments at crossings

Cross two needles lightly at mid-length, deciding which is nearer. Hide only a tiny rear segment and continue its original curve on both sides. Do not add a black knot or merge two needles into one tip. Check full curves in the hidden sketch, then inspect visible continuity. Change curves or gaps if counting becomes difficult rather than adding tips. Reduce crossings if rope-like. Require clear counting in the main bundle; note occlusion in companions without forcing every hidden needle through.

Give companion fascicles their own bases

After establishing the main bundle, add two quieter fascicles at other twig positions, each with its own small sheath rather than one large hole for all needles. Companions can be occluded or cropped, but record visible omissions without implying arbitrary within-fascicle counts. Add sparse axial twig marks, not dense cone scales. Lower companion contrast and sharpness if competing. Check attachment shadows and twig edges if it looks segmented rather than circling every joint with a black trench. Read shared bases first, then tip spacing.

Keep fine lines continuous against a pale background

Maintain enough value contrast with the pale background: tips may be quiet without all disappearing. At roughly quarter size, count three complete main needles, then enlarge to inspect sheath-mouth occlusion. Reduce companion density and crossings if a green fuzz-ball; soften curves and regular spacing if three swords. Keep background variation very faint rather than adding a dense forest. Adjust background and main colour instead of outlining every needle black. Separate numbered guides and complete hidden paths from visible outlines; exclude the checking sketch from sharing.

Save a traceable three-needle main fascicle

Keep the native file and open an exported PNG separately. Trace three tips to one sheath, its attachment to twig and each companion base. If fine lines break, check preview zoom and width, then widen near segments locally rather than making three thick bands. Record loblolly as the reference and three depicted fascicles as composition, not an identification card for all pines. A five-needle pine needs a newly sourced count and connection plan. Success is readable paths and enclosure, not proof of species, condition or origin; no automatic plant identification is involved.
