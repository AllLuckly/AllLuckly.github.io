Give two distinct recordings long titles sharing a prefix. Record visible text in the mini player, full player and queue, using duration and sound to verify identity. Deliver a view ledger for truncation, wrapping or full display, without treating ellipses as deleted tags or assuming in-app full-title copying or editing.

1. Make the recordings audibly distinct first

Externally make two owned short audios opening with 长名甲 and 长名乙, targeting twelve and eighteen seconds. Verify actual exports and preserve originals. Give working copies short identifiers such as short-A40.mp3 and short-B40.mp3 so long filenames and long titles do not create two confounds at once. They must not be the same recording renamed, since unclear display then has no independent audible reference. Title length does not imply audio duration. Short recordings only simplify repeated identification, not explain truncation. Mark unfinished preparation pending; 调调 is not claimed to supply these assets.

Save and read them back to confirm the suffixes really exist. Map short filename, full title, opening sound and actual duration in one external row. The tool prepares metadata; 调调 reads available titles without promising an in-app long-title editor. A field exposing only the beginning does not prove the full value was saved. Verify it through the tool’s actual capabilities. Keep artist and album under one exercise identity rather than change many fields at once. Preserve full titles externally instead of relying on the visible truncated portion.

3. Bind imported entries by sound first

Import the copies separately and retain responses. Hear 长名甲 or 长名乙 in an identifiable item and compare duration with the external ledger. Matching-looking list rows do not justify merging them, nor does a hidden title suffix establish duplicate content. If one is skipped, preserve the message and investigate selection or deduplication rather than change audio for screenshots while retaining the old ledger. After identifying an item, pause it to reduce natural track changes during view switching. Distinct audio assists recognition but unpublished deduplication means independent imports must be observed, not guaranteed.

4. Record only what the mini player actually shows

Observe the mini player in an actually reachable library or list, noting the displayed beginning, ending, wrapping or ellipsis. If unavailable, record that rather than invent a fixed gesture. It may truncate or show the whole value under another layout; do not force truncation. The paused item’s identity comes from the previous audible check and external ledger, not a shared prefix as sole evidence. Do not tap unknown regions to find full text. A public mini-player screenshot does not promise long-press full-title copying in every version. Keep visible range separate from the complete source value.

5. Inspect full player and queue separately

Open the full player through an identifiable control, then inspect current and upcoming items in the queue, recording visible title ranges and duration separately. Hold device orientation and theme fixed rather than also change window width. A wider view is a display condition, not regenerated metadata. If the current item changes, re-establish sound/title identity before mapping rows; do not attach B’s full suffix to A. If full text remains unavailable, deliver partial ranges with the external full value instead of inventing a hidden details view. Each view needs its own record, not one player screenshot reused as queue evidence.

6. Check B through suffix and sound

After saving A’s three-view ledger, explicitly select B, hear 长名乙, verify duration and pause before repeating the same views. Compare where 甲段 and 乙段 are distinguishable and where only the common prefix appears. If both full titles are visible, record that instead of creating a failure. A view that cannot distinguish them is insufficient alone for identity; cross-check external full title, duration or sound. That does not establish lost app data. Similar glyphs or wraps do not prove one file, and differing suffixes do not excuse selecting the wrong audible version. State which evidence supports each identity conclusion.

7. Diagnose source value, view and current item

If external readback also lacks the suffix, fix the working tag rather than call it screen truncation. Omission in only one view is a presentation difference; wrong sound requires current-item identification. Record actual scrolling or moving text if present without assuming a marquee or draggable-title feature. Change one factor per trial rather than shorten titles, replace art and reimport together while claiming one isolated cause. Delete no real same-named music, and do not treat external renaming as a guaranteed tag-refresh method. Preserve hidden portions in the source ledger; ellipsis length cannot establish how many original characters were removed.

8. Deliver three-view mappings to full source names

Retain full titles, short filenames, audible identities, durations and actual ranges in three views for each asset, marking unavailable views or nonindependent imports. Reopen and identify confirmed entries only before passing that step. Restore playback without requiring shorter names in the formal collection. Distinguish missing source tags, display truncation, natural track changes and wrong selection rather than a generic “long titles unsupported.” Findings apply to this material and display environment. New devices, fonts, themes or app versions need fresh range checks while full external names remain reviewable identity anchors.
