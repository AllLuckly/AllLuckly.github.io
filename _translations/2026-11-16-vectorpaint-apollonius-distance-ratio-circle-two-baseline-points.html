Equal-distance points form a line, but changing the ratio to one-half changes the locus. Draw the distance-ratio circle with a derivation, four checked points and a separate equal-distance comparison.

Fix both points and the direction of the ratio

Set A=(0,0), B=(6,0) and require distance PA divided by PB to equal one-half. Identify numerator and denominator before drawing; reversing the ratio changes the stated condition. P cannot equal B because its denominator would vanish. Keep distinct fixed points and ordinary nonnegative distances. A roughly 1600 by 1400 canvas needs room beyond the segment for an external point. This is a planar locus exercise, not a real-map distance or location-service result.

Square and complete the square for centre and radius

Multiply and square to obtain four times the sum of x squared and y squared, equal to the squared distance from B. Rearrangement gives 3x squared plus 3y squared plus 12x minus 36 equals zero. Divide by 3 and complete the square: centre (-2,0), radius 4. Keep the exact ledger instead of guessing the centre from the internal division point. Nonnegative distances and a positive ratio allow return from this squared equation, with a nonzero denominator still required. A centre at positive 2 calls for checking the cross-term sign, not flipping the picture.

A=(0,0); B=(6,0); PA/PB=1/2; P!=B
4*(x^2+y^2)=(x-6)^2+y^2
3*x^2+3*y^2+12*x-36=0
(x+2)^2+y^2=16
center=(-2,0); radius=4
P1=(2,0); P2=(-6,0); P3=(-2,4); P4=(-2,-4)
PA=PB -> x=3

Place the circle and both fixed points at equal scale

Place the mathematical origin at screen (850,650) with 70 pixels per unit. The centre becomes (710,650) and radius 280 pixels. Use actual circle/equal-dimension shape controls, or a grid-based schematic if precise entry is unavailable. A lies inside and B outside this locus; neither is a boundary point for shaping its circumference. Keep axes, circle and construction lines separate. An apparent ellipse caused by unequal axis scales needs a scale correction, not a changed equation. Preserve both fixed-point identities.

Check internal and external baseline points

The baseline intersections are x=2 and x=-6. Their distance pairs are (2,4) and (6,12), both giving one-half. One is internal to segment AB and the other lies beyond A; the external point must not be discarded because it is farther away. Use restrained construction lines to A/B. The coordinate -6 is not a negative ordinary distance. Keeping only an arc between A and B omits valid locus points. Check the complete boundary rather than treat one correct sample as proof for every point.

Use upper/lower points to check the two-dimensional locus

At (-2,4) and (-2,-4), squared distances to A/B are 20 and 80. Their distance ratio is square root of 20 over square root of 80, one-half; the squared ratio one-quarter is a different quantity. Draw one pair of distance lines clearly and label the reflected point without crowding. Four successful samples check the diagram locally. The algebraic equivalence establishes the full locus, not an assertion that four checked samples prove every manually placed curve node correct.

Compare ratio one and reversed point identities separately

For the same fixed points with ratio 1, draw the perpendicular bisector x=3, not the old circle with a new label. The general algebra divides by one minus the ratio squared, so equality needs separate handling. Swapping A/B while retaining one-half gives another circle; swapping them and using ratio 2 preserves the original relation. Record the changed identities/ratio before a variant. Name and retain the main circle and control separately rather than overwrite the evidence of which condition changed.

Diagnose denominator, completed square and display scale

For the wrong side, check numerator/denominator. For a wrong centre, revisit the linear-term sign and square completion. For a distorted circle, check axis scales. These are different errors, not one inaccurate shape tool. Do not drag until four samples appear to fit and call that a derivation. Change one condition and retain the prior version. Colours distinguish roles without implying a distance alarm. Mark approximate placement and keep exact equations separate from observations; do not claim untested snapping or automatic constraints.

Reopen and verify the condition plus four samples

Save the editable original and reopen an export, identifying centre -2, radius 4 and all four samples. State that PA is half PB and separately explain the ratio-one line. The deliverable needs a traceable distance calculation for each labelled point and a complete-locus equation, with consistent construction lines. Move crowded annotations instead of deleting the denominator condition or external point. This is an original ratio diagram, not an invented locus solver, live measurement feature or device geometry certification.
