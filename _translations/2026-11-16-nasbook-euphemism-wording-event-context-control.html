“Pursue other opportunities” can soften a delicate change or simply describe a destination elsewhere; isolated wording does not settle classification. Read an original fictional director’s notice, separating established role facts, replacement wording and tone. Preserve adjacent explanation in Book Isle and bound your interpretation rather than treating phrase search as rhetoric detection or a real personnel finding.

Keep the notice and adjacent explanatory sentence

Card U1 reads:“The notice said Ari would pursue other opportunities. The attachment stated that Ari’s appointment as director was revoked effective that day and Lin would take over.” This original fiction establishes only the ended directorship, not contract, income or every other status. Verify U1/names/attachment after PDF/EPUB import and retain both sentences. With only sentence one, leave the event unresolved rather than entering dismissed from employment. This is literary wording analysis, not a real appointment or legal-effect assessment.

Effective that day locates the fictional role change without real administrative assumptions. Even a short excerpt should retain whose appointment, what changed and who succeeds, rather than isolated revoked without its object.

List the event before comparing wording

In one column record only no longer director/Lin succeeds; in another copy pursue other opportunities. The attachment names the role change, while the notice redirects attention toward a future destination and softens direct removal language. This supports a euphemistic candidate without adding unstated coercion, choice or punishment. Context establishes reference, not pleasant sound alone. Put a possible softening of sensitive news in the interpretation column rather than pretending the notice declared its own motive.

Keep the event fixed in a direct paraphrase

Use external direct control“Ari is no longer director; Lin takes over.” Compare emphasis without changing it to voluntary retirement or punishment for wrongdoing, which add unprovided causes. Euphemism can reduce harshness/social pressure without denying every fact; misleading effect needs context and reference. Label the direct version your paraphrase, not an overwrite or another sentence supposedly written by the author.

Reassess the same phrase in a changed context

U2 reads:“Ari voluntarily left a rotating role to study restoration at another library, saying she wanted to pursue other opportunities.” Voluntary choice/destination are now explicit; the same phrase cannot import U1’s removal context. It may be generalized self-description, but identifying a softened adverse event requires further support. Retain an ordinary-language candidate if evidence is insufficient. Mark this a contextual control, not a continuation that retroactively supplies U1’s motives.

Separate softening, metaphor and negative understatement

“She set down a heavy burden” can be metaphor; whether it also softens a change needs context, and both functions can coexist. “This is no small change” requires checking negative understatement/emphasis rather than classifying every indirect phrase here. Euphemism concerns substitution around delicate/adverse reference, not all metaphors, indirect sentences or polite words. Retain the direct event beside wording so an attractive excerpt does not lose its referent.

Save wording together with its factual anchor

Save returnable references to pursue other opportunities and the attachment separately through visible highlight/note controls, pairing them externally. Excerpt the whole passage if precise selection is unavailable, without automatic euphemism/truth detection claims. On return identify U1/U2, speaker and attachment authorship rather than merging character speech with narration. An unknown-cause column keeps missing evidence explicit. Separate interpretation from source wording.

Check motive claims based only on softness

For certain deception claims, check whether facts were denied or clearly provided in the attachment; softer tone alone proves no malice. U2 refutes every occurrence means removal. A translation can alter mitigation, so do not automatically attribute its choice to the original author. Correct reference, fact or interpretive candidate one at a time without rewriting removal as retirement or letting visual wrapping change speaker identity.

Reopen and deliver a supported, bounded judgment

Reopen U1 without old notes, recover the role facts and explain possible softening, then use U2 to show why string matching fails. Deliver wording, referent, contextual evidence and unknown cause without a final verdict on author motive. Recheck changed passages/translations rather than turning this example into a keyword rule. This is original-text reading/navigation, not real personnel, legal or personality judgment, nor new automatic rhetoric/fact-checking functionality.
