A central rod reaching the ground can turn a tripod into a four-legged stool, while three loose lines omit shoulder attachment. Draw an invented tripod without a camera in Aquarell, separating three legs, vertical centre column and simplified head. A manufacturer’s manual informs component categories, not this illustration’s load rating, leg angles or assembly procedure.

Establish three separate ground contacts

Use an approximately 1600×1800 px portrait canvas with warm-grey ground. Mark three feet forming a projected triangle with area, not one horizontal line. Place the near left/right feet lower and the rear foot higher for this view. Put the shoulder above their interior region before details. Separate the rear foot if the stand resembles a flat A; revisit axis and contacts if it looks toppled rather than adding a fourth leg. Pixels and foot spacing are composition choices, not a completed load calculation.

Return all three legs to the shoulder

Draw one leg from each foot to a distinct place around the shoulder, not a rear leg attached halfway along a front leg. Check routes in one colour, then add tube width and rounded transitions. Occluded sections must connect to the same foot. Recover a visible rear segment if only two legs read; inspect shadows and centre column if four appear. A black shoulder cannot conceal every faulty attachment. Projected leg length alone establishes no actual extension equality or inequality.

Pass the centre column through the shoulder

Put a near-vertical centre column through the shoulder, with the head above and a short suspended section below, clearly stopping above the ground. Separate nearby column and legs through width, tone and occlusion. Do not join its lower end to the rear foot and turn that leg into a bent column. Show only exposed sections without transparency. Omit horizontal-column arrangements and hook accessories in this design rather than generalising specialised features to every tripod. Complexity must not erase the basic component count.

Seat the head and plate on the column

Simplify the head as a small connector and top mounting plate. A tilted plate must visibly contact the head rather than float. Leave it empty without inventing a lens. Align the head’s support with the column. A side knob may suggest exterior form but should expose no imagined ball-lock internals. Add plate area and head volume if it resembles another foot pad; repair its plane before glints if perspective conflicts. No screw specification, camera compatibility or load-tested connection is claimed.

Depict only the selected two tube sections

Invent two telescoping sections per leg, with a narrower lower tube emerging from the upper and one narrow locking band at their junction. Do not glue equal-diameter sticks end to end. Treat all three coherently, simplifying rear detail without inventing extra unexplained joints. Counts belong to this two-section illustration, not the reference model. Repair off-axis tubes and show a band wrapping the tube rather than hanging as a ring. Omit decorative text; drawn locks do not establish actual clamping or teach repair.

Keep foot pads and shadows from adding supports

Give each leg a small foot pad and narrow contact shadow, with cast shadows sharing one direction. Keep the rear contact visible. Stop the column above ground without its own contact shadow. Check a simplified feet/shadow view first; soften a shadow misread as a fourth leg and relate it to ground colour. Orient pads with their splayed legs instead of all facing the viewer. This expresses static space, not proven real stability from a projected triangle or any safety guarantee about shooting conditions and wind.

Troubleshoot from feet to shoulder to head

Reduce the stand and trace exactly three feet to the shoulder, then follow the column through it to the head with no fourth ground contact. Adjust the invented view to reveal a fully occluded rear segment rather than drawing hidden tubes through foreground legs. Repair a flying-saucer-like head through plate/head/column contact, not a decorative tether. Check volume without highlights before restoring materials. Keep the ground triangle, numbers and extended axes on guides, not as exported braces, cables or accessories.

Reopen the image and verify component roles

Save the native work, hide guides, export PNG and reopen it. Verify three grounded feet, suspended column end, supported head and bands around tubes. Enlarge the subject or simplify nearby background if a small preview loses the rear leg, without adding a foot. Remove exported checking triangles in the original, not by cropping actual pads. Record no camera, vertical column and two-section legs; inspect horizontal-column or multi-section models anew. Success concerns readable structure, not installation, measured load capacity or safe real support for any camera.
