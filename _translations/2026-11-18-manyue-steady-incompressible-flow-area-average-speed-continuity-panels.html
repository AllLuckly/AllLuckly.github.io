A longer velocity arrow at a narrow outlet can misleadingly suggest extra water per second. Read a leak-free pipe in ManYue, recomputing area, mean speed and equal-time volume. This is an original idealized steady incompressible-flow setting, not real flow estimated from drawn lines or an in-app fluid simulation.

Identify two sections of the same pipe

Externally draw water flowing left to right through a narrowing pipe: A1=15cm², A2=5cm² and inlet cross-sectional mean speed v1=0.40m/s. State steady flow, constant density, no branch or leak and no accumulation between sections. Make P1 assumptions, P2 volume ledger and P3 controls as a PDF. Check labels on import and bookmark the ledger. Speed means the section-wide mean, not the instantaneous speed of a centreline particle in a real nonuniform velocity profile.

Convert squared area units together

Convert 15cm² to 0.0015m² and 5cm² to 0.0005m², because 1cm²=0.0001m². Centimetres convert by 1/100 and areas by its square, not merely 1/100. Q=A1v1 gives 0.0015×0.40=0.0006m³/s. The units m²×m/s become volume per second, not length per second. A direct 15×0.40=6 needs its unconverted units examined before being labelled six cubic metres per second.

A1=15 cm^2=0.0015 m^2; mean_v1=0.40 m/s
A2=5 cm^2=0.0005 m^2
Q=A1*mean_v1=0.0006 m^3/s=0.6 L/s
mean_v2=Q/A2=1.20 m/s
10 s: volume=6 L; mass=6 kg for rho=1000 kg/m^3

Use the shared flow rate to find narrow-section speed

Under the stated conditions A1v1=A2v2, so v2=0.0006/0.0005=1.20m/s, three times the wide-section mean. No extra water appears per second; the same volume occupies a longer segment. Over one second, the wide plug is 0.40m long and the narrow plug 1.20m, each giving 0.0006m³ from area times length. Arrows may illustrate the threefold ratio but retain numeric labels. Zooming their displayed lengths creates no new speed measurement.

Separate ten-second volume from mass

The flow is 0.6L/s, giving 6L=0.006m³ in ten seconds. With an additionally stipulated density 1000kg/m³, mass flow is 0.6kg/s and ten-second mass 6kg. Litres measure volume and kilograms mass; equal numeric six depends on this density, not a universal one-litre-one-kilogram rule. Equal ten-second inlet/outlet volume does not mean the pipe contains only six litres. Its total capacity needs length and shape information absent here.

If flow reverses at the same magnitude, volume flux with right positive is −0.0006m³/s, while the magnitude passing in ten seconds remains 6L. The sign denotes crossing direction, not negative physical volume. Main-text mean speeds are positive magnitudes; add signs only for directed flux and identify the convention in the heading.

Do not infer diameter ratios or pressure automatically

For circular sections, area ratio three means diameter ratio √3, not three. A side-view width is not automatically cross-sectional area; find labels or define the sections. Faster narrow flow alone gives no pressure difference value without height, losses and an energy relation. Continuity expresses conservation. Leave pressure unspecified rather than invent pascals. Nor can this speed replace depth in a static-fluid pressure formula; the models require different inputs and conditions.

For an independent circular-section card, doubled diameter quadruples area. Changing the original narrow 5cm² to 20cm² at Q=0.6L/s gives mean speed 0.30m/s, one quarter of 1.20, not half. This changes the section configuration; retain the original fifteen/five-area problem separately rather than mix areas from two copies.

Rewrite conservation for branches or changing density

In a branching copy with 0.6L/s main flow and one branch 0.2L/s, the other carries 0.4L/s; each branch does not equal the main flow. Leaks or accumulation require separate outflow/storage terms. For a gas with appreciably changing density, steady leak-free mass flow ρAv is conserved, and unequal densities cannot cancel. Retain ρ1 and ρ2 symbolically rather than force the water speeds 0.40/1.20 into a new gas problem. Missing model conditions are not an import failure.

Troubleshoot section identity, mean values and squared units

A narrow speed of 0.133m/s suggests an inverted area ratio; 0.0012 may reflect a thousandfold area-conversion error. Compare volumes over the same one or ten seconds, not different intervals. A backward arrow may be a caption leader rather than flow: preserve an explicit velocity legend. Repair one copy condition and return to the two area labels. A real measurement at one centre point is insufficient; do not silently promote it to a section mean.

Reopen and explain different speeds with equal flow

Reopen without the ledger and predict 0.40/1.20m/s, shared 0.6L/s and 6L over ten seconds, then verify. Use equal-time water plugs to explain the longer narrow segment and add 0.4L/s to the branch copy. Deliver unit conversion, conservation assumptions and the unspecified-pressure note.
