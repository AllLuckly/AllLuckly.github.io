A filename can say volume one while internal metadata contains only a series title; an attractive cover may omit the volume altogether. Use six practice volumes to establish a clear sequence through Book Isle's metadata, series and sorting tools. Check content before changing display data, then verify browsing and resume behaviour separately.

List actual volumes from the content

Record each volume's title, author, number, edition and filename, using title pages, copyright or original release information. Do not infer order from folder names alone. Prequels, extras and collected editions may not follow simple numbering, so decide whether you want publication or story chronology.

Keep different translations distinguishable instead of silently assembling a mixed set to fill six positions. Mark missing volumes and duplicate sources explicitly. A gap then has an explanation; you need not rewrite actual titles merely to make the sequence appear complete.

Sample every volume's beginning and end

Open title pages and early chapters after import to confirm volume identity, then inspect endings for samples or incomplete collected editions. Similar covers, titles and sizes cannot replace text checks, particularly when files have passed through several rounds of collection and renaming.

For collected and separate editions, record their content overlap. An unlabelled omnibus in the middle can cause repeated reading. Book Isle organises metadata, while contents and headings establish the relationship. Do not pursue consecutive numbers at the expense of accurate content identification.

Organise titles, authors and series data

Use your installed metadata editor to update verified title, author and series information. Apply consistent series numbering where that field exists and inspect saved results, without searching for unavailable fields. Keep identifying names concise and place extended plot or ordering explanations in notes or an external list.

Shelf metadata is distinct from file contents. A display-name change does not rewrite EPUB text or server originals or fix incorrect chapters. Reimports from different sources still need edition checks; one earlier organisation pass cannot guarantee correct identification of every future matching filename.

Inspect the actual sorting result

Use list or grid and available sorting and filtering to compare the set. Text-based sorting can arrange digits and Chinese numerals unexpectedly. Consistent padded volume numbers in display names can help while preserving real titles; read the resulting order completely, including side stories.

Even an explicit series sort needs inspection for fractions, prequels and unnumbered extras. Saving an edit does not prove the browsing order. Keep a recommended reading order in your own list when useful while preserving factual shelf metadata; those records can serve different purposes.

Set rules for missing volumes and companion formats

When filling a gap, verify text and edition first. For EPUB and PDF of one volume, establish matching editions before treating them as companion formats. Matching volume numbers do not equate revisions; covers, pages and old note locations may differ.

Use clear series, edition and volume directories in external backups too. The shelf is a reading entry point; external storage preserves sources. Do not batch-rename the only originals and lose the previous relationship for display consistency. Keep a list and adjust reversible copies gradually.

Separate reading status from numbering

Finished volume one, current volume two and unstarted volume three are reading states, not volume-name edits. Use actual status or labels, then inspect search and filters. When moving into an omnibus midway, record corresponding chapters rather than marking every separate volume finished automatically.

Leave and reopen the current volume to check resume identity. Similar covers call for checking title and number before reading. On another device, verify series data and actual files separately; synced metadata alone cannot establish which edition's text opens there.

Cross-check the beginning, gap and final volume

Verify that the first volume really starts the series, the former gap's neighbours connect and the final volume ends completely. Read displayed names in sequence against the original list. Fix affected entries rather than deleting the whole set because one volume is misplaced.

Retain a short rule for edition coverage, extras, omnibus relationships and missing books. Apply it to later additions. Shelf numbering should help reading while actual volumes and editions remain grounded in content; tidy appearance must not create a book that does not exist.
