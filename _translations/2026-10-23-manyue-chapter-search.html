In a series with hundreds of chapters or multiple volume groups, scrolling can easily miss the target. ManYue's “Find Chapter” narrows the list by title, number, and volume name. Practice locating a chapter in a long list and checking it against your reading position.

Understand the chapter list's organization first

Open the work's details and confirm that its chapter list loaded. Is it a flat chapter list or volumes containing chapters? Record a specific target such as volume three, chapter twelve, or a distinctly titled chapter, not merely the twelfth button on screen.

Lists may be ascending or descending, and newly inserted chapters change visible positions. Display order is different from chapter identity, particularly for extras, specials, and prologues that may not follow ordinary numbering. Understanding the structure prevents mistaking reordered entries for reset reading progress.

Open Find Chapter

Find the magnifying-glass button named “Find Chapter” in the chapter area. Enter a distinctive title fragment such as “Rainy Night” and observe the results. Try a number if you know it, or a volume-name clue for volume-based works. Actual matching depends on the chapter text supplied by the source.

Do not enter a chapter title in online work search and expect the same result. Work search queries the catalog of works; Find Chapter searches only the current work's chapter list. Choosing the right scope avoids irrelevant results and accidental opening of another same-named work.

Read the complete title even after a numeric match

Entering “12” may match chapter twelve, chapter one hundred twenty, a volume number, or a number in a title. Read the full name and volume group before selecting, narrowing the query if necessary. Numbers are clues, not the entire basis for a jump, especially when sources use “012,” spelled-out numbers, or other numbering styles.

If a chapter has multiple versions, revisions, or parts, read each title and source description. Verify one with a known opening to confirm that its list name matches the content. Avoid rapidly opening several results merely to find progress, since opening a new chapter may update the current reading position.

Check the content after jumping

Select a result to enter the reader, check whether the opening belongs to the intended chapter, and inspect the current page number. Selecting the chapter you were reading may resume its recorded position; another chapter may begin at the start. Follow the actual result rather than expecting every selection to return to page one.

Check two or three consecutive pages to confirm narrative continuity. Same-titled chapters from different sources may include extra covers, advertisements, or rearranged pages, so old page numbers may not identify the same image. When recovering a position from another edition, plot and page details are more reliable than numbers alone. Save a clear page-level bookmark if necessary.

Use sorting to help find the latest chapter

To find recent updates, use the chapter area's ascending and descending sort controls. Descending can bring new chapters forward; ascending suits reading from the beginning. This changes list order, not the pages inside a file. Check the first title afterward so you know the current direction.

For grouped lists, volume names may help more than chapter numbers. “Volume Two” and “Extras,” for example, may both contain chapter one; distinguish groups first to reduce mistakes. Do not modify the source's chapter structure just for sorting convenience. For missing or disordered lists, check source refresh and maintenance instead of masking every issue with search.

Broaden the query gradually when there are no results

Remove punctuation and spaces first, shorten the title, and then try a chapter number or volume name. If nothing appears, inspect the full list to confirm the chapter actually exists. Find Chapter does not retrieve content the source has not supplied or automatically complete an unsuccessfully loaded list.

If the source just updated, refresh the details and check again. If a chapter was renamed or merged, compare its new title and content. Failure to find an old name does not immediately mean deletion; naming may simply have changed. Retained bookmarks and reading clues help rebuild the association in the new list.

Frequently asked questions

Why does searching a number return so many chapters? The query matches chapter information, and numbers may occur in several places. Add title or volume clues and read complete results rather than assuming the first match is the target chapter.

A chapter exists in the list, but its old name cannot be found. Why? Full-width characters, alternative number styles, abbreviations, or source renaming may explain it. Test a short distinctive word and compare the current name in the full list if necessary. Search does not guarantee semantic equivalence across all naming forms.

Will a change in chapter position always invalidate old bookmarks? Not necessarily. The app and source may retain stable chapter identifiers, but still recheck after changing sources or versions or after server-side rebuilding. Bookmarks, chapter sequence numbers, and file page numbers represent different positions; one number alone cannot settle the issue.

Retain the position you found

Once you confirm the target chapter, use Page-level bookmarks to save pages for later review. To find the work you last read, see Reading history. For many ongoing series, combine this with a Series-following checklist to reduce repeated manual scrolling.
