A colour diagram may lose distinctions when copied in monochrome. Adapt an owned category diagram using words, outlines and fill differences. Preserve its meaning without claiming identical results across all copying equipment.

List information encoded only by colour

Choose a simple owned diagram and list each colour’s meaning and colour-only state. Preserve the colour source and make an independent adaptation with unchanged content. Inspect actual greyscale/monochrome output first. Prioritise lost meaning rather than adding patterns to every decorative colour.

Add explicit category labels or identifiers

Label categories directly or use IDs with a complete key. Keep placement stable and meanings explicit. Inspect names and boundaries without fills. Replace tiny colour-only status dots with meaningful text labels where needed. Establish semantics before styling rather than merely desaturating the original.

Use restrained, consistent fill differences

Use a small vocabulary such as white, light grey and sparse hatching. Test hatch spacing and weight in one area before duplication, masking as needed. Apply the same treatment to the same category. Preserve text keys even with patterns because thin detail may disappear in copying.

Test line-style distinctions at small size

Define weight or continuous/segmented line conventions in a legend and test them at delivery size. Use supported controls or carefully maintained manual segments. Keep endpoints clear and meanings unambiguous. If styles remain hard to distinguish, use relationship labels instead of expanding a difficult symbol vocabulary.

Improve text contrast without crowding

Increase text/background contrast before size or heavy outlines. Keep patterns away from text through plain backing or placement. Preserve conditions and full wording when wrapping labels. Inspect small legend text too and retain a restrained hierarchy that separates primary information from notes.

Verify through actual monochrome output

Export and, where relevant, test on the actual copying equipment. Check every category, relationship and small key. Scope observations to those conditions. Repair disappearing patterns through spacing/weight or text alternatives, changing one rule at a time and retaining earlier proofs for comparison.

Cross-check content between colour and monochrome

Compare categories, counts, conditions and wording across both versions. Visual conventions may differ, but data and relationships must agree. Label colour/monochrome files and intended uses clearly. Update both from a common content list and reopen delivery files to exclude experiments or stale revisions.

Diagnose lost information before adding effects

Identify whether categories, connectors or wording failed instead of darkening everything. Use labels, clearer paths or text contrast appropriately. Reduce dense patterns and heavy frames that merge. Check output scaling. Verify that the monochrome version independently explains every region and relationship without referring back to the colour original.
