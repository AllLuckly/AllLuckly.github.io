Verify an owned clip locally, then use Apple system Screen Mirroring with your own compatible receiver and separately inspect picture, subtitles and sound.

Prepare a recognizable clip and your own receiver

Use an iPhone or iPad, an authorized receiver supporting Apple Screen Mirroring and an owned short video with clear image and sound. Record name, duration and one image/sound cue, and confirm the receiver’s required network conditions. Apple describes Screen Mirroring as a system feature; the iCouldPlay listing describes local and network playback but does not promise an in-app AirPlay button or universal casting. Without a compatible receiver, complete only the device baseline.

Verify the clip and subtitles on the device

Open the clip in iCouldPlay and check picture, playhead and sound on the device. If using subtitles, record the current visible track and one text cue. Do not change video, subtitle, audio track and playback engine together. Resolve existing local playback or sound failure before diagnosing mirroring. Set a suitable volume and record that the receiver has not yet participated.

Confirm the system receiver and environment

Following Apple’s guidance, verify the phone and compatible receiver meet the same-Wi-Fi requirement, and that the displayed TV or Mac is yours. Screen Mirroring can show other device screens and notifications, so use a private suitable setting and close content you do not wish to display. If the receiver is absent, check network and power; do not select an unfamiliar neighbor’s device or weaken router settings.

Start Screen Mirroring using actual system controls

Open Control Center, use the actual Screen Mirroring control and choose the verified receiver. If your receiver displays a pairing code, follow the real system prompt. Return to iCouldPlay and observe the same clip. This is system mirroring, not an assumed in-app casting menu, automatic format conversion or independent receiver player. If the control is missing, connection is refused or app content does not display, retain feedback and mark pending; not all devices and content are guaranteed.

Compare picture and sound on both screens

Near the same cue, inspect whether the correct video appears on the device and receiver and where sound actually comes from. If subtitles were visible locally, check the receiver’s text and picture placement; absent or unselected local subtitles cannot establish mirroring loss. Aspect ratio, orientation and visible area may differ. Record both screens instead of treating black bars as file damage or proof of successful HDR conversion. Casual viewing and listening cannot establish precise synchronization latency. If the receiver displays system screens but not the video, record the observed source and compare an owned clip known to play. Do not bypass content protection or generalize one restricted item to all files. Volume may be controlled on different devices; record the actual control used.

Change one condition in a reversible comparison

For a mismatch, keep source, subtitles and audio track fixed, record whether system mirroring remains connected and change only one manageable condition, such as seeking a few seconds before the same cue and playing normally. If orientation differs, record device orientation and receiver state first. Do not change file, network, player engine and display settings at once. Device, content or OS support can limit results; leave an unknown cause pending rather than promising mirroring for every app or protected item.

Stop system mirroring and verify local recovery

Use the system Screen Mirroring control to stop mirroring or the actual equivalent, and confirm the receiver no longer displays the device screen. Return to iCouldPlay to check the video position and current sound destination. Record any manual pause or resume rather than assuming automatic recovery. If audio still routes to the receiver, inspect system playback destinations and restore an available choice. Do not delete media, reset networking or unpair unrelated devices.

Deliver three bounded observations

Keep device baseline, mirrored and stopped rows with receiver identity, network condition, picture, subtitles, sound destination, user actions and pending checks; omit private network and device details from public sharing. Restore ordinary volume, subtitles and position. Apple documents system mirroring and the iCouldPlay listing describes media playback; support depends on the actual OS, receiver and content. No in-app AirPlay button or completed TV/device test is claimed.
