Ten equally long thick lines obscure the distinction between squid arms and tentacles, while counting fins adds another mistake. Reference California market squid for an invented oblique pose in Aquarell. Separate mantle, head, eight arms and two tentacles before fins and occlusion, without borrowing specialised hooks from colossal squid.

Set mantle and head direction

Use an approximately 1900×1400 px landscape canvas, blue-grey water and body angled down-right with appendages spreading left. Block a long tapered mantle, short head at the wider front and narrow rear; arms do not grow at both ends. Separate sketch, mantle, fins, head, arms and tentacles. Remove an invented fish-tail fork and repair balloon-with-noodles proportions through the head connection. Drawn length is not body size, age, catch specification or speed. Place the eye on the head side, not a fin or arm tip.

Attach paired fins to the mantle sides

Place triangular paired fins at the rear mantle sides, with the far fin partly occluded and a clear near transition. They are not long head appendages or pieces suspended on stalks. Check attachment in one tone before light edge modelling. Reduce butterfly-like fins dominating the body to the chosen rear arrangement. Verify the hidden counterpart in a sketch without exposing it through the mantle. Count fins separately from eight arms and two tentacles, without claiming all squid have identical triangular fins.

Branch eight arms from the head region

Mark eight numbered routes from the region around the mouth at the front of the head, then thicken them. They share an oral base region, not the rear mantle or eyeball. Choose a pose with mostly traceable roots and plausible hidden far segments. Check branching and duplicate contours if nine short arms appear; do not count two edges of one arm as two when only seven read. Numbers are private checks. A natural image need not expose all eight full arms, and occlusion is not proof of an actually missing appendage.

Give two longer tentacles terminal clubs

Add two longer tentacles in another checking colour, with slender stalks and modest terminal clubs. They are not two more equally short thick arms, nor ribbons from fin tips. Place them in clear space and trace each back to the head without swapping identities at crossings. Repair disconnected club/stalk transitions and reduce hand-sized clubs. Use the ordinary arm/tentacle distinction without unverified rotating hooks or giant suckers. The pose is not an actual predation demonstration or animal-handling instruction.

Place sucker hints on the appropriate visible surfaces

Suggest suckers modestly on selected viewer-facing inner arm surfaces and terminal clubs, not evenly over fins, mantle or every tentacle stalk. Use a few quiet shapes following curved surfaces and omit hidden sides. Soften pearl-necklace dots and retain continuous flesh; reduce outward rows if one arm splits visually into two. Exact arrangement/count needs a specific biological reference and is not reproduced here. Hidden mouthparts and internal organs need no transparency, and texture must not overwhelm attachment.

Keep soft-body continuity through restrained shade

Choose grey-pink and pale violet, broad soft mantle shade, thinner fins and a dark restrained eye. Keep appendages in one body palette with modest depth differences rather than detachable plastic rods. Hide suckers and check mantle/head/appendage continuity. Soften metallic hard-shell glints and repair detached fin roots. Invented colours diagnose neither freshness, health nor emotion and establish no observed glowing or rapid colour-changing behaviour.

Use three separate inventories

At reduced size, keep mantle/head, paired fins and oral appendages in separate inventories. Trace eight arm bases, then two slender tentacles and clubs rather than treating twelve visible shapes as success. Explain an occluded arm on private checks, not a transparent finished diagram. Redirect an invented foreground tentacle to reveal roots rather than deleting an appendage. Check that an extra eye, fin tip or sucker contour has not become another arm. Hide temporary numbers and complete hidden outlines before export.
