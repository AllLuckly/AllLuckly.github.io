Use a permitted collection reference to draw a racket head in perspective, its crossed strings and wrapped grip.

Choose a reference and permission

Choose a permitted photograph of a wooden tennis racket and record its source, viewpoint and access date. Smithsonian describes Arthur Ashe’s racket as having a wooden outer frame, clear strings and a textured leather grip. Those materials and colors describe that object, not every racket. If reproduction or sharing is not permitted, use the catalog only to understand the structure and find another image you are allowed to use.

Create a canvas and composition

Create a portrait canvas in Aquarell of about 1600 by 1800 pixels and save the original; the dimensions are an exercise choice. Use the visible layer controls to separate background, frame, strings, grip and sketch. First draw a slightly slanted center axis connecting head and handle. Leave margins around the head and end of the grip. Use the controls actually visible if the interface labels differ.

Compare head and handle proportions

Block the outer head with a large oval and mark the handle center and end below it. Compare head width with overall length, and observe whether the head faces the viewer or narrows because it is tilted. Avoid drawing individual strings before proportions are stable. At thumbnail size check for an oversized head or short handle, then adjust the whole construction rather than disconnecting its parts.

Draw a frame with thickness

Draw the outer contour and a nearby inner contour to give the frame thickness. Apparent frame width may vary with perspective and occlusion, but should not change abruptly without evidence. Choose one light direction and use middle browns to separate the face and side of the wood. Do not start with grain. With strings hidden, the frame itself should remain a continuous closed shape.

Connect the throat and grip

Observe how the lower frame narrows toward the center and joins the handle. Draw the two connecting edges and check them against the center axis. If strings or shadow hide the joint, mark it uncertain instead of inventing mechanical parts. Give the grip visible width and an end rather than reducing it to a line. When changing the angle, check that head and handle still read as one racket.

Arrange longitudinal and cross strings

On a separate layer draw a few strings along the head’s long direction, then add cross strings. Observe how visible intersections and spacing change near the narrowing frame. Stop the lines at the inner edge instead of extending them through the wood into the background. Strings can be thin and clear, with apparent color influenced by the background. Use subtle lines, then check intersections up close and the frame at thumbnail size.

Suggest the wrapped grip

Block the grip in a darker brown, then use a few diagonal lines or value bands to suggest wrapping. Follow the handle surface rather than placing floating parallel lines outside it; apparent band width can change slightly with perspective. Give the end a clear but restrained contour. At small sizes, fewer texture marks and a readable grip value work better than dense lines.

Place background and cast shadow

Use a muted background that separates strings and frame. A soft cast shadow beneath the racket can place it on a surface. Keep the shadow direction consistent with the frame lighting, usually more concentrated near contact and softer farther away, following the reference or stated scene. Avoid matching the fine strings and background exactly in value, or correct strings may disappear.

Compare readability before and after detail

Save a working copy and compare the broad frame-and-grip shapes with the version containing strings and texture. Reduce both and check that the center axis, head direction and handle connection remain clear. If dense strings become a black patch, reduce their contrast or number instead of enlarging the frame to hide the problem. Return to normal size and confirm that lines stop at the inner frame.

Check string crossings and boundaries

Follow one longitudinal string from the top to the bottom of the head and check that its crossings with the cross strings stay inside the frame. A drawing need not depict every woven junction, but lines should not stop arbitrarily or pass through the wood. Check which strings are visible near the throat in the reference; leave obscured portions unknown instead of completing the grid for symmetry. If thick lines turn the head into a dark patch, reduce string-layer opacity or redraw fewer lines, preserving a copy for comparison. Finally check that the reference license covers sharing and record the permitted photograph used.

Troubleshoot structure and save

If the head looks like a flat disk, inspect the inner-to-outer frame spacing and side values. If the grip looks detached, check both sides of the throat for a continuous join. Save the editable original and use the visible share or export control to create a separate PNG or JPEG. Reopen it to check the head, strings, grip end and shadow for clipping. Return to the original to confirm editable layers, record the source and mark unperformed device steps.

Check grip direction

Draw a temporary axis from the head center to the grip end and confirm that the throat still joins the handle. Hide the strings and inspect the joint. If frame edges cross like a knot, correct them before restoring the string layer.
