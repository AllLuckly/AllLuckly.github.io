When a character tells another story, retain both the telling situation and the narrated world. Use an original station passage in Book Isle to mark entry, continuation and return. Inner/outer labels describe relationships, not truth or importance, and do not add guesses to a character’s memory.

Preserve the complete four-part original

Save F1, I1, I2, F2 below; labels are analytical, not spoken words. The frame has narrator and sister waiting; the sister recounts finding a key at ten; the ending returns to today’s ticket and bus. Import EPUB or clearly numbered PDF, checking quotes, order and final sentences while retaining editable sources. Screen pages do not make I1/I2 separate stories, nor are labels terminology supplied by an original author. This is invented, not a real family event.

F1: I waited with my sister under the station roof. She said, "Let me tell you what happened when I was ten."
I1: "I hid a key in a hollow tree. The next day I could not find that tree."
I2: "A gardener helped me search the wall-side path. At dusk I carried the key home."
F2: My sister stopped telling and handed me today's ticket. Our bus arrived.

Record frame teller, listener and setting

In F1 the narrating I and sister wait at the station. Sister becomes the embedded teller and I the on-scene listener. Let me tell establishes a telling act inside the frame, not the real author appearing before readers. Record evidence for waiting, telling and listening without equating first person with author. The frame is itself narrated; relative labels suffice without forcing all theory into fixed numerical levels. Identify who speaks and where the telling occurs before entering.

Enter a new narrated event sequence

I in I1 refers to the sister’s younger self, not the frame narrator. Hiding the key and failing to locate the tree next day form her narrated events. I2 continues with a gardener’s help and carrying the key home at dusk. Keep inner time, place and participants separate; the gardener is not thereby present at the station. Both paragraphs belong to one telling. A new quotation opening per paragraph does not add another level. Save pronoun attribution with event order.

Use current actions and time anchors for return

F2 stops the sister’s telling, hands over today’s ticket and brings the bus. These restore the frame rather than place today’s ticket in her childhood. Mark stopped telling and today’s ticket as different cues, and connect bus arrival to waiting rather than an invented third day of the embedded story. Return does not independently verify its truth. I2’s homecoming can end the inner event sequence, but F2 is still needed to establish the frame return.

Keep two timelines: the inner account has next day and dusk; the frame gives waiting and bus arrival without telling duration. Inner elapsed days do not become station waiting time, and recalling age ten does not establish present age. Preserve unknown durations separately from narrated time.

Use past tense and quotation controls

In I remembered my tenth birthday while the bus was late, the frame narrator recalls directly without another character taking over a story; past time alone is insufficient. Sister saying Please open the window is a request, not an unfolded embedded event sequence. These controls reject time shift or quotation marks alone as sufficient. More complex literature may blur embedding; assess telling, voice, events and context rather than impose a universal punctuation rule.

Interpret what the framing does

The frame makes the old events something the sister chooses to tell a particular listener, not an unexplained historical insertion. Possible functions include sharing experience, filling a waiting interval or juxtaposing assistance in finding a key with handing over a ticket. These are candidates, not morals forced by levels. An inner story can exceed its frame in length without making the frame meaningless. Separate structural evidence from effects, and do not invent why she tells it today.

Create entry and return anchors in Book Isle

Highlight or locate F1’s telling act and F2’s stopping, with I1–I2 events in a separate note. Traverse entry, embedded sequence, return rather than search I alone. A gardener turned into a current passenger suggests a participant error; one level per quoted paragraph ignores continuity; the birthday control checks a forced embedding label. Use an external ledger if needed rather than assume automatic level detection. Reread all four passages after one correction and leave unperformed operations pending.

Reopen and deliver a voice-and-setting ledger

Reopen F1 and explain frame I, embedded sister and both settings before using labels, then identify two F2 return cues. Use both controls to explain why past time and direct quotation alone are insufficient. Retain that the key story is the sister’s account, not independently verified by the reader. Save full source, entry/return positions and effect candidates; leave reopening blank if unperformed. Longer works need wider context rather than excerpts assembled into a new frame.
