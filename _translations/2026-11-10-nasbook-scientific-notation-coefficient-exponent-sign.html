Check the coefficient range for standard form

Convert the small decimal and multiply back

Write 0.0042 as 4.2×10 to power −3 because that power equals 1/1000, and 4.2 divided by one thousand returns 0.0042. Establish the power value, not merely left/right movement. Distinguish negative exponent from negative coefficient

4.2×10⁻³ remains positive; −4.2×10⁻³ is −0.0042. One minus belongs to the power of positive ten and the other to the coefficient; they do not cancel. A parenthesised negative base such as (−10) raised to a power is another expression requiring its own rules. Record base ten, exponent −3 and coefficient ±4.2 separately without dropping similar-looking marks. Resolve unclear superscripts/parentheses visually in the original rather than relying on one read-aloud impression.

Adjust coefficient and power together

42×10⁻⁴ equals 0.0042. Normalising divides forty-two by ten and changes exponent −4 to −3 simultaneously. Changing only the coefficient makes the value ten times smaller; misadjusting both worsens it. Retain an equality chain and reverse decimal, not just a moved point.
