An exploded view is not an arbitrary collection of floating pieces. Separate a fictional toy rocket’s nose, body and base along one axis so their relationships remain understandable. Draw it by hand without assuming automatic disassembly or a 3D model, and treat it as illustration rather than manufacturing guidance.

Draw the assembled form first

On an approximately 1500×1900 px canvas, draw an assembled nose, cylindrical body and wider base with matching ellipse directions and one axis. Save this complete structure before separation. Limit it to three recognisable parts instead of adding bolts that conceal an incoherent original.

Separate parts along the common axis

Move nose up and base down while keeping the body central and all centres aligned. Expose useful joining faces without losing relation. Shorten gaps between unrelated-looking pieces or enlarge a needed opening, using separation for explanation rather than equal decorative spacing.

Make joining faces correspond

Match adjacent nose/body and body/base joining faces in size and direction. Simple lips or recesses may explain correspondence without representing a real locking mechanism. Compare pairs before guide lines. Correct incompatible contours rather than hiding them with more dashed connections.

Preserve each part’s volume

Model each part under shared upper-left light: nose planes, cylinder turns and base top/side. Establish grey volume before restrained colour. Add sidewalls to flat cutouts or soften uniformly metallic reflections, keeping identity readable beyond colour coding.

Connect corresponding points with guide lines

Use one or two pale fine dashed guides between corresponding centres, avoiding labels and hidden solid areas. Reduce competing guide weight. If correspondence remains unclear, correct part placement and joining faces; guides support existing relationships rather than repair incompatible geometry.

Use labels that explain the parts

Give each part one short name and simple fictional role. Place labels unambiguously nearby with clear pointers. Test the longest note before decoration and check any numbering. Do not add unverifiable material specifications or manufacturing precision merely to make the illustration seem technical.

Test correspondence with a small assembled view

Optionally add a subdued assembled inset matching the main silhouette. Mentally recombine the parts and check nose width, body length and base direction against the original. Correct genuine conflicts rather than dismissing them as schematic. Omit the inset if the main relationship is already clear.

Compare adjacent joining faces

Study adjacent nose and body openings side by side with matching axes and approximate diameters. Compare endpoints before returning them to separated positions. Repair proportions rather than explaining conflicts in text; no real mechanical specification is implied by this fictional visual correspondence.

Separate assembly guides from label pointers

Distinguish axial assembly guides from pointers naming one part. Test each set with the other hidden, moving labels instead of thickening tangled lines. Omit redundant guides where relationships already read clearly. The structure should organise the explanation rather than a dense web of helpers.

Keep assembled and separated versions

Keep assembled and exploded PNGs with editable parts and labels. Next, tilt the axis and rebuild ellipse compression, separation and labels rather than rotating the flat picture. Record the most error-prone connection. The result remains a visual explanation; real fabrication requires separately verified design information.
