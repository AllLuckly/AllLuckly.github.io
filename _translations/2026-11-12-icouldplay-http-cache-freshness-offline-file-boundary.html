max-age=600 is neither six hundred megabytes nor a ten-minute film. Separate HTTP freshness lifetime, current response age and possession of a complete offline file. Ordinary iCouldPlay viewing and external response evidence have different scopes. No cache-header viewer, system-clock change or data clearing is assumed.

1. Fix an authorized resource identity

Choose an owned small video or an identified resource from a source you manage. Record identity, version, source and acquisition time. Without response visibility, record viewing and mark headers unavailable instead of inferring them from smooth playback. Attribute existing desktop-tool or administrator evidence. Distinguish playlists, artwork, subtitles and media responses, which may have different policies. Artwork freshness does not establish video freshness, and matching filenames do not establish identity. Retain originals and use a small traceable sample.

2. Separate a header example from calculation assumptions

The header below is invented, not an observed server result. The two cases explicitly supply current age for paper arithmetic. Record seconds and convert 600 seconds into ten minutes of freshness, not clip duration. Actual age calculations involve protocol timing; neither file creation date nor one Age field automatically supplies all conditions. These cases stipulate ages rather than pretending server and transit evidence was obtained.

Example header: Cache-Control: max-age=600
Case A: freshness_lifetime=600 seconds; current_age=100 seconds
Case B: freshness_lifetime=600 seconds; current_age=600 seconds

3. Check the strict freshness boundary

RFC 9111’s freshness rule compares freshness lifetime strictly greater than current age. With no additional restrictive conditions in this paper example, A passes 600>100; B does not pass 600>600. A’s five-hundred-second difference is arithmetic, not a guarantee of retention or playable duration. Separate definition, calculation and actual app state. Missing real evidence remains unresolved rather than being certified by the teaching card.

4. Freshness is neither complete storage nor permanent identity

Freshness concerns reuse without validation of a response. It does not prove a complete video is on the phone or that the origin cannot change. File completeness, dependencies and offline playback need separate checks. A fresh saved HLS playlist does not contain all segments. Conversely, reaching a freshness boundary does not itself remove bytes from an owned complete copy. Do not describe the number as an app deletion timer or infer full storage from a resume position.

5. Check online and local-copy paths separately

For a real source and an independent complete copy, open them separately and check opening, a middle action and ending marker, retaining selected source identity. Do not certify full offline playback by disconnecting after caching one short portion. Complete offline preparation needs its established scope and reopen checks. Online-only observations remain online-only; unperformed local/offline stages remain unchecked. Path differences do not uniquely locate a cache layer. Record dates and versions so a later origin update does not silently invalidate the comparison.

6. Read other directives by definition

no-cache requires validation before reuse; its name does not simply mean never stored. no-store is a different storage constraint. Preserve directives and resource scope without interchanging them or claiming either deletes every existing film. Other directives and shared/private cache conditions can affect actual decisions; the two paper cases do not cover every combination. Consult definitions rather than changing server policies to force a result. Unseen app internals remain unknown, with external declarations and viewing kept separate.

7. Troubleshoot units, response identity and history

Correct megabytes back to seconds. For unexpected refresh behavior, identify request time, resource and final response; browser history state is not automatically HTTP freshness. Record the anchor and time for an old picture; do not immediately clear or request clearing all app data. Changed resources, playlist updates and incomplete downloads require separate evidence rather than the word cache as a diagnosis. Change one identified condition, preserve responses/copies and leave the system clock alone. Unexplained differences remain unresolved rather than proving a server or client violation.

8. Finish three layers of evidence

Keep response provenance/values, paper age conditions/results, actual online/copy observations and unknowns. Leave playback, then reopen the same material and verify source selection. Do not pass an unperformed offline stage. Check that ten-minute freshness never became film duration, caching never became complete downloading and expiration never became automatic deletion. Preserve originals and recheck a new resource. The outcome is a layered account of caching and observed readability, not permanent availability across all servers, networks or devices.
