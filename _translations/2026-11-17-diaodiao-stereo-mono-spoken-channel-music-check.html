Use original left/right spoken labels to separate channel content, audible direction and the current playback output.

1. Prepare a sample with explicit left/right content

In a familiar external editor make a twenty-second owned sample: spoken “LEFT” only in the left channel near five seconds and “RIGHT” only in the right near twelve, with recognizable opening/end content. Verify channel assignments before exporting a system-readable small file, labelled S22 externally. Spoken words are content labels, not routing commands: recording both in one channel and renaming the file does not create a stereo control. Preserve project/source instead of substituting an unknown online test whose preparation is unclear.

2. Preserve stereo while making a mono comparison

Create a separate twenty-second mono comparison M22 from that owned material, retaining both spoken cues and verifying them externally. Follow the editor’s mixing/gain rules to avoid clipping; maximum volume is unnecessary. Audacity distinguishes left/right splits, mono splits and stereo joining, which are not equivalent renaming operations. If preparation is unfamiliar, use authorised samples with independently verified channels rather than seek an unpublished mixer or channel splitter in 调调. Keep content identity and channel reports for both copies.

3. Identify both imported items separately

Import S22 and M22 separately and inspect feedback, title and approximately twenty-second duration. If exports are actually identical or already present, investigate duplicate handling without deleting music to force additions. Names may not display verbatim, so use verified metadata/opening sound as identity. Matching covers say nothing about channels. First establish that both LEFT and RIGHT are audible in each item before judging side; missing source content is different from a routing observation.

4. Record the actual listening output

Choose a two-sided listening device with identifiable left/right markings and record that it is connected and sounding. Speakers can verify content, but unknown directional behavior cannot pass a full channel-direction check. Match L/R wearing identity and use ordinary comfortable volume. Record any known mono, balance or other processing state rather than reset several settings together. The player is not promised to detect system processing. If channel merging is unknown, limit the directional conclusion; the word LEFT cannot itself prove the physical output side.

5. Listen to both cues in the stereo round

Open S22 explicitly in 调调 and play normally without changing output or theme. Near five/twelve seconds record word, apparent side and timer. If both sides are audible, retain that result instead of pre-filling separation. Speech lasts over an interval; onset and passage observations suffice without acoustic-measurement claims. Replay from before a cue rather than miss its onset and diagnose absence from a tail. Pause after both checks and retain this round’s conditions instead of copying mono observations into S. For example, LEFT on both sides at five and RIGHT on both at twelve retains both cues but does not establish separation. Hearing only LEFT throughout is a different missing-content pattern. Separate cells preserve more useful evidence than one blanket “audio normal” label.

6. Check content in the mono round

Keep output and volume fixed, select M22 and hear both cues from the start. Mono has one content channel; distribution to physical sides depends on the playback/system chain, so no universal spatial sensation is prescribed. Separate presence of both words from perceived direction and compare each row with S. A missing word in M calls for checking the external mix before claiming the app randomly removed a channel. These files characterize this particular chain, not headphone, hearing or coding-quality certification. Both S and M remain twenty seconds; S does not become forty because its channels run in parallel. A forty-second copy may have concatenated the channels externally. That preparation change alters the listening task and should be corrected before returning to the player.

7. Diagnose source preparation and output separately

For reversed stereo, check L/R identity, source assignments and selected item first. If S/M both expose only one word, inspect source and output without assuming a particular in-app conversion. A changed output needs a new two-cue round rather than one row combining devices. One silent side does not prove the original lacks another channel; inspect the report separately. Keep minimal owned samples and observations instead of private music or unsupported system changes made merely to produce a desired answer. With only one earbud, record audible content but leave the other side unknown. Do not delete S because RIGHT was absent. Preserve project and output conditions so the same sample can be repeated with a known two-sided route, rather than change material at the same time.

8. Return to stereo and deliver the four-column comparison

Return to S22 for one cue, verify identity, then stop and restore output conditions actually changed. Deliver four columns: file/source channels, spoken word, observed sound and output state, leaving unknowns explicit. Explain why mono can retain two words and why hearing LEFT alone does not prove independent left routing. Preserve both versions. New files/devices need new observations; one twenty-second round cannot establish every track/output’s separation or sound quality.
