A cross dividing an orbit into four displayed pieces does not establish one quarter-period per piece. Read an original ellipse in ManYue, separating centre, gravitational focus, curved swept area and period scaling. Use an ideal two-body approximation, not a precision ephemeris or an assumed in-app orbit calculation.

Separate the ellipse centre from its focus

Externally draw x²/25+y²/16=1 with O=(0,0), semimajor a=5, semiminor b=4 and c=√(25−16)=3. Put the dominant mass at S=(3,0); the other focus is a reference, not a second Sun. Use abstract length units, a much lighter orbiting object and ignored perturbations. Make P1 coordinates/orbit, P2 areas and P3 period controls as three PDF pages. Check O/S labels on import and bookmark P2, retaining editable work.

Measure near and far distances from S, not O

Right endpoint A=(5,0) is distance 2 from S, left B=(−5,0) distance 8. Both are five from O, but O is not the attracting focus, so those equal fives do not imply constant speed. Top P=(0,4) is five from S and lies on the ellipse. Draw radius lines from S and label model near/far points without converting abstract lengths to real Earth distances. Zoom changes no 2:8 ratio or stipulated a,b/focus values.

Keep the curved orbital boundary in the area law

Kepler’s second law compares area swept by the line from S over equal time intervals. A region is bounded by two rays from S and the intervening actual orbit arc, not an endpoint chord substituted as an exact triangle. For an equal-time pair label equal Δt and equal curved swept area. The law requires neither equal arc lengths nor equal angles about O or S. Finite intervals need full curved regions; a narrow triangle is only a small-step approximation.

Use the upper-half areas to refute equal-angle equal-time reading

Ellipse area πab is 20π and its upper half 10π. O-based ninety-degree regions O–A–P and O–P–B each have quarter-ellipse area 5π. Triangle SOP has area 3×4/2=6. Therefore S-based curved swept areas A to P and P to B are 5π−6 and 5π+6, unequal and requiring unequal times. Their sum returns 10π. Distinguish curved regions from the small straight triangle rather than moving S to O. This is a reproducible decomposition, not area inferred from shading darkness.

Starting a cycle at A, A-to-P time is (5π−6)/(20π) of a period, about 15.451%; P-to-B is (5π+6)/(20π), about 34.549%, totaling half a period. These percentages follow one constant area rate, not each arc divided by a common linear speed. They give concrete time fractions while leaving the actual period unspecified.

Separate linear speed from angular rate at the apsides

At near/far apsides velocity is perpendicular to the focus-to-body radius. Angular momentum gives r_near v_near=r_far v_far, so near speed is four times far speed here. Instantaneous area rate is also (1/2)r²ω; with radii 1:4, near angular rate is sixteen times far. Four and sixteen answer different questions, not conflicting answers. This ratio is for the two apsides, not a universal inverse-distance rule for speed everywhere. Elsewhere retain the velocity component perpendicular to radius and its angle condition.

A circular orbit is a separate limiting control: a=b and zero focal distance make focus and centre coincide at fixed radius. Equal angles then do correspond to equal areas and times. The present ellipse-centre counterexample does not invalidate circular equal-angle divisions. Identify orbit/focus conditions before applying a graphical shortcut.

Use the same dominant mass in a separate third-law control

On P3 use an independent example around the same Sun-like dominant mass with negligible orbiting mass: reference a=1AU,T=1yr. At a=4AU, the T² ratio equals the a³ ratio 64, giving T=8yr, not 4yr or 16yr. Semimajor four means full major axis eight; an arbitrary apocentre distance is not a. The proportionality needs the same gravitational parameter, not a different central mass. P1’s abstract five-four-three ellipse is not this 4AU reference orbit or a fragment of one real planet’s data.

Troubleshoot focus, curved edges and semiaxis conditions

If the two upper areas receive equal times, check O versus S as the origin of the sweep. An A-to-P area of four may replace the orbit arc with its chord triangle. A linear-speed ratio sixteen may confuse angular rate; a period four may omit the square/cube relation. Repair one copy item and recheck S at the bookmark. Missing semimajor axis or central mass stays insufficient; screenshot dimensions or planet size do not supply them. A nearly circular display does not establish exactly zero eccentricity.
