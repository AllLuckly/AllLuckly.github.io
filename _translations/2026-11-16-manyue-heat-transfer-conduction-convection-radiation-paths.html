An upward arrow does not by itself establish convection: ask whether matter moves in bulk and where energy goes. Draw three energy paths from one warm box in an original report and trace them in ManYue, checking declared energies over one time window. This is diagram reading and paper bookkeeping, not a heating apparatus or an assumed app measurement of temperature, heat flow or material properties.

Draw one warm box and a common environment

Draw a warmer box in a cooler room with three distinct paths: support to table, air circulation and surface radiation. Use relative temperatures first; red/blue does not supply degrees Celsius. Let solid arrows represent energy and hollow curves represent material flow, allowing both where needed. Name source, destination and medium for each. The mechanisms are not three material names. Label an original ideal schematic rather than a real device’s cooling certification, and do not count all stored internal energy as already transferred.

Conduction does not move the entire support

A support hotter at one end can transfer energy through local interactions without the whole support moving to the table. Put an energy arrow along it and note no bulk material flux, not motionless microscopic particles. Solids are convenient examples, but liquids/gases can also conduct; air presence does not exclude conduction. Contact alone does not imply indefinite one-way heating: temperature differences govern net transfer. Keep material boundaries distinct from energy paths and identify both before interpretation.

For convection trace both energy and fluid motion

Heating air can produce rising motion under suitable density/gravity conditions, with cooler replacement flow; show return flow before calling it a circulation. Convection carries energy by bulk fluid motion, driven naturally or by a fan. A fan is forced convection, not a silently retained natural-convection premise. Heat is not a substance that always travels upward: a downward warm stream can carry energy too. Without density, temperature or driving information, record a possible path rather than claiming a faster side.

Radiation crosses a gap without air transport

A warm surface emits electromagnetic radiation and exchanges energy across even a vacuum gap without fluid transport. Wavy lines are a legend, not proof of infrared or visible emission. Cooler surfaces emit too; net exchange depends on both sides. A single outgoing arrow does not mean the cooler side emits nothing. Use opposing and net arrows if useful, but without temperature, area and emissivity, arrow widths cannot yield actual power.

Add three declared energies over the same ten seconds

Use an original ledger for one 10-second interval: the box loses net 60 J through supports, 20 J through air and 10 J by radiation. The declared nonoverlapping paths total 90 J. Average power is 90/10=9 W, with components 6, 2, 1 W. Use one interval and system boundary: do not add a minute of support energy to ten seconds of air transfer. Joules measure energy and watts joules per second; 60/20/10 J are not 90 W total. These are stipulated numbers, not measurements inferred from the diagram.

If initial stored energy is 1000 J and no other input/work/path occurs, losing 90 leaves 910 J. An additional 90 J input can keep total stored energy unchanged. Inferring temperature still needs mass, heat capacity, phase and other conditions: energy balance alone does not make every location equally warm. This ledger answers only the defined system’s energy accounting.

Allow mechanisms to coexist

Near a wall, conduction within material, transfer involving air and radiation can coexist. Do not force exclusive columns into a claim that each object permits only one mechanism. Ledger categories follow chosen boundaries/paths to prevent double counting, not separate natural entities. A vacuum-gap control can reduce gas conduction/convection across that gap, but supports still conduct and radiation remains. Correct “vacuum prevents all heat transfer” by tracing each path instead of merely softening the sentence with usually.

For a stated control change only net radiation from 10 to 5 J over ten seconds, retaining 60/20. The premise gives 85 J and 8.5 W. This is no promise that reflective material achieves those numbers: real changes can affect other paths. Without evidence that the rest stayed fixed, retain multivariable uncertainty rather than presenting a tidy total as an insulation test.

Diagnose upward-arrow shortcuts and double counting

In ManYue follow the legend through support energy, the air loop and opposing/net radiation before circling any upward arrow as convection. A 110 J ledger calls for checking whether the same 20 J air path was counted twice or another value was copied incorrectly, not editing the total to pass. Bookmark the ledger and record ownership externally. Visible numbers are not measured heat flow, and a static drawing supplies no animation speed. Fix layout or zoom rather than guessing from color.

Reopen and deliver three paths and one unit ledger

Reopen and state bulk material motion, medium requirements and source/destination for each path, then recalculate 90 J and 9 W. Retain coexistence, surviving paths across a vacuum-gap assembly and the common-interval premise. If the same 90 J spans 20 seconds, average power becomes 4.5 W; changing only the time label is insufficient. Preserve drawings, ledger and corrections. No heating, temperature measurement or device-performance experiment occurred. Rebuild the ledger after changing materials/boundaries rather than reusing these proportions.
