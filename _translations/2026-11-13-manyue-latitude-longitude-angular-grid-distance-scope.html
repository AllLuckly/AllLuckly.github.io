Matching thirty labels on a graticule do not mean identical lines or distances. Read an original latitude–longitude grid in ManYue and separate latitude, longitude, order and ground distance. Use fictional points, not anyone’s real home. The app supports reading and return navigation, not map routing or conversion of screenshot pixels into kilometres.

Read coordinate and projection conventions first

Prepare a partial world-grid sketch with latitude vertically labelled, longitude horizontally labelled and north at top, without claiming an equidistant projection. Identify the equator and prime meridian instead of guessing axes from screen lines. Record title, location, legend and omitted regions. A fictional coordinate system needs its own conventions; this exercise uses Earth-style angular coordinates but no real private locations.

Locate latitude and longitude separately

Latitude gives north/south angular position relative to the equator; longitude gives east/west angular position relative to the prime meridian. Follow their own lines: meridians connect the poles, while latitude parallels circle the globe east–west in this conventional depiction. Straight sketch lines are a representation. Write thirty north and thirty east with coordinate type, direction and degrees, not just thirty. Use the legend rather than assume that every number on the upper border is latitude.

State this exercise’s latitude-first order

Here ordered pairs are latitude first: A is thirty degrees north, sixty east; B is sixty north, thirty east. The same numbers in reversed roles give different points. Other data formats may use longitude first, so inspect their declared order rather than universalise this example. Label A and B with full coordinate wording, retaining the convention instead of an unexplained parenthesised pair.

For a blind check of A/B, an answer of merely thirty and sixty is insufficient; require coordinate types and order. Deliberately swap fields and locate the changed point as a counterexample. The labelled sketch suffices without a real map service or route, and the locations remain fictional.

Retain signs and hemisphere letters

For C, thirty south and sixty west, locate hemispheres first and then apply this example’s north/east-positive sign convention. Do not treat a west label plus a negative value as an instruction to reverse direction twice. Zero latitude identifies the equator; zero longitude the prime meridian. Identical numbers do not make identical lines. This is not a date-line route calculation, and a zero-to-three-hundred-sixty convention needs reconciliation before comparison.

Do not turn equal angles into equal distances

NOAA explains that meridians are farther apart at the equator and converge toward the poles, so equal longitude differences do not give equal ground distances everywhere. Equal-looking map cells also depend on projection. Thirty degrees is not thirty kilometres, and a degree of latitude is not universally identical to a degree of longitude. Without an appropriate distance definition, model and parameters, retain the known angular difference rather than invent an exact kilometre conversion.

Trace both reference lines for each point in ManYue

Read the full legend, zoom A and trace one line to the latitude label and the other to longitude, checking directions. Return to the whole page before B and C instead of guessing from a high-zoom fragment. Restore cropped labels where possible; missing source fields remain unresolved. Record display mode and use a paper checklist without inventing an automatic coordinate-reading control.

Check swapped fields and screen rotation

If A lands at B, check swapped coordinate fields. For reversed hemispheres, inspect letters, signs and map north instead of screen up. Before copying elsewhere, verify order and units; degrees–minutes–seconds and decimal degrees are not interchangeable strings. No real address submission or location-permission change is needed. Use fictional points and the sketch for support, retaining unknown projection and making no real positioning-accuracy or navigation-safety claim.

Leave and return to read all three points fully

Leave and return to the legend and three points, restating latitude, longitude, hemispheres and order before comparing notes. Keep conventions, locations, angular differences, limits on distance inference, display observations and unknowns. Explain why matching numbers can identify different coordinates and why equal angular cells need not be equal ground lengths. Recheck field order and projection for every new map or data format.
