Visual transitions, gestures and haptics are separate. A paper-like turn does not reorder files, and absent vibration does not necessarily indicate a failed action. Compare available effects one at a time so forward and back remain understandable without conflating presentation and content.

Choose an identifiable local chapter

Use a complete local chapter or numbered sample to remove network waits from the comparison. Record mode and starting values, fixing orientation and gesture. Establish slow forward/back behaviour with familiar content. Judge both the actual destination and whether visual or tactile cues help identify it.

Compare ordinary page animation

Inspect the page-animation control and compare both states with identical single-step actions. Note transition, destination and timing. Animation is presentation rather than an ordering repair. Use a supported gallery context and record absent controls instead of requiring the same behaviour in continuous mode.

Test realistic turning separately

If realistic turning is available, change it separately and observe drag, release and destination. Do not assume arbitrary combinations simply because two controls exist. Check cancelled turns carefully: an unfinished visual transition is not proof that the reader advanced. Prefer practical usability over effect complexity.

Separate page and menu haptics

Test page haptics independently of menu haptics while animation stays fixed. Device capabilities and system conditions can differ, especially on tablets. Do not reset the app or alter volume to seek vibration. Treat haptics as an optional cue and verify page movement visually.

Inspect action units in paired views

In multi-image views, one action may advance a group rather than one source image. Covers and wide-image handling affect boundaries. Establish source order in single-page mode, then inspect before/after scenes in the target layout. Separate pairing issues from animation choices instead of compensating with unrelated toggles.

Read a short section at a normal pace

After controlled tests, read at ordinary speed and note waits, accidental reversals or unclear position. Compare stand and handheld use separately if relevant. Simpler transitions may suit lookup, while another effect suits reading. These observations guide personal use rather than benchmark devices or require file modifications.

Save in the intended scope

Use supported device or book scope where appropriate and check another comic. Reopen the sample and verify the chosen behaviour. Record the baseline and rationale. Do not assume every switch supports every scope or confuse preference changes with missing content; a new identity may have different settings.

Acceptance and common questions

Finish with clear destinations, helpful transitions, understood haptic scope and accessible menus. File editing is unnecessary, and absent vibration alone does not prove a failed turn. For repeatable faults, record mode, switch combination and actions with numbered pages. Keep the dependable option rather than ranking effects universally.
