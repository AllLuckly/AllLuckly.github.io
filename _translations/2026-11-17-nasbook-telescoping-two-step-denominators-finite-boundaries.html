Many cancellations do not justify crossing out every negative fraction. Expand a sum whose denominators differ by two, check signed denominator pairs in Book Isle and retain boundaries when limits change. Every calculation is external; finding a formula is not a completed proof.

Save limits and the general term first

Let n be a positive integer and Tn sum 2/[k(k+2)] for k=1 through n. Parenthesize the product denominator, rather than divide by k then multiply k+2. Make a separate n=4 card, import a clear PDF/text and distinguish definition from example by markers. Record both limits. k=0 or−2 would make the denominator zero; positive indices avoid them without treating undefined terms as zero.

Verify the split by a common denominator

The candidate 1/k−1/(k+2) has numerator(k+2)−k=2 over the required denominator, assuming nonzero denominators. Do not cancel k across subtraction of separate fractions. The alternative 1/k−1/(k+1) belongs to a different term even if it telescopes. At k=1 both correct forms give 2/3; at k=2 they give 1/4. Keep the algebraic identity after these spot checks rather than let samples replace the proof.

T_n=sum(k=1..n) 2/[k*(k+2)], n positive integer
2/[k*(k+2)]=1/k-1/(k+2)
T_4=(1-1/3)+(1/2-1/4)+(1/3-1/5)+(1/4-1/6)

Count eight signed terms before canceling

For n=4 list four parentheses containing eight signed fractions: +1, −1/3, +1/2, −1/4, +1/3, −1/5, +1/4, −1/6. Mark each k so a trailing negative is not lost as if it were another group. Highlight opposite signs with equal denominators if supported, or connect them externally. Keep original terms rather than delete them from the evidence. Screen wrapping does not create another summand.

Cancel only equal magnitudes with opposite signs

Cancel the±1/3 and±1/4 pairs, leaving 1+1/2−1/5−1/6. Denominator 30 gives(45−6−5)/30=17/15. Independently sum original terms 2/3, 1/4, 2/15, 1/12 over 60: (40+15+8+5)/60=17/15. A sum greater than one is possible and is not an error. Retain−1/5 and−1/6 because no corresponding positive terms exist.

The gap 3/2−17/15=11/30 equals 1/5+1/6, catching a missing tail. Also T4−T3=1/12, the original k=4 term. These independently check leftover negatives and the added summand rather than substitute for each other.

Keep two leading and two trailing boundaries

For general n, Tn=1+1/2−1/(n+1)−1/(n+2). Both trailing negatives matter. At n=1 this still yields 2/3, but boundary overlap means nonexistent interior terms cannot be drawn as actual terms. Check n=2 gives 11/12 and n=3 gives 21/20. Positive added terms make partial sums increase, yet each finite sum is below 3/2. A limit requires n→∞; large finite is not infinite.

One further term gives T5=T4+2/35=25/21. Its gap from 3/2 is 13/42=1/6+1/7, matching both new tails. Advancing the upper limit moves both trailing boundaries rather than retaining the old 1/5.

T_n=3/2-1/(n+1)-1/(n+2)
T_1=2/3; T_2=11/12; T_3=21/20; T_4=17/15
finite n: T_n<3/2
sum(k=2..4) 2/[k*(k+2)]=1/2+1/3-1/5-1/6=7/15

Re-expand boundaries when the lower limit becomes two

A separate k=2..4 card has three original groups. Keep +1/2, +1/3, −1/5, −1/6 for 7/15, independently T4−2/3. Changing the lower limit removes+1. Replacing n by 3 is not equivalent: three terms and upper limit 3 are different conditions. Record actual index sets so an excerpt’s ellipsis retains its scope.

For integers 1≤m≤n, the sum becomes 1/m+1/(m+1)−1/(n+1)−1/(n+2). At m=n overlapping boundaries cancel; check the one-term original rather than draw four original summands. Four boundary fractions in a simplified expression do not mean every original sum has four terms.

Diagnose identity, signs and indices

A result 3/2 may wrongly cancel both tails; 4/5 may copy a one-step-denominator exercise; 17/15 after changing the lower limit may omit removal of the first term. Zoom cramped fractions and inspect parentheses rather than turn adjacency into a new operation. Change one condition, keep before/after ledgers and recompute original terms. Paper markings or highlights are evidence aids, not symbolic computation; do not record automatic in-app cancellation.

Keep denominator parentheses: n+2 is the whole denominator, not 1/n plus 2. If an export shortens a fraction bar, compare the source rather than omit an unclear boundary for readability.

Reopen and rebuild the eight-term ledger

Return to the T4 bookmark, cover the answer and list four groups, eight terms, two canceled pairs and four boundaries before obtaining 17/15. Recheck 7/15 for k=2..4 and distinguish finite sums from limit 3/2. Identify every surviving fraction’s index instead of recalling only an answer. Save question, identity, independent sums and observations; reopening stays pending if unperformed. Different gaps or limits require new boundaries rather than a universal two-head/two-tail template.
