A chameleon foot may look like a two-lobed pincer, but each has five digits grouped as two and three, with opposite medial/lateral arrangements on forefeet and hindfeet. Draw a simplified chameleon resting along a branch in an oblique side view, checking the near forefoot and hindfoot closely. Show their grips manually in Aquarell. Every digit need not be fully exposed, but occlusion cannot justify invented digit grouping or a changed four-limb body plan.

Stabilise branch and body direction

Use an approximately 1900×1400 px landscape canvas with a pale warm-grey background. Angle a branch slightly lower left to upper right; face the animal left with its body following the branch. Establish back, belly and four limb roots. Keep the tail a simple curved silhouette rather than this lesson’s focus. Separate sketch, far feet, branch, near feet, body and shading, using muted yellow-green. The branch must permit gripping without being impossibly broad. Correct limb lengths and contact if the body floats, rather than hiding roots with leaves or inventing a fifth leg.

Define medial and lateral relative to the animal

Write that medial means toward the animal’s body midline and lateral means away, rather than equating the picture’s near side with lateral. Forefeet have three medial and two lateral digits; hindfeet have two medial and three lateral digits. Add temporary numbers beside the two near feet. A far foot may hide behind the branch, but its sketch must retain fore/hind identity. Correct grouping if both feet are duplicated, rather than treating mirroring as anatomy. Remove numbers from the finish. Two groups do not mean two digits or two separate miniature hands on each foot.

Wrap forefoot groups around opposite branch sides

Bend the near forelimb from shoulder to branch, ending in opposed three-digit and two-digit groups. Wrap them around opposite branch sides. Some tips may hide, but both groups must belong to one palm. Keep concealed paths faint in the sketch and remove see-through lines from the finish. Add limited within-group digit cues and a shared palm if two pincer points attach straight to the wrist. Regroup five splayed fingers into two gripping bundles. Avoid equally black gaps that fragment the palm. Check count in the sketch rather than forcing it through dense final lines.

Rebuild the hindfoot’s opposite grouping

Build the near hindlimb from the hip with a readable knee and ankle turn, then wrap two medial and three lateral digits around the branch. Place it farther right if useful, without passing through wood to expose the three-digit group. Show the near forefoot and hindfoot alone and read medial/lateral counts, then restore the body. Connect leg, palm and branch if glove-like. Replace a bird-like three-forward/one-back foot with five digits in two groups. This comparison is central; scales cannot replace it, and a translated forefoot is not a finished hindfoot.

Account for two far feet through occlusion

Keep far forelimb and hindlimb quieter and partly concealed by body and branch, perhaps exposing only a joint or toe end, while accounting for four feet in the sketch. Do not move far roots onto the back to expose them. Hide the branch to trace four legs to shoulder or hip regions, then restore plausible foot occlusion. If six legs appear, inspect tail, bark cracks and distant digits for false limbs. If only two feet register, add necessary far connections without displaying every toe in a row. Every visible fragment should have an assigned leg in the diagnostic sketch.

Suggest scales over established volume

Use warm yellow-green upper-left light, grey-green belly and inner feet, and muted grey-brown wood. Preserve broad continuous values, then add sparse fine skin cues on back and near feet rather than high-contrast round scales on every digit. Repair shoulder/belly turns and edges if rubber-like instead of adding dense scales. Reduce black joint and bundle gaps if bead-like. Keep branch contact shadows narrow rather than blackening feet. A small rounded eye is enough and should not distract from foot grouping. The palette does not encode mood or a real colour-change mechanism.

Distinguish gripping from passing through wood

Save a scale-free structural image. Check that two digit groups wrap around opposite branch sides and return to one palm, interrupting branch contours at occlusions. Repair layer order and boundaries if wood lines run through every foot rather than darkening toes. Recheck forefoot three-medial/two-lateral and hindfoot two-medial/three-lateral, always relative to the body midline. Change branch width or foot curvature separately instead of changing branch, legs and viewpoint together. New species and poses need fresh reference; not every angle exposes five separate toe tips in the same arrangement.

Retain foot-to-branch negative spaces at small size

Save the layered native artwork and inspect a PNG at use size. Both near feet should grip rather than form green lumps. Separate local values or remove scale dots if bundles merge instead of outlining every digit black. Quiet bark and clarify limb direction if a far foot resembles a twig. Keep the numbered sketch and note specific faults, such as a missing cue in the near hindfoot’s lateral three-digit group. All tips need not show, but the text must not turn five digits into two. This sourced drawing exercise is not guidance for keeping, grasping or handling animals.
