Paired maple samaras need two fruit bodies and their individual wings, not one dot with two leaves. Refer to Japanese maple and draw an invented still-connected pair without flight analysis. Arrange thickness, connection and occlusion manually in Aquarell. Opening angle is a drawing choice, not a fixed rule for all maples. A samara is not a leaf, and the image establishes no seed viability.

Establish both axes and basal bodies

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px landscape canvas with pale grey-cream background. Spread wings upper left/right from two adjacent small oval bodies lower centre. Sketch two axes and two bodies, not wings from one circular centre. Separate far/near wings, bodies, stalk and shadow; choose grey red-brown and pale warm yellow. Modest asymmetry is allowed within one viewpoint. Clarify two thick bodies transitioning to thin wings if seedling-like. Pixels, palette and angle identify no cultivar, and dense veins cannot distract from structure.

Connect each body to its own wing

Connect left body to left wing and right to right, transitioning from thick to thin. Do not attach both wings to one body and leave the other floating. Use diagnostic colours to trace pairing, then restore the palette. Recover two bodies if butterfly-like; thin wing tips and soften transitions if spoon-like. Allow modest differences and curvature. Pairing is the main check, not fine veins or a familiar V symbol. Keep any concealed short connection traceable in the sketch rather than unexplained.

Keep the junction from becoming a third fruit body

Join the pair near their bases, with an optional short stalk from above/behind the junction according to the chosen view. Do not add a third equally large central sphere. Lighten a black knot and separate bodies, or restore proximity and stalk occlusion if detached. This is a connected pair, not two fallen fruits forced onto an incompatible common stalk. Stalk length is compositional, not a developmental or shedding-time indicator. An unclear visible stalk end may be omitted while the sketch still explains the junction.

Model thin wings without leaf serrations

Use smooth, slightly varied wing edges, gently rounded or tapered tips, not serrations turning them into leaves. Establish pale warm-yellow or grey red-brown planes with subtle turns and no thick white border. Soften blade-like points/highlights or broaden and thin stick-like wings. They are not clear glass; restrained translucency is a pictorial cue, not an optical measurement. Hide veins to test separate directions and widths, then add only useful texture. Dense lines cannot replace complete contours.

Keep wing veins within each wing

Fan a few quiet veins from each body toward its own tip, quieter far away, without a line crossing the junction into both wings. Follow curvature rather than equal spacing. Reduce feather-like central shafts and dense symmetric barbs or cloth-like deep grooves. Body texture may differ from thin wing texture. Hide detail to verify two distinct bodies, then reject a vein falsely linking left body to right wing. Painted line count does not establish vascular anatomy, species or habitat.

Distinguish thickness under one light

Light upper left, rounding fruit bodies more strongly and keeping wing contrast lighter, modestly deeper near joins. Do not reverse lighting on the right wing. Reduce sharp white dots if plastic-like or add body-side turning if sticker-flat. Ground shadow is tight under low bodies and soft under raised tips, not equal black strips. Hide it to count two bodies/two wings, then reject false extra wings. This pose and palette establish no flight trajectory or aerodynamic simulation.

Audit the two-body/two-wing pairing

Keep left-body/left-wing and right-body/right-wing labels, tracing thickness to tip and then junction. Correct totals do not excuse incorrect pairing or a merely V-like symbol. Adjust one join or vein with angle/light fixed. Reassess fruit and angle for another maple rather than generalising this invented opening. A detached-fruit variant needs revised stalk and occlusion, not mixed connected/detached descriptions. Hidden guides stay out of the finish, which must not expose invented internal anatomy through thick bodies.

Keep both basal bodies distinct in export

Save the layered original and inspect body separation, both wing roots and stalk in a PNG at use size. Separate local values/contours if bases become one dot rather than outlining everything. Reduce vein noise. Record a fault such as left-wing shadow wrongly covering the right root. Judge paired fruit bodies, individual wings and coherent junction, not two leaves. Sources establish identity; angle, pixels and colours are original. No sowing or flight test occurred. New views need revised thickness/depth, not mirrored old veins.
