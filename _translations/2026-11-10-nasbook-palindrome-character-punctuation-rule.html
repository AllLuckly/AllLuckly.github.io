A sentence that looks similar at both ends has not yet passed a palindrome check. Retain the original while reading in Book Isle, then declare comparison units in your own notes and reverse a small example character by character. This is neither extracting initial characters from separate lines nor loosely reversing word order. The app provides reading and available recording controls; the reader defines and checks the rule.

Retain the edition and exact short sentence

Choose a short text you may lawfully read, recording title, author, edition, chapter and a nearby identifying phrase. First preserve punctuation and spaces exactly; do not edit the book to force success. Select only the necessary excerpt where possible and compare every character with the page. Transcribe unselectable scans manually without claiming automatic recognition. The invented sentence is “山映水，水映山”; attribute it to no historical poet. Keep five fields: original, comparison rule, forward sequence, reversed sequence and judgment. Chapter and phrase remain anchors when reflowed page numbers change.

Define units before excluding punctuation

Use Merriam-Webster’s palindrome definition for the basic idea of identical forward and backward reading. This exercise specifically compares six Han characters and excludes the comma: 山, 映, 水, 水, 映, 山. Do not switch to multi-character word units merely to avoid a mismatch. Excluding punctuation is a declared rule, not a claim that punctuation lacks a reading function; the original field still retains it. A rule including every character requires a separate check rather than mixing both outcomes into an alleged automatic app verdict.

Check all three mirrored position pairs

Number the characters 1–6. Positions 1 and 6 are 山, 2 and 5 are 映, and 3 and 4 are 水. Six is even, so the centre lies between positions 3 and 4, with no single central character. In an odd-length example the middle character maps to itself and still retains its position. Independently write the reversal 山映水水映山 and compare the whole sequence, not just its ends. Record each pair’s characters and equality in words; numbering and explanations should remain readable if export loses colour distinctions.

Change only the last character for a clear control

Change only the final character to 月 and save “山映水，水映月” separately. Under the same rule compare 山映水水映月 with 月映水水映山. The outer pair differs, so it fails even though the inner two pairs agree. Preserve the successful sentence and label the control as your own alteration; do not silently restore 山 and claim the source always said that. This tests a formal condition, not literary merit. Failing it implies neither meaningless language nor a Book Isle display fault; distinguish textual content from your chosen task.

Read meaning separately from formal symmetry

Return to the comma-separated original: its two halves say that mountains reflect water and water reflects mountains. Record formal symmetry separately from interpretations of the image. Successful reversal proves no specific authorial intention, and the invented sentence is no geographical or optical account. For variant, traditional/simplified or historical forms, first compare the actual edition’s characters. A normalized comparison needs a separately declared mapping and rationale, not silent equivalence. Label interpretation as your own and keep original order separate from normalized copies for later review.

Do not transfer character symmetry to a gloss

An English gloss can read “Mountains reflect water; water reflects mountains,” but that does not automatically form a letter-by-letter palindrome. Place the Chinese sequence, English gloss and each language’s comparison units side by side. Fluent translation does not verify the Chinese form, and a nonpalindromic English gloss is not automatically a failed translation. For published translations record translator and edition separately, preserving the language’s spacing, case and character rules. Mark any unperformed English letter check as unchecked rather than sharing a single success mark across different scopes.

Reopen and retrieve the original by its source

Use available bookmark or note controls to retain the location, or an external record if no suitable control exists; no new palindrome panel is required. Close the reading page, return by chapter and phrase, and recheck the end characters and comma. If exporting notes, open the result and inspect all six characters and the declared rule. Successful export alone does not guarantee unchanged characters. For failed search first inspect scope and text availability, rather than immediately deleting and reimporting the book. Screen wrapping changes display positions; this task does not extract each displayed line’s initial character.

Locate disagreement in text, units and rules

If two judgments disagree, check transcription, punctuation inclusion, character versus word units and any script normalization in that order. Date each changed rule, retain the previous judgment and repeat all three pairs rather than merely changing the final status. A reproducible record includes the original, six-character rule, three pair results, 月 control and translation boundary. It establishes a bounded character check, not automatic recognition of literary devices, proof that every sentence in the work is palindromic, or a uniquely correct interpretation.
