A rotary dial is not a clock face with ten holes substituted for numbers: its gap and fixed stop have different roles. Draw an invented old telephone close-up in Aquarell, checking ten coplanar holes and a separate stop, then plastic and metal. References inform component relationships, without copying collection artwork text, recordings or composition, and without producing a functional dial template.

Set the body slope and dial centre

Use an approximately1600×1500 px canvas with pale warm-grey background. Tilt the front body face toward the viewer and place the dial centre slightly low. Block that face, then a coplanar outer ellipse and central circular area, not an overhead outer ring with a frontal centre. Separate sketch, body, fixed base, rotating plate, holes and stop; use charcoal grey. Add supporting slope and table shadow if wall-clock-like; flatten excessive bulging if bowl-like. Pixels and proportions are original layout, not collection measurements or evidence that a phone works.

Arrange ten holes along an arc rather than forcing full-circle divisions

Locate ten hole centres on a checking layer along a substantial arc, leaving a larger gap near the stop instead of filling a whole circle with equal divisions. Temporarily number one through ten for counting; final digits may be omitted. Flatten and shrink far holes slightly, broaden near holes, and keep every centre on one tilted ring. Repair ring and diameter if random dots; remove an accidental eleventh using the count guides. Actual number placement needs a specified reference. Invent no letter groups and do not treat checking numbers as a telephone number.

Reveal the fixed region beneath the holes

Show near inner walls and the darker fixed base underneath, more wall near and less far. Holes belong to the rotating plate: they are neither raised buttons nor tunnels through the phone to the table. Compare them without highlights against the underlying base, not random colours per hole. Remove raised rim lights and add walls if rivet-like; restore the base if the body looks excavated. Retain material between central area and hole ring instead of merging holes into one groove. Hole edges and central axis must share the body plane.

Attach the stop to the body beside the dial

Place a short metal finger stop near the gap, rooted in the body or fixed base, its curved end close to the hole ring’s outer edge. Do not attach it to a hole wall or make it an eleventh transparent opening. Temporarily distinguish fixed stop and rotating plate in different greys, checking separate edges and occlusion. Simplify a loose-paperclip shape and establish its root; relocate a hand-like extension wrongly growing from the dial. Depict static structure, not rotation or finger contact. A still life establishes no pulse mechanism or unverified extra dial switch.

Separate transparent plate edges and the centre cover

Choose slightly translucent pale-grey plastic with narrow rim lights and faint underlying base colour between holes. A warm-white centre cover must sit on the plate, not float as another sphere. Limit highlights to two or three short arcs and keep holes easy to count. Reduce separate hole glints if bubble-like; restore unified plastic. Lighten rim and walls locally if solid black rather than whitening the whole plate. Transparency serves layering, not invented views of hidden wires or every covered number.

Keep any numbers consistent with the hole plane

Digits may be omitted. If retaining one through nine and zero, verify a particular dial’s ordering, then place them on the corresponding fixed base. Numbers must not float in transparent holes or all face screen-up while the base tilts differently. Check them on a separate text or drawing layer without copying artwork-specific phrases. Omit illegibly small numbers and record that choice, preserving ten holes and stop first. Correct digits cannot hide bad hole walls. Alignment is specified for this static pose, not every rotational angle.

Check component ownership at reduced size

Hide digits and highlights, reduce to roughly quarter size, count ten holes and trace the stop to the fixed body. Read the fixed base through holes and retain a shared axis for centre and ring. Smooth spikes and outer rim if gear-like. Recover a lost stop with local metal/background contrast rather than blackening the entire plate. Crop the body as needed without forcing handset and cord into the close-up. If added, check their separate connections instead of hanging them from the dial. Exclude counting guides and hidden outlines from export; visible edges should explain the structure.

Save clear separation of moving and fixed parts

Keep the layered native work and inspect an exported PNG for ten holes, underlying base, gap arc and fixed stop. Separate merged holes through spacing or scale rather than deleting one. Repair stop attachment and occlusion if fused with the plate instead of adding a brighter highlight ring. Record an original static close-up and omitted text. A different dial model needs fresh hole/stop references. Success concerns component ownership, not phone lines, dialing function or artwork interaction, with no automatic instrument recognition.
