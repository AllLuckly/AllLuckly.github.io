Combine owned twenty-, thirty- and ten-second passages externally, map local passage times to track time, then locate them in the player.

1. Give the three source passages separate identities

Prepare three owned or authorised short passages: A twenty seconds, B thirty, C ten, with distinguishable topics. Verify real durations and opening/end cues rather than trust numbers in filenames. Use compatible source-channel/export conditions and preserve each original. 调调 is not assumed to edit or join audio. If only protected service playback is available without editable files, use owned recordings. Write A–B–C on the preparation card rather than infer composition order from a player list.

2. Make one end-to-end file externally

Place B after A ends and C after B in a familiar editor, checking for overlap, inserted silence or fades that change timing. Audacity distinguishes end-to-end alignment from aligning tracks together; three tracks mixed from time zero are not this sixty-second composition. Listen to the export and verify sixty seconds before retaining a system-readable T24 copy. Keep project, sources and combined file separate. Any added transition requires its actual new timing rather than the original no-transition premise.

3. Separate whole-track time from passage-local time

A occupies track time zero–twenty, B twenty–fifty, C fifty–sixty; each passage still has its own local zero. Make rows for passage, track start, duration and track end rather than an unexplained 20/30/10 list. B-local six maps to track twenty-six, C-local five to track fifty-five, and track forty-five maps back to B-local twenty-five. This table is an external navigation reference, not a promise of automatically imported chapter navigation. Add a reverse check: track fifty-nine minus C’s start fifty equals C-local nine, still before the final endpoint sixty. Forward/reverse rows expose missing start offsets. A negative local result first calls for checking passage identity rather than claiming time ran backward.

4. State how boundary positions are assigned

For the ledger assign A to [0,20), B to [20,50), and C from fifty to the final endpoint sixty; sixty is the file end, not a fourth passage. Listen across a small neighborhood rather than classify content from a rounded 20-second label. If A’s final word crosses the cut, inspect the editing source instead of rename passages to hide truncation. Boundaries come from the external timeline. The progress control approaches them; without sample-accurate positioning, retain an approximate range and identifiable sound as another check. B’s local endpoint thirty maps to track fifty, where C begins under the stated convention. Do not count an extra final/first second at the shared boundary and inflate total duration. A shared timeline boundary remains distinct from exact sample positions or speech onsets.

5. Enter the combined item rather than a source passage

Import the combined file and verify title, roughly sixty-second duration and opening identity, establishing T24 rather than source A. If sources are also in the library, label them separately instead of relying on covers. Approach track twenty-six using visible progress controls and compare B-local six, then track fifty-five against C-local five. Source B’s own twenty-six seconds is another timeline. An accidental source selection requires returning to T24 and repeating that row, not merging both clocks in one result.

6. Play across both joins to inspect continuity

Play forward across the A/B join from near eighteen seconds and across B/C from near forty-eight. Record audible order, progress and unexpected pauses separately. This is one file, so passage changes need not change the player title. For a short gap, inspect the external export before attributing it to playback/output. This does not certify gapless performance or separate-track transitions; observations concern only the two joins within this prepared file. An uninterrupted eighteen-to-twenty-two segment covers four seconds, not all of B. Hearing B’s first sentence leaves its later content unchecked. Keep the same distinction for C: a complete map is not a complete listening pass over the entire file.

7. Use a changed-transition control to diagnose a stale map

If a later revision inserts two seconds between A/B while retaining passage lengths, B starts at twenty-two, C at fifty-two and total duration is sixty-two. Track twenty-six now maps to B-local four. Update the map before listening rather than diagnose a stale table as inaccurate seeking. Label the transition interval and its content. This can remain a paper control without exporting another file; mark it unperformed. Preserve editions/maps after changes to order, trimming or transitions so earlier observations remain interpretable.

8. Deliver three mappings and two join observations

Keep sources, combined file, edition and the three-row boundary map. Re-find T24 in 调调 and repeat one checked B/C position. Convert local/track time in both directions, distinguish the file endpoint from a new passage and deliver actual join observations separately. Player records and the external map are distinct; do not invent chapter bookmarks or lyric-tag importing. A revised composition needs rechecked map/duration. A surviving title does not establish unchanged content, and paper boundaries are not precision validation across devices.
