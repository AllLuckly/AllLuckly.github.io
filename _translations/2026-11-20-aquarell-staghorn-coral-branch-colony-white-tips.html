Paint a staghorn coral colony, establishing a shared base and irregular upward branches before using golden brown and pale tips to clarify direction. NOAA describes staghorn coral branching upward from a central trunk, with golden-brown color and lighter tips. This original illustration does not diagnose the health, age or growth rate of a particular colony.

Plan the base, branching area and underwater space

Create an approximately 1800 × 1500 landscape canvas. Put a rock surface at the bottom and the colony left of center, leaving water above and to the right. Sketch one trunk rising from the base, then four or five branch directions of different lengths. Separate background water, rock, near branches, far branches and details; save an editable draft. Branch count and composition are exercise choices, not a fixed count for the species. If the reduced silhouette becomes crowded, first adjust gaps rather than adding many dots.

Connect the trunk to the rock

Draw the lower trunk as a continuous broader base meeting a readable point on the rock, narrowing upward while leaving room for forks. Suggest contact with a narrow darker patch instead of a whole branch cluster floating in water. The base may hide part of the rock but should not hide every branch origin. If the trunk looks like a pasted sticker, hide details and trace the contour from rock upward; repair its connection before adding texture and light.

Build irregular upward forks from main branches

Extend thinner branches from both sides of the trunk, mostly upward with gentle local bends. Transition thickness continuously instead of making every junction suddenly a hairline. Separate near and far branches and decide which covers the other at crossings. NOAA describes antler-like form; every branch need not have a perfectly symmetric partner. Begin with a few main branches, trace each end back to the base, then add short branches if useful. Avoid unattached lines floating in the water.

Separate golden-brown body and pale ends

Block the colony in warm golden brown, with a somewhat brighter lit side and a darker brown side away from the chosen light. Add pale color sparingly at visible tips, transitioning or placing it locally rather than covering the whole colony. Keep one lighting direction rather than repeating identical stripes on every branch. Light tips are an appearance described in the reference; color alone cannot diagnose health. Do not label all white areas as disease or use an illustration palette for field diagnosis.

Use negative space to distinguish near and far branches

Inspect water-colored gaps between branches, retaining several openings from above into the interior instead of filling every gap gold. Near branches partly cover far contours; a far branch must not abruptly emerge in front. If similarly colored branches merge, adjust adjacent value slightly or shorten one branch. Avoid heavy black outlines on every branch merely for clarity. With background detail hidden, main branches and their junctions should still be traceable.

Keep a simplified copy with only three main branches beside the version with short branches. If an added branch changes an overlap, decide which main branch it joins before fixing the crossing. If small branches become visual noise when reduced, remove some instead of thickening all of them. Use the same light and background in both copies so the difference mainly comes from branch count and overlap.

Let background and texture support structure

Use low-contrast blue-green water and a few soft distant marks; ground the colony on a stable gray-brown rock. Suggest surface texture with a few short strokes or dots rather than drawing or counting every polyp. If it resembles a dead tree on land, inspect branch thickness, rock contact and consistent planes; do not cover the subject with a crowd of fish just to imply water.

Hide layers and check connections

Hide surface dots and water decoration, trace one near and one far branch from the base, and check each fork and tip. Their crossing order differs, but each remains part of its own continuous path. Hide far branches; near branches should remain. If they disappear too, inspect whether layers needed for separate editing were merged. For small white gaps inspect overlap, brush edge and selected layer before repainting the entire colony. Temporarily mark two main branches in different colors on the sketch, trace each and hide the marks afterward. These are checking aids, not evidence of two coral species or colonies.

Save, export and preserve the evidence boundary

Save the native draft, export through an actually available entry and reopen the image elsewhere. At reduced size inspect the negative space; enlarged, inspect pale tips meeting the body. Reopen the editable draft and independently hide far branches or texture to confirm further editing remains possible. Describe it as a staghorn coral form exercise and leave unperformed export or reopening pending. NOAA lists the species as threatened; this lesson does not call for collecting, touching or field intervention and does not claim device or marine field observation.
