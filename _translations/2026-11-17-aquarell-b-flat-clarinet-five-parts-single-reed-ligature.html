Reserve space for five principal parts

Use an approximately 1200×2100 px canvas. Along a faint axis, name mouthpiece, barrel, upper joint, lower joint and bell. The short slightly bulging barrel differs from the long body joints; these are not five equal blocks. Flare the bottom bell and narrow the top mouthpiece while maintaining one tilted axis. Attach a single reed against the mouthpiece

Draw a wedge-like mouthpiece and place one pale reed against its visible flat side, aligned toward the tip rather than projecting sideways like a flag. It is one reed, not one on each side as a double reed. A thin tip edge distinguishes material without an internal airway section. If the chosen view hides it, use a framed side-view inset instead of twisting the main mouthpiece ninety degrees merely to expose it. Label that alternate view so it cannot imply a second mouthpiece or reed.

Wrap the ligature around mouthpiece and reed

Wrap a ligature around the lower mouthpiece, its near band crossing the reed and far contour turning behind the mouthpiece rather than forming a flat badge. Add selected fasteners according to the reference without universal screw counts. Leave the reed tip exposed and keep the ligature above the barrel. Trace the reed beneath the band enlarged, with narrow contact shadows. If the band seems to sever it, soften the heavy edge and restore continuity rather than add another reed to repair the illusion.

Maintain the five-part fitted sequence

Join mouthpiece to short barrel, upper joint, lower joint and finally bell. Indicate four principal boundaries through narrow rings and diameter changes, not large exposed cork blocks everywhere; fitted parts may conceal interiors. Keep every joint on the axis rather than making upper/lower sections parallel separate pipes or a detached bell. Hide keywork and trace the dark silhouette continuously top to bottom. Use subtle volume changes at seams instead of broad white rings severing five disconnected objects.

Give keys supports and distinguish holes from highlights

Place selected rods and cups along upper and lower joints; some rings can surround visible tone holes chosen from the reference, not equally spaced decoration. Draw supporting posts before arms and cups, stopping hidden far hardware at the tube. Differentiate small upper rings from longer lower arms without counting them as extra main sections. If silver marks crowd the body, remove excess highlights rather than whiten the tube. This is a selective exterior, not a fingering chart; document omitted mechanisms.

Flare the bell while retaining its opening direction

Flare the bell gradually rather than attach a broad cup abruptly to a thin stem. Align its opening ellipse to the common axis, firm near rim, softer far rim and a dark cavity with thickness rather than a black disc. Use warm dark and cooler reflected grey on the body, with small metal highlights. Barrel bulge and bell flare need distinct contours instead of a uniform cylinder. Fitted small, retain a readable mouthpiece tip, short barrel and bottom opening. Lighting establishes neither wood species nor tone quality.

Troubleshoot reed count and main-part order

Count five main parts and one reed before counting shiny keys, checking that the ligature has not shifted onto the barrel. An oboe-like top may have reeds drawn on both sides; a missing barrel may have merged into a long upper joint. Reconnect a floating funnel-like bell through continuous walls. Repair local arms and hidden lines rather than bring all keywork forward and make far rods penetrate the body. Preserve a failing copy and trace mouthpiece to bell after each correction to ensure order remains intact.
