A nautilus section is more than a spiral with radial lines. Partitions, shell walls and the outer open region occupy distinct positions; centre-origin rays can turn it into a pie chart. Make an original partial outer-whorl section with simplified inner coils, not complete anatomical reconstruction or an automatic spiral, golden-ratio template or growth simulation.

Define the section scope and references

Read the Australian Museum account to distinguish partitioned inner chambers from the outer living chamber, and Smithsonian’s chambered-shell explanation for section recognition. Retain structural ideas without copying images. Title a simplified partial outer-whorl section. Eight small chambers are a teaching reduction, not an animal-wide count. Do not infer age from curvature or claim a universal golden spiral.

Draw the outer and preceding-whorl walls

On a square canvas reserve room for the outer opening. Use a few Bézier nodes for the curling outer edge and the preceding-whorl wall, bounding the outer region to partition. Indicate inner coils lightly before detail. The gap may widen outward without wall crossings creating unexplained islands. Reserve a wider opening region rather than closing everything into concentric rings. Hand-drawn curves communicate approximate morphology, not specimen-measured curvature or a precision replica.

Join neighbouring walls with partitions, not central rays

Place curved partitions across the selected outer band, joining its inner and outer walls and following local coiling. Do not send every partition through inner whorls to the canvas centre. Prototype two and check for a chamber rather than triangular pie slices, then extend gradually. Enlarge endpoints because small gaps can suggest unrestricted chamber connections. Use temporary checking colours for partitions versus walls, later unifying them without losing attachments. Treat partitioned and outer wider regions separately initially.

Count eight chambers through nine boundaries

In the selected stretch, identify start/end boundaries and seven intervening partitions: nine boundaries enclose eight small consecutive chambers. Nine lines do not mean nine chambers; count spaces from first through eighth. The limited section continues into inner coils, so its endpoints do not represent the entire shell ending there. Temporary labels one through eight may help without cluttering the final. After adding/removing a partition recount spaces rather than retaining an eight-chamber caption over seven or nine.

Keep the living chamber distinct from its opening

Extend a wider outer living chamber beyond the partitioned stretch without filling it with equally spaced small partitions. Retain an outward opening. The chamber is a space and the aperture its exterior opening; do not swap labels. An empty-shell section need not invent a complete soft body to establish occupancy. Finer anatomy such as the siphuncle is omitted, explicitly labelled incomplete internal anatomy rather than absent in the animal. With decorative stripes hidden, the outer chamber and aperture should remain readable.

Do not turn surface patterns into partitions

Put optional brown-orange surface stripes in a separate exterior inset or clearly distinguish decoration from the section; do not run them through chambers as partitions. Complete monochrome structure first. Overlapping pattern and partition lines can mislead chamber counting, so reduce patterns rather than adding terminology to explain confusion. After mirroring, recheck aperture and labels without inventing another species identity from curl direction. Colour, direction and chamber count do not identify an animal here.

Trace walls and opening in the exported image

Save the source and open a PNG at viewing size. Trace walls outward, count the eight selected spaces and locate living chamber/aperture. For merged thin partitions reduce decoration/stroke weight rather than deleting an unacknowledged chamber. Hide manual numbering/check colours when unwanted; references remain artwork objects. On transparency inspect false white holes against another background and recheck endpoints. Clear screenshots do not verify every export format; open each separately.

Distinguish declared simplification from structural faults

For a pie-chart appearance inspect centre-directed partitions; for a sealed doughnut inspect living chamber/aperture; for nine-lines/nine-chambers wording count spaces instead of changing the title to conceal it. Keep omission notes, but “simplified” does not excuse disconnected walls. Preserve structure checks, scope, eight-space count and actual output so readers recognise the partial view.
