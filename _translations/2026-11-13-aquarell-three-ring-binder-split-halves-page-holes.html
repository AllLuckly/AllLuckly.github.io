Connecting binder rings with one long line turns them into a spiral, while closed circles cannot describe an open mechanism. Draw an invented three-ring binder with paired split halves. Use manual drawing and checking layers in Aquarell, not spring simulation. Spacing is not a usable punching specification, and no disassembly or real mechanism operation is required.

Establish two open cover planes

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px landscape canvas with two open cover planes and a narrow spine. Place the metal mechanism slightly right of centre. Block cover supports and a paper stack, leaving room for three separated ring pairs. Separate covers, mechanism, rings, paper and guides; choose pale blue and cream. Add cover edges and spine width if map-flat, and establish which cover supports a floating mechanism. Dimensions organise the illustration, not its sheet capacity or a manufacturer’s spine specification.

Align three ring positions along one mechanism

Mark three positions along the mechanism’s axis instead of aligning each ring with screen vertical. Near spacing may appear wider and far spacing compressed, but all pairs cross the same binding direction. Place simple left/right half-arcs without glints and number pairs temporarily. Correct an accidental fourth position and separate rings wrongly entering one paper hole. A tidy copied arrangement does not establish shared perspective. Real products differ in ring shape and spacing; this illustration specifies three pairs only.

Pair the six open tips correctly

Show all three pairs open. Each half returns to the mechanism and a small gap separates its tip from its partner. Count six free tips and three paired gaps, not six complete rings. Tip direction and height should plausibly meet when closed rather than pointing incompatibly. Temporarily colour-code pairs and trace their roots to the mechanism. Add attachment and consistent opening if loose-earring-like; remove inter-pair links if chain-like. Six tips count this open depiction, not every internal metal component.

Pass paper holes around the correct half-rings

Put a cream paper stack to one side with three holes along its binding edge. Pass each half-ring through its corresponding hole and distinguish edge occlusion from the hole interior. These are paper holes, not dark pits in the metal base. Place any unthreaded stack separately. Establish one visible top sheet, then suggest lower pages with a few offset edges instead of dense repeated ellipses. Recover entry/exit segments if hidden, and align holes with rings lying wrongly on solid paper. No exact sheet count or universal three-hole paper standard is claimed.

Separate thickness of base, rings and cover

Use a low metal base, rounded wire rings and thin coloured cover edges, all under one light direction. Establish complete mid-grey metal before local glints and shadows, emphasizing root contact. Add inner/outer edges and tonal turning if rings look like flat white lines; lower an oversized wall-like base that hides holes. Do not expose invented springs or hinges to explain the movement. Hidden assembly remains omitted. Colours are invented, without transferring a reference product’s patented name, capacity or branding to the cover.

Use a closed thumbnail to verify pairing

Draw a small closed-state check on a separate layer, joining each pair into one ring at the same three positions without moving the paper. It verifies how six tips form three joins and may be hidden; it is not an operation animation. Repair pairing if closure would pass through another ring, and revisit mechanism placement and arc height if rings vanish behind the cover. Do not superimpose both states as extra metal. A graphic join proves neither real locking nor any safety or repair conclusion about a mechanism.

Check three separate rings without highlights

Hide glints and temporary numbers, reduce the image and count three ring positions, six free tips and three top-sheet holes. Trace each root. Enlarge a merged opening modestly rather than outlining all metal thickly. Show paper thickness and emerging arcs if holes resemble decorative dots. Check for an erroneous longitudinal wire joining separate pairs: binder rings are independent, unlike one spiral through successive holes. Restore materials while keeping openings and holes legible, and prevent cover decoration from taking over the structural task.

Save the open-state artwork and checking thumbnail

Keep a layered native file, name and hide checks, export PNG and reopen it to confirm only the selected open state. Verify ring positions, paired halves, paper occlusion and mechanism attachment, repairing muddled areas in the source. An extra closed set calls for checking guide-layer visibility, not deleting main half-rings. Record three pairs, six tips and the paper-stack side; plan afresh for a two- or four-ring design.
