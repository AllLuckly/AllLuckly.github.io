Lines, arcs and arrows can suggest motion without tracing an object’s actual route. In ManYue, examine a completed action passage and distinguish depicted objects, explanatory symbols and inferred paths. The task concerns representation of motion, not evidence manufactured by rapid flipping, automatic action recognition or trajectory measurement.

Choose a passage with before-and-after states

Choose three to five panels with identifiable before/after states, recording edition, chapter, the starting and ending states and reading direction. Inspect full pages so a cross-panel line is not cropped into an apparent arrow. Follow one ball, hat or paper rather than an entire chase. Resolve file-order doubts first instead of using motion cues to repair them. An action with an omitted middle is usable if the omission remains explicit rather than assuming continuous unshown movement.

Separate symbols from depicted objects

Inspect independent outlines, handling, shadows and occlusion before separating objects from arrows, speed backgrounds and emphasis rays. These are clues, not absolute tests: a playful work may turn a symbol into an object. Describe three right-hand arcs before naming a literal trajectory. If a line could be rope or a motion arc, retain both possibilities and check endpoints and physical interactions in adjacent panels instead of deciding from curvature alone.

Check attachment and direction

Check where a line sits relative to the object, whether it attaches to limb action, whether it has an arrowhead and whether it spans the background. Speed backgrounds may convey intensity or focus rather than parallel displacement. In an invented rightward run, lower-left slants do not prove lower-left travel. Log arrow direction, object orientation and positional change separately. An arrow in an explanatory box may guide reading rather than the character’s movement.

Test a proposed path against positional change

Compare positions relative to floor, doorway or another figure to support a direction. Changed orientation need not mean translation, and camera movement can change screen position. A ball moving from above to below a table establishes a height relation, not a calculable trajectory or speed from one arc. Describe a broad route or an unstated middle instead of a precise curve. Everyday movement knowledge can generate questions but cannot supply missing visual evidence as certainty.

Separate repeated motion shapes from multiple objects

Several arm contours may represent motion or afterimages rather than extra limbs, but genuinely multiple objects must not all be dismissed as traces. Check shared attachment, counts in adjacent panels and wording. Repeated shapes without measurable intervals do not establish one second each. Separate count from motion interpretation and initially describe overlapping contours. Apply cloning or duplication rules only where established within your completed reading, not through unreached plot information.

Find a comparison within the same work

Find one completed passage where the same symbol accompanies clearer action, comparing attachment, repetition and explanation. Different creators need not use one convention, so a tutorial’s definition is not universal. If uses differ, note context: one may emphasise emotion and another suggest physical motion. One useful comparison suffices without collecting every slant in the book. Scope the explanation to these passages rather than building an unsupported universal symbol dictionary.

Preserve action and symbol references

Keep supported references or an external log for endpoint panels and the comparison, identifying line, object and landmark. Return from zoom to full-page view to catch a line extending into another panel. Tall images need action and background cues beyond progress. A self-made diagram may explain the method if labelled schematic rather than an original screenshot or exact reproduction. Recheck order and cropping after changing sources, without assuming notes automatically attach to objects.

Finish the path judgement and check common errors

Finish with objects, symbols, established state changes and inferred paths separated. Slants may be background emphasis; multiple contours may suggest motion but need adjacent support. Withdraw a path that contradicts established states rather than altering the page. End with one supported action and one unknown, avoiding physical measurements without time and scale. The record clarifies expression rather than judging exaggerated comics as faulty engineering.
