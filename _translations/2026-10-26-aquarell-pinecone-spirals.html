A pine cone contains many details, but identical triangular scales can resemble roof tiles. Draw one lying diagonally in Aquarell, establishing its volume before adding staggered scales that wrap around its axis. The goal is to show turning structure rather than count every scale.

Establish the silhouette and axis

Create an approximately 1400×1400 px square canvas with a pale cream background. Sketch an elongated egg pointing diagonally upward, with a rounded base and tapered tip. Add a gently curved axis and mark the tip, widest section and base. Small asymmetries should still belong to one coherent volume.

Keep background, sketch, body and details on separate layers. Paint a solid brown silhouette with a larger brush, then zoom out. Widen the lower half if it is too pointed, or lengthen the axis if it looks spherical. Correct these proportions before adding texture.

Lay out curved rows of scales

Draw four or five faint arcs across the long axis. Spread them across the middle and gather them near the ends. Offset each row by roughly half a scale. Straight horizontal guides would flatten the pattern, so let the rows wrap around the body.

Place three fuller scales facing the viewer in the centre, then narrow them toward both sides. Edge scales reveal only part of their surface. Reduce scale size near the tip and allow a few looser outward scales at the base. Repetition should communicate perspective and growth.

Separate lit surfaces from gaps

Use three values: light ochre, brown and dark umber, with light from the upper left. Lighten the exposed upper faces and darken the areas covered by neighbouring scales. Avoid outlining every scale in black; reserve the deepest darks for overlap gaps.

Resolve two central rows before extending the pattern. Make the shaded side darker overall with smaller lit areas; neighbouring lit scales can share similar colours. Hide the detail layer occasionally. The body should retain its broad light-to-dark division without fine marks.

Add grain that follows each scale

With a smaller brush, add short lines radiating outward on selected scales. Follow each surface and shorten the lines near its edges. Keep the central and illuminated scales clearer, while simplifying the edges and shadows. Texture density helps establish a focal area.

Add occasional pale strokes to thin outward tips, varying their placement. If the grain resembles random hair, reduce the marks and give them a shared origin. Study an enlarged single scale beside the drawing to distinguish what its contour, root shadow and grain each contribute.

Anchor the cone to the surface

Place a shadow extending to the lower right, darkest at contact and lighter farther away. Follow the broad silhouette instead of copying every jagged scale. A few exposed scales can interrupt the shadow edge, but keep the deepest contact dark concentrated rather than forming a black border.

View the image at a small sharing size. If the cone floats, check that its shadow starts at the base. If it looks flattened, narrow the dark contact strip. Keep the background quiet, with at most a few gentle colour changes that support the subject.

Correct mechanical repetition

If the scales resemble bricks, check equal spacing and widths. Preserve a clear central rhythm but compress both ends and remove overly complete dividing lines. Overlap, occlusion and incomplete forms are part of the visible structure; the image need not explain every hidden scale.

If the colours become muddy, test the three values in a clear area before adding brighter yellow. Confirm a visible value separation, then correct three or four important scales. When local paint becomes too dense, return to a saved version and reorganise the values instead of adding more lines.

Review the structure and save

Review three scales: thumbnail for silhouette and lighting, normal size for curved rows, and close view for accidental gaps. Identify the strongest scale group and one area to simplify. Avoid sharpening everything equally; a few softer or omitted passages preserve hierarchy.

Save an editable native project and export a PNG preview named with the subject, date and version. Reopen the exported image to check the shadows. For another study, change only the cone angle while retaining the same values and light, so you can compare how clearly the rows turn.
