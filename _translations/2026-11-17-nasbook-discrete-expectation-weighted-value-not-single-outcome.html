An expected score 1.75 does not predict that score on the next trial. Use an original nonmonetary score card in Book Isle to align values, probabilities and weighted products, separating expectation, mode and observed sample mean. Numbers are stipulated, no draw was performed and no statistical engine is assumed.

List the whole distribution rather than three scores alone

E1 gives X=0, 2, 5 with probabilities 1/2, 1/4, 1/4. Outcomes are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, probabilities nonnegative and summing to one. Align each value and probability, import a clear PDF/EPUB and bookmark E1 after checking rows. Three values do not imply equal chances. Add any missing outcomes from an encountered table before applying this three-row example. Units are fictional exercise points, not real returns.

Multiply each value by its own probability

Compute 0×1/2=0, 2×1/4=1/2 and 5×1/4=5/4, not the deliberately proposed 4/5. Their sum is 0+2/4+5/4=7/4=1.75. Probability weights a score; do not divide by it. Also retain reciprocal error 4/7 as a control, since expectation does not invert the result. Products have score units while probabilities are dimensionless.

x       P(X=x)    x*P(X=x)
0       1/2       0
2       1/4       1/2
5       1/4       5/4
sum     1         E[X]=7/4=1.75

Check using four equally likely selector positions

Represent the model by four equal-chance positions 0, 0, 2, 5. Their mean 7/4 agrees with weighting. Two zeros give one score two probability shares, not a fourth distinct score. This probability partition does not guarantee four successive trials visit every position once. Independent selection with replacement permits repeats; without replacement is a changed process and cannot silently supply a fixed four-trial total. Link both zero positions to the probability 1/2 row.

Answer mode and expectation as different questions

The most likely single score is 0, with probability 1/2. Expectation is 1.75 although P(X=1.75)=0 because it is absent from the support. This is valid; replacing expectation with mode 0 is not. Keep separate questions for the likeliest outcome and weighted center. A particular future outcome remains undetermined until observed. Expectation lies between 0 and 5, a plausibility check that cannot alone establish correctness.

Need not be possible does not mean never possible. If Z is constantly 2 with probability 1, expectation 2 is attained every time. Separately, Y’s mode 1 still differs from expectation 4.5. Each control needs its own distribution; Z’s certainty cannot predict X’s next trial.

A sample mean need not equal expectation

A fictional four-trial record 5, 5, 0, 0 has mean 10/4=2.5 and is possible under the distribution. It is not a performed experiment. Long-run averaging under independent identically distributed repetition needs its conditions and limiting meaning, not a guarantee at four or any fixed number of trials. Keep model probabilities separate from observed frequencies; two fives out of four do not silently replace stipulated 1/4. Short samples can prompt model questions without rewriting its definition.

Recalculate a doubled-plus-one scoring rule

For Y=2X+1, scores become 1, 5, 11 with corresponding probabilities unchanged. Weighted expectation 1×1/2+5×1/4+11×1/4=18/4=4.5, independently 2×1.75+1. Add the constant to every possible score, not only the maximum. Doubling scores does not double probabilities. This is a transformation of the same variable and needs no second independent experiment. Keep X/Y tables separate to avoid a stale score row.

Y=2*X+1
possible Y: 1,5,11
E[Y]=1/2+5/4+11/4=9/2=4.5
2*E[X]+1=2*(7/4)+1=9/2
