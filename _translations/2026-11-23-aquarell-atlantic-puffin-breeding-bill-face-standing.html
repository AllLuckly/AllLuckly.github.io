Observe the colorful bill, dark back and pale belly of a breeding Atlantic puffin from a permitted profile photo.

Distinguish species and season

Audubon notes that an adult Atlantic puffin’s bill is more colorful in breeding season, becoming smaller and less colorful in winter while the face is grayer. Choose a permitted side-view photo of a standing breeding adult and record species, season and source. Do not combine features from different puffin species or seasons. Observe a photo without approaching or disturbing wild birds, and do not infer an individual bird’s age or sex from the illustration.

Compose the canvas and main shape

Create an approximately 1400 by 1800 portrait canvas in the current Aquarell interface and save an editable original; dimensions are suggestions. Place the standing bird in the lower half with room before the bill and above the head, and space for a rock below the feet. Separate sketch, bird, rock and background using available layers; labels may differ. Draw a ground guide and standing body axis, comparing head, belly and feet before details.

Build one continuous head and body

Block the body as a full shape and attach a smaller head, observing the neck-to-back transition. A standing profile is not a perfectly vertical rectangle: adjust belly and back curves according to the photo. Before adding the colorful bill, shrink the view and inspect head-body connection and balance. If the head looks like a ball stuck on the torso, revise the neck transition rather than hiding the join with feathers.

Place the breeding-season bill

Observe the tall, laterally compressed bill in the reference. Block its overall outline extending from the face, then distinguish upper and lower edges and the base. Connect it clearly to the head and follow the photographed tip direction rather than making a generic long thin bill. Suggest the colorful breeding plates with restrained orange, yellow and darker shapes; do not invent fine boundaries that cannot be seen. A profile is a two-dimensional projection, not a measurement of real bill width.

Separate the pale face and eye

Place the lighter cheek area inside the dark head according to the photograph, then add the visible eye on the head rather than the bill. Separate pale face, dark crown and bill base using large shapes first; omit details too small to resolve. If the face merges with a white background, adjust background value or local edge contrast rather than outlining the entire face heavily. Seasonal face colors vary; this painting follows the selected breeding reference.

Arrange dark back and pale belly

Use blue-black or dark gray for the back and folded wing, and pale gray-white for the belly. The folded wing follows body volume rather than floating like a separate sticker; check its overlap with the back in the reference. Light cool gray can suggest shade on the white belly while the dark back retains some value variation. At thumbnail size verify that the black-and-white arrangement still describes the standing silhouette.

Place visible feet on rock

Position short legs and visible orange feet from the photograph, noting contact with the rock. A side view may clearly reveal only one foot or part of the toes; do not force hidden parts into view. Use a little dark color at contact, but hide the shadow first to check the sole position instead of covering floating feet with shade. If the reference has no rock, a simplified one is a composition choice, not a claim about the photographed location.

Keep volume in the colorful bill

After blocking the large color shapes, observe lighter and darker edges in the reference and use a few directional bands to suggest the surface. Avoid making every color an equal-width stripe or carrying a bill highlight into the pale cheek. At thumbnail size the bill height, front end and connection to the head should remain legible without fine detail. If it looks like a flat triangle pasted onto the face, revisit upper and lower edges and the base.

Keep separate seasonal notes

In reference notes separately record the breeding photo’s bill and face colors and Audubon’s description of winter appearance. These are not two color layers of the same photograph; changing one detail does not automatically make an accurate winter-plumage illustration. For a winter study obtain a separately permitted winter reference. This painting records the breeding view only and leaves unseen structures unknown.

Check contours and occlusion

Zoom into the bill base, eye, wing boundary and foot-to-rock contact, ensuring background lines do not pass through the body. If wing and back merge, use local value or a short edge rather than outlining every feather. At phone reading size verify the pale face, colorful bill and pale belly remain distinct; check the corners for accidental bill-tip or foot cropping. Mark unclear details for later observation.

Save and reopen

Save the editable original and use a share or export entry actually shown in the current version to save an image separately. Reopen it to inspect bill, face, wing and rock, then return to identify sketch and subject layers. If color or a part is missing, check visibility, crop and location first; a flattened image without layers does not establish damage to the original. Keep source and permission notes and mark unperformed device or export steps pending.

Keep observation boundaries

In profile, the far wing, foot and body edge may be hidden by the near side. Record what the photo actually reveals rather than drawing both wings and feet equally complete merely because the bird has a pair of each. When painting rock, establish foot contacts before adding limited texture; texture should not cross feet or cover visible toes.
