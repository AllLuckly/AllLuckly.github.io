Filter paper must sit inside a dripper rather than float like a flower above its rim. Draw an empty invented conical dripper and pleated paper, explaining nesting, paper edge and support without hot water, brewing or drink-quality claims. Establish separate contours manually in Aquarell, then materials and shadow. Cone angle, fold count and pixels are illustration choices, not brand compatibility or filtration specifications.

Establish outer rim and base direction

Use an approximately 1600×1800 px portrait canvas with pale blue-grey background and a slightly elevated view exposing cavity and near wall. Establish a broad elliptical rim and tapering body with a simplified support ring below. Separate outer cup, paper, rim, support and shadow; use grey-cream ceramic. Align mouth and base. Increase taper if bowl-like or establish support if floating-funnel-like. Leave margins above paper and finish the empty cup structure without folds or lettering initially.

Let the paper envelope rise modestly above the rim

Draw a thin paper-cone envelope inside the dripper, upper edge modestly above its rim and lower portion tapering inward, not a separate fan behind it. Use broad pale warm white. Let the near rim conceal some paper exterior and retain readable rear-rim relations. Lower a floating hat into the cavity or expose a little upper edge if invisible rather than enlarging it outside the cup. This establishes pictorial nesting, not physical compatibility. Hide the dripper to inspect continuous paper, then restore; concealed paths remain in the sketch, not through ceramic.

Converge folds inward rather than making stripes

Vary pleated rim spacing and lead main folds downward into the paper cone rather than filling it with vertical stripes. Use six illustrative near folds and quieter distant cues, not a total-fold-count claim. A fan-like result may converge at the mouth edge instead of below in the cone. Correct face widths/directions if blind-like. Darken valleys and lighten ridges selectively, not equally. Match rim undulations to inward folds rather than random serrations, keeping the six-near-fold description consistent with the drawing.

Distinguish paper interior from cup interior

Shade paper interior warm grey, deeper downward, with a narrow cup/paper gap rather than a black rupture. Expose more near paper interior and compress the far edge consistently. Hide folds and inspect a hollow cone instead of a black ball at the bottom. Reduce solid flat darks if coffee-filled; this empty-paper study adds no grounds. Repair ceramic occlusion if glass-like rather than exposing rear folds through the wall. Distinguish paper, dripper and cavity instead of using darkness as a universal internal-structure substitute.

Keep the rim from swallowing thin paper

Give the dripper rim modest thickness with soft near light and underside shade, while paper stays thin and irregular rather than an identical white frame. Upper-left light produces broad ceramic light and quieter paper-fold contrast, reserving deepest darks for useful contacts. Reduce paper glare/thickness if two plastic bowls appear; clarify outer rim and wall if only a paper cup remains. Hide reflections to test nesting, then restore without bright joins into paper interior. Optional ribs, brands and unreferenced grooves are unnecessary; thickness, edges and value scale explain materials.

Keep support and tabletop contact coherent

Represent a simplified support ring with a flattened ellipse and narrow thickness beneath the cone, not a separate floating disc. Use short contact darkness at its junction and lower-right ground shadow, avoiding equal black borders around cone and ring. Repair connection/plane if top-like or ground contact if floating. Do not invent visible drainage channels or a cone tip through the support side. The support is illustrative, not universal cup compatibility, stability or thermal-safety evidence.

Compare empty-dripper and paper-insert versions

Compare empty-dripper and inserted-paper versions, checking unchanged rim, axis and support, then six near folds converging inward. Repair thin edge and nesting if only dense lines identify paper rather than adding more. Remove diagnostic near/far/rim labels from the finish. Change paper height or one occlusion with values fixed. A smooth-paper variant needs folds removed and wording revised, not hidden detail still described as pleated. A new view needs revised rim ellipse and paper edge; rotating an old image does not guarantee correct nesting.

Separate paper and ceramic rims in export

Save the layered original and inspect paper edge, near ceramic rim and cavity in a PNG at use size. Separate local temperature/height if rims merge into thick white rather than outlining the whole cup. Reduce unnecessary fold marks and contrast while retaining the stated six near main folds. Record faults such as right paper exterior incorrectly in front of the rim. Judge thin paper inside thick ceramic, readable cavity and grounded support, not brewing or filtration.
