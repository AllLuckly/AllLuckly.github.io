Adding feathers can make a bird less coherent. Begin with four masses—head, chest, wing and tail—then suggest texture with a few directional groups. This is a simplified bird study: establish posture and weight before markings, without needing to identify a particular species.

Set up a separate study

Use an approximately 1400×1600 px portrait canvas with more space in front of the bird’s gaze and room around its tail. Separate sketch, body, wing feathers and branch. Test a fine short-feather brush and a broader body brush rather than seeking a random feather-texture preset.

Review the bird and branch together at small size, checking feet under the centre of weight and head orientation against the body pose. Save a native structural version without feather detail. Keep the body and branch independently editable so later claw corrections do not repaint chest shading.

Set the perched pose through head and chest

Sketch a tilted egg for the chest and a smaller head above and forward. Connect them with a short neck curve. Mark the eye, beak root and forehead in one consistent direction. A slight head turn should move all three, rather than turning only the beak.

Use a narrow wedge for the tail before separating feathers. Place the feet below the chest’s centre of weight, gripping the same branch. Check the balance at small size. If the bird leans backward, correct body and foot placement before adding eyes, feather tips or leaves.

Paint the wing as one covering form

Paint a teardrop wing on the side of the chest, broad at the shoulder and narrowing toward the tail. Its edge should not simply trace the body contour. Establish one darker mass, then distinguish the rounded shoulder from the longer lower feathers before drawing individual divisions.

With light from the upper left, brighten the crown and lit chest, and darken the underside and covered areas. Shade around the chest volume rather than outlining it. Hide the wing briefly: the body should still feel rounded. Restoring it should add a covering, not a hole.

Change feather direction by group

Let chest feathers follow the downward chest curve, shoulder feathers slant backward and flight feathers extend along the wing. Begin each group with three or four representative marks. Vary their length and spacing while preserving direction. A few crown strokes are enough; dense random marks turn the head into a fur ball.

Suggest selected edges with a lighter colour rather than outlining every feather. Use three to five longer divisions near the wing tip, breaking some to show overlap. Replace an evenly spaced comb with several unequal groups. Keep the tail unified and separate only a few tips.

Control the focal point with the eye and beak

Place a small dark eye in the upper front of the correctly oriented head. Add only a tiny highlight if useful. Build the beak from two short wedges attached to the face, aligned with the eye and forehead. Correct placement before shading, rather than enlarging the eye to repair expression.

Keep the face relatively clear while softening selected body edges into the background. Choose one marking, such as a short eye stripe or chest patch. Avoid equal brightness and sharpness everywhere. Cover the eye briefly to assess the body, then restore it and check that it becomes a natural focus.

Show contact through the branch and claws

Bring a tapered branch in from one side through the grip positions. Wrap two or three bent claw lines around it with front and back overlap, rather than suspending fork shapes above it. Add a small contact dark. The short legs may remain partly hidden by belly feathers.

Limit branch texture to a few knots and value changes. If the bird is unstable, check that both grips belong to the same branch surface. If the feathers fragment the body, simplify chest marks and restore the wing mass. Change one area at a time to identify the cause.

Review and plan the next study

Check pose in a thumbnail, face-versus-wing hierarchy at normal size and accidental gaps near the beak, claws and selected feather edges close up. Judge balance before detail. Record one successful directional feather group and one fragmented area to simplify.

Export a PNG of the perched pose and retain editable wing and claw layers. Next, change only the head turn while keeping body, branch and light fixed. Reposition the eye and beak root and compare expression, separating structural improvement from the distraction of new markings.
