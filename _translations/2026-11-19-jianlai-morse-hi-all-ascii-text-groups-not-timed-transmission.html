Transcribe HI ALL into this explicitly defined offline dot-and-dash notation using periods, ASCII hyphens, spaces and a word-separator slash. Check letter mappings externally against the ITU International Morse table. Display separators are this card’s convention, not Jianlai encoding, decoding, sound generation or radio transmission.

Fix the English source and an offline draft

Establish Jianlai in a new ordinary English/symbol draft, holding scheme, font and host fixed. Source HI ALL has H/I, then A/L/L, separated by one ASCII space, without period/newline. This original greeting exercise is neither a call sign, distress signal nor outgoing message. Preserve the source and use a separate notation draft. Choose a private text editor if Return sends. Record text only, without microphone, light, radio or network transmission, and do not call keyboard piano sounds encoded Morse audio.

Check four distinct letters against the ITU table

The ITU table maps H to four dots, I to two, A to dot-dash and L to dot-dash-dot-dot. Build four external mappings, then expand five occurrences in source order; both L occurrences require groups. This card transcribes short/long signals as ASCII period and ASCII hyphen-minus, a display convention rather than key-hold durations. Preserve lookup and entered text separately. Correct lookup does not prove output, and a similar long dash does not automatically match the target character.

Enter two HI groups with an explicit separator

Type four periods, one ASCII space and two periods, producing a seven-character HI card. Six dots form two groups; six uninterrupted dots supply no such unique boundary. Use actual symbol controls without an assumed long-press shortcut, checking committed text after each group. Preserve any host substitution of repeated periods with an ellipsis before a controlled revision. One ellipsis does not pass three ASCII periods. Keep HI externally rather than append it to the notation.

Expand ALL under this card’s slash convention

After the HI notation enter space, slash, space for the word boundary, then A as .-, space, L as .-.., space and a second L as the four-symbol group ‘.-..’. The slash marks a displayed word boundary; it is neither the Morse encoding of a literal slash nor another English word. Follow the exact target below with declared spaces and no final punctuation/newline. Preserve host replacement of hyphens with longer dashes before correction rather than trust resemblance. Recheck the preceding group to avoid repeated chunks while entering dense symbols.

.... .. / .- .-.. .-..

Decode groups externally and count character kinds

Under the display rule split at the slash and then letter-group spaces, externally recovering H/I and A/L/L as HI ALL. The notation has thirteen dots, three ASCII hyphens, five spaces and one slash: twenty-two characters. These count text, not elapsed time or every definition of signals. Length twenty-two proves no group correctness, so check mappings/order independently. Recovery is external/manual, not automatic Jianlai decoding. Without an executed counting tool retain the manual ledger rather than fabricate output or transmission range.

Separate text spaces from standard timing units

ITU separately defines dash duration as three dots, within-letter gaps as one dot unit, inter-letter gaps as three and inter-word gaps as seven. These are temporal relations: an ASCII space is not inherently three dot times, nor does a slash generate a seven-unit pause. No timed audio is produced, so claim no words-per-minute rate or verified rhythm. Enabled key sounds remain input feedback, not transmission of HI ALL. Keep timing model, external notation and app entry as distinct layers rather than turn formatting into audio generation.

External notation convention: period=dot; ASCII hyphen-minus=dash
H=....; I=..; A=.-; L=.-..
One ASCII space separates letter groups; space/slash/space marks the word boundary.
ITU timing: dot=1 unit; dash=3; intra-letter gap=1; letter gap=3; word gap=7
Text spacing is NOT a timed audio signal.

Use a missing-dot and missing-separator control

In an external failure copy change the first four dots to three, turning H into S; unchanged ALL does not pass HI ALL. Removing the HI group space creates six uninterrupted dots and loses the declared H/I boundary; do not reconstruct it by guessing the source. These controls need no additional phone entry and imply no automatic app error. Inspect grouping/characters before commitment or host substitutions, revising one point with preserved drafts. Do not change source, symbols and spaces together or invoke other extended conventions to relax this card’s declared letter grouping.

Deliver mappings, notation and an explicit non-transmission scope

Retain source, four mappings, actual initial/revised notation and two external controls, passing exact characters and manual recovery separately. Add reopening only after inspecting retained text. Restore ordinary scheme/key sounds and transmit no real code. Verify five letter groups, two words with one word boundary, and twenty-two characters against the declared convention, not all Morse letters or radio skills. Claim no automatic encoder/decoder, transmitter, light communication, emergency-contact function or acoustic device test. A new source/separator convention needs new targets/counts.
