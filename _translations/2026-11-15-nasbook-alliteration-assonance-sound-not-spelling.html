Several words beginning with c do not necessarily repeat one consonant sound. Read original English sound cards in Book Isle, marking initial consonants and stressed vowels separately. Examine sound organisation within a phrase. Silent or quiet personal comparison suffices without speech scoring or automatic prosody detection.

Keep original words and the sound-analysis goal

Keep original cards A Cold coins clink., B stone road home and C city coins, with separate identifiers/context. Chinese glosses may assist meaning but do not replace the English sound evidence. Record source, location and accent; examples use common American pronunciation without asserting identical sounds everywhere. Preserve complete words rather than isolated c/o letters, which cannot establish pronunciation and position.

Check A’s initial consonants word by word

Cold, coins and clink begin with /k/ in the stated pronunciation. Map each sound to its word before calling it alliteration; the letter alone is not auditory evidence. BYU notes broader historical usage, while this exercise adopts the common initial-consonant scope. Sound repetition requires neither identical meanings nor one story referent. Keep sound and semantic relations separate rather than invent narrative links from a pattern.

Use C to test spelling-only classification

City typically starts /s/ and coins /k/: same initial letter, different sounds, unlike A. Record spelling agreement and sound difference without claiming no other similarities exist. Special names or another pronunciation need their own evidence rather than forced ordinary-word readings. Matching font shapes do not establish matching sounds; displayed text supplies spelling, while pronunciation needs the reader or reliable lexical evidence.

Inspect B’s stressed vowels rather than endings

Stone, road and home contain similar /oʊ/ vowels in the stated accent with differing surrounding consonants. Use BYU’s assonance definition to record stressed-vowel repetition. Their final consonants differ, so shared vowels do not automatically establish full end rhyme. Spellings differ too; inspect stressed sounds rather than count letter o. This phrase is not automatically a three-line poem or fixed metre.

Discuss effects only after locating repetitions

List A’s three initials and B’s three stressed vowels as different pattern levels, not six instances of one figure. Percussive or expansive effects are possible readings, not guaranteed feelings. Without actual reading aloud, describe phonetic analysis rather than an auditory experiment. A pattern also does not prove deliberate authorial design at every position; intention needs additional evidence beyond a count.

Change one word for a controlled comparison

Save Cold coins ring., changing only the last verb: two /k/ initials remain and the third becomes /r/. The three-word run changes without eliminating every alliterative pair. Keep other words/order fixed and record location and possible rhythmic effects. Multiple simultaneous wording/punctuation changes cannot be attributed to one letter. Retain A separately; renaming a file does not alter text or sound.

Check wrapping and unstated accent assumptions

Reconnect wrapped phrases before analysis: screen lines do not create end rhyme. Missing phonetic glyphs require a clear source, not invented silent sounds. State pronunciation differences instead of forcing one accent to preserve a label. Repeated letters may be visual rather than auditory evidence. Share original cards and sound notes, not private recordings. Any available app voice is one reading, not a verdict on every accent or character voice.

Reopen and explain both sound categories

Reopen without labels, identify A’s /k/, C’s /s/ versus /k/ and B’s /oʊ/ with differing consonants. Retain originals, positions, accent, minimal word change and possible effects, not only labels. Reassess translations or reading versions rather than transfer English patterns to Chinese glosses. Bookmarks locate the evidence; another reader should verify it without automatic prosody detection, hearing diagnosis or guaranteed delivery effects.
