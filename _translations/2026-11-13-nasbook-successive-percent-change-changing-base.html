3. Increase, then decrease from the new base

Apply the original-value/change relationship in OpenStax percent applications. A adds twenty to one hundred, yielding 120. B subtracts twenty-four from its new base 120, yielding 96. 4. Use the initial base for the overall change

Overall change is 96−100=−4, divided by initial 100 for −4%. Its base is the start, not final 96 or an average. Final 96 is 96% of the start, not a 96% increase. Write “fraction of original” and “relative change” as separate complete statements. Recalculate counts after an input change rather than editing the question to obtain a familiar answer.

5. Reverse the order and retain its own amounts

For a reverse-order control, reduce 100 by twenty to eighty, then add twenty percent of eighty, sixteen, to reach 96. 6. Recalculate recovery from the current base

Recovering from 96 to 100 requires four, with rate 4/96=1/24, approximately 4.1667%. Adding only four percent adds 3.84, yielding 99.84, still short. One hundred suggests reusing the old base; 144 suggests an increase instead of a decrease. Overall 4/96 suggests borrowing the recovery base.
