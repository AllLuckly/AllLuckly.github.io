To add some rain while listening to your own music, try iPhone’s built-in Background Sounds alongside a local recording played in 调调. The background sound comes from the system, not a track generated by 调调, and simultaneous playback does not automatically write it into the original file. Three listening rounds separate the music, environmental sound, and system settings.

Prepare and record the initial state

In 调调, choose a short imported recording you are authorized to use and verify its name, duration, and output destination. A sample without continuous rain is easier to distinguish from the system sound. Listen in a quiet place at a comfortable level; there is no need to turn the volume up. Record a position so that each round can return to the same passage. Mixing can vary with headphones, speakers, and system version, so do not assume every device will sound alike.

On the iPhone, open Settings, then Accessibility, Audio & Visual, and Background Sounds. Record the original switch, selected sound, volume, and visible “Use When Media Is Playing” setting. Names may differ slightly with system language or version. Apple documents rain and other sounds and their use while media plays. Some sounds must first download; if the network is unavailable, choose one already available or pause the exercise instead of treating a failed download as a 调调 problem.

Listen in three separate rounds

For the first round, turn Background Sounds off, return to 调调, and play the chosen passage. Confirm you hear the recording itself and make a brief note. Pause 调调 and return to Background Sounds settings. Select rain or another recognizable system sound and play it alone briefly to confirm the device produces it, then set its volume comfortably so it does not mask the music. This adjusts system background sound, not the volume of the music file.

For the second round, enable “Use When Media Is Playing” in Background Sounds and play the same passage in 调调. Record whether you can distinguish both music and background sound. The system may offer a separate background volume while media is playing; adjust only the relevant slider actually shown and record your setting, rather than assuming identical interfaces across versions. If you cannot distinguish the two, lower the background instead of raising total volume, or choose another owned recording with more distinct content.

For the third round, turn off only “Use When Media Is Playing,” leave other conditions unchanged, and play the same passage again. Record what actually changes in the music and background sound. After pausing 调调, listen briefly to see whether system sound resumes or continues; sound after the pause should not automatically be attributed to the player still playing. This is an observation, not a guarantee for every interruption, headphone, or system version.

Record the two volumes separately

If Settings shows both the ordinary background volume and its volume during media, record their starting positions separately. You can change only the media-time background volume, return to the same passage in 调调, and observe whether the rain is easier to distinguish from the music. Pause and observe the background sound without media as well. Follow the controls and changes actually visible on the device; do not collapse system volume, player state, and these settings into one vague “volume.”

If only one slider is visible, record that item without inventing a missing setting to fill the table. No exact decibel reading is required: this method finds a comfortable, distinguishable combination and does not measure headphone output or prove hearing safety. When you need headphone level information, consult actual system readings rather than estimating from a background-sound slider.

Check source and file boundaries

Make a three-row record for background sound off, allowed during media, and disabled during media. For each, record whether 调调 shows playback, whether music is audible, whether background sound is audible, and the output device. A moving playhead, system sound, and two distinguishable sounds are separate observations. Mark a result uncertain when necessary and keep the conditions; do not keep raising the volume to force a perfect result.

Background Sounds comes from the system. Hearing both sounds from the headphones does not mean 调调 generated a mixed file, added the sound to the track, or created a new server version. To produce a deliverable mix, use a separate audio tool on material you are authorized to process, export a copy, and retain the original sources. This exercise checks system output during playback and does not export a mix.

Troubleshoot and restore

If you cannot hear rain after enabling it, first leave 调调 and verify that Background Sounds works alone. Check its volume, download state, and current audio output, then inspect the setting for use during media. If it works alone but not simultaneously, record the observation and system version rather than immediately claiming incompatibility. If the music itself is silent, turn the background sound off and confirm the track plays and the output destination has not changed before checking the player state.

If sound changes when the screen locks, inspect the system’s Stop Sounds When Locked option and record that condition separately; it is not the same as 调调’s background playback. At the end, restore the Background Sounds switch, sound selection, and relevant volumes to their recorded starting values, and leave 调调 playing or paused as you prefer. On a shared device, restoring the earlier settings avoids unexpected sound for the next user.
