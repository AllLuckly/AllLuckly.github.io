Use editable shapes, curves and arrows to illustrate a bar magnet’s external field direction and check for crossings.

Define the diagram boundary

OpenStax uses field lines to represent magnetic field direction: outside a bar magnet they go from the N pole region toward S, while the field lines themselves are continuous closed loops. This exercise depicts only external portions; state in the caption that internal paths are omitted. The number of drawn lines is illustrative, not a measurement. Do not present the drawing as measured field strength, a device safety distance or an experiment.

Set up document and layers

Create a landscape canvas in VectorPaint and save an editable original. Use layers or object groups for the magnet, field lines, arrows and text, following the controls visible in the installed version. Leave room above and below for curves and at both ends for pole labels. Draw a pale axis and frame as placement aids. Ordinary vector lines may export, so hide or remove them before output rather than assuming they are nonprinting guides.

Draw the bar magnet

Use a rectangle or rounded rectangle for a horizontal bar magnet. Distinguish its left and right halves with separate colored shapes and label them N and S. Color is a visual convention; the letters carry polarity, so do not depend on red and blue alone. Check that the color boundary aligns and the whole still reads as one object before temporarily grouping it. Do not present the two drawn halves as isolated magnetic poles.

Draw upper external lines

Starting near the N end, use the pen tool to draw a smooth path curving above the magnet toward the S end, keeping it outside the body. Add two farther curves and adjust nodes and handles so the lines do not intersect. They illustrate direction rather than a fit to measurements of a specific magnet; do not claim exact geometry or spacing represents numeric values. Hide arrows first to inspect the curves.

Add lower paths and clearance

Draw corresponding external arcs below the magnet. A vertically flipped duplicate can be a starting point, but inspect its nodes for accidental passage through the magnet. Symmetry makes the illustration easier to read and does not prove a real field in its environment is perfectly symmetric. Leave room near the ends for N, S and arrows. If lines crowd together, use fewer illustrative lines rather than drawing through labels.

Place arrows one by one

Place a small arrow on each external arc tangent to the curve and pointing from N toward S. Upper and lower arrows follow the same external direction; the lower arc does not reverse merely because it is below. If making arrows from triangles and short lines, point each tip along the forward path and group it with the line. If the interface offers an arrow style, inspect its final direction rather than trusting a preset name.

Check crossings and continuity

Trace each visible external line from N to S and check that different lines neither cross nor share an ambiguous segment. Magnetic field lines do not cross. If copying or flipping produces a crossing, adjust nodes rather than hiding the mistake with an overlay. Ending the drawn portions at the magnet is a visual omission: explain that the internal direction returns from S to N as part of closed loops, not that field lines disappear at the surface.

Add caption and legend

Use the text tool for a title and caption such as “External direction shown; internal paths omitted.” Explain that arrows show direction and line count and layout are not a quantitative strength measurement. Dashed and solid lines may distinguish visual layers but do not create different physical entities. Place text outside the curves and view the N, S and caption small; shorten wording before enlarging text if it is crowded.

Check color, layering and edges

Make a temporary monochrome copy and verify that letters and arrows still communicate polarity and direction; color cannot be the only cue. If field lines cover the pole labels, adjust order or shorten the visible portions while retaining the omission note. Inspect canvas edges for cropped arrowheads. If a path enters from outside the frame, make clear that the frame is a viewing crop rather than an origin of the field line.

Troubleshoot and export

If a lower arrow points toward N, rotate or redraw it and trace the line from N to S. If curves cross, zoom in and adjust a node or handle. Save the editable original; hide the reference frame in a copy and export SVG, PDF or PNG using an available control. Reopen the output to inspect labels, arrowheads, cropping and caption. A bitmap checks appearance only and does not retain editable paths. Record the source and any measurement steps not performed.

Check what a reader understands

Before sharing, consider asking someone unfamiliar with the draft to identify N, S, the direction of two external lines and whether internal paths are shown. This is a suggested reading test, not a claim it was performed. If the reader thinks each arc ends independently, revise the caption to say complete field lines close and the internal portions are omitted. Keep both drafts for comparison without mistaking the diagram for an instrument result. If an arrowhead disappears when scaled down, enlarge it and retain clearance from neighboring curves, then check direction again. Do not move it onto a crossing line.
