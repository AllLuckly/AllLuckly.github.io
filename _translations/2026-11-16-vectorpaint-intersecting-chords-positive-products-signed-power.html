Crossing lines alone do not establish that all endpoints share a circle. Draw three chords through one point and audit their segment products, keeping signed circle power separate from positive lengths.

Fix the circle and an off-centre intersection

Use mathematical centre (0,0), radius 5 and intersection (1,0), inside the circle but distinct from its centre. Establish those first rather than relabel a symmetric cross. On a roughly 1600-square canvas, an 80-pixel unit and screen origin (650,650) give a 400-pixel radius. Keep equal axis scales and reverse vertical direction for screen placement. Reserve label space. Without exact coordinate controls, use a reference grid and label the drawing schematic; paper equalities are not pixel measurements.

Write three endpoint pairs in one ledger

Use the horizontal diameter, a vertical chord through the intersection, and an oblique chord joining two integer-coordinate endpoints. The ledger fixes pair membership: every length starts at the same interior point, so do not multiply a whole chord by a part. Keep square root of 24 exact, using about 4.899 only for placement. Test all six endpoints in x squared plus y squared equals 25 before drawing. Algebraic membership catches pair mistakes that apparent contact with a thick circular outline can hide.

O=(0,0); r=5; P=(1,0)
A=(-5,0); B=(5,0); PA=6; PB=4
C=(1,sqrt(24)); D=(1,-sqrt(24))
E=(4,3); F=(-3,-4)
PE=3*sqrt(2); PF=4*sqrt(2)
PA*PB=PC*PD=PE*PF=24
power(P)=OP^2-r^2=-24

Draw the horizontal chord and its six/four parts

Draw a thin horizontal chord, labelling length 6 left of the intersection and 4 right of it. Their total is diameter 10, but the relevant product is 24, not diameter times radius. Put labels beside their own parts without obscuring the intersection. Distinguish centre and intersection with different marks. Equal drawn parts suggest the intersection was misplaced at the origin. Keep circle, chords and labels separately selectable, retaining the baseline while adding chords rather than stretching a segment to suit an attractive label.

Check the vertical chord with two equal radical lengths

Place the vertical endpoints square root of 24 above and below the intersection, keeping x=1. This chord does not pass through the centre. Equal parts follow symmetry about the horizontal axis, not universal bisection of every chord through the point. Their product is 24 while the complete chord is about 9.798, shorter than diameter 10. Use distinct restrained colours with one shared intersection mark. Endpoints at the circle’s top/bottom would incorrectly substitute radius 5 for the radical; retain the circle rather than force a symmetric cross.

Check collinearity and three-to-four lengths on the oblique chord

The vectors from the intersection to the oblique endpoints are (3,3) and (-4,-4), opposite and collinear. Their lengths are three and four times square root of 2, whose product is 24 rather than 12. Draw the line and establish that the intersection lies between endpoints before labelling each side. Equal products do not imply equal complete chords: this one measures seven times square root of 2, about 9.899. Save a view isolating this chord to catch endpoint pairing errors hidden by several overlapping colours.

Record negative power without negating ordinary lengths

The point is distance 1 from the centre, so its signed power is 1 squared minus 5 squared, or -24. Ordinary positive segment lengths multiply to +24. These are different conventions, not contradictory calculations: directed products for an interior point are negative. Give each quantity its own labelled column without placing negative ordinary distances on the diagram. An exterior-point variant needs renewed endpoint ordering and sign conditions. This deliverable concerns three chords of this fixed circle, not an assertion about arbitrary intersecting lines.

Diagnose concurrence, circle membership and lengths separately

For unequal products, first check the shared starting point, then common-circle endpoints, then radicals/scales. Thick strokes hiding a gap do not prove the equation, while small hand-placement error does not justify altering exact paper values. Correct one pair and retain the other chords as controls. No automatic circle-power or intersection solver is assumed. An external calculation ledger is not a VectorPaint report. Hide/show each chord after correction and verify that labels and intersection still match, retaining a copy identifying the changed condition.

Export three products and one sign explanation

Save the editable original and reopen an SVG/PDF exported through available controls. A bitmap reference does not become vector automatically. At reading size, identify all endpoint pairs, centre and intersection, with three positive products of 24 and a separate power of -24. Deliver circle-membership checks, collinearity and length calculations rather than only crossed lines. Move unclear radical/sign annotations instead of dropping conditions.
