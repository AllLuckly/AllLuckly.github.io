Align UTC, Shanghai and Tokyo on three timelines to check a shared instant and date change.

Define a fixed example

This diagram labels one instant in three time standards; it does not schedule a real meeting. The fixed example is 25 November 2026 at 15:30 UTC. On that date Shanghai uses UTC+8 and Tokyo UTC+9, giving 25 November 23:30 in Shanghai and 26 November 00:30 in Tokyo. The sources list offsets and daylight-saving status for that year. Recheck other cities or years rather than applying this difference universally.

Calculate and verify the dates

First add eight hours to 15:30 UTC for 23:30 on the same date. Adding nine hours reaches 24:30, written as 00:30 on the following date. Do not leave 24:30 as an ordinary time on the same date. Reverse the conversion: Tokyo 26 November 00:30 minus nine hours returns 25 November 15:30 UTC. Verify both the time and date.

Create canvas and three axes

Create a landscape VectorPaint document, for example 1800 by 1100 pixels, and save an editable original. Label three rows UTC, Shanghai UTC+8 and Tokyo UTC+9, reserving space at left for names and at right for date notes. Use available object controls to separate axes, ticks, alignment guides, text and emphasis markers. Do not assume the app converts time zones or connects to a calendar.

Set a common time range

Use a two-hour window from 15:00 to 17:00 UTC on 25 November and let all three rows represent the same elapsed time. For example, make each hour 240 canvas units wide, each half-hour 120, and the window 480. The scale may be resized uniformly. Align all starts and ends vertically; a different local date is not a reason to shorten or shift the elapsed-time length.

Fill the UTC row

Label the first row 15:00, 15:30, 16:00, 16:30 and 17:00 at equal intervals and state the date is 25 November. Mark 15:30 with a short tick, not as a different duration in another location. Verify five ticks create four half-hour intervals totaling two hours. Check both endpoint labels remain 15:00 and 17:00 UTC on 25 November; they define the drawing window rather than additional appointments. If the endpoint is misaligned, fix the scale before copying it to other rows.

Fill the Shanghai row

Add eight hours at the same five horizontal positions: Shanghai 23:00, 23:30, 00:00, 00:30 and 01:00. The first two ticks are 25 November; 00:00 onward is 26 November. The date boundary aligns with 16:00 UTC, not the beginning of the row. Check Shanghai 23:30 aligns vertically with 15:30 UTC.

Fill the Tokyo row

Add nine hours at the same five positions: Tokyo 00:00, 00:30, 01:00, 01:30 and 02:00, all on 26 November. Tokyo midnight aligns with the window start at 15:00 UTC. Tokyo 00:30 aligns with Shanghai 23:30 and UTC 15:30. Do not shift the whole row a day to the right merely because its labels show the next date; the rows share elapsed time.

Highlight the shared instant

Draw a light vertical guide through the three rows at the horizontal position of 15:30 UTC. Label 25 November 15:30 UTC, 25 November 23:30 Shanghai and 26 November 00:30 Tokyo. Color may distinguish places, but full dates, times, offsets and city names must be readable as text. The guide represents one instant, not three separate half-hour events.

Check dates around midnight

On the Shanghai row, 23:30 to 00:00 is thirty minutes while the date changes from the 25th to the 26th. Tokyo 00:00 to 00:30 stays on the 26th; do not add another day. UTC 15:00 through 17:00 remains on the 25th, so differing local-date labels do not break the shared timeline. Use a temporary table of all five UTC, Shanghai and Tokyo ticks and check each column before hiding it.

Check readability and source limits

Reduce the graphic to intended reading size and check dates, offsets and times, especially 25 versus 26 and 23:30 versus 00:30. Do not use only “today,” “tomorrow” or an abbreviation such as CST in place of full dates and cities, since reader location and abbreviations can differ. For another date or city, recheck its historical or future offset and daylight-saving rules rather than relabeling this example.

Troubleshoot, save and export

If minutes differ on one vertical guide, first inspect tick spacing. If Tokyo is mistakenly labeled 25 November 00:30, recompute UTC plus nine hours and its date. Provide a text alternative listing the shared instant and the two-hour window. Save the editable original and use an available export control for a separate SVG or PNG. Reopen it to inspect dates, axis ends and labels for clipping, then confirm editable text in the original. Mark unperformed device steps pending.

List all five aligned ticks

In scratch work list five corresponding instants: 15:00 UTC is Shanghai 23:00 and Tokyo next-day 00:00; 15:30 is Shanghai 23:30 and Tokyo next-day 00:30; 16:00 is Shanghai next-day 00:00 and Tokyo next-day 01:00; 16:30 is Shanghai next-day 00:30 and Tokyo next-day 01:30; 17:00 is Shanghai next-day 01:00 and Tokyo next-day 02:00. Here next day means 26 November. Project each set onto one vertical guide. If one row skips or repeats thirty minutes, correct the ticks before arranging text.

Separate instant, date and duration

The thirty minutes from 15:30 to 16:00 UTC appear as Shanghai 25 November 23:30 to 26 November 00:00 and Tokyo 26 November 00:30 to 01:00. These are three labels for the same elapsed interval, not ninety minutes to add together. Shade equal-width half-hour spans in all three rows and align both ends with the same guides. Midnight changes the local calendar date but does not itself lengthen this interval. Fixed offsets here apply only to the verified cities and date; for a region with daylight-saving changes, check the rule at each instant before drawing.
