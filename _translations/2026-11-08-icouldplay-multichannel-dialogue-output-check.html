A multichannel file can be mixed for headphone output, and quiet dialogue is not necessarily just low volume. Record selected track, channel labels and actual output device for permitted material, then compare at comfortable volume. Do not mask a problem by raising volume or treat this as Dolby/home-theatre certification.

Choose a dialogue-and-background passage

Choose a permitted passage with dialogue and ambient sound, avoiding sudden loud peaks. Record edition, duration and anchors and confirm it is not a music-only or commentary track. Preserve the original and use familiar headphones/speakers at a comfortable level, stopping if uncomfortable instead of repeatedly increasing volume. Compare intelligibility, not hearing, sound pressure or speaker power.

Copy actual audio and channel labels

Copy selection, language and other visible audio labels. If channel data is absent, state that and optionally consult an existing trusted metadata tool. A 5.1 label describes five main channels plus a low-frequency-effects channel, not six active devices or proof of dialogue in only one channel. Codec labels such as AC3 or DTS do not establish the actual transport/output format.

Separate file channels from device routing

Record the active route separately: device speaker, wired headphones or connected Bluetooth headphones, verifying current state rather than past pairing. Avoid switching during important calls/audio work. File channels and device layout are separate layers with possible system, engine or device processing. Listening alone does not identify the mixing stage or justify an invented fixed app downmix coefficient.

Observe dialogue and background separately

Play normally from before the dialogue and separately note speech intelligibility, ambient presence and music masking. “Sound exists” is insufficient; use short anchors rather than copying long dialogue. Replay once from earlier to exclude missing the onset. Bound quietness to the observed passage instead of declaring a whole-track fault or inferring a missing channel from louder background.

Compare stereo only when genuinely available

Only compare when another corresponding stereo track is genuinely available in the same file, and record the original choice first. Play again from before the same line of dialogue. Verify language, commentary status and edition; Mandarin stereo versus English commentary is not a channel-only comparison. Skip absent tracks without expecting conversion. Different mixes can produce different results without proving a downmix algorithm defect.

Keep a comfortable baseline when changing output

If convenient and without interrupting other tasks, pause, verify another familiar output route and restart comfortably low on the same passage. Record device and volume steps without equating equal steps to equal sound pressure. Keep track and speed fixed. Quiet dialogue across routes still permits a source-mix explanation; a route-specific issue is a clue, not proof of hardware damage or a reason to buy equipment automatically.

Compare engines after identity checks

After verifying media, track and route, compare another visible engine once if available and restore the original. Record absent/failed controls without inventing effects switches. This can narrow conditions but does not replace output-format diagnostics. Keep silence distinct from quiet dialogue: first inspect selection/routing for silence and relative mix for low speech, not one volume-slider remedy for everything.

Prepare a scoped report

For reproduction, preserve media/track identity, visible channel labels, output device, engine, interval and observation with a shareable minimal sample. Do not disclose private paths/accounts or complete commercial audio. “Speech unclear while background continues here” is clearer than “5.1 broken”. Public codec support does not supply missing device diagnostics; mark them unknown and do not claim device multichannel testing. W3C Web Audio describes channel-layout and downmix concepts only; this does not imply that iCouldPlay uses that web API or its mixing coefficients.

Restore choices and retain a limited conclusion

Restore normal track, route, speed and comfortable volume, then reopen and verify the dialogue anchor. Separate metadata, selection, listening and unresolved processing stages. Completion explains conditions and observations rather than theatre loudness or independent output of every channel. A single-route record remains useful with explicit untested comparisons; repeated volume increases must not manufacture a pass.
