Pins and teeth in a music box belong to different parts rather than one decorative pattern. Hand-draw a simplified cylinder mechanism in an open box, using axis, proximity and occlusion to distinguish cylinder and comb. Six drawn teeth are an illustrative ledger, not an instrument’s actual range or a composed or played tune.

Divide the base into cylinder and comb areas

Use an approximately 1800×1400 px canvas with an elevated quadrilateral opening and a light internal base. Reserve the upper-left for a horizontal cylinder and the lower-right for the comb, adjacent but distinct. Begin with base edges and one cylinder axis, leaving both ends visible before case decoration. Keep one coherent viewpoint without assuming generated mechanics. If a tall near wall hides every tooth, lower its drawn height or change the view rather than show all parts transparently through solid wood.

Use end ellipses and axis for the cylinder

Place related ellipses at both axis ends and join their upper and lower cylinder boundaries. Give short projecting axle ends two supports on the base rather than suspend the drum. Use a warm metallic grey, subdued far edge and broad near highlight. The highlight describes curvature, not another solid axle. For a box-like cylinder, repair end faces and tangent boundaries; for a flattened disc, restore axial length while preserving one line through both end centres.

Attach six teeth to one comb spine

Draw one fixed comb spine and six slender teeth extending from it. All roots join that solid spine, with tips facing the cylinder’s engagement region and roots away from it. Do not leave six floating rods. Varied lengths are compositional rather than labelled pitches. Preserve narrow gaps, resolving near edges more clearly than far thickness. Count six roots and six tips before colour. If a seam becomes a seventh tooth, return to pale silhouettes and count the individual solids.

Turn pins along the curved drum

Add several short pins on the visible curved drum, using a small root and raised stroke rather than identical flat dots. Pins near a turning edge compress or disappear; they do not all stand vertically on the page. Keep roots on the cylinder, not the spine or base. Resolve one small region near tooth tips and simplify elsewhere. This is no complete pin score or one-pin-per-tooth scheme. If random dots obscure curvature, remove half and retain a few informative examples.

Clarify the engagement region

Enlarge the adjacent region and place one illustrative pin near one tooth tip, identifying where they can meet without driving the whole comb through the drum centre. This static study may show approach rather than deformation; note that choice. Restore boundaries if pin and tooth merge into one welded block. Motion blur or drawn sound waves cannot establish operation: actual sound also requires real geometry, materials and drive conditions that this image does not verify.

Limit drive detail to the stated exterior

Optionally add one simplified round drive element beside an end support, tonally distinct from the cylinder face, without inventing an entire gear train. Two illustrative fasteners anchor the spine to the base; their count is not a product standard. Keep wood grain and scratches weaker than axis and tip relations. Remove foreground decoration before squeezing tooth gaps. This is a supported mechanical still life, without winding, opening or adjusting a real music box.

Hide decoration and audit moving versus fixed parts

Temporarily colour the cylinder one way and the comb another. Trace pin roots to drum and tooth roots to spine. Remove a tooth wrongly joined to both base and cylinder; restore a missing axle support rather than extend a case wall. Check six teeth, opaque near walls and a common end-face axis. Colours are manual reading aids, not motion analysis. Restore materials and inspect small. If drum and comb still merge, change broad tones before adding more pins.

Reopen with the static mechanism and scope notes

Save native work, hide axis and checking colours, export PNG and reopen. Inspect the opening and component areas fitted, then roots and supports enlarged; the preview does not replace editable saving. Narrow a highlight covering tips, or lighten competing dark edges merging the six teeth small. Reopen case, cylinder and comb as editable elements. State that six teeth and pin placement are invented simplifications without a playable score. Completion verifies visible relations, not tuning, mechanical operation or software music playback.
