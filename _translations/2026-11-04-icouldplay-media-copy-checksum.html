Same name and duration do not establish identical bytes, and playback alone does not prove a complete copy. Compare two files you can access and then verify viewing in iCouldPlay. Use an existing trusted hashing tool without assuming an in-app checksum button or access to hidden internal storage. Work on permitted samples, avoid uploading private films to online hashing sites and do not treat a digest as proof of rights, safety or authentic provenance.

Choose the actual original and copy

Keep a small self-made original and transfer it through the method you actually use. Record both locations, names, sizes and date, ensuring two shortcuts do not point to one object. Compare actual media rather than posters, captions or a share page. “Original” here means the chosen reference, not necessarily official or safe provenance. Avoid editing, replacement or compression during checking so the objects do not change midway.

Compare exact byte counts where available

Prefer exact byte counts from file information or an existing tool; rounded MB or GB values cannot establish equal bytes. Different size establishes a byte difference but may reflect intentional transcoding, repackaging or metadata editing rather than damage. State whether the operation should preserve bytes. Only then can a size mismatch be interpreted against the intended copy or conversion, and the expected digest relationship becomes clear.

Calculate the same digest locally

With an existing macOS terminal, type shasum -a 256, add a space and drag the sample from the file manager to insert its path; confirm the selected file before executing. Repeat for the copy and retain the complete digest with filename, not only a prefix. Without a suitable tool, keep size and playback observations rather than installing unfamiliar software. The command reads and hashes, not repairs, syncs or imports. Sanitize private path information before sharing records.

Compare complete results for the same algorithm

Use SHA-256 both times and compare complete digests, not results from different algorithms. Compare the digest portion when filenames are also shown; renaming alone does not alter media bytes. Hashing a file and hashing a URL’s text are different inputs. Confirm tool settings and actual inputs before interpreting differences. Local records can map reference A and copy B to full paths while public reports expose only necessary identity.

Play even when digests agree

Matching digests strongly support byte identity for this comparison, not playability or safety of the reference. Open the target sample in iCouldPlay and check beginning, a middle anchor and ending with track and caption conditions. An equally copied incompatible original remains incompatible. Do not open untrusted attachments merely to validate hashing. A known self-made sample separates copy identity from provenance risk and playback compatibility.

For different digests, check for conversion first

For unequal digests, inspect compression, transcoding, optimization, metadata rewriting and edition identity first. Similar appearance and duration can accompany re-encoding; unequal bytes do not necessarily mean packet loss. If exact copying was intended, transfer a new copy to a separate location without overwriting the original, then compare again. Preserve the earlier result. State an unexplained byte difference honestly rather than claiming the tool located damage at a specific playback second.

Limit conclusions when internal copies are inaccessible

If import exposes neither a file location nor a verified unchanged export, do not claim to hash the internal app copy. Compare only accessible reference and transfer target, recording app playback separately. A shared or exported same-name file may also be converted. Mark inaccessible storage unexamined rather than bypassing system restrictions. Explicit checking boundaries are more reliable than a polished digest table for a target never actually read.

Keep identity and provenance records together

Keep algorithm, both identities, complete digests, byte counts, time, conversion conditions and iCouldPlay results. NIST Hash Functions explains message digests without certifying your sample or player. Recalculate after editing or metadata changes and preserve version relationships rather than assigning an old digest to a new file. Identify originals and recoverable copies before cleanup. The record answers whether this transfer preserved a file, not whether every playback or safety question is resolved.
