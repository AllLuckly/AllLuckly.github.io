Preserve the old position and manually locate the new edition using chapters and short phrases, then check pause, reopening and offline state.

Identify both audio sources and editions

Prepare two authorized audio editions of the same work, such as an original and a revision with changed pauses. Record filename, format, chapters, total duration, source and device separately. Even matching titles and covers do not establish the same narration, content or edition. Keep both originals and existing records; do not overwrite the old file or delete shelf entries for this exercise. With only one edition, practice recording the current anchor and mark new-edition positioning untested and keep the old-edition inventory; there is no need to purchase, download or make another recording.

Leave an audible anchor in the old edition

Open the old edition through Book Isle’s actual available audiobook entry. Pause in the current chapter and record chapter name, displayed time and a nearby short non-sensitive phrase, plus surrounding sound or story context in case the same words repeat. Verify time and actual sound together. If pause or exit was not performed, persistence remains unknown. Preserve existing notes and keep the practice marker separate from long-term excerpts. Do not upload restricted audio or share long passages.

Inspect the new file instead of trusting its name

Select the new edition through the actual import entry, wait for its result and identify it on the shelf. If entries have the same title, check edition, duration and chapters before claiming an old record was replaced or the two merged. If useful, distinguish your own copies with clear filenames before import. If the new shelf item cannot be identified, stop rather than deleting the old record as an experiment. Listen briefly near the new beginning and end to check voice and work identity; an extension alone does not guarantee a decodable format.

Use chapter and context to find the same phrase

In any chapter list actually available for the new edition, find the corresponding chapter and listen near the likely position until the old phrase and its context are heard. Without a chapter entry, use actual duration and known story context to narrow the location; not every audio file contains chapter markers. Pause when found and record the actual new time as a user-established match, not automatic migration by the app. If the phrase is missing, consider editing, a different edition or an insufficient anchor and keep the match unconfirmed.

Use two anchors to reveal uneven changes

This is a paper example, not a device measurement: one phrase is at 02:10 in the old edition and 02:25 in the new, a 15-second offset; another is at 12:00 and 12:08, an 8-second offset. Different offsets do not support adding 15 seconds throughout the book. Similar total durations do not guarantee proportional timing within each chapter either. The editions may also assign the same passage to different chapter numbers: a revision might separate a preface that the old file included in chapter one. Confirm chapter title and spoken context before recording the time; matching or differing chapter numbers alone establish neither identity nor difference. If a phrase repeats in multiple chapters, seek a neighboring distinctive sentence or actual sound cue. In an actual comparison, find at least two audible anchors, record each edition and position, and listen near the target rather than copying these fictional times as your result.

Record pause, reopening and offline state separately

Pause at the actual target in the new edition, exit using the current interface and reopen, listening around the target. Record time, phrase and reopening outcome separately. If a complete local copy already exists and conditions permit, temporarily disconnect and test the same location, then restore network settings. A playable beginning does not prove the ending is on-device; remote streaming or an unperformed offline test leaves offline status unknown. This does not assume both editions share sync progress or promise persistence in every background state.

Check identity first for same titles or a reset position

If the new edition returns to the start, verify which shelf entry opened, whether pause and exit actually occurred, whether the same chapter was used and whether the file is readable. When only one edition is affected, record its source, duration, pre/post reopening times and message rather than generalizing to every audiobook. If devices retain different progress, preserve each time and phrase first and check the existing sync scope without repeatedly overwriting or changing accounts.

Deliver both edition anchors and a recoverable record

Deliver both edition identities, at least two paired times with context, target position and actual reopening and offline results; leave unperformed steps untested. Return to the old edition and confirm the old original and record can still be located. Successful playback of the new file does not make the old material disposable. This manual handoff requires no app waveform comparison, automatic sentence matching or position conversion. A successful trial applies only to the observed editions and device; other revisions need their own check.
