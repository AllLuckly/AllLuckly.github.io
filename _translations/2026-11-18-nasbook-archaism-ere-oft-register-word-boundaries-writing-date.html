An unfamiliar expression may be old-fashioned or still active literary diction; neither alone dates a work to antiquity. Read original ere/oft cards in Book Isle, retaining meaning, dictionary register, whole-word position and proposed effects separately. Reading marks are not automatic dating, and apparently old-looking spelling is insufficient.

Preserve original A and modern-word replacement B

Use A below as two authorial lines, B as modern-word replacements preserving the basic sense, and C only as a substring control. Labels/line numbers are not text. Retain editable work and export fixed-line PDF or clearly labelled EPUB; check line endings/comma and bookmark A. Wrapping Ere onto a separate screen row does not remove its grammatical link to the following clause. Highlight only with visible format controls, otherwise record whole sentences externally rather than strip context for convenient selection.

A
L1: Ere the lamps went out, I left the gate.
L2: I oft return to hear the river.

B
L1: Before the lamps went out, I left the gate.
L2: I often return to hear the river.

C
L1: I stood here before the gate.
L2: The soft rain crossed the river.

Keep the two dictionary labels distinct

RPO describes archaism as obsolete/archaic diction with current alternatives. Merriam-Webster labels ere old-fashioned and literary, meaning before, but oft chiefly literary, meaning often. Literary does not mean entirely vanished, and the two entries do not establish no present users. Record form, meaning and original register labels separately before proposing archaic colouring in context. A word’s earliest-use history is not the writing date of the current lines.

Return ere to its complete time clause

A’s Ere introduces the lamps went out: the speaker left the gate before the lamps went out. This orders departure before extinguishing, not here meaning this place or ear as a hearing noun. B’s Before retains past-tense went/left and the same ordering. Record departure before extinguishing externally, then verify the complete sentence. The conjunction use fits a full following clause, rather than mechanically applying a dictionary’s first listed sense everywhere.

Use oft for frequency without changing tense

I oft return means the speaker often returns to hear the river. Oft modifies frequency, not one visit or a completed past event replacing the habitual present. B’s often retains that basic frequency. A has nine/seven words by line; B also nine/seven. Equal word count does not guarantee identical sound rhythm. Common pronunciations of ere/before and oft/often may affect rhythm, but two words cannot prove a whole metre or establish automatic in-app narration/syllable measurement.

Use C to distinguish substring hits from target words

Here in C contains letters ere and soft contains oft, but neither occurrence is the whole target word. If search returns substrings, inspect boundaries instead of marking two old-fashioned uses in C. Each word has its own meaning; deleting here’s h or soft’s s would alter source rather than identify a device. Ignore only initial case and surrounding punctuation for comparison, not prefixes or internal matching letters. Preserve spelling and explain rejected hits separately.

Separate register effects from historical facts

A may sound older or more poetic than B’s everyday wording, a register reading supported by the contrast. It proves neither a medieval speaker nor an author’s historical era or universally solemn response. A is original to this exercise, reminding us that present writers can deliberately choose old forms. Dating needs manuscript/edition/publication evidence; interpreting tone needs lamps, gate and river context. One dictionary label cannot supply author identity, story time and text date together.

Troubleshoot whole words, labels and syntax

For ere translated here, return to the complete word and temporal clause. For oft called entirely obsolete, check chiefly literary. Reject extra here/soft hits by boundaries, not deleted letters. With OCR, compare the source page before deciding ere is not a damaged here; unresolved glyphs remain unconfirmed. Correct notes while retaining dictionary labels/links. Do not overwrite A with B or treat highlight colour as automatic dictionary classification.

Reopen with three evidence layers, not an ancient-poem label

Reopen A with ere’s before sense/time ordering and oft’s often sense/habitual frequency. Use B/C to explain equal word counts with changed register and equal substrings without equal whole words. Deliver source, register ledger and an evidenced but discussable tone candidate, leaving unperformed bookmark/highlight/reopen actions pending. Do not retain only an ancient-poem label or claim automatic Book Isle archaism detection, dating, author identification or dictionary evidence as device testing.
