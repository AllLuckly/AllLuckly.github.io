Choose owned audio already providing usable timed lyrics and an instrumental gap between vocal passages. Build a ledger from audible boundaries and visible-line changes. A start cue does not automatically supply an end cue. A retained prior line, blank display and missing words need different evidence; no lyric editor or import format is invented.

1. Start with a genuinely usable lyric source

Choose authorized music that already plays and shows timed lyrics in 调调. Record source, title, version and two identifiable sung lines. Without usable lyrics, prepare only the paper ledger; the article sample is not a successfully injected source. No LRC, tag or server-protocol entry is promised. Choose a gap confirmed through audio and a reliable lyric source as instrumental without sung words, not inferred from a screen that has stopped changing. Preserve original audio and lyrics. Any permitted source modification belongs to a separately documented external workflow, not an unpublished in-app synchronization editor.

2. Keep audible regions separate from line starts

For the actual material, first record approximate end of the first sung passage and beginning of the next, then separately record line-start cues confirmed by the source. Audible vocal activity and lyric timing metadata need not contain identical information. If source timestamps cannot be inspected, mark them unknown rather than infer a stored value from highlighting. Use elapsed time from track start consistently, converting a remaining-time display only after identifying its direction. Distinguish a vocal tail from instrumental reverberation; record a range if unclear instead of rounding still-audible singing into a supposedly voiceless gap.

3. Understand absent end cues with an original paper example

The original paper example below supplies line starts at five and thirty seconds. A separate audible-region ledger stipulates the first singing ending at twelve and the next beginning at thirty, an eighteen-second instrumental gap. The Chinese practice lines concern lamplight and evening wind; they are neither bundled lyrics nor an import syntax. The two start cues alone cannot establish clearing at twelve, which belongs to a different ledger rather than a lyric end cue. That missing information explains why a retained line does not alone prove broken synchronization. A source carrying actual end cues or other states needs its own analysis; not every lyric format lacks endpoints.

4. Observe the state after singing ends in a natural pass

Play naturally from before the first passage, identify the first line’s sound and continue through its ending into the instrumental region. Record whether the visible line remains, dims, clears or behaves otherwise. Keep display and sound in separate columns: visible words do not establish ongoing singing, and blank display does not establish an omitted line. Hold track, theme and output fixed and do not skip the gap. A seek creates a separate positioning round rather than continuous evidence. Use approximate positions and identifiable words. An incompletely heard gap remains range-limited; the paper eighteen seconds is not a measured result for the chosen track.

5. Check the next line at the next vocal onset

Continue to the second passage and inspect the corresponding line at its recognizable vocal onset, preserving neighboring context words. Both lines matching with a retained prior line during the gap differs from new words being sung but absent from the source. If a present line appears early or late, record timing separately rather than measure synchronization through gap-display behavior. A source abbreviating a repeated chorus has a separate content convention; do not invent full lyrics. Compare this recording version with its own source, not a different performance or subtitle transcript used to declare the current line wrong.

6. Recheck the same gap boundaries separately

After retaining round one, you may reposition to a known point before the gap and replay both boundaries. Check for the same lyrics and instrumental content. Seeking may change the current-line state, so distinguish the immediate post-seek display from later natural playback instead of diagnosing a stable result from one transient old line. Preserve positions, source state and actions for differing rounds rather than replace one with the more favorable screenshot. Without precise time entry or line navigation, use actual visible playback controls for an approximate check. Invent no tap-a-lyric seek function. An unperformed or uncertain replay stays pending.

7. Diagnose sound, text, timing and view separately

If singing continues inside a supposedly voiceless region, correct the audible map first. For absent source text, check version and content; for a present line mismatching onset, inspect timing; for words merely outside the viewport, return to the actual lyrics view. These are not all “missing interlude lyrics” and do not justify reimporting the whole library. Audible instruments show the region is not wholly silent but do not prove lyric completeness. Correct one layer while preserving sources. Do not add an end value and retroactively call it original metadata. Keep interruptions, automatic track changes and output changes separate. Unobserved portions remain unknown.

8. Deliver gap evidence rather than a universal clearing rule

Retain actual track/source identity, both boundary ledgers, observed line states and unfinished stages. Reopen and recheck the passage only before marking reopening passed. Explain which paper example owns the eighteen-second interval, where an actual instrumental gap was heard and whether the source supplies end cues. Public support for source-provided synchronized lyrics does not promise one clearing behavior at every instrumental onset. New recording versions, lyric sources or display states need renewed checks rather than treating two lines and one gap as full-track, whole-library or universal format certification.
