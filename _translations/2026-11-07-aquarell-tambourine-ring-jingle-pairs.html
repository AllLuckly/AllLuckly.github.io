A tambourine is more than a circle beside floating silver discs. Draw an invented headless frame with six slots containing two metal discs each, twelve total, using counts and overlap to inspect attachment. This count is a drawing choice, not a universal specification. No playing or sound demonstration is involved. Hand-draw frame, jingles and shading so every disc occupies an understandable position rather than an arbitrary decorative spot.

Establish an elliptical frame with depth

Use an approximately 1600×1600 px canvas with a mid blue-grey background. Draw an oblique outer ellipse and smaller inner ellipse for frame width, then offset the rear edge slightly to show depth rather than three separate rings. Separate sketch, front frame, rear frame, metal discs and shadow. Leave background visible through the open centre instead of filling a drumhead. Inspect frame continuity in one colour. Correct an unexplained vanished side through inner/outer boundaries rather than concealing it with jingles. This design is explicitly headless; adding a white head later would require different overlaps.

Distribute six slots around the frame

Mark six slot positions around the ellipse, clearer nearby and compressed farther away, comparing spacing around its perimeter rather than dividing horizontally on the canvas. Temporary labels one to six can stay out of the final image. Put slots in the frame’s side thickness, not in the empty centre. Draw only narrow dark openings and check that they do not sever the frame into six disconnected pieces. If the last crowds its neighbour, redistribute the group rather than squeezing it smaller. Six is the design rule; hand variation must not accidentally create a seventh equally deep decorative hole.

Count two discs in each slot

Place two partly overlapping thin discs in each slot, their elliptical flattening changing with frame direction. Distinguish each pair in two sketch greys and count six pairs/twelve discs, excluding the dark slot floor from the count. Near pairs show more; far ones may hide partly behind wood, but their slot membership should remain clear in the sketch. Record an occluded second disc instead of pushing it outside the frame merely to make twelve visible. Leave plausible visual clearance within slots without piercing both wooden walls. Check count separately from placement, completing one clear pair first and then the rest.

Join short pins and slot overlaps

Suggest a short pin or central attachment through each pair, connecting across opposing wooden slot edges rather than appearing from the background. Full hardware detail is unnecessary, but discs should not look pasted outside. Interrupt a far disc where the near wooden edge hides it. If they resemble hanging earrings, connect pins to slots. If the frame is filled with thick coins, reduce disc thickness and heavy outlines. Hide reflections and inspect frame/pin/disc junctions instead of using a bright star to conceal a missing connection. Fine distant pins may simplify without losing the main attachment relationship.

Give wood and metal different value rhythms

Use warm ochre-brown for the frame, lit upper left and shaded lower right, with restrained grain following its arc. Use cool-grey metal with one or two narrow bright bands amid visible midtones and dark faces, not identical white centres and black rims everywhere. Differing orientations require differing reflections. If wood looks metallic, reduce fragmented highlights. If discs look papery, add modest edge turns and narrow reflections without thickening them. Compare with texture hidden and restored. Material differences support attachment rather than covering slots and depth under brightness.

Keep distant pairs from becoming a flat wreath

Hide all highlights and compare broader near pairs with compressed far ones, including rear-frame portions hidden by front frame or near discs. Do not arrange twelve identical circles around the silhouette. Inspect the open centre: small intrusions must correspond to slots, without a disc floating centrally. If the frame resembles a flat paper ring, restore depth and overlap rather than adding more shadow outlines. A three-slot simplified version can test attachment logic; explicitly label that changed design three slots instead of retaining a six-slot description after deletion.

Inspect count, attachment and gaps slot by slot

Inspect sketch slots one through six, counting two discs, locating their central attachment and checking continuous wood on both sides. A correct total alone cannot replace these three checks. Compare a disc deliberately detached from its slot to see the floating-decoration error, then repair the original. Change attachment only, not material or count. If detail becomes confusing at reduced size, simplify internal reflections rather than losing pair ownership. These are manual layer/sketch checks without component recognition or a claim that the depicted instrument is in a validated playing state.

Preserve paired relationships after export

Save the layered original and inspect the nearest two slots and farthest one in the PNG at use size, ensuring a pair has not merged into a thick silver strip. Fade reflections or slightly separate visible edges where needed instead of forcing all twelve discs to the front. Check that the centre stays unfilled and the frame continuous. Keep the numbered-slot sketch. A changed viewpoint requires redistributing near/far groups rather than rotating every disc by one angle. Record a specific fault, such as a third-slot rear disc piercing the front wooden edge, instead of merely asking for more shine.
