Make an eight-page left-bound booklet from two nested folded sheets. Establish reading order with a paper model, then draw each sheet's two printed sides in VectorPaint. Adobe's booklet-printing documentation explains how double-sided sheets are collated and folded into page order. This exercise checks a manual layout; it does not assume VectorPaint offers automatic imposition or printer settings.

Prepare two sheets and a blank model

Use two equal sheets of scrap paper. Place each landscape and fold in half, nesting the second inside the first. With fold at left and opening at right, pencil reading page numbers 1–8 from cover to back cover, one per page. Mark the top edge with a small dot while the cover faces up, so orientation remains identifiable when unfolded. Folding alone is sufficient; staples and cutting are not required. Record outer versus inner sheet before opening, avoiding confusion between sheet order and page numbers.

Establish four sheet-side sketches

Unfold each sheet completely and label both sides of the outer and inner sheets, recording reading page numbers on left and right halves. For this exercise's left binding and consistently oriented sheets, one outer side should read “left 8, right 1” and the other “left 2, right 7”; one inner side should read “left 6, right 3” and the other “left 4, right 5.” If your model differs, inspect nesting, writing surfaces and how the sheet was flipped rather than forcing it to match. Page numbers establish folded reading order, not printer feed order.

Draw four two-page panels

Create a landscape canvas with four equal rectangles for outer front, outer back, inner front and inner back; divide each with a center fold line. Separate sheet boundaries, folds, numbers and explanations into layers and save an editable draft. Fill each pair using the verified paper model and maintain the direction indicated by its top-edge mark. “Front” and “back” here name the two sides of each paper model; real printer flipping must be tested separately.

Separate reading sequence from flat sides

Place a separate line “Reading: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8” alongside the four flat pairs. Label 1 “cover” and 8 “back cover”; do not mistake “left 8, right 1” on a sheet for reversed reading order. Use numbers large enough to read and a fold line that does not cover them. Two light colors can temporarily distinguish outer and inner sheets, but write each sheet identity as well rather than relying only on color.

Check that each page appears once

Verify each number 1–8 appears exactly once and every pair matches the paper model. A table can list sheet, side, left page, right page and agreement with paper. At print-preview size check numbers do not cross folds and text is not close to paper edges. If a page is repeated, return to the appropriate paper side instead of swapping three other numbers to make the count work. The check applies to this specific nesting, not automatically to right binding or a different fold.

Leave space at fold and edges

Leave even blank space on both sides of each fold and a visible margin around the outside; important text should not touch the fold. This is a layout illustration, not a printer's bleed, trim or binding tolerance. If adding a picture, do so in a copy and keep page numbers and folds visible; do not obscure model numbers with a spread. Keep elements individually selectable and record the actual structure if they cannot be selected separately.

Separate software imposition from actual printing

Here the four spreads are drawn manually in VectorPaint; no automatic booklet-imposition feature is claimed. For real printing, choose suitable duplex and binding options in your own print software, testing on ordinary paper for feed direction, flip method and page order. Adobe distinguishes automatic duplex from manually flipping sheets. Printers differ, so this diagram cannot guarantee output orientation. If a print sample disagrees with the model, retain the draft and inspect print settings before reversing every page number.

Make a page-position control

Duplicate the digital schematic and deliberately swap 8 and 1 on the outer panel. Mark a separate scrap sheet according to that copy, refold and observe the positions of cover and back cover. Label this “incorrect control” and keep the original model: it demonstrates that each number appearing once does not establish correct position and orientation. In another copy invert one page number and compare it with the paper model's top-edge mark. Restore the original four pairs and read 1–8 in order. If the count is right but cover or top edge is wrong, inspect the relevant sheet side and orientation rather than merely recounting. This control does not test a physical printer.

Export and deliver the comparison

Save the editable draft, export an image or other available format through a visible control and open it elsewhere to inspect all pairs, folds, top-edge marks and labels. If SVG output is available, reopen it and try selecting a fold and a page number; record merged text or objects. Deliver the paper model's photo or handwritten record, digital illustration, editable draft and four-row table. Mark unperformed printing, reopening or device work pending; the example is not claimed tested on every printer.
