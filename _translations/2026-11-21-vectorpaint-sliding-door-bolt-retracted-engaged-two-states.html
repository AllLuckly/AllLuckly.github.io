Make two sliding-door-bolt illustrations: retracted on the left, extended into a frame keeper on the right. Hold door, frame, barrel and horizontal axis fixed while the bolt and attached handle move. A published British patent describes a bolt sliding inside a barrel and engaging a keeper. This is a simplified drawing exercise, not the complete patented mechanism, installation instructions or a security design.

Declare the invariants

Create a landscape canvas with two equal panels. Each contains door, frame, gap, door-mounted barrel, movable bolt and frame-mounted keeper; only the bolt and its attached handle move horizontally. Label the panels “Retracted” and “Engaged” instead of relying only on color. Separate background, fixed parts, moving parts, labels and guides, then save an editable draft. Canvas units organize the illustration, not manufacturing dimensions.

Build door, frame and gap

In the left panel draw adjacent rectangles for door and frame with a narrow gap between them. Fix their relative positions and draw one horizontal guide across both. The gap must show where a bolt could cross without making the two sides look unrelated. Duplicate door, frame, gap and axis together into the right panel and verify corresponding positions and heights. Moving the frame alone would make later motion ambiguous: was it the bolt or the fixed structure?

Draw the barrel and visible bolt on the door

Place a short horizontal barrel on the door along the guide axis. A dark outline and lighter upper plane can suggest thickness while leaving an opening where the bolt end is visible. Draw a narrow differently colored bolt through the barrel, not floating above or below it. In the first panel its right end retreats to the door side, leaving no rod across the gap. If showing a handle, connect a short handle to the bolt and record them as one movable component. The patent's actual slot and installation can be more complex; this diagram does not claim all parts.

Place a keeper on the frame

Draw a small keeper on the frame along the same axis, using a frame and visible opening to indicate where the bolt may enter. The keeper stays fixed to the frame. Align its opening with barrel and bolt centers and magnify the gap to verify the bolt does not cross it in the first panel. If the keeper overlaps the door incorrectly, correct both fixed panel backgrounds rather than moving only the rod; corresponding backgrounds must remain the same for the state comparison.

Make the engaged state and inspect overlap

Copy the left panel to the right, then slide the movable bolt and handle together along the axis until the end enters the keeper opening. Leave barrel, door, frame and keeper fixed. A rod segment should now cross the gap; the keeper's front edge may hide its entering end, but the rod must not pass through the keeper's outside wall. Compare: the retracted gap has no rod, the engaged gap does and its end meets the keeper. If motion is unclear, reduce keeper-edge occlusion or adjust contrast before moving any frame.

Explain movement with labels and an arrow

Label the panels “Retracted” and “Engaged” and put a short arrow between them for the change from one to the other. The arrow belongs to the diagram, not the actual mechanism; keep it out of the door gap so it is not mistaken for the bolt. Add a small legend distinguishing fixed and moving parts with words and shapes, not only red and green. If the drawing tool offers grouping, use an actual visible control to group rod and handle; otherwise keep independent layers and note their relationship in layer names.

Check overlays rather than color alone

In a working copy align the fixed backgrounds of both panels at the same reference position and hide moving parts. Door gap, barrel and keeper should coincide. Restore moving layers and check that only rod and handle differ. If overlay is inconvenient, record and visually compare each fixed part's distance from its panel edges; do not claim a precision measurement. Restore the side-by-side layout, hide guides and the check copy, and verify labels still match their states.

Keep screw marks and gap fixed

Add two symbolic screws to the barrel plate and two to the keeper, including them with fixed parts before duplicating. Their positions should agree across panels. If grouping is unavailable, label their fixed relationship in layer names and compare each mark's distance from its plate. These marks help inspect fixed geometry; they do not specify actual fasteners or installation. At reduced size, arrow, gap and bolt tip must remain distinct; adjust spacing and label placement before changing line width.

Export and deliver

Save the editable draft, export a separate image using a visible control and reopen it to inspect gap, keeper opening, arrow and labels for cropping. If SVG export is available, save and reopen a copy, trying to select fixed and moving parts independently. Record any merge instead of inferring retained groups from appearance. Deliver the two-state illustration, editable draft and checklist of fixed parts, moving parts, overlap and pending items. Mark unperformed reopening or device operations pending. Do not use this as installation or security-performance guidance or a substitute for manufacturer instructions.
