The two long runs of a safety pin are not separate floating wires. They connect through a coil at one end, while the pointed end enters the clasp when closed. Draw one stationary closed exterior, focusing on continuity, slender metal and clasp occlusion without demonstrating opening or piercing. Arrange forms manually with Aquarell layers and brushes, hiding highlights during structural checks so attractive reflections do not conceal broken connections.

Choose an angle with a readable clasp opening

Use an approximately 1800×1200 px landscape canvas with a pale grey-violet background. Angle the pin lower left to upper right, coil left and clasp right. Sketch a long narrow envelope with space between the two wire runs. Separate sketch, wire, clasp, reflection and shadow. Avoid a perfectly edge-on view that merges both runs, and do not combine incompatible viewpoints merely to expose every part. If clasp and coil dominate, adjust proportion and spacing. Even at thumbnail size, the object should read as one slender closed pin.

Trace a continuous path through the coil

Start along the wire beside the clasp’s fixed end, follow it left into a small coil, then return along the other rightward run. Check one uninterrupted centreline without invented branches, then give it slender width. A readable one-and-a-half-turn appearance is a drawing simplification, not a universal product specification. If the coil resembles a detached key ring, reconnect both long runs. If crossings form a black knot, establish front-to-back order and interrupt the rear contour locally. Continuity takes priority over highlights; a large white spot cannot substitute for a joined endpoint.

Enclose the pointed end inside the clasp

Draw a small metal clasp at the right, defining its upper rim, side and opening toward the wire. The movable run enters that opening and its final point is hidden; do not add a bright spike outside. Keep the concealed path as a low-contrast guide and remove it from the finish. If still apparently open, check that the wire reaches the opening and that the clasp is not merely a neighbouring block. If a sealed lead lump appears, suggest a narrow dark mouth without exposing all inner mechanisms. This is a closed exterior, not an assembly cutaway.

Keep both wire runs slender and rounded

Lay in mid blue-grey wire, add a narrow upper-left light band and a slightly darker lower-right turn. Let reflections follow the wire but break near bends, crossings and clasp rather than staying uniformly white. Keep comparable widths along both runs and avoid a rope-thick coil. A flat icon needs a little light/back-edge distinction; a thick pipe needs narrower wire, not a darker background. Keep background visible between the runs. Slender volume depends on both silhouette and value rather than paired solid black and white stripes.

Reserve deep darks for useful overlaps

Place deepest darks in the clasp mouth and selected coil overlaps rather than a heavy black base along the whole wire. Flat clasp reflections may be wider than wire reflections but must share the light direction. Hide reflections to check enclosure and entry, then restore them and ensure the opening has not become a white sticker. Improve local value turns and cool/warm differences if metal reads as plastic instead of adding random specular dots. One or two slight colour variations are enough; rust, lettering and ornament do not help verify closure.

Distinguish grounded and raised portions

Place the pin on a flat tabletop, with lower coil and clasp areas near contact and middle runs slightly raised. Tighten shadows at contact and soften them under raised portions, casting generally lower right. Do not offset every contour identically as if all heights matched. Add endpoint contact cues if floating; reduce unnecessary black contact bands if flattened. Shadows explain still-life placement, not real elasticity, point pressure or clasp security. A closed-looking illustration cannot replace a physical safety check.

Use a plain path sketch to reject false joins

Save a diagnostic sketch containing the wire centreline, clasp outline and dashed concealed path. Read fixed end, long run, coil, return run and clasp entry in order. Dashed hidden lines help the sketch but must not remain as see-through marks in the finish. Investigate a second loop as either coil overlap or an incorrectly joined long run. Reconnect any return run ending in empty space, then restore reflections. Change paths while holding light and background fixed for a useful comparison. Keep the diagnostic sketch in the native file; export visible exterior forms only.

Check clasp entry at small output size

Save the layered original and inspect the PNG at use size. Locate the clasp mouth and trace the returning wire into it. Repair local continuity if thin wire breaks, rather than thickening everything. Correct an exposed point at the occlusion layer, not merely with a white reflection. Simplify inner coil marks if they reduce to a black dot while retaining both connections. Record a definite fault such as an excessive background gap at the right-hand entry. Readable slender metal, continuous path and closed occlusion are the criteria, not real product strength or operational safety.
