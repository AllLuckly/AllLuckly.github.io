Dragonfly wings are not four identical transparent leaves. Give each its own root, angle and overlap. Paint a simplified insect on a twig, establishing body and four-wing directions before allowing background through the wing areas and adding selected structural veins by hand.

Begin with head, thorax and abdomen

On an approximately 1700×1400 px landscape canvas, place a short head, broader thorax and tapering abdomen on a diagonal. Leave room for wing tips and separate near and far wings. Establish enough thorax width for four roots before eyes or fine detail.

Spread four wings from distinct roots

Place front-wing axes and slightly rearward hind-wing roots at distinct thorax positions. Use narrow roots, fuller middles and rounded tips, with unequal projected widths. Repair a four-petal-flower appearance through wing directions and body axis rather than added veins.

Retain background inside the wings

Use muted grey-green behind a very pale blue-grey wing tint, preserving background variation. Deepen roots and clarify selected edges. Opacity assists but does not create structure. Reduce continuous hard lights on glass-like wings, or strengthen only a few key edges if they vanish.

Branch major veins from root to tip

Give each wing a major longitudinal vein and a few tapering branches toward its edges. Clarify one near wing and simplify farther ones. Test density before continuing. Remove grid-like internal marks from a tennis-racket appearance, preserving the major axis, branches and background gaps.

Use interrupted lines at overlaps

Where wings overlap, weaken or interrupt lower veins instead of showing every layer equally. Use only a modest colour shift. Check far wings alone before restoring near ones. Repair confusing crossings through root identity and overlap rather than heavy black junctions.

Keep body shading and eyes coherent

Use two related body values and progressively smaller abdominal divisions. Suggest eyes as modest paired forms under the same upper-left light. Avoid independently highlighted beads; soften segment darks and restore the continuous long body when divisions become excessive.

Use legs and twig to show a perch

Draw only necessary bent legs from the underside of the thorax, gripping one subdued twig. Omit selected far legs. Correct floating through one clear grip, and quiet competing twig texture. At small size, the perched action matters more than counting every leg.

Compare three vein densities on one wing

Make three identical wing studies with main veins only, a few branches, and denser mesh. Compare them at final display size against the same background. Reject a dense version that becomes grey despite looking attractive enlarged. Use the result differently for near and far wings rather than repeating one density.

Check roots through their intervening gaps

Examine gaps between adjacent wings and the body. Extend their axes back to the thorax to check distinct roots before restoring outlines. Resolve crowding by changing an angle rather than fading everything. Small root darks may support connection without becoming mechanical hinges.

Test lightness without fine veins

Export vein-free and finished versions with separate near and far wings. Next, change only the front-wing angles and rebuild widths and overlaps. Record one background gap that best supports transparency and preserve it before deciding how many lines to add.
