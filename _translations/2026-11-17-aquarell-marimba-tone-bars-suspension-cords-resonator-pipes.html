Draw a marimba excerpt from slightly above, separating wooden bars, suspension and lower resonator pipes. Twelve representative bars make connections and gaps auditable, not a standard full range. Establish support before grain so bars do not become a solid tabletop or piano keys.

Use the frame to establish a two-row plane

Use an approximately 2000×1500 px landscape canvas. Establish near/far frame edges and ends from a shallow elevated view that reveals pipes below. Show seven front bars and five rear bars, twelve total, with the rear five grouped two plus three as an excerpt, not a complete instrument. Locate frame and legs simply before grain. Separate sketch, bars, suspension and pipes. Pixels are compositional. Label a cropped view as an excerpt rather than leave an apparently unsupported floating board.

Keep bars separate and vary their lengths

Sketch twelve independent top faces with fine background gaps. Make representative lower-pitched bars longer and wider, tapering toward the higher side instead of duplicating rectangles. Front and rear rows remain separate, with readable staggered ends. Indicate thickness through near-side shadows and restrained far edges. These lengths summarize appearance, not a pixel-to-pitch calculation; matching lengths alone do not establish matching notes. Temporarily number bars so later cords and pipes refer to existing bars without introducing a hidden thirteenth.

Run suspension cords through supported paths

Following the official suspension reference, place fine cords and short posts along the bar rows at their mounting positions. Do not lash thick rope across every bar’s centre. Stop hidden cord sections and resume beyond, rather than draw decorative stitches through upper faces. Connect posts to the frame and retain a readable bar–frame gap instead of nailing a tabletop. Show enough exterior evidence for suspension without precise node distances or maintenance instructions. Concealed holes remain concealed rather than becoming identical invented black holes in every bar.

Orient resonator openings toward the bars

Place representative resonator pipes beneath corresponding bars with mouths facing upward, not horizontally toward the viewer. Show visible openings as narrow ellipses and upright walls. Do not pull a concealed mouth forward to expose it artificially. Pipes may be longer on the low side, shortening across the excerpt according to the reference, without deriving lengths from bar pixels. Leave air between separate bars and pipes instead of welding them with gold fill. Some mouths may be hidden; document representative assignments and omitted internal tuning.

Let the frame carry the instrument

Connect the top frame to visible uprights and lower braces, using short contact shadows at the floor. Keep pipes within the support envelope rather than turn the longest pipes into four legs. Show two near legs if appropriate, explaining hidden far supports without claiming only two exist. Suspension cords support bars, not floor-anchored guy ropes. Inspect the frame with bars hidden, then restore them and check uprights do not penetrate bars. Add wheels or locks only when visible in the selected reference; omission is preferable to invented assembly details.

Differentiate wood, cords and pipe walls

Use muted warm-brown bars with sparse lengthwise grain, smoother grey pipe walls and restrained cord contrast. Keep gaps dark without an equally heavy black grid; firmer near edges and softer rear bars reveal height. Shade pipe interiors and add narrow wall reflections without a barcode effect. If twelve bars merge into a tabletop, restore ends and gaps before reducing grain. Subdue dominating pipes while retaining upward assignment. Invented lighting and wood colours guarantee neither species of wood, pitch nor resonance.

Audit support and pipes without deriving a range

Hide materials and audit seven front plus five rear labels. Identify each bar’s suspension and corresponding or concealed pipe position. Check occlusion before filling a supposedly missing bar with an extra wood block; a surplus may be frame mistaken for bar. Repair cord occlusion locally rather than bringing every cord forward. Reorient misplaced mouths upward consistently. Preserve a failing version and change one relationship at a time. Twelve remains an illustrative count, proving neither range, equal-temperament tuning nor playability.

Reopen and retain scope and three-layer structure

Save native work, hide labels and reopen a PNG. Read bars, suspension and pipe rows as three levels, then inspect gaps, cord interruptions and frame attachments enlarged. If pipe mouths merge with bars, restore narrow air gaps rather than arbitrarily shift all pipes and break their supports. Reopen editable structural layers separately. Document a twelve-bar excerpt, omitted far mouths and no acoustic measurement. Leave unperformed actions pending; a manual suspension drawing is neither app vibration simulation nor a construction plan.
