Draw intersecting circles of unequal radii, deriving their equal-power line before their intersections. The radical axis is the full line; the common chord is only a segment. Recompute coordinates externally and use VectorPaint for manual circles, lines and labels, not an assumed radical-axis solver.

Fix distinct centres and signed powers

Set O=(0,0), radius 5, and C=(6,0), radius 3. Define each power as squared distance to its centre minus squared radius, allowing negative values. Centre distance 6 lies between radius difference 2 and sum 8, giving two intersections independently of a sketch. Save a definition card and separate circles, axis, points and notes. Units are dimensionless. Distinct centres are necessary for the ensuing line result; concentric circles require separate treatment.

Subtract the equations to eliminate quadratic terms

Equate powers: x²+y²−25=(x−6)²+y²−9. Expand and cancel squares to obtain 12x=52, hence x=13/3. Retain intermediate constants, especially 36−9, rather than put the line at midpoint x=3. Unequal radii mean the radical axis need not bisect the centres, though it is perpendicular to their horizontal line. No y restriction remains, so this is a full vertical line, not yet circle intersections. Do not call every point circular or compare absolute powers instead of signed values.

circle 1: x^2+y^2=25
circle 2: (x-6)^2+y^2=9
equal powers: x^2+y^2-25=(x-6)^2+y^2-9
radical axis: 12*x=52 -> x=13/3
A=(13/3,2*sqrt(14)/3); B=(13/3,-2*sqrt(14)/3)
T=(13/3,0): both powers=-56/9
U=(13/3,3): both powers=25/9

Place circles and an axis window at equal scales

Use a suggested 2200×1800 canvas, screen origin (750,900) and 140 pixels per unit on both axes: X=750+140x, Y=900−140y. First radius is 700 pixels; second centre (1590,900), radius 420. Leave label margins. Put the radical axis at screen X≈1356.667 as a dashed displayed segment with continuation marks; the window does not restrict its domain. Without numeric fields, approximate using guides while retaining exact fractions. Dragging accuracy is not decimal theorem precision.

Substitute into a circle for two intersections

Substitute x=13/3 into the first circle: y²=25−169/9=56/9, giving A/B at ±2√14/3≈±2.494. They share x and reflect across the horizontal axis. Verify the second circle: (13/3−6)²+y²=25/9+56/9=9. Plot both points and a solid common chord AB, retaining dashed axis extensions. The overlap lens is not the radical axis. Keep radicals exact until placement; rounded screen points do not establish exact equations.

Test off-circle points on the equal-power line

At T=(13/3,0), both powers are −56/9, inside both circles but off their boundaries. At U=(13/3,3), both are 25/9, outside both. These controls show that equal power extends beyond the intersections and need not be zero. Distinguish T/U markers from A/B. Negative power is not negative distance, and positive 25/9 is not an ordinary distance either. Keep powers separate from length labels and never place those values as measured radius segments. At centre midpoint M=(3,0), powers are −16 and zero, directly excluding the perpendicular bisector as this radical axis. Keep M in a control panel and the main axis at 13/3. Record both powers: membership in circle two does not establish membership in circle one.

Retain concentric and identical-circle controls

On paper, move C to O while retaining radii 5 and 3: powers differ by 16 everywhere, so no equal-power point or arbitrary axis exists. With identical radii too, equality holds over the whole plane, not one unique line. Keep these in a separate note, without moving the main centres and retaining obsolete intersections. Distinct nonintersecting circles can still have a radical axis but no real common chord. Lack of intersections does not refute the definition; recompute changed configurations separately. For a separate C=(10,0) control with the same radii, subtraction gives x=29/5. Nonintersecting circles still give equal power 216/25 at (29/5,0), proving an axis without a real chord. Do not insert that value into the original six-unit-centre configuration without redrawing.

Check constants, scale and point categories first

An axis at x=3 calls for rechecking constants; missed intersections call for radicals and screen-Y subtraction. Elliptical-looking circles require equal axis scales. Never move centres or alter radii to accommodate wrong points. Repair one owned-copy fault, keep T/U controls and recompute both powers. Restore extensions if the axis became only a chord, and correct categories if every point was labelled an intersection. More colours cannot fix the concept, and ordinary layers maintain no automatic constraints.

Lighten guides if lines merge small without altering mathematical endpoints. Deliver subtraction, two-circle substitutions, T/U powers and concentric controls, not just overlapping circles.
