“It is good” gives a friend little basis for judging suitability and can lead straight into spoilers. Use an authorised sample already read in ManYue to write a short recommendation card describing audience, reading conditions and verified scope. You write the recommendation; the app is not assumed to rate material, generate content warnings or filter spoilers.

Define the recipient and spoiler boundary

Identify the recipient’s preference for short works, long serials or a chapter on a phone. Set a concrete volume/chapter spoiler boundary based on what they have read, rather than “the early part.” Choose an authorised passage you have completed and record edition, language and scope. A first-chapter card should not predict the ending’s quality. Share lawful source information and commentary instead of unauthorised comic files.

Open with a premise that avoids the turn

Describe the publicly established opening situation and reading interest in one or two sentences, omitting hidden identities, betrayals and outcomes. Check every fact against the recipient’s boundary. Even public marketing copy may exceed their chosen spoiler limit. Separate your inference from premise facts: sensing a mystery differs from naming a presumed mastermind. Keep enough opening context to explain suitability without compressing the chapter’s plot into the card.

Record layout demands that affect reading

Inspect dialogue density, spreads, tall sequences and detail size, recording concrete device-related demands. Say that this edition’s first chapter contains many spreads requiring phone zoom, not that every edition requires a tablet. Recheck full-page views before attributing a setting-dependent issue to layout. An absent feature in the sample is not absent from the whole work. Distinguish file quality from style: blurry small text in this copy does not prove deliberately difficult art. Suggest an authorised clear sample when appropriate.

Write neutral content notes

Mention only sample content useful to the recipient, such as explicit violence, difficult relationship conflict or dense technical vocabulary, without inventing an official rating. Avoid naming who is hurt or which relationship turns; broad categories with stated scope may suffice. Not seeing something does not establish its absence throughout the series or guarantee suitability. Mark unverified concerns and suggest reliable official information rather than substituting your tolerance for theirs. Content detection is not assumed to be an app feature.

Support personal preferences with two observations

Choose two recoverable, spoiler-light observations, such as action carrying information over several pages or subtle expression changes within a scene. Explain your preference without universalising it. Retain chapter and image references privately while keeping the shared card concise. Replace examples beyond the spoiler boundary with earlier ones. Time spent or completion percentage does not automatically prove quality: fast reading may reflect available time rather than tight pacing. Separate feeling from observable support.

Suggest a small first step

Suggest an authorised sample or starting chapter and one thing to notice, without requiring a full-series import or immediate catch-up. Give only verified main-story/spin-off ordering rather than inventing an official sequence. A device suggestion might be to inspect the complete page before zooming, not an unverified automatic mode. Exclude private credentials and protected download links: your NAS availability does not make it a public resource for the recipient.

Audit spoilers and facts

Check names, chapter scope and claims such as ongoing/completed status, omitting unstable claims without current reliable support. Read as someone who lacks later knowledge and remove hints that expose an answer, such as “watch that insignificant person.” Revisit both evidence locations to confirm the sample basis. Date a retained card and state reading scope; expand it explicitly later rather than silently converting a first-chapter impression into a whole-work judgement. Remove private paths and credentials before sharing.

Finish the card and leave room for feedback

The final card needs an opening premise, suitability conditions, two personal reasons, necessary content notes and a small first step, at a useful length for the recipient. A declined recommendation may reflect preference, device or energy rather than poor quality. Do not reveal the ending to persuade someone past their boundary. Use feedback to refine conditions and recognise insufficient reading, not to label different taste an error. Keep evidence references privately and the shared card concise so the recipient can choose independently.
