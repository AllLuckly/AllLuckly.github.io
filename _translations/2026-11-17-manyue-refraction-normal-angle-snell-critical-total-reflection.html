Before interpreting a ray bending at an interface, identify its incoming side and angular reference. Prepare original panels for both propagation directions and inspect normals, refraction angles and critical conditions in ManYue. Calculations use stipulated indices in ideal geometric optics; arrows do not represent in-app simulation or measured glass properties.

Draw interface, normal and propagation arrows

Externally draw a horizontal interface with air n=1.00 above and glass n=1.50 below. Add a vertical dashed normal at incidence. In the first panel a ray travels from air into glass. Stipulate one wavelength, homogeneous media, negligible absorption and fixed indices. Put glass-to-air on a second page and a ledger with counterexamples on a third. Use page markers and distinguish arrows rather than infer the incoming medium from color. If a source diagram lacks arrows, keep propagation direction unknown before assigning an answer.

Measure incidence from the normal, not the surface

Label first-panel incidence θ1=30° between incoming ray and normal. Its angle to the interface is 60°; these are not interchangeable inputs. Measure θ2 from the normal on the other side, not the canvas edge. Mentally rotating a tilted diagram upright leaves definitions unchanged and requires no actual source-file rotation. External angle measurements describe drawing approximation; stipulated 30° is not infinitely precise screenshot measurement.

Apply Snell’s relation before deciding the bend

Use n1 sinθ1=n2 sinθ2. Air to glass gives sinθ2=(1.00/1.50)×0.5=1/3, hence θ2≈19.471°. It is smaller than 30°, bending toward the normal; dividing the angle by 1.5 to get 20° is wrong. Separate sine values from angle values and check calculator degree/radian mode. The drawing is approximate; do not change n to fit a hand-drawn ray. Normal incidence gives both angles zero as an additional reference check.

n1*sin(theta1)=n2*sin(theta2)
air -> glass: sin(theta2)=1/3; theta2=19.4712206 deg
glass -> air: sin(theta_c)=2/3; theta_c=41.8103149 deg
glass -> air at 50 deg: required sin(theta2)=1.1490667 > 1

Find the critical-angle condition for reverse travel

Now let glass be incident and air transmitted, so n1>n2. Set θ2=90° to get sinθc=1.00/1.50=2/3 and θc≈41.810°. This higher-to-lower-index direction has the stated critical angle. Copying the process to air-to-glass demands a sine greater than one; it does not create the same air-side threshold. At the critical angle the refracted ray runs along the interface, a boundary case distinct from incidence above it. Write medium order with each calculation.

Check a fifty-degree ray that cannot be refracted normally

At glass-side incidence 50°, Snell’s relation requires sinθ2≈1.1491>1. No ordinary transmitted propagating ray exists in this model: total internal reflection occurs. The reflected ray stays in glass with angle 50° from the normal, not across the interface in air. At glass-side 30°, sinθ2=0.75 and θ2≈48.590°, so refraction still occurs. Separate below,at,above-critical rows instead of one thick arrow. This exercise does not analyze evanescent fields or thin-layer coupling.

Add a deliberately surface-referenced 50° example: its normal angle is 40°,below 41.810° for glass-to-air,so the previous total-reflection conclusion does not apply. The same printed 50 gives another result under a different reference; inspect its angle arc rather than memorize only the threshold.

Limit interpretation with speed, frequency and wavelength

A note can state v=c/n, giving glass phase speed two-thirds of vacuum speed here. Frequency remains unchanged across a stationary interface while wavelength follows speed; refraction does not necessarily lower frequency, and a blue drawing does not prove a color change. One stipulated wavelength’s n does not give equal indices for every color. Different material or wavelength needs new values and critical angle; 1.50 is not universal glass data. These are reading conditions, not requested speed measurements or a screen-based spectrum experiment.

Reverse the first path as a check: glass incidence 19.471° returns to air at 30°,different from glass incidence 30° producing 48.590°. Reversibility does not mean arbitrary equal input angles remain unchanged across both media. Keep enough calculation precision and round only the reported result.

Recheck arrows, angle arcs and ledger in ManYue

Import the PDF, inspect the air-to-glass arrow and 30° arc, then reversed media and the three-row ledger. Bookmark critical conditions and revisit both indices, not only 41.81. A ray away from the normal labelled 19.47 calls for repairing the external diagram; 20 suggests dividing angles; air-side 50° called total reflection suggests lost high-to-low conditions. Change one item and keep the faulty version. Unperformed reading/layout operations cannot be recorded as an observed in-app optical effect.

Reopen and deliver the boundary plus counterexamples

Reopen the bookmark and restate air-to-glass 30°→19.471°, glass-to-air critical 41.810° and total reflection at glass-side 50°, with glass-side 30° still refracting. Check normal-referenced angles, reflection on the incident side and rounding that preserves inequalities. Deliver panels, medium order,calculator units and observations, leaving unperformed reopening pending. Curved interfaces, inhomogeneous or strongly absorbing media require renewed modeling rather than this plane-interface exercise as a universal proof.
