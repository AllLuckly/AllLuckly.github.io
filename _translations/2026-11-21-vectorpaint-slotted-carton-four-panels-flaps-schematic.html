Draw a simplified slotted-carton blank: four sides wrap into the box, a side joint connects the ends, and flaps extend above and below. FEFCO's 0201 material shows the side strip and upper and lower flaps. This exercise uses that structural relationship with original proportions and labels to practice vectors; it is not a production die line, load rating or shipping-compliance instruction.

Plan a paper model and scope

On paper draw a strip of four panels labeled “Side A, Side B, Side A, Side B,” with a narrow joint tab at the left; connect one flap above and below each panel. Trace folds with a finger and check that the joint connects the ends rather than being a fifth side. Create a landscape canvas with room for blank, legend and explanation. Separate sides, flaps, joint, lines and text in layers and save an editable draft. A paper model helps explain relationships, but cutting or gluing is not required.

Build a continuous four-panel strip

Draw four equal-height rectangles left to right with alternating widths, matching the paper model. Adjacent panels share a vertical boundary rather than a wide white gap. Temporarily use two light colors for A and B and label each. Align upper and lower edges despite the color difference. If the installed version has align, duplicate or distribute controls, use those actually visible; otherwise inspect with guides and zoom. Widths here are illustrative and cannot establish manufacturing dimensions.

Add the joint tab and keep its identity

Attach a narrower tab to the far left labeled “joint tab.” One long edge meets the first panel and its outer edge is the blank boundary. Do not leave a gap or call it another A side. Once folded, the final panel joins this tab, but the two are not adjacent in the flat layout. Use a separate arrow or legend to explain the end-to-end relation; an explanatory arrow is not a cut line and should not cross other labels.

Attach upper and lower flaps individually

Extend one flap above and one below each of the four sides, sharing a boundary with its own side. Keep separations between neighboring flaps legible instead of drawing a single upper sheet. Number them “upper 1–4” and “lower 1–4,” matching the side immediately below or above. Flap proportions are simplified; actual carton style, overlap and material thickness require professional specifications and cannot be cut directly from this drawing. Check that every flap traces to its side.

Distinguish outline, slots and fold lines

On a separate line layer use solid lines for the schematic outline and slots between flaps, and dashed lines for folds between sides and between each side and flap. Mark the joint-tab boundary as a fold as well. Do not extend a fold through a gap intended as a slot. Label the legend “solid: schematic cut/outline” and “dashed: schematic fold,” rather than relying solely on color. These are conceptual marks without tooling, crease widths or production tolerances.

Check ends and all eight flaps

From the tab, count A, B, A, B across the side strip, then count four upper and four lower flaps. Make a small table mapping each side to its upper and lower numbers so copying cannot silently omit or swap one. Hide temporary colors and inspect solid lines, dashes and labels alone; if folds depend on color, strengthen the line-pattern and text distinction. Restore colors and ensure fills do not cover lines.

Add a small assembled relationship sketch

Beside the blank draw a simple open box using connected quadrilaterals for front, side and interior and a few flap outlines to suggest the opening. Reuse A and B labels to associate it with the blank without claiming precision perspective projection. If front and side fail to connect, adjust their shared edge and overlap. The small assembled view is not a second production blank. Keep it clear of the tab and fold-line explanations on the flat drawing.

Spot-check fold endpoints

Magnify the junction of the upper flaps on sides one and two. Trace the dashed side-to-side fold upward: it should stop at the body edge, while the separation between the flaps is a separate schematic slot, not a fold continued to the outer boundary. Check the corresponding lower location uses the same legend. If a dashed line looks solid after export, zoom and inspect dash spacing and export resolution before checking format; color alone is not a substitute for line pattern. Hide auxiliary numbering copies before saving, while retaining the actual labels.

Export and inspect editable structure

Save the layered draft, export a separate image through a control actually offered in your version and open it elsewhere to inspect four sides, eight flaps, legend, labels and frame. If SVG export is available, save and reopen a copy and try selecting one flap and one fold line. Record merged or converted paths and text rather than inferring editability from appearance. Engineering use, dimensions and materials have not been validated; actual packaging requires qualified design and production against specifications. Mark unperformed reopening or device work pending.
