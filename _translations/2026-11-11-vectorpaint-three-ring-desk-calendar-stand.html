Draw an invented three-ring desk calendar labelled Practice A rather than a real month. Check how rings pass through paper, how pages turn over the top and how the stand reaches the surface. Arrange groups and occlusion manually in VectorPaint without page-turn simulation or printing/punch specifications. Apparent support does not prove physical stability.

Establish the stand before pages

Use a landscape view with pages facing slightly front-left and a triangular stand visible on the right. Mark top ridge, front foot and rear foot in one supporting arrangement rather than floating steps. A page width of 600 and stand height of 400 are drawing choices, not centimetres. Check side triangle and frontal width before adding paper; do not turn one face frontal to expose labels. Keep titles outside and geometric references independent of text centres. Group pages and stand separately so hiding paper still reveals a coherent support.

Place three hole groups on one paper plane

Place three holes along the page top with side margins. The middle hole follows that edge’s midpoint relation, not necessarily the screen’s horizontal centre. Small elliptical openings share the page perspective. Number them 1–3 and retain paper around each instead of cutting through the top edge. Check duplicated placements individually; duplication does not automatically give perspective spacing. Three is this design’s count, not a universal calendar rule. Thin rivet-like rims and clarify openings. Repair a hole reaching the edge through its contour rather than darkening it.

Thread each ring through its own location

Assign one thin ring to each numbered hole with traceable front/back arcs. Sketch complete rings first, then occlude the portion behind paper rather than showing all metal through it. Make the local passage readable without floating the whole ring in front. Handle-like rings may never enter their holes; connections to neighbouring holes need numbered repair. Rings may share a ridge region but cannot become one long line through all three holes while retaining a three-ring claim. Add reflections only after establishing each ring’s identity.

Keep front and turned pages on the same rings

An optional page turned behind still uses the original three rings, not another invented row. Let the front page conceal lower rear paper, exposing only enough top edge; do not make the stand transparent to show everything. Hide the stand to inspect paper/rings, then restore occlusion. Reconcile hole rows and shared ridge if two separate books appear, or add thin page layers if it looks like a bent board. This stationary pose does not establish collision-free real motion. Mark concealed rear portions only in the sketch.

Distinguish paper layers from support thickness

Use warm-white paper with two or three pale layer edges and a slightly thicker grey-blue stand, not identical heavy borders. Upper-left light gives narrow metal reflections without white outlines on every paper edge. Place the Practice A label on the page plane, omitting dates/weekdays to avoid unverified calendar information. Thin plastic-box-like page walls and glare; establish thickness and feet for a shadow-like stand. Decoration is optional and cannot conceal ring errors. Edges and values suggest materials without proving paper weight, metal specification or printing results.

Test pairing with a displaced middle ring

In a separate copy shift only the middle ring between holes 2 and 3. Three rings remain, but the second hole is no longer threaded. Diagnose the broken pairing rather than counting bright loops. Restore and trace all three, checking that the page was not accidentally moved. Hide rear paper to reject a shadow masquerading as a fourth ring, without simultaneously changing viewpoint. Preserve the correct original and label the control. Move vector objects only; real disassembly, paper tearing and ring-fastener tests are outside this exercise.

Inspect small openings and stand contact in output

Save and inspect PNG three holes/rings, rear top edge and both feet at use size. Increase presentation size or contrast for lost openings rather than enlarging them through page edges. Missing highlights do not necessarily break the ring; inspect structure with reflections hidden. Hide unwanted numbers and manual axes, which otherwise export as objects. Check holes on light/dark backgrounds; white patches are not automatically transparent. Open other formats separately, isolating occlusion/path problems in a missing ring arc rather than redrawing the whole page or overwriting editable source.

Troubleshoot support, hole rows and ring arcs

For a skewed stand revisit shared perspective; for crossed pairings use numbers; for a separate-book rear page inspect ridge identity; for floating paper inspect feet/contact shadow. Recheck all three groups after each local edit: count does not establish pairing. Retain bare stand, hole row, complete-ring sketch, occluded version and control output for reconstruction. This is a calendar appearance study, not automatic binding, date generation or real stability verification, and it establishes no universal hole spacing or movement range.
