In a mathematics ebook, one bracket or number-line endpoint can change a set. This lesson addresses no real deadlines, medical values or investment thresholds. Use self-made real-number intervals to check the book’s convention, notation, endpoint membership and drawing. Book Isle supports reading, location and actually available note functions; the reader checks examples manually. No inequality solver or formula validator is assumed.

Record the author’s notation first

Open the interval-definition page and record book, edition, chapter, position and the stated convention. A common convention uses square brackets to include endpoints and parentheses to exclude them, but some textbooks use different open-interval notation. Read this book before rewriting from habit. In external notes, create four columns: notation, verbal condition, endpoint tests and number line. For the practice exercise, explicitly use square brackets for inclusion and parentheses for exclusion, with a real-valued variable. Do not mix integer solutions with continuous intervals when the real-number domain is unspecified; that premise affects the meaning of intermediate points.

Make a main example with different endpoint types

In an external document, write the interval with a left square bracket, two, comma, five and a right parenthesis: [2,5). It means two is less than or equal to the variable and the variable is strictly less than five; both conditions must hold. Use one consistent mathematics font for both endpoint symbols in the actual practice file; the Chinese prose version merely uses its text punctuation. Retain the editable original and export a readable PDF or EPUB. The comma does not indicate two isolated solutions, and the right endpoint five is not included. An asymmetric example makes habitual copying errors easier to notice.

Test endpoints and points inside and outside

Test two, five, three and one separately. Two meets the included lower endpoint and is below five, so it belongs. Five fails the strict upper inequality, so it does not. Three lies inside and belongs; one is below two and does not. For each test, record both conditions rather than only right or wrong. Including two does not include all smaller numbers. Excluding five does not exclude every number close to five; four point nine can illustrate this without endless enumeration. Test points help interpret the definition; four examples do not by themselves prove a statement about every real number.

Align the number-line endpoints and extent

Draw a number line externally, with a filled dot at two, an open dot at five and only the region between them marked. Filled or open indicates endpoint membership, not relative importance. Confirm that values increase left to right, avoiding confusion from a rotated or cropped scan. If colour marks the interval, also inspect line segments, arrows or wording; redness alone does not establish inclusion. If low resolution obscures whether a dot is open, return to notation and definition and explicitly leave the drawing detail unconfirmed. A blurred filled-looking dot is not a mathematical conclusion.

Do not treat infinity as an included real endpoint

Next represent a variable greater than or equal to two with an included lower endpoint and extension toward positive infinity. Under this real-number convention, positive infinity is not a real endpoint that can be substituted. Do not use a square bracket to include it; show unbounded extension with a right-pointing arrow. Do not draw a filled infinity point, or read no upper bound as every number qualifies: negative numbers still fail the condition. The same principle applies at negative infinity, with direction determined by the actual inequality rather than copying one arrow whenever infinity appears.

Distinguish union from simultaneous conditions

For an extension, take the union of an interval from two included to three excluded and one from four excluded to five included. Record two included, three excluded, four excluded and five included; the gap between three and four is not covered. Union means membership in either part, not simultaneous membership in both. This differs from the main example’s lower and upper bounds holding together. Do not merge the disjoint intervals into one continuous span from two to five. If the book introduces intersection, copy its symbol and explanation before comparing them instead of replacing similar-looking symbols from memory.

Check the source page before changing a definition

If a prose inequality conflicts with the drawn endpoint, confirm example number, variable domain and edition, and check that the drawing does not belong to the next exercise. If zoom cannot resolve a bracket, consult a clearer authorized edition or author errata without presenting a guess as a confirmed correction. Reflow, clipped scans or missing glyphs may affect visible symbols, but do not diagnose a particular cause without evidence. Record both source locations and the disagreement, leaving it pending. Do not silently choose an inclusion rule merely to continue calculating.

Rebuild the drawing from notation after returning

Leave the reader and return. Look only at the main interval and reconstruct its endpoints and marked region by hand, then compare with the source drawing and notes. Recheck inclusion of two and three, exclusion of five and one, and the convention against including infinity as an endpoint. Preserve variable domain, bracket rule, bound relations and unresolved typography in the summary. Completion means agreement among notation, wording, test points and drawing, not merely memorizing left-closed, right-open. No app solver has been used and no real high-stakes threshold is inferred.
