An octopus arm may curl intricately, but its suckers still belong to one continuous soft surface. Draw one simplified arm with a modest body attachment rather than filling the image with eight. Establish root-to-tip continuity, identify the visible sucker-bearing surface, then add selected openings. This is not a complete species or anatomical reconstruction.

Establish a tapering root-to-tip axis

On an approximately 1600×1600 px canvas, lead a broad lower-left root upward into a curl and narrowing tip. Mark axis and width before layers of body, suckers and detail. Preserve a gap in the curl. Repair pipe-like uniform thickness through taper and root volume before adding suckers.

Mark where the sucker-bearing surface faces

Choose side-facing root, viewer-facing middle and turning-away tip. Mark the sucker surface as a band narrowing at turns, not a constant-width decoration. A temporary different grey can help trace its continuity. Establish that surface before placing suckers wherever convenient.

Model the soft mass under one light

Use muted red-ochre, warm middle grey and violet-brown shadow under upper-left light, with a slightly lighter sucker surface. Judge lighting separately from inner-bend occlusion. Restore broad turns to a flat band or soften hard reflections on a metal tube, letting the form carry movement without busy underwater decoration.

Place a few suckers along the surface

Begin with a few paired middle-arm suckers, shrinking with the arm. Use rounder frontal openings, narrower side ellipses and partial hidden edges. Establish placement and orientation before interiors. Correct button-like forms through attachment, thickness and surface turns rather than repeating identical dots along the whole arm.

Give each opening a rim and shallow interior

Simplify each to a shallow ring and interior darker than its surroundings, not pure black. Resolve one enlarged example with aligned ellipses and simplify farther ones. Reduce protrusion when they resemble cups, or restore a narrow lip to flat holes. These cues explain attachment and concavity without claiming complete anatomy.

Clarify depth where the curl crosses

Interrupt lower contours and suckers beneath the returning arm. Test overlap in flat colour before small contact shadows. Trace root to tip if it seems like two arms, or temporarily colour the axis to diagnose impossible crossings. Flexibility does not make every crossing coherent; repair one problem without disturbing correct sections.

Keep skin texture subordinate

Use a few subdued patches or folds following local curvature, quieter than the sucker structure. Hide and reduce texture that fragments taper. Avoid unnecessary saturated blue outlines. Continuity, volume and surface orientation carry the soft-body identity; distant spots can be omitted without losing it.

Compare three sucker orientations

Study frontal, oblique and edge-view suckers at one size and palette, changing ellipse and visible inner wall. Go beyond horizontal squeezing by varying near/far walls. Apply the rule selectively: the point is reliable simplification, allowing some main-image suckers to become short arcs rather than fully rendered rings.

Judge the curl through its negative space

Inspect the curl’s background opening, varying it with taper and bend rather than a perfect ring. Move a segment when contours merge instead of fading it. Review gesture small, sucker-surface continuity normally and tiny errors close up. Correct reversed scale before texture and retain copies to protect the overall path during local edits.

Keep the plain arm and reverse the curl

Keep axis, plain mass and finished versions with separate surface detail. Next, reverse only the tip curl and rebuild which sucker surfaces remain visible under unchanged root and light. Do not paste the old rows onto the new curve. Record a round opening becoming an edge, retaining the stated limits of a simplified anatomical illustration.
