Draw a retractable tape with its case on the left, a short blade extending gently rightward and a metal hook bent down at its end. Concentrate on mouth occlusion, two edges of the curved blade and the hook’s turn. Numbers are illustrative marks, not a calibrated measuring drawing.

Reserve separate space for case and extended blade

Create an approximately 2000×1300 px landscape canvas. Give the left case about 600 px of width and the blade about 1000 px of rightward extension with an end margin. Block a rounded solid rather than a perfect circle, revealing a little top and right side. Separate sketch, case, blade and graduations, then save native work. The 600/1000 dimensions control composition, not case size, range or blade strength. At reduced size the blade must emerge from this case rather than lie beside it as a separate ruler, and both share one perspective.

Let the mouth surround the blade root

Place a short narrow mouth near the case’s lower-right region. Let the blade emerge from inside, with the near lip covering a little of its root. Sketch the full blade first and remove concealed portions rather than leave a large background break. Darkness in the mouth indicates occlusion, not a black blade. If the chosen design has a front-upper lock, attach a small block to the case, not midway along the blade or as a second exit. Internal spring and coil remain concealed because no cutaway is shown.

Use both blade edges to describe one curved strip

Draw two continuous edges rightward from the mouth with readable width. A slight sag must affect both edges rather than bend one beside a straight other. Use pale yellow with darker sides to suggest a transversely curved section. This specifies no mechanical curvature or supported reach. If showing a small twist, turn both edges consistently; do not swap them without explaining the crossing. Distinguish the blade’s own shadowed surface from its shadow on the table instead of merging both into one outline.

Attach the bent hook at the blade end

Place a short metal plate against the blade’s far end and bend its front downward, giving a readable L-shaped side. Rivet marks stay inside blade width. The reference model shows two rivets, so use two illustrative dots here without generalizing their count. The hook is not a separate key ring dangling from the tip. Retain inner and outer bend edges rather than a thickness-free right angle. Some end hooks slide slightly to compensate thickness; select one static position without depicting mandatory gluing or claiming an accuracy test.

Align graduations to the blade and state illustrative units

Plan illustrative centimetre marks 0,5,10 on a straighter section, with zero at the hook and 5/10 toward the case, with equal 0–5 and 5–10 intervals before perspective, then arrange them in the chosen view. Short marks run across blade width and turn with its surface, not independently upright on table axes. Use only a few numbers instead of fabricated dense graduations. Check zero against the hook rather than arbitrarily starting at the case edge. The feet-based reference informs structure only; its units are not copied. Canvas lengths do not measure physical millimetres, and texture cannot conceal wrong numerals.

Separate case, blade and hook through reflections

Give the case broad soft light planes in dark grey or chosen two-tone colours, the yellow blade a lengthwise reflection and the hook narrow cool-grey edges. Keep case and blade shadows consistent on one table without filling the entire area below the blade with a huge black wedge. Hide graduations and check blade volume before adding numbers. Branding is unnecessary: a circular case label can remain blank. Case colour establishes neither range, locking mechanism nor calibration class.

Trace mouth to hook for breaks and numerical errors

Hide highlights, trace the near edge from mouth to hook and return along the far edge, checking that graduations have not visually cut the blade apart. If hook direction contradicts its label, correct one and review the whole. Restore plate overlap if rivets float in the background. For confused 0/5/10 spacing revisit the perspective plan rather than call drawing error a product defect. Preserve copies and compare graduations on/off after each repair. A static image cannot establish whether the lock grips the blade; no operating conclusion is supplied.

Reopen for continuity and readable markings

Save native work, hide axes and helper points, then reopen a PNG. At fit-to-window size read one continuous blade from case to hook; enlarge mouth overlap, two rivet marks and hook bend. Three clear numbers suffice; more graduations do not define success. Reopen separate graduation and blade-colour layers so number edits do not erase the surface. Document illustrative centimetres, uncalibrated drawing and concealed internals, leaving reopening pending if unperformed. Saving an image is not physical measurement or tool certification.
