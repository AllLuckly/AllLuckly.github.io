A passage reporting someone’s report of another person can tempt readers to attribute the innermost words to the outer author. In Book Isle, map who writes, reports and is represented as speaking, with each layer’s support. This is not another catalogue-search workflow, and correctly parsing quotation marks does not verify that historical words were spoken verbatim. Attribution and source validation differ.

Choose a short, contextualised quotation chain

Choose two or three layers from authorised completed material and record edition, chapter, passage and introductory/following explanation. Avoid a whole chapter or many unnamed speakers initially. Record quotation marks, indentation, italics and indirect report without assuming only marks indicate another voice. Check closure across pages before copying and never invent an ending for an incomplete excerpt. Preserve sufficient context before shortening notes so attribution remains visible.

Map three roles with an invented example

Use an invented case: author A writes that B’s memoir reports C saying this discussion concerns only sample one. A is the current author, B the reported intermediary and C the alleged original speaker; A did not necessarily hear C. Put each role on a separate line and state the innermost proposition’s scope. Labels name no real people. Without a recording or C’s direct text, the example establishes A’s representation of B’s report, not verified verbatim speech by C.

Find reporting verbs and qualifications at each layer

Track verbs such as writes, recalls, allegedly, doubts and denies to distinguish endorsement from reporting or distance. A’s quotation does not imply agreement, nor does B’s report certify memory. Preserve possibility, paraphrase and time qualifications. “B denies that C said it” cannot become “C said it.” At each layer, state who presents what with which stance, leaving unspecified stance unknown rather than inserting your own approval or dislike.

Resolve pronouns and demonstratives within their layers

Words such as this time, here and we may belong to the inner speaking situation rather than the outer author’s writing. The example’s discussion scope belongs to the reported C event, not all A’s research. Check context for ambiguous pronouns and retain alternatives where unresolved. Nearest-name matching is insufficient when quotation changes the reference centre. You may insert role labels in paraphrase, explicitly personal rather than quoted additions to the original.

Distinguish direct quotation, paraphrase and second-hand report

Quotation formatting describes presentation, not recording-level accuracy; retain explicit paraphrase or translation labels. If you inspected only A, say A reports B reporting C rather than pretending to have read B or C. Source verification can follow lawful access separately, with actual access limits and unverified gaps. A clean attribution chain can still contain memory error or rewriting. Record a translation layer too; fluent wording does not establish identical original speech.

Preserve attribution and negation when shortening

A shortened note needs outer source, inner proposition, reporting relation and qualifications. Do not shorten the example to “A claims every discussion is limited to sample one”; retain A reporting B’s account of C’s discussion scope. A three-role chain plus proposition can be concise. Ellipses must not remove negation, conditions or stance; separate interpretation. If a quotation card cannot accommodate the chain, reduce scope or add explanation rather than turning second-hand material into an attractive direct aphorism.

Revisit the passage to check boundaries and layers

Keep supported references or an external locator for the outer beginning, inner statement and ending, then leave the reader and find the passage again from the record, confirming that you have not landed on another utterance by the same person. Check quotation marks, indentation and negation after copying/export. Explain attribution where styling is lost without claiming to repair the original file. Recheck reporting verbs and scope across editions or translations rather than transferring by topic alone. Record necessary public source details, not private accounts or an attached entire ebook.

Finish attribution and evidence-boundary checks

Finish by identifying current author, reporting chain, proposition scope and actually inspected layers. Citation is not endorsement without contextual support, and mapped layers may remain second-hand rather than verified. “This time” and “only” constrain the claim. Leave ambiguous identities or pronouns unresolved instead of filling them with familiar names. Separate source presentation, personal paraphrase and verified-access scope to produce a recoverable record within the evidence.
