A half-position volume control is not a file peak of minus six decibels or half the perceived loudness. Define a digital peak reference, calculate two level cards and record actual iCouldPlay conditions with an owned clip. External arithmetic/measurements are not an assumed in-player analyzer, and impressions are not calibrated sound-pressure or loudness measurements.

1. Separate file amplitude from output conditions

Choose a short owned video and record identity, track, device, output route and visible volume position at a comfortable fixed level. Preserve the original. Attribute an existing external peak measurement if available; otherwise calculate the paper cards without claiming the file was measured. Output devices/system processing affect sound, so matching control positions do not establish equal output. Mark unavailable analysis fields rather than inventing an iCouldPlay panel.

2. Define one sample-peak full-scale reference

Use normalized sample peaks with full-scale peak one, P=0.5 and Q=0.25, taking maximum absolute sample amplitude. Do not mix mean, RMS or reconstructed true-peak measures into this field, nor infer amplitude from video brightness. The stated formula defines this exercise’s peak convention and requires a positive ratio; zero is not a finite logarithm input. Fix the reference before comparing dB values.

Reference peak: 1.0
P sample peak: 0.5
Q sample peak: 0.25
Sample-peak level (dBFS) = 20 * log10(peak / 1.0)

3. Calculate both cards and their difference

Using a base-ten logarithm calculator or approximation gives P≈−6.0206 dBFS and Q≈−12.0412 dBFS. Q’s linear peak is half P’s, with a difference about −6.0206 dB, not minus fifty-percent decibels. Consult Audacity’s amplitude/dB explanation and retain formula/rounding. These are stipulated cards, not measurements of a named film without separate evidence.

4. Do not infer equal loudness from equal peaks

Consider one clip with a brief peak and another sustaining higher amplitude longer. Equal maximum samples alone do not establish equal perceived loudness. Frequency, duration, dynamics and output conditions can differ; this lesson computes neither loudness units nor sound-pressure level. More negative level values do not mean slower sound: they have no seconds unit. Keep peak, whole-clip impression and distortion separate, leaving unknowns unresolved.

5. Listen to the same passage with conditions fixed

Select the owned clip and correct track in iCouldPlay, then play from an identifiable anchor. Record intelligibility rather than announcing an exact dB value. Compare an already prepared lower-level copy if available, fixing speed/output/control position. Without a copy, do not change multiple enhancement settings. A heard difference does not prove halved sample peak. Replay the same passage rather than mistaking different dialogue intensity for a changed control; retain originals/defaults.

6. Distinguish playback control from file modification

A playback volume change is an output condition, not proof that the source file’s peak was rewritten. Audacity’s meter documentation distinguishes scales and output controls. Applying gain to a copy externally is a separate processing/save/identity/measurement step. Do not fill that field from a player-control observation. Restore the prior control position after comparing rather than silently reusing it as the baseline for different material.

7. Troubleshoot signs, references and measurement type

Positive six for 0.5 suggests an inverted ratio. A three-dB difference suggests a power formula or different measure. For conflicting tool readings, identify sample peak, RMS, true peak or output level before declaring a fault. A volume percentage cannot become this amplitude ratio without an established calibration. Change one condition and preserve readings/provenance. Record no audible difference honestly rather than raising volume to force a conclusion or clearing media.

8. Reopen and deliver three evidence layers

Leave playback, reopen the clip and verify track/output/prior volume before listening to the same anchor. Deliver paper calculations, attributed external measurements and actual playback as three layers, marking unperformed measurements. Do not equate −6 dBFS with half loudness, control percentages with digital amplitude or impressions with calibrated pressure. Preserve files and start a fresh device record. The result is distinguishable evidence, not professional metering certification for the app.
