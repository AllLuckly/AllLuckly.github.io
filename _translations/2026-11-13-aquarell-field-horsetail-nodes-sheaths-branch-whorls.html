Horsetail’s green side branches can resemble needles, yet have their own joints and hierarchy. Use the vegetative shoot of Equisetum arvense for an invented pose in Aquarell. Focus on main-stem nodes, sheaths and whorled branches without mixing in a separate brown fertile shoot or treating the exercise as foraging, growing or field identification guidance.

Choose a green vegetative shoot and its axis

Use an approximately 1400×1900 px portrait canvas, pale beige-grey background and a main axis rising from below with a slight right bend. Block the whole envelope and continuous stem before tiny branches. Separate sketch, main stem, sheaths, near/far branches and guides in mid-green. Choose the green branched vegetative state without adding a brown fertile cone from another stage. Remove a woody trunk and flat needle bundles if conifer-like; separate nodes if brush-like. Height, bend and displayed levels are invented, not age or growth-rate evidence.

Keep internodes within one continuous stem

Place several nodes along the axis, separated by longer green internodes with modest longitudinal grooves. Nodes are not isolated beads: the main stem remains continuous. Vary thickness gently before short dark nodal bands instead of identical broad black rings. Reduce exaggerated end faces if bamboo-cylinder-like and soften grooves read as cracks. Spacing is illustrative, not necessarily equal; drawn grooves are not a measured ridge count, and hidden sides need no complete striping.

Wrap sheaths around the stem rather than adding blades

Wrap a short sheath around each nodal region, with a few tiny tooth-like tips if visible, and branch from that region. Reduced leaves form the sheath; do not add broad lance-shaped leaves and relabel them branches. Enlarge one node privately to separate central stem, short outer sheath and emerging branches. Add near rim and far occlusion if collar-floating, and shorten flower-crown-like teeth. Keep the enlarged check separate, not another plant attached to the main illustration, and infer no internal anatomy from it.

Spread each branch whorl from one nodal level

Arrange slender branches around a chosen node, with near branches spreading laterally/forward and far ones partly hidden by the stem. Do not distribute parallel bristles anywhere along an internode or reduce every whorl to two flat rows. Place quiet far branches, then stem and near branches, tracing each root. Simplify counts for readability without a universal number per node. Bring scattered roots back to the nodal region and occlude far branches rather than showing them through the stem. A whorl need not expose every branch or have equal projected lengths.

Keep side branches distinct from pine-needle bundles

Suggest joints lightly on two or three near branches, simplifying others by distance rather than adding dense black ticks. These are green branches, not a three-needle pine bundle sharing a fascicle sheath; do not import that earlier counting rule. Taper tips with modest drooping, not every tip converging into an inverted umbrella. Lighten rope-net-like branches and separate local gaps. Omit underground rhizomes and do not turn the stem upside down because branches resemble roots. Counts establish neither reproduction nor wild-plant management instructions.

Separate cylindrical stem and fine branches tonally

Shade one side of the cylindrical main stem with a narrow light side, wrapping nodal bands around it rather than flat black stripes. Keep near branches clearer than far ones without whitening all distant growth. Verify nodes and whorls in a neutral draft before details. Soften bands that sever the stem visually and restore its edge. Use quiet background rather than grass hiding roots. Green indicates the selected vegetative form, not edibility or harmlessness. Missing flowers are not a software omission: the reference plant reproduces by spores.

Inspect branch roots and sheaths level by level

At roughly quarter size, work upward: identify node, short sheath, then branch roots. Repair a branch that would need to cross a neighbouring node to connect. Reduce rear contrast if sheaths become black knots rather than deleting the stem. Hide temporary level numbers instead of exporting them as natural markings. Recheck that the top has not acquired a brown fertile-shoot form. Depict one state, recording omissions where necessary; obscured reference branches must not be filled in from a counting template.

Reopen the export and retain the hierarchy record

Keep the native layered work and reopen PNG to check continuous stem, nodal sheaths, branch whorls and near/far occlusion. Enlarge or separate selected illustrative branches if brush-like at small size, retaining whorls instead of turning it into another unbranched form. Fix layer order for branches showing through the stem, without hiding errors through transparency. Record a green field-horsetail vegetative reference with invented count, pose and omissions. Success concerns hierarchy, not identification of every horsetail or claimed field measurement/device testing.
