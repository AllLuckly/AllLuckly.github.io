Peas in a pod are not green beads suspended on a line: the pod walls need thickness and containing space. Draw a simplified opened pea pod with five visible peas as a compositional choice, not a universal natural count. Organise two valves, concave inner surfaces and pea occlusion manually in Aquarell. Focus on containment and proximity to the seam without shelling instruction or food-quality assessment.

Establish the long pod’s endpoints and axis

Use an approximately 1900×1300 px landscape canvas with grey-cream background. Curve the pod slightly lower left to upper right, short stalk cue left and tapered tip right. Establish an elongated envelope and axis with a broader middle, not a straight equal-width tube. Separate sketch, rear valve, peas, front valve and shadow. Without peas, check both walls belong to one pod. Reconnect shared ends and seam regions if detached-leaf-like; soften excessive symmetry if boat-like. Proportions are illustrative, not cultivar dimensions or identification.

Give both valves concave inner surfaces

Expose the rear valve higher and front lower, distinguishing pale yellow-green interior from deeper-green exterior. Give rims modest fleshy thinness, neither wooden frames nor a black-filled cavity. Establish broad valve planes, then turning edges; any exposed outer patch must follow an actual turn. Add inner curvature and slight rim depth if paper-flat or reduce uniform black edges and white glare if plastic-box-like. Opening width is compositional. Do not break ends merely to expose all five peas; maintain the enclosing form.

Place five peas prior to modelling spheres

Place five temporary numbered circles along the curved interior axis, middle slightly larger and ends modestly smaller rather than identical equal spacing. Keep every centre within containing walls. Count five, then allow close contact or slight overlap without filling the pod with copied dots. Restore containment if grape-cluster-like; remove a dark through-line if necklace-like. Account for hidden portions in the sketch and do not add a sixth bright sphere while retaining a five-pea description. The stated count and visible evidence must agree.

Avoid a rod passing through the peas

Suggest proximity to the seam with very short quiet attachment cues, never a rigid rod through five sphere centres. Establish each pea’s relation to the inner wall, adding details only if useful. Omit invisible fine connections from the finish and retain locations in the sketch. Repair contact shade and front-wall occlusion if floating rather than adding long stems. Remove a through-rod if screw-chain-like. This simplified exterior does not expose hidden placental structures or turn peas into berries growing from an outer leaf surface.

Occlude lower pea edges without slicing spheres

Let the front wall hide parts of lower pea edges while exposed surfaces stay rounded, not identical semicircles cut by one horizontal line. Keep rear-wall texture behind peas. Hide front wall to inspect five complete spheres, then restore varied occlusion along the axis. Reduce outlined rims and adjust depth if button-tray-like. Remove accidental flat faces if peas appear cut open. Full exposure of every pea is unnecessary. Establish sphere volume and pod space separately, then combine them instead of relying solely on a row of highlights.

Separate similar greens through value and temperature

Light upper left. Use moderately vivid but muted mid-green peas with soft highlights and grey-green shade; make interiors yellower and exterior cooler/deeper. Separate adjacent greens locally rather than with black outlines. Reduce sharp white sphere dots if toy-like or restore local green and soft rounding if pebble-like. Add only two or three quiet valve veins, not lines across peas or ring seats beneath every one. Palette choices do not indicate ripeness, nutrition or edibility; these checks concern visible form.

Audit pea count separately from wall continuity

Keep an untextured sketch. Count peas one through five, then trace both valves from stalk to tip without breaks introduced to expose seeds. Correct pea count does not establish correct walls, and attractive walls cannot excuse a sixth pea. Change one position or front-rim segment with light and palette fixed. A closed-pod variant should suggest contents through exterior bulges, not expose five spheres through skin. Save it separately with revised visibility checks rather than merely erasing inner highlights and declaring closure.

Keep useful pea-to-rim separations in the output

Save the layered original and inspect five peas, two valve rims and both ends in a PNG at use size. Separate local values and neighbouring contours if sausage-like rather than heavily outlining every pea. Lighten a hollow-looking interior while retaining front-wall thickness. Record numbered faults such as rear texture crossing pea two. Count is an original exercise choice; sources constrain basic pea/pod identity. New counts or opening angles need new occlusion. Judge containment, row arrangement and continuous walls, not complete botanical anatomy or food inspection.
