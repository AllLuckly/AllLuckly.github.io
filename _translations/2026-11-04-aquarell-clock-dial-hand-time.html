Numerals in a clock illustration are more than decoration, and the hands must show one consistent time. This study draws a small front-facing clock, using 10:10 as its checkable example and twelve positions to establish the dial. All marks and angles are placed manually; Aquarell is not assumed to provide an automatic clock component. Make the time readable first, then add shell thickness and a few reflections to complete an object illustration whose information can be checked.

Establish a front-facing dial and centre

Use an approximately 1500×1500 px canvas with a pale-grey background. Draw a roughly circular shell and a slightly smaller dial. Mark the centre, then faint horizontal and vertical axes meeting at that same point. Separate background, guides, dial, numerals and hands into layers. Keep this first view frontal so ellipse perspective and time placement do not have to be solved together. The circle may retain hand-drawn variation, but its centre must not drift sideways. Correct a crooked circle or axes before thickening the frame to conceal an off-centre structure.

Build twelve positions from four main directions

Place 12, 3, 6 and 9 first, then divide each quarter arc into three intervals to find the remaining hour positions. Keep marks at approximately the same radius from the centre, judging gaps around the circumference rather than only their horizontal distances. Check a complete circuit of twelve provisional dots before drawing short tick marks. Move a crowded upper-right group instead of shrinking numerals to fit it. Accurate division comes first and gives the lettering stable positions; manual placement does not require an automatic geometric subdivision tool.

Place numerals on one inner ring

Place the numerals inside the tick marks. Start with the wider 12 and 10, then arrange the others at similar visual size. Hand-letter or use the currently available text entry, first testing glyph shape and legibility. Keep numerals oriented for normal reading; they need not each rotate along a radius. If 1 looks too slight, adjust its visual weight rather than forcing all characters to the same width. Temporarily cover the shell and read all twelve numerals in order. Reading every value aloud helps reveal an old number accidentally left in a copied element.

Place the minute hand before the hour hand

For 10:10, the minute hand points to 2 because ten minutes span two five-minute intervals. The hour hand is slightly beyond 10, one sixth of the hour-long journey toward 11, and must not remain exactly on 10. Mark both hand axes in the sketch before adding width. Verify placement on paper or with your own calculator; the app is not required to calculate it. Even a schematic drawing must agree with its stated time: a 10:10 title with hands resembling 10:00 fails this study’s specific acceptance check.

Clarify hand lengths and the central joint

Make the minute hand longer, approaching the minute marks without covering the numerals. Make the hour hand shorter and broader. Both must extend from the same centre. A small circular cap may cover their roots, but should not resemble a third heavy hand. If adding a second hand, give it one clear direction and a finer line so it remains subordinate. Hide the cap to check that the two principal hands really join, then restore it. Hands must not originate beside a numeral. Even a thin off-centre hand makes reading unstable, so repair attachment before adding shadows.

Give the shell a narrow side plane

Leave a narrow plane between outer frame and inner dial, with a little thickness visible below or on one side. Under upper-left light, the upper edge is pale and the lower-right edge slightly darker. Keep the dial quiet rather than strongly mirror-like. Explain the layers in grey before choosing a muted shell colour. Break a continuous pure-white rim if it becomes a glowing ring; narrow the frame and reduce black area if it resembles a thick tyre. The shell supports the dial and should not be more complex than the numerals and hands. Scale detail to the information task.

Keep reflections away from essential time cues

If suggesting glass, add only a small pale-grey curved reflection on one side, leaving the centre and the areas near 10 and 2 clear. Do not cover both hands with a large white patch or fade all numerals into illegibility merely to suggest transparency. Read the time with reflections hidden, then restore them and compare. Reduce their area if reading becomes difficult. A reflection is an optional hand-painted cue, not a claim of physical glass simulation. Omit it when appropriate: complete information is more useful here than making every surface shine.

Test division with numerals hidden

In a copy, hide numerals and inspect only the twelve positions and two hands. The minute hand should still align with the second hour mark and the hour hand sit just beyond the tenth. Then inspect numerals alone to catch ordering errors, keeping the two checks from concealing each other. Review clock proportions small, read the time at normal size and inspect stray tick strokes close up. Move a misplaced mark rather than widening it to hide a neighbouring gap. A correct mark count does not prove correct spacing; check both separately.

Compare 10:00 with 10:10

Save a simple 10:00 copy by moving the minute hand to 12 and returning the hour hand to 10, leaving numerals and shell unchanged. Compare both versions: ten minutes change both hands, not only the longer one. If the hour hand has not moved in the 10:10 version, correct it in the sketch. This counterexample clarifies the information. Angle calculations assist but need not achieve engineering precision. What matters is that a reader does not interpret the example as another time, even when the decorative style is freely chosen.

Keep a reflection-free version and a time checklist

Keep division guides, a reflection-free finish and a PNG, with numerals and hands independently editable in the native project. For the next study, change only to 3:20. First note outside the drawing that the minute hand goes to 4 and the hour hand one third beyond 3, then apply that to the unchanged palette. Do not copy old hand positions and change only the title. Reopen the export and check stated time, numeral order and a common centre. If fine hands disappear at use size, improve them before adding shell texture; the finished clock must still be readable.
