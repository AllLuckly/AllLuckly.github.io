Draw a platypus moving slightly toward the front-left, omitting a water surface that would conceal its limbs. Connect broad bill, low furred body, short limbs and flat tail into one silhouette before adding local fur edges. This is neither species identification nor a swimming measurement; pose and proportions are drawing plans.

Arrange head, body and tail in a low elongated pose

On an approximately 2000×1300 px canvas put the head left, body centrally and tail extending back-right. Join head and body through a short smooth transition rather than a long bird neck, and continue the tail root from the rear body. Separate sketch, body colour, bill/feet and fur edges on pale grey-green, then save native work. The slightly elevated view reveals near forefoot and hindfoot, with only justified far-side fragments; do not make the body transparent to display every limb. Pixels provide no body-length, sex or age measurement.

Extend the broad bill smoothly from the head

Extend a broad flattened bill with a compressed rounded upper surface and a little near-edge thickness, not a bright-yellow duck-bill icon. Blend its base into the head and taper gently toward a rounded front rather than a hard hook. Grey-brown separates bill from dark fur without a heavy black cut around its base. Add small nostril marks only where the reference supports their placement, otherwise omit them. Invent neither a tooth row nor engraved lines claiming to map actual electroreceptors.

Keep eye position and fur behind the bill

Place a small near eye behind and above the bill base, not at its tip or as a circle as wide as the bill. Short soft fur edges connect head to shoulder; add no long ear flap behind the eye. A documented ear opening is not an upright external ear. Hide fine fur to inspect head shape before adding one or two narrow accents, avoiding a white eye ring that erases the bill junction. This chosen open-eye illustration establishes no underwater-foraging sensory state; a diving scene requires renewed reference checking.

Spread forefoot webbing from a short connected limb

Connect a short forelimb to the body’s front side and spread a broad webbed foot at its end. The web spans between toes rather than form a circular skirt around the wrist. A few radial guides indicate toe directions without turning every decorative line into an extra digit. Retain a traceable foot-to-shoulder connection, not a wing growing below the bill. Show only the justified exposed edge of the far forefoot in this swimming pose. The reference distinguishes limb roles, but this drawing calculates neither speed nor thrust from web area.

Give hindfeet and the flat tail distinct attachments

Attach short hindlimbs and partially webbed feet at the rear body sides, not the tail tip. Broaden and flatten the tail from the body rear into a substantial elongated rounded surface, not a forked fish tail or scale grid. Retain fur colour with a smoother boundary than the torso. Sketch the full tail before concealing an edge behind the near hindfoot; the far hindfoot need not be completely visible. Feet and tail are separate organs and must not merge into one fin simply because they function in water.

Turn fur colours around the body instead of filling every hair

Organize the torso with deep brown, warmer light and cool-grey shadow. Group short strokes from head toward shoulder and back toward tail root. Break the back highlight and keep the darker underside from becoming a black hole. Bill and web surfaces remain smoother rather than share the same hair density. Add fur tips at two or three contour areas and leave broader internal planes, simplifying the tail further. Sparse strokes suggest dense fur without counting real hairs or identifying geographic origin or physiological condition from colour.

Check bill, forefeet, hindfeet and tail root for misplaced parts

Hide texture and trace bill to head/body, then near forefoot, hindfoot and tail root separately to the torso. An ear-like forefoot may have its root misplaced above the rear head. A tail resembling a third webbed foot needs its broad rear surface restored. Correct far-foot occlusion before detail. Preserve the faulty copy and change one connection, checking full and enlarged views. Male ankle spurs are not represented, and omission does not determine sex. Document simplification rather than present it as complete anatomy.

Reopen and inspect bill, webs and furred tail surfaces

Save native work, hide guides and reopen a PNG. At reduced size inspect the low silhouette and tail width; enlarge bill base, toe-web connections and tail root, with no extra wings or forked fish tail. Reopen separately editable bill/feet colours and body fur edges, documenting view and occluded feet. Leave unperformed export/reopening pending and record predictions only. Retain exterior-study, invented-pose and omission notes; reference reading and static drawing review are not field observation, device animal recognition or a swimming experiment.
