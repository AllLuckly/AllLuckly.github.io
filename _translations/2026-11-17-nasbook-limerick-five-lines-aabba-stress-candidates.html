Five lines do not automatically make a limerick, and similar spelling does not establish rhyme. Read an original light poem in Book Isle, retaining authorial lines while checking AABBA and a long/short stress pattern. Scansion is a reasoned reading, not automatic app detection or one uniquely correct performance.

Save the complete five-line original

Make card L1 from the five lines below, placing analysis numbers in margins or notes. A baker keeps a key in a cup, the cup falls, he searches, and the lock remains firm; do not rewrite the ending to claim that the lock has opened merely to fit expectations. Import a line-preserving PDF/EPUB and check endpoints and punctuation. Screen wrapping or zoom does not create authorial lines. The poem is original, not by Edward Lear or the source defining the form.

A baker who lived by the quay
Kept a key in a cup for his tea.
When the cup took a fall,
He searched by the wall,
But the lock was as firm as could be.

Separate form from a usual rhythmic tendency

Poetry Foundation describes five lines, AABBA rhyme and generally anapestic light verse. Generally does not ban substitutions or make an eight-syllable line automatically fail. Use a common three-stress long/two-stress short working reading without calling it the only historical variant. Record definition and scansion convention separately. Form, sound and humor need separate checks; an amusing story does not establish rhyme.

Group end words by sound

Endings are quay, tea, fall, wall, be. Common quay /kiː/ groups with tea/be as A; fall/wall form B in the same selected accent, giving AABBA. Do not infer quay rhymes with bay from ay or require matching initial consonants. Compare corresponding stressed-vowel endings. Check uncertain pronunciation in a reliable dictionary and retain the chosen reading; spelling is not phonetic evidence. Exclude final punctuation only from rhyme comparison, not the saved source.

Mark major stresses in the three long lines

One reading is a BAKer who LIVED by the QUAY; kept a KEY in a CUP for his TEA; but the LOCK was as FIRM as could BE. Each has three major beats. Capitals are analytical, not source edits. Syllable counts are 8, 9, 9; the first line has a shorter opening rather than requiring an inserted the. Tapping three beats does not make every content word stressed or baker’s weak ending a fourth beat. Keep the reading compatible with grammar and ordinary lexical stress.

Check two short beats and opening variation

When the CUP took a FALL has two major beats and 6 syllables; he SEARCHED by the WALL has two and 5. The shorter opening does not require adding had and calling the altered line unchanged. The reading gives beats 3, 3, 2, 2, 3 and syllables 8, 9, 6, 5, 9. These are different quantities: three beats are not three syllables. Retain reasons for another plausible stress reading rather than let display width determine metre.

Word counts are 7, 9, 6, 5, 9; line one has 7 words but 8 syllables because baker normally has two. Feet need not follow word boundaries: a BAK / er who LIVED / by the QUAY gives a two-syllable opening and two three-syllable feet. Slashes are analysis, not source respelling.

Change one rhyme to show five lines are insufficient

In a control change only third-line fall to flight. It can retain 6 syllables and two beats but loses rhyme with wall; five lines and approximate beats do not establish AABBA. A narrower reading window changes wrapping, not authorial lines or rhymes. Keep textual-sound and display-space changes separate. Actually combining lines 3/4 changes this card’s five-line arrangement; historical combined-line variants need their own context rather than blanket rejection.

Keep source lines and scansion separately in Book Isle

Bookmark or highlight poem and rhyme ledger, recording end words, letters, syllables, stress candidates without inserting missing syllables. A failed quay search calls for a text-layer check, not replacing it with bay. Seven display rows need authorial-line verification; uniform nine-syllable counts may confuse punctuation or beats. Correct one count and reread meaning so rhythm edits do not change key/lock states. Leave unperformed reading, zoom or reopening pending; no automatic scansion is claimed.

Searched normally has one syllable; written ed does not automatically add a syllable or beat. For another accent or performance, check actual pronunciation and its rhythmic fit rather than count letters or revise every line mechanically. Keep dictionary evidence, performance and fixed text separate.
