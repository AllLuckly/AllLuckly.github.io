When organizing your own recordings, recording date, first release date, and encoding date need not match. ID3v2.4 defines separate recording, release, and encoding time fields. 调调 publicly describes displaying title, artist, album, and artwork, but does not promise to display these dates. First make a time record outside the file, then check what 调调 actually shows; do not turn one generic year into every event’s date.

Prepare a traceable recording

Choose an MP3 you are authorized to use, preserve the unmodified original, and make an experimental copy. Record its source, duration, recording time, and whether it was actually released. If a time is uncertain or the recording was never released, write “unknown” or “unreleased” rather than inventing a date. These states differ: an unknown item may already have been released. An illustration may use fictional recording date 2024-03-01, release date 2025-04-01, and encoding date 2025-04-03, but label them as teaching data rather than facts about your file.

Preview the working copy in Files or a trusted player and confirm it plays and matches the original duration. Filesystem creation and modification times can change during copying, downloading, or editing and are not automatically recording times; record file times separately from audio events. Give the copy a recognizable filename but do not assume 调调 will use it as the song title.

Inspect available ID3 date fields

Open the copy in a familiar trusted local tag viewer and first observe the ID3 version and existing fields; there is no need to install unfamiliar software. ID3v2.4 defines TDRC as recording time, TDRL as release time, and TDEN as encoding time. Other versions and tools may use different display labels or omit fields. Record only what is actually visible. If the mapping is unclear, mark it unchecked rather than interpreting a generic “year” as all three events.

If the tool clearly supports editing these fields independently and you know the real dates, enter only verified values on the copy. Do not invent a release date for unreleased material. Before saving, ensure the operation will not re-encode or overwrite the original. If the tool does not expose the fields, do not force a write or pretend a year in the filename is a tag. Reopen the saved copy and inspect the actual result; compare with the backup if needed. Viewing a field, writing it, and seeing it in a player are separate states.

Import into 调调 and observe

Use the visible file-import entry in 调调 to select the working copy. Wait for import, find the item, and play the beginning. Identify it by sound, duration, and visible title. Observe what the app actually displays: it may show only publicly described title, artist, album, or artwork, or other information depending on version. Mark an absent date as not displayed; do not infer that the file necessarily lacks that field.

If identical content already exists in the library, import may yield duplicate-content feedback. Record the actual result rather than attributing a missing new item to the date field. If useful, repeat with another recording you own, preserving source and operation order for each round. Do not delete existing tracks or alter original archives merely to make the demonstration look complete.

Make a three-column time record

Create columns for recording, first release, and encoding, and note the date, evidence source, and confidence for each. Separately record filesystem times, fields shown by the tag viewer, and what 调调 displays. For example, the recording date may come from your own session notes, the release state may be unreleased, and encoding time may come from an export log. Leave unsupported cells unknown. Matching years do not mean the three events happened on the same day. If evidence gives only a year, preserve that precision rather than inventing a month and day. If an exact time and zone are known, include the zone in the external record so a cross-region handoff does not mistake the same instant for a different day.

If delivering to a collaborator, share an appropriately authorized working copy and the record after confirming permission to share both audio and metadata. The file may also contain author, location, or other personal information; inspect it before sending and avoid revealing private paths in public screenshots. A date in a tag is an assertion, not independent proof of copyright ownership or publication time.

Troubleshoot and restore

If the edited audio cannot play, stop using the copy, make another from the backup, and inspect the editor’s save behavior. If a viewer’s year differs from your notes, identify which field and ID3 version it represents and examine the source before copying one value into every date field. If 调调 plays normally without displaying a date, keep the external time record; public information does not promise that every metadata field will be displayed.

Keep the original and any needed working copy, verifying identity before cleanup. A complete record explains what each date means, which fields were actually checked, and what 调调 displayed. Leave unverified portions unknown instead of inventing dates to fill a table.
