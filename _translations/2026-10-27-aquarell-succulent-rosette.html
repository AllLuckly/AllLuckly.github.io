A rosette succulent has thick leaves with hidden roots and a tightening centre. Paint a simplified plant through three staggered leaf rings, using grey-green values for fleshiness. Natural asymmetry is welcome and no automatic radial or mirror tool is required.

Establish three scales around one centre

On an approximately 1500×1500 px square, mark a slightly high centre and three rings. Keep the inner ring small, middle clear and outer larger with perspective compression. Separate rings and establish their size hierarchy before pointed leaves, avoiding a flat wreath or a large empty centre.

Vary leaf axes around the centre

Place four to six representative leaves per ring, tips outward and roots inward. Broaden near leaves and compress far ones, staggering neighbouring rings. Resolve the middle ring first. Correct scattered leaves through their root directions rather than outlining everything into artificial unity.

Build thick leaves from ridge and side planes

Paint each leaf in mid grey-green with a broad raised ridge and shaded side, rather than a thin vein or a spherical blob. Taper its root into shadow. Under upper-left light, compare three differently oriented sample leaves before extending the modelling to the plant.

Place overlap shadows on the lower leaf

Let inner leaves cover middle roots and middle leaves cover outer roots. Place narrow shadows on the lower surfaces, not around every leaf. Hide covered roots rather than merely darkening outlines. Keep selected overlaps clear and quieter ones simplified, especially on the far side.

Suggest a powdery surface with restrained light

Use muted blue-green or grey-green without sharp white spots on every leaf. Keep warm tips or optional red edges tiny and selective. Broad shading and subdued reflections suggest a powdery surface better than fading the whole layer. Reduce mirror-like lights for plasticity or clarify selected planes when it becomes muddy.

Allow the outer ring to be incomplete

Occlude parts of outer leaves, allowing one near leaf to extend and far ones to soften into the background. Avoid filling every gap for symmetry. Resolve accidental tip tangencies by separating or clearly overlapping them, making incompleteness a result of space rather than missing connections.

Review centre and perimeter separately

Check the tightening centre small, middle-ring thickness normally and accidental root gaps close up. Repair a flower-like result through leaf thickness, or a gear-like result through less regular tips. Hide surface detail to verify stacked masses before allowing red edges or highlights to dominate.

Use turning-leaf studies to unify light

Paint three grey-green leaves facing left, forward and right under one fixed light. Reconsider ridge and side values for each rather than copying the same highlight symbol. Apply the corrected studies selectively to matching middle-ring leaves before repainting the whole plant.

Give the central gaps direction

Treat the gaps between three inner leaves as directional shapes. Avoid identical template-like openings. Move one leaf to study changed negative space before adding dark paint, keeping gaps near the deeper leaf values rather than pure black. This tests real inner-ring overlap instead of a dark central hole.

Keep the palette and vary the viewing angle

Keep ring-structure, modelled and PNG stages with editable rings. Next, lower the viewing angle and rebuild near expansion, far compression and increased centre occlusion without changing colours. Reconstruct overlaps rather than squashing the finished image, noting which root becomes most hidden.
