A building briefly visible across a face may be an overlap between scenes during a transition, not a transparent object in the story. Continuous playback plus a staged record is more informative than one paused still. Inspect permitted footage without assuming iCouldPlay creates transitions, separates layers or recovers original unblended images.

Choose distinct before-and-after scenes

Choose permitted footage with distinct backgrounds and an observable transition, recording identity, duration and author notes. Use a separately saved self-made card/table edit only with a familiar editor, otherwise existing material. Do not install unfamiliar effects software for this exercise. Preserve the original without sharpening or recompression. Without a clear example, label overlap suspected rather than call every pale image a dissolve.

Choose distinctive anchors for both scenes

Choose a stable first-scene detail such as table corner/red card and a distinct second-scene window/blue card. Name scenes A/B externally rather than identify them by colour alone. Establish anchors in their complete shots, replacing cropped references with visible ones. When two scenes look similar, retain the file version and neighbouring content to confirm their identities. Similar actors do not prove the same time/place. Anchors identify visible source scenes, not invented plot clues.

Watch the complete transition first

Play normally from before scene A through stable scene B, distinguishing gradual change, cut and other effects. Do not begin with a middle still that makes a transient blend seem sustained. Record titles/black intervals separately. Narrated elapsed time is separate evidence; overlap alone does not establish minutes or days passing.

Record contours and backgrounds in three phases

Revisit before, during and after, logging A’s anchor, each anchor’s overlap visibility and B’s anchor. Fading, occlusion and composition may prevent complete visibility. Describe contours softening/background appearing without invented blend percentages. Whole-scene change supports a transition interpretation; local-object fading permits other explanations. Do not fill unseen regions to force a dissolve verdict.

Separate editing from in-story transparency

Compare layouts, viewpoints and states and whether the blend ends in a different scene. Glass, reflections or story effects can also produce overlap and need context/author evidence. Two contours do not prove a character crossed a wall or identify one object. Separate observed process, plausible explanation and missing evidence.

Keep blur and compression questions separate

For overlap only after one seek, replay continuously and check recurrence at the same content. Repeatability supports a source-effect hypothesis without proving production cause. Block damage, freezing or sound continuing over a stuck image need separate playback diagnosis. An engine comparison alone cannot establish the author’s intended image; originals/production notes may be needed.

Use pauses to locate evidence only

Label any pause/crop as before, during or after with its file time. Do not present a transitional still as a permanent story object or erase one scene and claim recovery. Keep coarse times coarse without frame-accuracy claims. Share minimal permitted descriptions instead of a restricted film segment.

Keep edition identity when consulting notes

For author commentary, record title, version, time and referenced shot, then verify the same edition. Observation can stand without commentary but interpretation stays bounded. Other edits may omit/retime the blend; do not apply another edit’s timetable to this version. Words such as later/simultaneously are not sole proof of transition type; preserve separate textual/visual scopes and unresolved disagreement without altering dialogue.

Return to continuous playback for the conclusion

Reopen the same file and play from A’s anchor through B, checking all three records. Restore normal viewing and preserve original/notes. Describe visible replacement and unresolved causes without automatic effect recognition, ghost repair or layer recovery. This is content reading, not production acceptance or device validation of every media source.
