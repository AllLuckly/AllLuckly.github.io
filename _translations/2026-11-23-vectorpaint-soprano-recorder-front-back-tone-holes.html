Observe a soprano recorder from front and back, then draw its mouthpiece, labium and tone holes in VectorPaint and check each view.

Choose a reference and views

Choose a soprano recorder you own or are authorized to photograph. Identify its mouthpiece, head joint, labium, body and foot joint. Yamaha distinguishes these parts and the tone holes; joints and hole shapes vary by model. Draw front and back views, not a universal fingering chart, and do not infer tuning or internal dimensions from an illustration.

Create a two-column canvas

Open VectorPaint, create an approximately 1800 × 1500 landscape canvas, and save an editable work; the dimensions are only a suggestion. Use the left column for the front and the right for the back. Leave space above and below and use light centerlines and a shared baseline to compare height. Organize the sketch, views, holes and labels with available layers or groups.

Draw the front silhouette

Use a shape or pen path for a long pale recorder silhouette with mouthpiece above and foot below. A body need not be a perfectly uniform rectangle: observe head, body and foot widths and adjust the edges with a few nodes. Leave holes and decoration until later. Zoom out and check the proportions and centerline before placing holes.

Separate mouthpiece and labium

Draw the relatively flat mouthpiece at the top with a transition into the head. On the front of the head, draw the labium window and its edge separately; do not merge it with the opening at the mouthpiece. The visible shading depends on viewpoint, so prioritize the distinction. Zoom in to keep the window inside the outline and do not invent unseen internal parts.

Place the front tone-hole positions

Using the chosen model as reference, mark seven front finger positions with light dots from top to bottom and check spacing. Some recorders have paired small holes at lower positions; draw the pair as adjacent circles and do not count it as two independent finger positions. Follow the reference for size and offset instead of making every gap identical.

Make the holes editable objects

Use dark ellipses for visible holes, with no stroke or a fine stroke and enough contrast against the body. Keep each circle of a paired hole independent, then compare its size and spacing. Do not claim that a Boolean cut simulates the actual bore. Here the dark shapes are editable visual symbols, separate from the real material thickness.

Build the back view

Duplicate the body silhouette into the right column and align top and bottom with the shared baseline; do not copy the front holes. Add the thumb hole visible on the reference back and check its position relative to head and body. If the front labium is hidden from the back, do not add it at the same spot. The two columns are different views, not a superposition of every feature.

Add joints and the foot

Following the reference, add a narrow line or ring where the head meets the body and where the body meets the foot. Do not label every decorative ring as a detachable joint; if uncertain, identify it only as a visible ring. Adjust the foot silhouette and bottom alignment, keeping ring edges within the body rather than extending into the background.

Label and compare both sides

Write “Front” and “Back” in open space and use short leaders for the mouthpiece, labium, front holes and back thumb hole. Leaders should end beside the correct feature without covering a hole; do not point one label at both the window and mouthpiece. Recheck after moving text. Keep overall proportions consistent without assuming the back hole shares an exact height with a front hole.

Suggest a round tube with light

Add a narrow, low-contrast highlight along one side and a slightly darker edge on the other to suggest volume; do not cover holes with the highlight. Use consistent small shadows around the window and holes without needing photorealistic texture. Zoom out to ensure mouthpiece, labium and holes remain distinguishable in both views, then adjust value or label size as needed.

Check structure and objects

Hide the sketch. Check the seven front positions, any paired holes in the chosen reference, the back thumb hole, labium and both ends. Select several holes and the body separately to confirm the artwork was not accidentally merged into one object; adjust nodes or objects individually if alignment is wrong. One model does not establish the hole shapes, joint placement or fingering of all recorders.

Save, export and recheck

Save the editable work, export a separate PNG or SVG through the available controls, and reopen it to inspect hole counts, labels and body edges. A bitmap establishes appearance, not editable text or paths. Retain the original if SVG object structure changes. If the window looks like another finger hole, adjust its outline and leader; if the back hole disappears, check layer order and contrast.

State the scope before sharing

The finished illustration should distinguish front, back, head window and holes drawn from the selected model. Note the reference model and views, including any paired holes; this is not playing instruction or a universal fingering chart. Confirm the reference and editable original still exist before sharing the export. This publicly sourced exercise claims no physical-device test.
