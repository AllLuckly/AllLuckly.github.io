A star-shaped outline is not a sea star body plan. Choose the upper surface of a five-armed ochre sea star, using broad arm roots and a central disc to show continuous tissue rather than five spikes glued to a pancake. Not all sea stars have five arms; this count belongs to the selected subject, not the entire group. Arrange slightly varied arms, rough texture and rock contact manually in Aquarell without combining upper-surface and oral-side tube-foot views.

Specify the animal and visible surface

Use an approximately 1700×1700 px square canvas with quiet grey-green rock and a grey-purple ochre sea star viewed slightly from above. Establish a broad disc and five outward arm axes, one upward and four to sides and below, with margins around tips. Separate sketch, broad body values, surface detail and contact shadow. Purple is a palette choice within this species’ documented colour range, not an age indicator. Broaden roots and shorten excessive points if geometrically star-like. Omit texture initially. Do not add a central mouth or see-through underside organs to this upper-surface view.

Grow five arms continuously from the disc

Join each arm broadly to the disc and taper it outward without a black root ring separating it into a block. Trace the silhouette from the upper arm around all five, counting both roots and tips. The disc is not a hard-edged circular pancake; spaces between arms suggest its boundary. Broaden pinched roots and remove a closed central circle if flower-like. Check for texture accidentally creating extra short arms if urchin-like. Gentle curves are enough, without knots. This is an intact five-arm specimen, not a regeneration study excusing a missing endpoint.

Allow unequal spaces between arms

Give the five spaces between arms slightly different depths and widths while retaining body continuity rather than ruler-perfect fifths. Tilt the upper arm slightly right or bend the lower-left one, checking smooth roots after each change. Vary one or two directions if badge-like instead of randomly distorting everything. Open negative spaces if five arms are unclear rather than cutting black cracks through the disc. Natural irregularity does not excuse miscounting; retain five sketch axes. Foreshortening may alter apparent lengths but cannot make one arm branch from the middle of another.

Build upper-arm volume along each length

Light from upper left, retaining broad grey-purple light on the disc and gently raised arm tops turning toward dark purple-grey edges. Keep one light direction rather than giving all five white centre stripes and equally black sides. Hide texture to test continuous thick form, then strengthen only useful edge turns. Reduce uniform highlights and smooth plastic spots if inflatable-toy-like; separate selected side values from rock if paper-like. Simplified thickness is not a species measurement, and volume does not require heavy black outlines around every arm.

Attach rough texture to the body surface

Suggest roughness with a few pale grey-purple dots and short ridges on disc and near arms, quieter on far surfaces and varying with volume. Do not turn each dot into a black hole or each ridge into a new arm. Test one patch small, then extend it while retaining quiet areas. Reduce equal-sized regular dots if sesame-biscuit-like; reduce silhouette spikes if prickly-ball-like. Sources support a rough appearance, not a one-to-one organ identity for every painted mark. Upper-surface texture must not be labelled oral-side tube feet.

Use rock contact to explain visible placement

Use narrow contact shadows where arm edges approach rock, with deeper shade under the centre and small gaps beneath locally raised tips. Keep shadows cool grey rather than equally black around five arms. Paint broad quiet rock patches and only one or two cracks, avoiding a crack joined to a tip as a false sixth arm. Inspect low edges if floating; break continuous black contact rings and restore side turns if flattened. An upper view does not expose all underside attachment details. Describe visible contact without inferring attachment strength or movement direction from shadow.

Keep upper and oral-surface information separate

Keep a texture-free five-axis sketch and count five roots and tips, ensuring no closed circle detaches the disc. Study the oral surface on a separate reference page, for example recording the underside’s central mouth, rather than adding it to this upper-view finish. Check tips against rock cracks and mark any concealed extent in the sketch. Change one arm bend or contact shadow at a time with body colour fixed. Label actual references as upper or lower surfaces; details from opposite orientations cannot be combined into an impossible simultaneously visible exterior.

Check that texture does not swallow the five arms

Save the layered original and first count five arms in the PNG at use size, then inspect disc-to-rock value separation. Reduce texture count and contrast if it becomes black noise rather than brightening the entire animal. Repair negative space and tip positions if adjacent arms merge, not black trenches. Record a fault such as rough dots forming a dark ring around the lower-right root for direct layer correction. Retain the chosen ochre-species/five-arm qualification instead of saying all sea stars have five arms. Count, continuity, consistent upper view and readable contact matter alongside texture.
