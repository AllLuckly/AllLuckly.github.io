Ten panels may show an instant, while one can skip a day. A flashback timeline concerns order; this exercise asks how long one scene lasts. In ManYue, manually log temporal evidence, possible omissions and reading pace in a completed passage, keeping reading time, panel count and story duration distinct. The app is not assumed to measure time within images.

Choose a passage with identifiable endpoints

Choose a completed sequence with clear endpoints, such as opening and closing a door or waiting for and boarding a vehicle, and record edition, chapter and endpoint images. Read it intact without timing your turns or converting page counts to minutes. Check chapter boundaries for jumps or missing pages. Begin with one action strand; another strand’s screen space is not automatically this character’s waiting time. Narrow unclear boundaries rather than inventing a precise schedule for an entire series.

Find explicit wording or timekeeping objects

Locate wording such as minutes later, one instant or all night and identify its scope. Compare clocks, countdowns or announcements only when appropriate. A character’s “long wait” may be subjective. Ten-to-eleven clock readings require checks for day, clock identity and function, including stoppage or a changed day. Preserve “later” as vague instead of inventing five minutes. Quote original wording and separate your calculation so an inferred number is not attributed to the work.

Distinguish multiple views of an instant from sequence

Several reaction panels may offer views of one event rather than equal successive intervals. Check shared objects, sound cues and actual action changes. In an invented sequence—a cup leaving a table, two people opening their eyes and the cup still airborne—multiplying three panels by one second per panel does not establish three seconds. A later grounded cup establishes change without necessarily establishing duration. Separate repeated views, advancing action and uncertainty so cuts do not become an imagined regular clock.

Check omitted processes and pauses

Look for clothing, light or location changes suggesting omitted processes. Undrawn travel is not teleportation, and an empty panel need not mean stillness. Dialogue pauses and scenery can slow the reading experience without providing measurable elapsed time. Locate the suspected omission and its support, marking duration unstated when needed. Do not insert your own walking-time estimate: fictional conditions, travel methods and narrative omission may differ. An explained unknown is more useful than a complete-looking guess.

Use bounds only when supported

Use a difference for a continuously verifiable countdown, or only the relevant upper bound if action finishes before an announcement ends. Do not force an unsupported lower bound. An explicit “two minutes later” in an invented example supports approximately two minutes for that transition, not automatically the entire chapter. Attach location and conditions to bounds, revising them if a fictional timer proves inaccurate. Keep inference no more precise than the source; definite and vague durations can coexist in the log.

Log reading pace separately as personal experience

Reread and note where you pause, revisit or move quickly, with possible reasons such as dense lettering, reactions or empty space. Compare that experience with story duration without treating it as temporal evidence. Any available reading statistics follow their own scope, not the characters’ elapsed minutes. Label the experience as rereading because remembered outcomes affect pace. Do not generalise one reader’s speed into a universal judgement of sluggishness; retain sample and viewing conditions.

Preserve endpoints and timing evidence

Save supported references or an external log for beginning, end and decisive timing wording, including panels and landmarks. Leave the reader, then return and verify that the two endpoints still belong to this passage rather than a similar scene. Check tiny timing text against a readable source before transcription; zoom cannot reconstruct blurred digits. Rematch by content across changed pagination rather than expecting old percentages or pages. Preserve the original sequence instead of deleting reaction panels to shorten reading, which would change the material under observation.

Finish with three distinct kinds of conclusion

End separately with established duration, possible omitted processes and personal reading experience. Many panels may depict one instant; clock differences need matching day, clock and reliability checks. Leave unsupported intervals unknown. Completion requires recoverable support and no confusion between feeling and objective timing, not a number of seconds. Reuse these three categories for later samples to compare narrative methods rather than length alone.
