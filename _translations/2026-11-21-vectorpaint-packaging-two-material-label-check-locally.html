Make a material-information card for fictional packaging, using separate illustrations for a paper carton and plastic bottle and the instruction “Check local rules for handling.” The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says acceptance of a material depends on the local program and a package's chasing-arrows symbol does not necessarily establish recyclability. This exercise organizes information; it does not classify a real product or issue local disposal guidance.

Define the card's factual scope

Assume a fictional product consists of one paper carton and one plastic bottle. Do not draw a real brand or an unverified certification mark. These are the two assumed parts; cap, label and residual contents have undetermined materials or handling and belong in a “check separately” field. Do not turn “plastic bottle” into “recyclable everywhere.” Prepare a table for part, material description, rule to check and source, using it later to ensure the graphics do not imply extra conclusions.

Build a two-column card

Create a landscape canvas, a card with outer margins and a center divider. Reserve left for carton, right for bottle and a common line below. Keep background, icons, divider, text and notes in identifiable layers and save an editable draft. Block the layout with two pale areas before drawing. Color can distinguish regions, but part names must also be written. Choose canvas size for its actual use; this schematic does not define a printer's label size.

Draw the intact carton in the left column

Draw the front as a rectangle, add a narrower side with a shared edge and modest value change, and optionally suggest the lid boundary with a short line. The side must meet the front rather than resemble a second detached carton. Put “Paper carton” in the front area with space from its outline. Not every seam or inner layer needs to be shown, but the image must not imply that unseen materials were inspected. If the side disappears in a thumbnail, adjust edge contrast and width before adding decoration.

Draw a distinct bottle body on the right

Make a simple closed bottle outline, narrowing into a neck with a separate small cap. Join shoulder smoothly to body. Keep the cap as an independent object or layer and label it “Cap material to be checked”; do not automatically assign it the body's material. Label the body “Plastic bottle body,” avoiding an icon interpreted as the complete package. Use path or shape operations actually visible in your version; a simple closed shape works without assuming a particular Boolean control. Keep a recoverable copy while editing.

Add the shared instruction and pending table

Below both columns write “Check local rules for handling.” Add a short three-column table “Part / Material / Local requirement,” with known rows for paper carton and plastic bottle body and “To be checked” under local requirement. List cap and label separately as pending; an arrow icon is not a replacement for a written conclusion. The EPA source reminds readers that local programs differ. It is a U.S. source and does not issue rules for other jurisdictions.

Keep distinctions beyond color

The carton uses straight edges and a side plane; the bottle has shoulders, neck and cap, with text explicitly naming each part. Even if both columns temporarily use the same gray, outline and label should distinguish them. W3C use-of-color guidance says color should not be the only visual way to convey information. Here shape, text and layout provide additional cues, but do not automatically complete an accessibility audit. If the divider merges with a bottle edge in grayscale, move the shape or increase spacing rather than changing only hue.

Check for misleading details

Check whether the carton label refers only to the carton, whether the bottle-body label accidentally includes the cap, and whether the pending instruction is cropped or too small. Do not rewrite “bottle body” as “all plastic” or fill a local requirement with an unverified fixed answer. For an actual use case, consult the responsible local authority or collector, then update an independent version and record the source; leave this sample pending. Visual symbols do not establish material, cleanliness or processing capability.

Check information limits in two views

On a copy temporarily cover the bottom instruction and inspect only the two drawings and part labels. Ask whether the cap material or local disposal rule can be established; the correct record for this exercise is that they cannot. Restore the instruction and pending table, checking their visibility at ordinary reading size. If “to be checked” is visible only when enlarged, adjust type size or spacing instead of replacing the explanation with a more conspicuous recycling mark. Record the two observations separately as graphic communication and text legibility, not as identification of real materials.

Export and complete the record

Save the editable draft and export a separate image using a control actually visible in the application. Open elsewhere to inspect text, divider, cap and frame. If SVG export is available, save and reopen a copy and try selecting carton, bottle and text independently. Record merged objects or converted text rather than guaranteeing editability from appearance. Deliver the sample card, editable draft and part-check table; mark unperformed reopening, grayscale or device work pending. This card is not a real product classification or public disposal instruction.
