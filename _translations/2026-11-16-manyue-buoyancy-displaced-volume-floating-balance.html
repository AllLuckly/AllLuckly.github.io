Buoyancy equals weight at floating equilibrium, but full immersion need not displace the same volume. Use an original 2-kilogram, 4-liter block diagram to calculate displaced-fluid buoyancy and compare fully submerged versus floating states. Read an externally authored PDF in ManYue and calculate separately. This is an ideal static-water model, not weighing, immersion testing or automatic physical measurement by the app.

Fix the block and static-water assumptions

Draw a tank and block with mass 2 kg, total volume 4 L, water density 1000 kg/m³ and g=9.8 m/s². Assume incompressible water, a nonabsorbing undeformed block, negligible surface tension/air buoyancy, no bottom contact and no supporting string. Put assumptions on page one and retain them later rather than silently introducing a flooding hollow object. The diagram is not to scale; calculate the waterline later. Keep mass, volume, density and force units separate:2 kg is not 2 N and 4 L is not 4 m³.

Convert four liters before substitution

One liter is 0.001 m³, so 4 L=0.004 m³. Under unchanged assumptions, full immersion displaces that volume; partial emergence uses only submerged volume. List total, submerged and displaced volume separately; the latter two coincide here. Surface waves are symbols, not lengths from which to calculate water quantity. Porous or flooding materials need a redefined displaced region rather than unqualified reuse of this closed-block relation.

Calculate full-immersion buoyancy and weight separately

Archimedes’ relation gives water density×g×displaced volume=1000×9.8×0.004=39.2 N upward. Weight is 2×9.8=19.6 N downward, leaving 19.6 N upward. This is the force tendency at full immersion, not equilibrium, and drag is omitted. Greater buoyancy does not make that fully submerged picture already floating at rest: position will change. Weight does not cancel the existence of buoyancy. Label force directions/values from arithmetic rather than treating arrow lengths as measured forces.

At floating rest displace two liters rather than four

At free floating rest, vertical balance requires buoyancy 19.6 N. Displaced volume is 19.6/1000/9.8=0.002 m³=2 L, half the total 4 L. Volume fraction translates directly to height fraction only with appropriate shape/cross-section. Use an upright constant-section block here and state why its waterline is half-height. A tapered object need not be half-height at 2 L displacement. Keep the full-immersion page instead of overlaying two states with one ambiguous force label set.

If the constant horizontal section is 0.02 m², total height is 0.004/0.02=0.2 m=20 cm and floating submerged height 0.002/0.02=0.1 m=10 cm. These use the same cross-section assumption, not a visual half. They describe immersion depth of the block, not a 10 cm rise in the tank’s waterline, which needs tank geometry.

Cross-check with average density

Average object density is 2/0.004=500 kg/m³, half water density, agreeing with the half-volume floating result. Use total mass and external volume, not one constituent’s material density. A result 2000 calls for checking liter conversion and copied mass before moving the waterline. Density and force checks must share premises: calculating with 2 kg in one ledger and silently switching to 3 kg in the other is no agreement.

A heavier equal-volume object still has buoyancy

For object B keep 4 L but change mass to 6 kg. Fully submerged buoyancy remains 39.2 N, weight becomes 58.8 N and net force 19.6 N downward. Sinking does not mean zero buoyancy. Before bottom contact, full immersion at constant water density does not increase displaced volume merely with depth. Bottom support requires another force diagram. State that only mass changed and keep A’s 2 L floating result separate; a heavier object does not change water density.

In a separate constrained B case, a string from above holds the 6 kg object fully submerged at rest: explicitly replace the no-string premise. Tension plus 39.2 N buoyancy balances 58.8 N weight, giving 19.6 N tension, not buoyancy. Keep this separate from freely sinking B, and do not present paper tension as a measured spring-scale reading.

Trace equations by state while reading

Put A fully immersed, A floating and B fully immersed on separate PDF pages marked accordingly. In ManYue identify the state before following its equations. Use an existing bookmark for the confusing waterline and record state, displacement, two forces and net tendency externally. Enlarge unreadable labels rather than guessing from arrow length. Reader zoom does not change displaced volume, and screen rotation does not redefine gravity in the physical scene: follow the declared scene direction when the entire diagram rotates.

Reopen and state forces before floating or sinking

Reopen all three pages, verify units/states and interpret 39.2 versus 19.6, 19.6 versus 19.6 and 39.2 versus 58.8. Explain the 2 L equilibrium result, the constant-section condition for half-height and buoyancy on a sinking object. Salt water, compressible objects or strings require changed premises before recalculation. Archive drawings and both methods. Values are original-model deductions; successful display verifies neither real material density, device sensors nor immersion performance.
